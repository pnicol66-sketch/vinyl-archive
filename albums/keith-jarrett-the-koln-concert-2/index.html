The Album

Recorded live at the Cologne Opera House on 24 January 1975, The Köln Concert captured Keith Jarrett's completely improvised solo piano performance, played on an undersized, out-of-tune rehearsal instrument substituted at the last minute after the concert grand promised by the venue was unavailable. Jarrett, exhausted and reportedly ready to cancel, was persuaded by an eighteen-year-old promoter to go on anyway, and the resulting concert became a landmark of spontaneous composition, blending gospel, folk, minimalist vamps and classical figuration into long continuous arcs. It arrived at a pivotal moment in Jarrett's career, following his American and European quartet work, and established him as the pre-eminent solo improviser in jazz. Produced by Manfred Eicher, it became ECM's best-selling release and remains the best-selling solo piano album and one of the best-selling jazz albums in history. Critics praised its lyrical, meditative sweep, and it is widely credited with bringing improvised piano music to a mainstream listening audience well beyond the jazz world. Its influence persists in the solo-piano concert tradition and in the broader crossover appeal of instrumental music.

The Label

The label photographed is teal/green with the boxed "ECM" logo only (no "ECM Records" wordmark), a GEMA rights box, "33 M" speed marking, and rim text beginning "ALLE URHEBER- UND LEISTUNGSSCHUTZRECHTE VORBEHALTEN" - no LC label code is present. The very first Nov-1975 German pressing carried an "ECM Records" wordmark logo, which was replaced by the box-only "ECM" logo in 1976, while an LC rights-society code was only added on subsequent German pressings; the absence of both the wordmark and an LC code places this copy in that 1976 window, after the true first German issue but before the LC-coded pressing represented by the collection's other copy. Deep groove is not present on this copy. Back cover carries the German sub-catalogue number 2641 064 and the München address used on early-to-mid 1970s ECM German pressings, confirming domestic German manufacture.

Fidelity

Mastered by Sterling Sound (for the US pressings) with German pressings cut from the same ECM master tapes at a German facility; ECM's own engineering (Martin Wieland) produced the warm, reverberant piano sound collectors associate with the label's mid-1970s solo recordings. Collectors on the forums rate original US and German pressings as sonically comparable and both superior to later CD transfers and most digital reissues, with individual stamper variation (some copies dubbed "hot stampers" by dealers) accounting for more difference than country of origin. The 2010s 180g European reissue is generally considered a solid but not clearly superior remaster next to a clean original German or US pressing. This copy, an early-to-mid 1970s German pressing, sits within the well-regarded vintage tier, below only the very first German and US issues in strict chronological terms but not in sonic quality.

Notes

This copy's runout numbers ("2301 064"/"2301 065" prefixed) and "MADE IN W. GERMANY"/München back-cover address show it is a German-manufactured pressing, not the US Columbia Terre Haute pressing (Sterling-cut, T-etched runouts, Polydor Inc. distribution). Within the German pressings, the boxed "ECM"-only label logo and absence of an LC rights code place it after the Nov-1975 first German issue (which used an "ECM Records" wordmark) but before the LC 02516-coded German pressing represented by the collection's other copy of this catalogue number. This copy shows no deep-groove, promo, or eye-count markings.
