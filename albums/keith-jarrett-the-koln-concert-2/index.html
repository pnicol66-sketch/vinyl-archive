This Pressing

Later German pressing (c.1977-80) - boxed ECM logo label, no LC code, rim text dated not before 1977; postdates the 1975 wordmark first issue and 1976 boxed-logo issue, predates the LC-coded German pressing in the collection.

The Album

Recorded live at the Cologne Opera House on 24 January 1975, The Köln Concert documents a single continuous solo improvisation by pianist Keith Jarrett, performed on a small, under-rehearsed studio instrument substituted at the last minute after the promised concert grand could not be used. Jarrett, exhausted and reportedly close to cancelling the performance, went on anyway, and the resulting movements - spanning gospel-tinged vamps, folk-like melodies, minimalist ostinatos and sweeping classical figuration - became one of the most celebrated acts of real-time composition in modern music. The album arrived at a turning point in Jarrett's career, following his work with the American and European quartets and his earlier solo recordings, and it established him as the foremost unaccompanied improviser in jazz. Produced by Manfred Eicher, it went on to become the label's best-selling release, the best-selling solo piano album, and one of the best-selling jazz recordings of all time. Critics responded to its lyrical, meditative sweep and its ability to reach listeners far beyond a specialist jazz audience. Its success reshaped expectations for solo-piano concerts and helped define a spacious, introspective style of improvised music that remains a touchstone for later generations of pianists.

The Label

The label photographed is teal/green with the boxed 'ECM' logo only, a GEMA rights box, the '33 M' speed marking, and rim text beginning 'Alle Urheber- Und Leistungsschutzrechte Vorbehalten'; no rights-society (LC) code is printed on this label. ECM's German pressings moved from an 'ECM Records' wordmark logo at the November 1975 launch to this boxed logo-only design in 1976, and the rim-text wording seen here was only introduced in 1977, so the design places manufacture in 1977 or later even without an LC code, which was only added on a subsequent redesign. That later redesign, with a plain-text 'ECM' logo and the LC 02516 code, appears on another German pressing of this catalogue number in the collection and marks the next stage of the same chronology. The label on this copy is flat, with no deep-groove ring visible.

Fidelity

German pressings of The Köln Concert were cut from the same master tapes used for the US Sterling Sound cut, with ECM's house engineering producing the warm, reverberant piano sound associated with the label's mid-1970s solo recordings. Collectors on the forums generally rate vintage German and US pressings as comparable to one another and superior to the CD transfers and most later digital reissues, with stamper generation mattering more than country of pressing. As a later-1970s German repress rather than the true first issue, this copy sits a step below the earliest German and US pressings in strict seniority, though its sonic quality remains within the same well-regarded vintage tier and clearly above the 2010s 180g European reissue, which collectors treat as solid but not a clear improvement on a clean original-era pressing.

Notes

This is a later German pressing of The Köln Concert, not the November 1975 first issue. The label carries the boxed ECM logo introduced in 1976 and the GEMA rights box with no rights-society code printed, but its rim-text wording was only introduced in 1977, which places manufacture in 1977 or later rather than the 1976 window the catalogue entry assigned it. The runout numbering follows German plant conventions rather than the US Pitman or Terre Haute pressings. It therefore sits after the 1975 first issue and the plain 1976 boxed-logo reissue, and before the later German pressing in the collection that carries the LC 02516 code and a redesigned plain-text ECM logo; that later copy's own notes describing it simply as 'post-1976' should be refined the same way, since its LC code dates it no earlier than 1977 as well.
