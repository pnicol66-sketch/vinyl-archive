This Pressing

Special Re-Release by OJL (mono) - a later Testament pressing of the 1966 original, identified by the OJL re-release line on the label and the RE-suffixed runouts on both sides.

The Album

Otis Spann grew up in Belzoni, Mississippi, before moving to Chicago in the mid-1940s, where he became the pianist most closely identified with Muddy Waters's postwar band and absorbed his style directly from Big Maceo Merriweather. By the mid-1960s he was recognised by his peers as the leading blues pianist of his generation, equally at home behind a singer or stepping out as a bandleader. This Testament session, overseen by blues scholar and producer Pete Welding, paired Spann's solo and small-combo performances - unaccompanied piano blues alongside full-band Chicago shuffle numbers cut with musicians drawn from the Waters circle. The record captures both sides of his musicianship: the deep, Delta-rooted solo pieces and the driving, horn-less ensemble blues that defined the postwar South Side sound. It stands as one of the definitive studio documents of Spann outside the Waters band, valued by blues historians for showing the breadth of his vocabulary from barrelhouse boogie to slow, spoken-word blues. The album has long been regarded as a cornerstone of the Testament label's documentary approach to Chicago blues and remains a touchstone recording in Spann's catalogue.

The Label

The side labels are a pale grey-white ground with black print, a line-drawn seated guitarist under an arched “TESTAMENT RECORDS / BLUES JAZZ FOLKSONG” rim legend, and the small print beneath the copyright line reads “Special Re-Release by OJL.” That OJL credit, paired with the two runouts both carrying a RE (re-cut/repress) suffix, places this copy with the batch of Testament titles that Origin Jazz Library took over distributing and repressing after the label’s first 1966 run, not with the original Chicago-address pressing. The plain first-issue Testament label for this title carries no OJL line; later copies of the same catalogue number were pressed in tan and in gold-label variants before the OJL-credited run. This copy therefore sits below the 1966 originals in the issue history, even though it reproduces the original cover and track listing unchanged.

Fidelity

The metalwork is the same Testament mono cut used for the original 1966 pressing - the runouts carry only an added RE suffix rather than a new mastering code - so this copy shares the same basic sound as the first pressing, pressed from a later stamper generation. No audiophile remaster or specialist reissue of this title exists; the Testament mono cut, in whichever pressing, remains the only vinyl source, and collectors treat the plain first pressing as the most desirable of the vinyl copies on sonic grounds given its earlier stamper generation.

Notes

This copy carries the “Special Re-Release by OJL” line printed on both side labels, and its runouts read “T2211 Side A Re” and “T2211 Side B Re,” matching the re-release pressing of this catalogue number rather than the plain 1966 Testament issue. The label design (pale ground, line-drawn guitarist, arched rim lettering) is unchanged from the original artwork, but the OJL credit and RE-suffixed runouts place it after the original pressing and the tan- and gold-label repressings in the issue history. The ℗/© 1965 date on the label is the record's earliest possible pressing date, not evidence of an early copy.
