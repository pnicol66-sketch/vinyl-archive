The Album

Delightfulee was assembled from two 1966 Van Gelder Studio sessions that found Lee Morgan working in two very different settings: a tight quintet with Joe Henderson, McCoy Tyner, Bob Cranshaw and Billy Higgins, and a larger, more ambitious nonet-plus session featuring Wayne Shorter, Phil Woods, Philly Joe Jones and a horn section arranged with unusual colors like French horn and bass clarinet. The album arrived at a pivotal moment in Morgan's career, coming after the massive crossover success of "The Sidewinder" (1964) had made him Blue Note's most commercially valuable young trumpeter, and it shows him balancing that newfound visibility with genuinely exploratory hard-bop writing rather than repeating a hit formula. Original compositions like "Ca-Lee-So" and "Zambia" sit alongside a soulful reading of the Beatles' "Yesterday" and the Broadway standard "Sunrise, Sunset," reflecting the era's growing jazz appetite for pop and show tunes. Morgan, a Philadelphia prodigy who had already passed through Dizzy Gillespie's band and the Jazz Messengers before his Blue Note stardom, plays with the fluid, blues-drenched brilliance and biting tone that made him one of the defining hard-bop trumpet voices of the 1960s. Liner notes by noted critic Leonard Feather situate the record within Morgan's ongoing evolution as both composer and bandleader. Though somewhat overshadowed commercially by the earlier boogaloo hits, Delightfulee is valued by collectors and critics as a showcase of Morgan's compositional range and his gift for pairing top-tier sidemen in service of adventurous small- and medium-group hard bop.

The Label

The labels on this copy are white with a blue rim and read "BLUE NOTE RECORDS · A DIVISION OF LIBERTY RECORDS, INC." arcing above the spindle, with no NY address text - this is the design cvinyl.com/bsnpubs class as the "sixth" Blue Note label, used c.1966-1970 after Liberty's 1965 acquisition of the company. Because Delightfulee was recorded in 1966 and not issued until November 1967, it was released entirely within this Liberty-ownership window, so this white/blue Liberty label IS the original first-pressing design for this title (there is no earlier Lexington Ave. or W. 63rd St. label for this specific album, since those addresses predate the recording date). The BNST 84243-A / BNST 84243-B matrix with VAN GELDER stamp on both sides matches the design and era, confirming this as an original first pressing rather than the later 1970 United Artists-address label or subsequent reissues.

Fidelity

Both sides were cut and stamped by Rudy Van Gelder directly from his original 1966 session tapes, as confirmed by the VAN GELDER etch in the runout, giving this pressing the direct, punchy Van Gelder studio sound typical of period Blue Note stereo LPs. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally regard these Liberty-era Van Gelder-mastered originals as sonically very close to the true first pressings, since Van Gelder continued to cut the lacquers himself through the late 1960s despite the change in label ownership. This pressing outranks the 1970 United Artists-address reissue and the 1985 Manhattan-era reissue, both of which used later stampers, and it is considerably more desirable than the 2007 CD reissue, which some listeners have criticized as sounding harsher than the analog originals; no audiophile (Music Matters/Analogue Productions/Tone Poet) reissue of this specific title currently exists.

Notes

This is an original 1967 Blue Note stereo first pressing of Delightfulee (BST 84243). The white label with blue rim reading "A Division of Liberty Records, Inc." is the design Blue Note used from 1966-1970 following the Liberty buyout, and since this title wasn't recorded until 1966 and not released until late 1967, this Liberty-era label is the true first-issue design for this catalog number. Matrix BNST 84243-A / B-1 with the VAN GELDER stamp on both sides confirms Van Gelder mastering from the original session tapes and matches the earliest known runout configuration reported for this title, distinguishing it from the later United Artists-address labels and 1970s/1985 reissues.
