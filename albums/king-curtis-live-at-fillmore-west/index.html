The Album

Recorded over three nights in March 1971 at Bill Graham's Fillmore West, this album caught King Curtis at the peak of his powers as a bandleader rather than purely a session saxophonist, fronting a crack unit built from his longtime Kingpins - Cornell Dupree, Jerry Jemmott and Bernard Purdie - augmented by Billy Preston on organ and the Memphis Horns. Curtis, who had built his reputation cutting classic instrumental breaks and backing Aretha Franklin, Sam Cooke and the Coasters, used the set to fuse his Southern R&B roots with the rock audience he was touring alongside, having recently opened shows for the Rolling Stones. The programme reworks the era's biggest pop and rock songs as horn-driven soul-jazz vehicles, turning Procol Harum's ‘A Whiter Shade of Pale’ and Led Zeppelin's ‘Whole Lotta Love’ into extended instrumental showcases alongside Curtis's own staples like ‘Memphis Soul Stew’ and ‘Soul Serenade’. Critics at the time and since have singled it out as a high point of live soul-jazz recording and one of the definitive documents of Curtis's bandleading, its reputation only deepened by his murder later that same year. It remains a touchstone recording for the Memphis and Muscle Shoals school of session musicianship transplanted to a rock-era concert stage.

The Label

The label shows the yellow ATCO design with black print and the eight-segment colour pinwheel logo in a black arch over a black ATCO box, set at the left of the spindle hole, with the manufacturing line reading MFG. BY ATLANTIC RECORDING CORP., 1841 BROADWAY, NEW YORK, N.Y. This is the third ATCO house label, introduced for numbers in the low-to-mid 33-200s and used through the end of the 33-100/300 numbering into the SD-7000 series, the design current at this title's 1971 release. The label has a flat edge with no deep groove. The runout reads ST-C-712265 PR on side one and ST-C-712266 PR on side two, carrying the same plant suffix as the Philips Recording Company-pressed US stereo gatefold issue, placing this record with the original US stereo pressing rather than a later repress.

Fidelity

This is an analog cut from the original 1971 multitrack masters pressed at a commercial US plant of average quality for the era; collectors generally rate the original ATCO stereo pressing as a lively, punchy cut well suited to the horn-heavy live mix, if with the modest surface quality typical of early-1970s commercial pressings. It sits above the various later reissue-label copies in collector esteem but below the 2021 180g Sterling-cut audiophile reissue, which was mastered directly from the analog tapes by a noted mastering engineer for a dedicated vinyl subscription series and is generally regarded as the cleanest-sounding cut of the album to date.

Notes

This is the original 1971 US ATCO stereo release, SD 33-359, in the yellow ATCO house label current for this catalogue range, pressed in gatefold format. The runout carries the PR plant suffix of the Philips Recording Company-pressed stereo gatefold issue on both sides, distinguishing this record from the white-label mono and stereo promos and from the later reissues on different numbers and labels.
