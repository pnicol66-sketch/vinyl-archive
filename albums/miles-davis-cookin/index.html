The Album

Cookin' With the Miles Davis Quintet was assembled from two marathon single-day sessions, May 11 and October 26, 1956, that Davis booked to fulfill his Prestige contract before moving to Columbia Records. Rather than a carefully planned studio project, producer Bob Weinstock simply had the working quintet - Davis, John Coltrane, Red Garland, Paul Chambers, and Philly Joe Jones - run through its live club repertoire in one or two takes with no overdubs, capturing the band's road-tested interplay at its peak. The results, later parceled across four LPs (Cookin', Relaxin', Workin', Steamin'), are prized as some of the most unguarded, natural small-group jazz of the era, pairing standards like the ballad feature with hard-swinging vehicles by Sonny Rollins and Davis himself. This was the first great Miles Davis Quintet with Coltrane, a group whose brief run reshaped both men's careers and became a cornerstone of hard bop small-group vocabulary. Critics and historians have long ranked the quintet's chemistry here among the most influential in jazz, and the album is consistently cited among the essential entries in the Davis-Coltrane discography.

The Label

This copy shows the yellow Prestige "fireworks" label with a single yellow starburst/fireworks graphic, the "PRESTIGE RECORDS INC. \u2022 446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C." address on the rim, and a deep-groove ring pressed into both sides, all hallmarks of the earliest New York-era Prestige LP design used from the mid-1950s until the label's mid-1960s move to Bergenfield/Englewood Cliffs, NJ, when the design switched to the blue Prestige label. The back cover's "447 West 50th Street" address is a minor, well-documented in-house discrepancy on original-era Prestige jackets and does not indicate a later pressing. Discogs variant research confirms this exact label/address/deep-groove combination as pressed by Abbey Record Manufacturing Co., matching the typed runout's "AB" etch. The RVG hand-etch plus a low "1" stamper code on the A-side places this copy at or very near the first stamper generation for this catalog number, ahead of the later Bergenfield-address second pressings (which retain deep groove and RVG but drop the NYC address) and well ahead of the blue-label and Fantasy/OJC reissues.

Fidelity

Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder directly from the original 1956 session tapes, with the hand-etched RVG stamp confirming an original-metal cut rather than a later re-mastering. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv consistently rate these original mono Van Gelder-cut Prestige pressings as the most vital and immediate versions of the Marathon sessions material, ahead of the 1960s Bergenfield-address second pressings (still RVG-sourced but later-generation stampers) and well ahead of 1970s-80s Fantasy/OJC reissues, which used more processed remastering. Modern audiophile cuts such as Analogue Productions' 200g APRJ 7094 (Kevin Gray) offer quieter, high-fidelity surfaces but are generally regarded as smoother and less raw than this original. The Abbey Record Mfg. pressing here is a solid, standard-quality period plant job rather than a premium pressing, but the early metal generation is the decisive factor for sound quality.
