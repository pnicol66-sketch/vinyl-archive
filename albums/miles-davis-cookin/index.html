The Album

Cookin' With the Miles Davis Quintet was drawn from two marathon single-day studio sessions - May 11 and October 26, 1956 - that Miles Davis booked with his newly formed quintet to fulfill his contract with Prestige before departing for Columbia Records. Rather than carefully producing a single planned album, Davis and Bob Weinstock had the working band - Davis, John Coltrane, Red Garland, Paul Chambers, and Philly Joe Jones - simply play through its nightly club book live in the studio with no overdubs or retakes, capturing the group's working-band chemistry at its peak. The resulting music, split later across the four LPs Cookin', Relaxin', Workin', and Steamin', is often cited as some of the most natural, unguarded small-group jazz on record, mixing standards like the ballad staple with hard-swinging originals and Sonny Rollins's

The Label

This copy carries the classic yellow "fireworks" Prestige label with the "PRESTIGE RECORDS INC. \u2022 446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C." address text and a deep-groove ring stamped into both sides, hallmarks of Prestige's earliest New York pressings run out of the 446 W. 50th Street offices used from the mid-1950s until the label's move to Bergenfield/Englewood Cliffs, NJ in the mid-1960s (when the labels switched to the blue Prestige design). The back cover shows the near-identical "447 West 50th Street" address, a small, well-documented in-house discrepancy seen on many original Prestige jackets of this era and not indicative of a later pressing. The typed runouts show RVG (Rudy Van Gelder) hand-etched stamps on both sides plus an "AB" etch identifying the Abbey Record Manufacturing Co. pressing plant, and the A-side stamper is coded "1" - together these place this copy solidly as an original 1957 first-pressing run, at or very near the earliest stamper generation, rather than a later yellow-label restrike.

Fidelity

Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder directly from his original 1956 session tapes, with the RVG stamp in the deadwax confirming this is an original-metal cut rather than a later re-mastering. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv consistently rate these original mono Van Gelder-mastered Prestige LPs as the most vital and immediate-sounding versions of the Marathon sessions material, ahead of the 1960s blue-label Bergenfield repressings (still RVG-sourced but from later-generation stampers) and well ahead of the 1970s-80s Fantasy/OJC reissues, which used newer, more processed mastering. Modern audiophile reissues such as Analogue Productions' 200g APRJ 7094 (mastered by Kevin Gray) are prized for quiet, high-fidelity surfaces but are generally regarded as smoother/more modern-sounding than the original's raw immediacy; the Abbey Record Mfg. pressing on this copy is a solid, standard-quality period pressing rather than a premium plant, but the metal and stamper generation are what matter most here.
