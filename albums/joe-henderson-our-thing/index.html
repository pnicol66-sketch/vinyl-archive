The Album

Our Thing was recorded in September 1963, the second album Joe Henderson led for Blue Note following his debut Page One earlier that year, and it reunited him with trumpeter Kenny Dorham, who had brought the young tenor saxophonist to the label. Henderson, barely into his mid-twenties, had already distinguished himself with a big, assertive tone and a harmonically inquisitive style that set him apart from the prevailing hard-bop tenor voices of the day. The session paired Henderson's and Dorham's original compositions with a simple two-horn, no-chordal-clutter format built around Andrew Hill's spare, exploratory piano, Eddie Khan's bass and Pete La Roca's drums, giving the music an open, searching quality that pointed toward the more abstract directions jazz was taking in the mid-1960s. Dorham, an important but often underrated composer and mentor figure, contributed several of the album's most memorable themes, while Henderson's own pieces showcased the rhythmic displacement and tonal ambiguity that would become hallmarks of his mature style. Album notes from the period, written by a leading jazz critic, framed the record as a demonstration of the mutually beneficial partnership between Henderson and Dorham, with Hill's increasingly abstract harmonic sensibility pushing the group toward new territory without abandoning swing. The record has endured as one of the stronger entries in Henderson's run of 1960s Blue Note albums, valued by critics and collectors for its blend of hard-bop drive and modernist exploration, and it remains an important document of the transitional period in jazz just before the mid-decade avant-garde breakthroughs.

The Label

The labels are white with a blue perimeter band carrying a vertical BLUE NOTE wordmark and the rim text “BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC · NEW YORK USA,” reproducing the design the label used between 1961 and 1967. The same labels, however, print an LC 00133 rights-society code and a ©/℗ 2026 Capitol Records line; a label code of that kind did not exist before 1976 and a copyright year of 2026 cannot predate the record it is printed on, so this is a modern reissue label that faithfully copies the vintage New York USA design rather than an original pressing of that era. The small print states the record was “originally released in 1964 on Blue Note Records,” a historical reproduction of the label text rather than an early pressing.

Fidelity

This pressing is cut by Kevin Gray at his mastering facility from the original analog tapes and pressed on 180-gram vinyl in Europe as part of Blue Note's Classic Vinyl Series. Collectors generally regard Kevin Gray's all-analog cuts favorably for clarity and tonal balance, placing this series above standard reissues and digitally sourced pressings, though it sits behind the earlier 45 RPM audiophile two-LP reissue of this title, which collectors widely rate as the sonic benchmark for the album, and behind the original-era pressings cut directly by the studio engineer who recorded the session. Within the modern vinyl market it is viewed as a strong value option for those seeking an analog-sourced cut without the premium commanded by the 45 RPM edition.

Notes

The label design reproduces the 1961-67 New York USA Blue Note look, but the ©/℗ 2026 date and the LC 00133 rights code on the same labels date this copy itself to 2026 or later, since a label cannot be printed before the year and the code it carries. The runout carries a “KG” etch for Kevin Gray's mastering at Coherent Audio and plant codes for a European (Optimal/MPO-family) pressing, the identity of the Blue Note Classic Vinyl Series audiophile reissue on 180-gram vinyl. The jacket likewise notes the album was “originally released in 1964,” with the New York USA label text standing as historical reproduction rather than an original pressing.
