The Album

Stanley Turrentine came up through rhythm-and-blues bands before settling into the Blue Note stable in the late 1950s, where his big, vocal-like tenor tone and blues-soaked phrasing made him one of the label's most commercially reliable voices through the 1960s. This compilation gathers performances from across that decade, from the soul-jazz organ-combo dates with Shirley Scott that opened his Blue Note tenure to the lusher, larger-ensemble sessions he recorded later in the 1960s pairing his horn with strings, brass choirs and pop and Brazilian repertoire. The earliest selections, cut with Blue Mitchell, Shirley Scott, Earl May and Al Harewood, capture the soul-jazz organ-combo sound that first established Turrentine's popular following. The later sessions, arranged for larger horn sections and featuring pianists Hank Jones and McCoy Tyner alongside guitarists Kenny Burrell and Bucky Pizzarelli, show Turrentine adapting his hard-bop foundation to the mood-music and pop-song repertoire that Blue Note increasingly favored as the decade wore on. Turrentine's blend of gospel-rooted feeling and polished, radio-friendly arrangements helped keep him commercially viable even as the jazz mainstream fractured in the late 1960s, and his work from this period remains a touchstone of the soul-jazz and crossover-jazz style. Critics have long treated the anthologized sessions here as a mixed bag - the organ-combo sides are generally prized by hard-bop and soul-jazz collectors, while the string- and pop-song-laden later sessions divide opinion between those who hear mellow sophistication and those who hear commercial dilution.

The Label

All four labels carry the all-blue Blue Note design used for The Blue Note Re-Issue Series: a large white b carrying a musical note at the upper left, the curved wording THE BLUE NOTE RE-ISSUE SERIES around it, and a rim crediting United Artists Music and Records Group, Inc., Los Angeles, California 90028. The label field is flat, full-colour blue rather than a stamped ring: this is a flat label, with no deep groove. This is the United Artists-era Blue Note design that replaced the earlier Liberty/United Artists trademark labels once the Re-Issue Series of double-LP compilations began appearing from the mid-1970s. The label carries a 1975 copyright line, placing this pressing within the first catalogue-number issue of this anthology. As a compilation drawn from several original Blue Note sessions, this pressing is a later anthology issue, not a first pressing of any underlying studio album its tracks were first issued on.

Fidelity

The tracks here were remastered from the original Blue Note session tapes for this mid-1970s compilation rather than cut fresh by Rudy Van Gelder for the occasion, and the United Artists-era Blue Note pressings of this period are generally regarded by collectors as routine commercial pressings rather than audiophile products. Collectors rate the sound as serviceable but secondary to the original single-album Blue Note pressings of these same sessions, several of which carry Van Gelder-cut stereo or mono masters prized well above this anthology's fidelity. This compilation has not been the subject of any audiophile reissue (Tone Poet, Music Matters, Analogue Productions or similar), so it ranks below the original LP pressings of the source albums but is not superseded by any audiophile cut of its own.

Notes

This is a 1975 US pressing of the Blue Note Re-Issue Series double-LP compilation BN-LA394-H2, gathering Stanley Turrentine Blue Note session tracks from 1963-1968. All four labels carry the all-blue, United Artists-era Blue Note Re-Issue Series design with a 1975 copyright line, which places the pressing in the Re-Issue Series era and rules out any pressing before that year. The runout for each side reads the catalogue prefix BN-LA394 with a sequential side number, the form of a United Artists-manufactured Blue Note pressing of this period. As a compilation anthology it has no bearing on the original individual album pressings its tracks were first issued on.
