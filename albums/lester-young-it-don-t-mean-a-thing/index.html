The Album

Lester Young, the tenor saxophonist whose light, floating tone and behind-the-beat phrasing had already reshaped jazz saxophone during his years with Count Basie and on the classic Billie Holiday sides of the 1930s, spent much of the 1950s recording prolifically for Norman Granz's Norgran and Verve labels. This set gathers a group of small-combo studio sides, most cut with a rhythm section drawn from Oscar Peterson's working trio, pairing Young's famously relaxed, singing style against the crisp, driving support of Peterson, Barney Kessel and Ray Brown. The album takes its title from the Ellington-Mills standard that anchors the second side, a tune Young - who insisted a musician should always know a song's lyrics before improvising on it - treats with characteristic restraint rather than bravado. Alongside durable standards like "Somebody Loves Me" and "I'm in the Mood for Love," Young contributes three originals ("Another Mambo," "Kiss Me Again," "Big Top Blues") that show his writing hewing to the same understated, blues-tinged lyricism as his horn work. By this point Young was past his mid-1930s and Basie-era commercial peak but still deeply admired by a generation of tenor players who had absorbed his cooler, more melodic alternative to Coleman Hawkins's muscular approach. The record was received as a solid, unhurried entry in Young's extensive Granz discography, valued less for innovation than for the warmth and consistency of a mature stylist still very much in command of his voice. It remains a representative document of Young's Verve years and of the intimate, blowing-session format Granz favored for his stable of swing and bebop-adjacent veterans.

The Label

The side labels are the grey Verve "Clef Series" design with a single white trumpeter/musician silhouette logo at left, script "Verve / Records" logotype at top, and an arc of rim text reading "MICROGROOVE LONG PLAYING" with "VERVE RECORDS, INC. MADE IN U.S.A." beneath - the design Verve used circa 1956-57 to carry over the Clef/Norgran catalog after Norman Granz consolidated his labels into Verve. This copy is stamped deep groove and its runout carries the old Norgran matrix prefix (MGN-1100) alongside the new Verve number (MGV-8187), confirming it as the first Verve pressing cut from the same lacquers originally intended for the (unissued as such) Norgran MGN-1100/reissue of MGN-1022. A later-1961 Verve pressing replaced this label with a plain black 'Verve' script design and a freshly cut matrix (block-shaped "S"), which places any copy with that label as a second/later pressing rather than this one.

Fidelity

Mastered from the original Norgran/Verve session tapes for this Clef Series pressing; Verve's mid-1950s mono cutting for the Granz catalog is generally solid if unspectacular by later audiophile standards, with warmth typical of the era's flat mono transfers. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally regard these deep-groove Clef/Verve trumpeter-label pressings as the most desirable vintage cut for Young's Granz-era sides, ahead of the plain-label 1961 Verve reissue and far ahead of later Japanese Polydor/Verve pressings. No audiophile reissue (Analogue Productions, Mobile Fidelity, etc.) of this specific title is documented, so this first pressing sits at the top of the fidelity hierarchy for the album.

Notes

This is the 1957 Verve Clef Series first pressing of MGV-8187, reissuing the Norgran MGN-1022 recordings under a new catalog number. The grey trumpeter-logo label with deep groove and the runout's retained MGN-1100 prefix (alongside MGV-8187) are the decisive markers of this first Verve issue, predating the plain black-script Verve label used from circa 1961 onward. No later reissue elements (e.g. re-cut block-"S" matrix, non-deep-groove stampers) are present on this copy.
