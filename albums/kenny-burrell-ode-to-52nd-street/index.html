The Album

Recorded in September 1967 at Chess's Ter Mar Studios in Chicago, Ode to 52nd Street paired guitarist Kenny Burrell - on loan from Verve - with the ambitious arranging of Richard Evans, Cadet's in-house writer-producer who was pushing the label's jazz artists toward larger orchestral canvases in the late 1960s. The centerpiece is the four-part 'Suite for Guitar and Orchestra,' a through-composed work that let Burrell stretch beyond the blues-drenched hard-bop persona he'd built at Blue Note and Prestige into something closer to a concerto, alternating his lyrical, unhurried guitar lines against string and horn charts. The second side balances that ambition with more digestible material: two Evans/Burrell originals, a reading of Dizzy Gillespie's 'Con Alma,' Evans's own Latin-tinged 'Soulero,' and a moody instrumental take on 'Wild Is the Wind.' Burrell, already one of the most versatile and in-demand guitarists of his generation - equally at home behind vocalists, in organ trios, or fronting his own dates - used the album to reassert his standing as what one contemporary account of the sessions called 'the whole player,' a musician as comfortable with formal, orchestrated writing as with straight-ahead swing. The record sits alongside his more celebrated Blue Note and Verve work as a lesser-known but distinctive entry in his catalog, valued by collectors for its adventurous suite and Evans's inventive charts. It never achieved the canonical status of Burrell's Midnight Blue or Guitar Forms, but it remains an interesting document of a guitarist testing orchestral jazz at the height of his career, and of Cadet/Chess's late-'60s push into more ambitious jazz production.

The Label

The side labels show the classic late-1960s Cadet design: a blue-to-white vertical gradient field, the pink-to-cream 'CADET' wordmark at top, and the circular spiral/pinwheel 'flower' device at right - the standard Cadet house label used for Chess-group jazz product from roughly 1966 through the early 1970s, with 'MFG. BY CHESS PRODUCING CORP., CHICAGO, IL 60616' printed around the rim. This label design and address place it as a first-issue Cadet stereo pressing, with no later Cadet label style used. The rim text and Chess Producing Corp. manufacturing credit place this squarely in the original 1967 pressing run before Cadet's catalog was reissued under Chess/GRP/Chessmates branding in later decades.

Fidelity

This is a Chess/Cadet in-house Chicago production (Ter Mar Studios, engineer Doug Brand), not a Van Gelder or RVG-style audiophile cut, and there is no known audiophile reissue (Tone Poet/Music Matters/Speakers Corner) of this specific title. Collectors on the forums generally rate the Chess/Cadet Chicago pressings of this era as solid but unspectacular, full-bodied but occasionally a touch bright on orchestral material. Within this title's own reissue history (Spain 1983, Japan 1997/2021 remasters, 1998 GRP CD/LP reissue), the 1967 US Cadet stereo original is considered the most authentic-sounding vinyl source, ahead of the later reprocessed reissues and well ahead of the digital-sourced GRP CD reissue.
