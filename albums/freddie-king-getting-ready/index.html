2011 Analogue Productions reissue (pressed no earlier than 1980, per its barcode) of the 1971 Shelter original; Shelter-era originals rank above it.

The Album

Getting Ready… marked Freddie King's move to Leon Russell and Denny Cordell's Shelter Records at the dawn of the 1970s, a label built to carry blues and roots artists to a rock-era audience without softening them. The sessions were cut at Chess's own Ter-Mar studio in Chicago - the room that had hosted the Chicago blues giants King grew up on - and mixed at Ardent in Memphis, pairing King with Russell's circle of players including Don Preston, Duck Dunn and Chuck Blackwell. The track list balances vintage Chicago blues staples by Elmore James, Eddie Boyd and Big Bill Broonzy with new songs written for the date, reflecting a conscious bridge between the blues tradition and the rock audience Shelter was courting. The album became a touchstone of King's comeback period, reasserting him as a vital electric-blues guitarist to a new generation of listeners discovering the blues through rock. It sits among the most celebrated records of his Shelter years and is regarded by blues historians as one of the defining documents of blues-rock crossover in the early 1970s. Critics have long praised the record's muscular playing and the chemistry between King and Russell's studio band, and it remains a benchmark in King's catalogue and in the broader story of electric blues reaching a mainstream rock audience.

The Label

Both sides show a salmon-orange label with dark red print, a green triangle-and-reel logo and the script ‘Analogue Productions’ credit beneath it, reproducing the 1971 Shelter catalogue text (℗ & © 1971 Shelter, SHE-8905/SHE 1-8905) line for line. Shelter Records pressed the title from its own Jacksonville and Winchester plants in 1971; the label design here is a faithful facsimile of that original rather than the original itself, made decades later when Analogue Productions licensed the title for an audiophile remaster. The copy's own barcode could not exist on a pressing made before 1980, so however closely the label copies the 1971 text, this is a modern reissue sitting below the Shelter-era originals in the pressing hierarchy.

Fidelity

The lacquers were cut from the analog master tapes at a respected mastering house and pressed on virgin vinyl at a top-tier audiophile plant, with the jacket itself guaranteeing original-tape sourcing and tight quality control. Collectors on the forums rate this cut as quiet, full-bodied and a clear sonic improvement over the Compact Classics-era CD reissues, standing as the best-regarded modern vinyl edition of the album. Original first-pressing Shelter copies are still prized by purists for their vintage cutting character, but this remaster is considered the strongest widely available alternative to them.

Notes

This copy is a 2011 Analogue Productions reissue of the 1971 Shelter original, not an original-era Shelter pressing. Its barcode alone rules out any date before 1980, and the runout carries an etched mastering stamp and an APB-8905 prefix matching the modern audiophile edition rather than the plain SHE-8905 stampers of the original Jacksonville or Winchester pressings. Jacket stickers for a leading audiophile pressing plant and a 100% analog mastering seal point to the same modern manufacture. The side labels otherwise faithfully reproduce the 1971 Shelter orange design and catalogue text.
