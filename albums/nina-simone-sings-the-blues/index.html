The Album

Recorded across sessions at RCA's Studio B in New York in late 1966 and early 1967, this album found Nina Simone deep in her most politically engaged period, refusing easy categorisation as either jazz singer or pop vocalist. The program mixes her own compositions with a Bessie Smith-style blues, a Lil Green standard, an aria drawn from Porgy and Bess, and a reworking of the Animals' arrangement of a traditional folk song, welding blues, gospel-inflected soul and her classically trained piano into a single voice. "Backlash Blues," written with her friend the poet Langston Hughes, stands among her most direct civil-rights statements, delivered with the same command she brings to the playful double-entendre of "I Want a Little Sugar in My Bowl." A small combo of guitar, bass, drums and organ frames her piano and voice throughout rather than an orchestra, giving the record an intimacy that critics and later biographers have singled out as central to its lasting reputation. It remains one of the most personal and frequently cited albums of her RCA years, prized for showing how completely she inhabited the blues idiom while keeping it entirely her own.

The Label

The side labels are a dark grey/black RCA Victor field with silver unboxed "RCA VICTOR" lettering, a single Nipper-and-gramophone logo, "STEREO DYNAGROOVE" arced along the lower rim, and a boxed "LC 00316" rights-society code beside the catalogue number; no side-duration timing appears between the number and the "SIDE 1/2" text. RCA's German pressings used this basic grey Nipper design through the late 1960s and into the 1970s, but the LC code itself is a European labelling requirement that did not exist before 1977, so it cannot appear on a label actually printed in 1967 no matter how closely the rest of the design matches an earlier German issue. That single mark places this specific copy in a run manufactured in 1977 or later, behind both the 1967 US originals and any genuinely contemporaneous 1967 German pressing in the release history.

Fidelity

This German pressing derives from RCA's Dynagroove-era stereo mastering of the 1967 US tapes rather than a dedicated audiophile cut, and as a later repress it was made from further-generation stampers than the earliest German lacquers. Collectors on the forums rate the small-group arrangement and Simone's vocal as vivid on a good Dynagroove pressing, if era-typically more compressed than a flat transfer. It ranks below a clean 1967 US stereo original and well below the widely praised 2003 audiophile remaster mastered flat from the tapes, but above later mass-market reissues and the CD remasters.

Notes

This is a German-manufactured stereo pressing of RCA LSP-3789 in a grey unboxed-logo Nipper label with no side-timing text, but the boxed "LC 00316" rights-society code on the label is a mark that did not exist before 1977, so the copy cannot date to 1967 as its catalogue number and label design might suggest.
