The Album

Recorded across three sessions at RCA Studio B in New York in December 1966 and January 1967, Sings the Blues arrived after Nina Simone had spent several years moving between Philips and RCA, restlessly refusing to be filed away as either a jazz singer or a pop chanteuse. The album gathers original compositions alongside covers of Bessie Smith-style blues, a Lil Green standard, an aria from Porgy and Bess, and Eric Burdon/Animals' arrangement of "The House of the Rising Sun," welding folk-blues, gospel-inflected soul and Simone's classically trained piano into a single, unsentimental record. "Backlash Blues," written with her friend the poet Langston Hughes, stands as one of her most pointed civil-rights statements, delivered with the same steely control she brings to the album's more playful double-entendre numbers like "I Want a Little Sugar in My Bowl." Coming at the height of her activist period, the record reinforced Simone's identity as an artist unwilling to separate craft from conscience, and it remains a touchstone in her catalogue for showing how completely she could inhabit the blues idiom while keeping it entirely her own. Critics and later biographers have singled out the record's emotional directness and its small-group intimacy - guitar, bass, drums and organ framing her piano and voice rather than an orchestra - as key to its enduring reputation among her most personal RCA albums.

The Label

The label in the photo is a dark grey/silver-toned RCA Victor "Nipper" design with unboxed silver "RCA VICTOR" lettering across the top, a single Nipper-and-gramophone logo, "STEREO DYNAGROOVE" arced along the bottom rim, and a German rights-society box reading "LC 00316" - there is no orange background and no blue band beneath the logo, and no side-duration timing is printed between the catalogue number and "SIDE 1/2". This design and the LC/GEMA rights-society marking match Discogs's documented "first German stereo release" of LSP-3789, which used this same grey unboxed-logo label before RCA Germany later re-set the label to add side timings. The absence of the timing text and the plain unboxed logo place this copy at the head of the German pressing run, made shortly after the US 1967 original rather than at the later German re-set.

Fidelity

This is a Dynagroove-era RCA stereo cut manufactured in Germany from US-supplied metalwork/tapes rather than a Sterling or Van Gelder-style audiophile mastering; collectors generally rate the small-group arrangement and Simone's vocal as vivid but note Dynagroove's era-typical compression relative to a flat transfer. It sits below the Speakers Corner 180g audiophile reissue (mastered flat, widely praised on forums.stevehoffman.tv for surface quiet and tonal balance) and below a clean US orange-label original, but above later mass-market reissues and the CD remasters, which are often described as harsher and more compressed.

Notes

This is the first German stereo pressing of RCA LSP-3789, not the US original: the grey/black label with silver unboxed "RCA VICTOR" wording, single Nipper logo, "STEREO DYNAGROOVE" rim text and the German "LC 00316" rights-society code match Discogs's documented first-German-stereo variant, which lacks the side-duration text found on the later German re-set label. The runout (UPRS-4211/UPRS-4212) matches that same German variant's documented matrix. Because it is a German manufacture rather than the US "Living Stereo" orange-label original, it ranks below the US first pressing in desirability but above the later re-set German label and modern reissues.
