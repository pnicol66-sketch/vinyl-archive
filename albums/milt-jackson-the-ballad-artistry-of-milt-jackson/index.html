The Album

Recorded in 1959 for Atlantic under producer Nesuhi Ertegun, this album finds Modern Jazz Quartet vibraphonist Milt Jackson stepping outside the MJQ format to explore ballads in a lush orchestral setting arranged and conducted by a young Quincy Jones. Jackson, celebrated for the singing, blues-tinged lyricism of his vibraphone playing, had by this point established himself as one of the instrument's defining voices through his work with the MJQ, and this session let him linger unhurried over standards like "Tenderly," "Alone Together," and Django Reinhardt's "Nuages" alongside his own compositions "The Cylinder" and "Tomorrow." Jones's string- and woodwind-laced charts gave Jackson a Sinatra-style "concept album" showcase rare for a jazz instrumentalist of the era, cushioning his improvisations without smothering them. The record extended Atlantic's mid-century strategy of pairing its top modern jazz soloists with sophisticated orchestral backdrops, a lineage that also produced string albums for other Atlantic artists. Critically the album has long been regarded as one of the most persuasive demonstrations of Jackson's ballad artistry outside the MJQ, valued for its restraint and warmth rather than displays of technique. It remains a touchstone for listeners exploring the intersection of hard bop sensibility and orchestral pop-jazz arranging in the late 1950s.

The Label

The photographed label is red on the top half and magenta/purple on the bottom, split by a white centre band reading ATLANTIC / SIDE with a black multi-blade pinwheel ('fan') logo at right - the design Atlantic collectors call the 'Black Fan' label. This design succeeded the earlier black 'bullseye' label used at Atlantic 1342's 1959-60 first issue, and also succeeded an interim red/purple 'white fan' variant, remaining in use roughly 1962-66. Its presence here (rather than the black bullseye label of the true first pressing) places this copy several pressing generations after the original issue, in the mid-1960s repress window while Atlantic kept the catalog number in print with updated label stock, corroborated by bsnpubs' listing of SD-1342 in the Atlantic 1300s catalog run.

Fidelity

Atlantic's late-1950s jazz sessions of this kind were typically engineered for mono cutting by the label's in-house team; this pressing reuses the same 11713/11714 mastering numbers as earlier pressings but on later 'black fan' label stock, suggesting a stamper generation removed from the earliest lacquers. Collectors generally rate true first-issue black-label Atlantic mono jazz pressings from this era as the most vivid and dynamic, with 1962-66 black-fan repressings a small step behind due to stamper regeneration, though still full analog cuts. No audiophile remaster of this specific title is known to exist, so within the album's fidelity hierarchy this pressing ranks below a genuine first pressing but above later 1970s stereo-only reissues or any digital transfer.

Notes

This is Atlantic 1342 in mono, but the red-top/purple-bottom label with the black pinwheel ('black fan') logo dates the pressing to Atlantic's c.1962-66 label era, not the album's 1959-60 first-issue window (which used a black label with a white/red bullseye graphic). The typed runouts (11713-D W AT / 11714-C AT W) retain the original mastering numbers reused across pressing generations, so the label design is the decisive dating evidence here rather than the matrix prefix alone. Track listing and personnel match the original 1959 sessions; only the pressing generation differs from a true first pressing.
