The Album

Recorded for Blue Note at Manhattan Towers on May 14, 1958, Blue Lights was one of two LPs (Volumes 1 and 2) drawn from a single marathon 'blowing session' led by guitarist Kenny Burrell, then just twenty-seven but already established as one of the era's most fluent and lyrical hard-bop guitarists. Rather than a tightly arranged date, producer Alfred Lion assembled an all-star sextet-plus-rhythm lineup - trumpeter Louis Smith, tenor men Junior Cook and Tina Brooks, pianists Duke Jordan and Bobby Timmons, bassist Sam Jones and drummer Art Blakey - and let the group stretch out on standards and blues heads in the loose, informal spirit that defined many of Blue Note's late-1950s sessions. Burrell's warm, economical playing anchors the date, most memorably on the relaxed, blues-drenched opener 'Yes Baby' and the atmospheric reading of 'Autumn in New York.' The session captures Tina Brooks and Junior Cook, both still emerging tenor voices, alongside a characteristically driving Art Blakey and the contrasting piano styles of Jordan and Timmons. Blue Lights helped cement Burrell's standing as a leader in his own right beyond his prolific work as a Blue Note session guitarist, and it remains a favorite among hard-bop collectors both for its music and for its striking cover, illustrated in its earliest form by a young, pre-fame Andy Warhol working for Blue Note's art department under Reid Miles.

The Label

Side 1 carries a Record Technology Incorporated (RTI) blue-on-white 'TEST PRESSING' label with the handwritten date 'AUGUST 22 2005' and matrix 'BN-ST-81596 A/BRE'; Side 2 is a blank, unprinted white RTI label. This is not a retail Blue Note or Classic Records commercial label at all but a pre-production factory test pressing struck at RTI (Camarillo, CA) ahead of Classic Records' Blue Note stereo 200g reissue series, which used RTI for its all-analog Quiex SV-P pressings from 2003-2007. The printed jacket used with this copy is the standard illustrated Andy Warhol/Reid Miles Blue Lights Vol. 1 stereo cover and the 1958-style back cover text (with the EMI-Capitol license line that appears on the Classic Records-era reprints of this artwork, not on the true 1958 original). The copy also comes with its genuine plain-white test-pressing sleeve (hand-typed 'AUGUST 22 2005 / BN-ST-81596 A/BRE'), confirming this is the actual factory test pressing rather than a later commercial copy dressed up in old artwork.

Fidelity

Mastered by Bernie Grundman (matrix suffix 'BG') for Classic Records' all-analog Blue Note reissue program and pressed at RTI on Quiex SV-P 200g stock; this being a pre-production test pressing, it represents the earliest possible playback of that Grundman stereo cut, ahead of any commercial run. Collectors on forums such as Steve Hoffman's generally rate the Classic Records Blue Note reissues highly for quiet, heavy vinyl and faithful analog mastering, though some prefer the original 1958 Van Gelder stereo cut for its immediacy and tonal balance; Classic's cut is typically ranked a strong, musical alternative rather than a strict sonic upgrade over a clean original RVG pressing. Within the fidelity hierarchy for this title, the original 1958 mono/stereo Van Gelder pressings sit at the top for authenticity, this Grundman/RTI Classic Records stereo cut (and its test pressing stage) ranks as the leading modern audiophile alternative, and the various Japanese Toshiba-EMI/King CD and vinyl reissues sit below both in collector esteem.

Notes

This is not an original 1958 Blue Note pressing but a 2005 RTI (Record Technology Inc.) factory test pressing struck for Classic Records' audiophile 200g stereo reissue of BST 81596. The Side 1 label is a printed RTI 'TEST PRESSING' label hand-dated 'AUGUST 22 2005' with matrix 'BN-ST-81596 A/BRE'; Side 2 carries a blank, unprinted RTI white label, both hallmarks of a pre-production test rather than a retail copy. The matrix suffix 'BG' identifies Bernie Grundman as the mastering engineer, consistent with Classic Records' known practice for its Blue Note reissue series. The copy is accompanied by its original plain test-pressing jacket as well as the standard illustrated reissue cover.
