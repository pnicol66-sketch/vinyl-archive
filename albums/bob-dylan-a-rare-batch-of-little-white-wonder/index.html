The Album

This unofficial collection draws on the trove of early-1960s home recordings, radio broadcasts and outtakes that first circulated in bootleg form as "Great White Wonder," the record widely credited with starting the rock bootleg trade. The material spans Dylan's earliest years in Greenwich Village and his first Columbia sessions, mixing his own compositions with his readings of traditional and borrowed material, a reminder of how deeply he drew on folk and blues sources before his songwriting voice fully emerged. Tracks such as his take on a country blues standard and a Guthrie tribute sit alongside originals that would remain unreleased on official albums for years, giving fans a glimpse of a young artist absorbing and reworking the folk canon in real time. The set captures Dylan at the hinge between interpreter and author, when he was still finding the balance between the tradition he inherited and the songs only he could write. Its unauthorized circulation helped cement Dylan's stature as an artist whose smallest studio leftovers were treated as essential listening, and it fed the mystique that surrounded his vault long before official archival releases caught up. For collectors, releases like this one are valued less for sound quality than for preserving performances that were, for decades, otherwise unavailable in any form.

The Label

The side labels are dark grey/black with silver-white print, a circular device at the top centre on side 1 and a plain JOKER white band on side 2, two white dots flanking the spindle hole, a boxed SIAE rights mark, and a printed copyright line of (c)/"P" 1973 with "Distribuz.: SAAR Milano" and "Made in Italy." Joker's SM-series unofficial pressings of this title were run under the same SM 3605 number across the 1970s and into the 1980s with only minor label and jacket changes, so the design alone does not separate the earliest run from a slightly later one. The printed 1973 copyright and the 14.11.73 runout etch place this specific stamper run in late 1973, the earliest dateable run of this catalogue number, with any undated-but-identical-number copies from 1974 or later standing behind it.

Fidelity

As an unofficial compilation assembled from circulating tape sources rather than studio masters, this pressing was mastered and cut without the quality control of an official release, and surface and tape noise are typical of copies of this title. Collectors generally rate these Joker pressings as adequate but unremarkable sonically, valued for the material rather than the cut. No audiophile reissue of this bootleg exists, so this pressing sits within the ordinary range of the title's many Italian pressings rather than against any superior remaster.

Notes

This is a 1973 Italian bootleg pressing on Joker, catalogue SM 3605. The labels are dark grey/black with silver-white print, carrying a printed 1973 copyright line, "Distribuz.: SAAR Milano," "Made in Italy" and a boxed SIAE mark, with no deep groove and no pressing plant identified on the label. The runout is etched 14.11.73, fixing this pressing to late 1973 and placing it ahead of other pressings issued under the same catalogue number in 1974 and into the 1980s. As an unofficial, unlicensed compilation assembled from circulating tape sources, there is no official first-pressing hierarchy for this title, only the order of Joker's own repeated runs under this same number.
