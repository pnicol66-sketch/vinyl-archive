The Album

Recorded at RCA's Studio B in New York in February 1971, Here Comes the Sun was Nina Simone's penultimate studio album for RCA and came at a turbulent, transitional point in her career, shortly before she left the label and the United States for an extended period of self-exile. Rather than original compositions, the record is built almost entirely from contemporary pop, folk and rock covers - songs by George Harrison, Bob Dylan, Paul Anka and others - reimagined through Simone's classically trained piano and her uniquely commanding, dramatic vocal phrasing, framed by Harold Wheeler's lush orchestral arrangements and a backing chorale. Her version of "My Way" recasts the Sinatra standard with an insistent, Africa-tinged rhythmic drive, while her reading of "Here Comes the Sun" and "Just Like a Woman" show her singular gift for inhabiting other writers' songs and making them unmistakably her own. Coming after the more overtly political Black Gold, the album is a quieter, more interior statement from an artist who had already redefined what a singer-pianist steeped in jazz, blues, gospel and classical training could do in American popular music. Critical reception at the time was mixed to favorable, and the record has since been reassessed by collectors and critics as an underrated late-period entry in Simone's catalog, valued for the strength of her interpretive artistry even when the material itself was drawn from elsewhere. It remains an important document of Simone bridging the soul and singer-songwriter idioms of the early 1970s while retaining the emotional intensity that made her one of the era's most singular vocal interpreters.

The Label

The side labels are the orange RCA Victor "nipper-less" Dynaflex design with the split-ring RCA logo at left and "Victor" spelled out at right, catalogue number LSP-4536 (APRS-4750/4751) above the title, matching RCA's early-1970s orange stereo house style used from 1969 until the changeover to the black Dynaflex label around 1975-76. Two plants pressed this design concurrently: copies stamped "I" in the runouts came from RCA's Indianapolis plant, while copies stamped "R" in the runouts - APRS-4750-1S-A AA R / APRS-4751-7S-A2A R, the matrix numbers carried on this pressing - came from the Rockaway, New Jersey plant, active 1954-1973. Both plant variants sit in the same first-issue orange-label window (1971); the later black-label pressing is a distinct, later reissue design. This copy's orange label and "R" runout stamp place it squarely in the original 1971 pressing run, just from the Rockaway rather than Indianapolis line.

Fidelity

This is a standard-issue early-1970s RCA Dynaflex pressing, mastered from the 1971 Studio B session tapes with no evidence of a specialty audiophile cut; Dynaflex's thin vinyl stock is generally regarded by collectors as sonically adequate but not in the same league as RCA's earlier heavier pressings, and Rockaway-plant copies are considered by collectors on the forums to be roughly on par with Indianapolis pressings from the same era rather than clearly superior or inferior. There is no known audiophile reissue (Analogue Productions, Mobile Fidelity, Speakers Corner, etc.) of this title, and later reissues (1976 black-label RCA, 2011 4 Men With Beards, 2014 Music on Vinyl) are the main alternatives collectors compare against; among vintage US pressings, this orange-label 1971 first-issue configuration is considered the top of the fidelity ladder for the LP.

Notes

1971 RCA Victor LSP-4536 stereo - orange Dynaflex label first-issue pressing. The matrix (APRS-4750-1S-A AA R / APRS-4751-7S-A2A R) carries the "R" plant stamp used by RCA's Rockaway, New Jersey pressing plant, distinguishing it from the concurrently pressed Indianapolis ("I") variant of the same orange-label design. Both plant variants belong to the same 1971 first pressing run and predate the later black Dynaflex label reissue design. This pressing's stamper suffixes do not correspond to a commonly cited Rockaway sub-variant, and the specific stamper generation is not otherwise identified on the record.
