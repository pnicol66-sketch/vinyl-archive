The Album

Recorded September 7, 1956 at Rudy Van Gelder's Hackensack studio, Tenor Conclave is one of the classic Prestige 'battle of the tenors' summit sessions that producer Bob Weinstock built his catalogue on, pitting four contrasting saxophone voices - John Coltrane, Hank Mobley, Zoot Sims and Al Cohn - over the same two standards and two originals so listeners could compare styles directly. At the time Coltrane was still a sideman in the Miles Davis Quintet and not yet the leader he would soon become, making this one of several mid-1950s Prestige dates that captured him developing his harmonically dense 'sheets of sound' language alongside more mainstream swing-rooted players like Sims and Cohn. Mobley, fresh off his own hard-bop dates and contributing the two originals 'Tenor Conclave' and 'Bob's Boys', anchors the session's compositional identity, while Red Garland, Paul Chambers and Art Taylor - Coltrane's Miles Davis Quintet rhythm-section colleagues - provide a tight, blues-inflected pulse throughout. The record is prized by hard-bop collectors as a document of the era's informal jam-session ethos, where individuality of phrasing mattered more than novelty of material, and it remains a touchstone for studying how differently schooled tenor players (bebop-inflected Coltrane and Mobley versus swing-lineage Sims and Cohn) could co-exist within a single blowing date. It has held a steady place in Coltrane discography surveys and hard-bop reissue programs as an accessible, high-quality example of the mid-1950s tenor-battle format.

The Label

The label on this copy is the yellow-and-black 'fireworks' Prestige design with the swirl-and-splatter pattern in the rim, 'PRESTIGE' in a curved black-outlined box at top, and the address 'PRESTIGE RECORDS, INC., 203 SOUTH WASHINGTON AVE., BERGENFIELD, N.J.' printed above the title - the Bergenfield fireworks mono label used from 1958 to 1964, replacing the earlier 446 W. 50th St., NYC address used on the 1957 first pressing of this recording (then catalogued PRLP 7074). Deep groove is present on both sides. The catalogue number on the label, PRLP 7249, matches Prestige's 1962 renumbering/reissue of this 1956 session in mono, not the original 1957 LP 7074 issue; the runout etchings (PRLP-7249-A/B, RVG stamp, 'AB' Abbey Manufacturing plant code) are consistent with the Abbey-pressed Bergenfield-era Prestige mono run. This is a mono Bergenfield-label pressing of the 1962 PRLP 7249 reissue, one step below the 1957 PRLP 7074 first pressing in the hierarchy.

Fidelity

Original recording and lacquer cut by Rudy Van Gelder at his Hackensack studio; this 1962 Bergenfield mono pressing uses the same Van Gelder-cut lacquer lineage as the 1957 original but is a later stamper generation, pressed by Abbey Record Manufacturing. Collectors on the forums generally regard early Van Gelder-stamped Prestige mono pressings (both 7074 and its 7249 renumbered mono reissue) as sonically close and clearly preferable to the 1962 'Remastered for Stereo' PRST-7249 rechannel, which is not a true stereo recording. Within the album's fidelity hierarchy this pressing ranks below the 1957 PRLP 7074 true first pressing but above the stereo reissue, later Fantasy/OJC pressings, and CD reissues; the 2013/2022 Analogue Productions APRJ 7074 mono LP is the modern audiophile benchmark for the title.
