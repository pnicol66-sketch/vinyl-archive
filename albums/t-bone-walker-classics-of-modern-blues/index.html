The Album

T-Bone Walker was the pivotal figure who fused the single-string vocabulary of jazz guitar with the shouting urgency of blues singing, effectively inventing the vocabulary of modern electric blues guitar that every post-war player from B.B. King to Chuck Berry drew from. This compilation gathers his prolific Imperial Records sides cut in Los Angeles between 1950 and 1953, a period when Walker, already a star from his earlier Black & White sides, was at a commercial and creative peak, backed alternately by Maxwell Davis's house band and by the Jim Wynn and T. J. Fowler orchestras that gave these sides their jump-blues horn punch. The songs range from slow, aching blues like “Cold, Cold Feeling” to driving shuffle numbers, all delivered with Walker's instantly identifiable bent-note guitar lines and easy, conversational vocal phrasing. Walker's Imperial period extended the influence he had already established with “Call It Stormy Monday,“ carrying his sound into the early 1950s as rhythm and blues coalesced into a distinct commercial genre. His guitar style - playing behind his head, down on his knees, full of showmanship married to genuine musicality - set the template for the flamboyant blues guitar hero that would define the genre for decades. Critics and fellow musicians alike have long credited Walker as the essential bridge between prewar acoustic blues and the amplified, urban sound that shaped rock and roll. This set preserves a prolific, important stretch of that catalog for listeners who came to him later through his more famous Black & White era sides.

The Label

The side labels are an all-blue field with a cream b-and-note logo set in a dark circle at the upper left, THE BLUE NOTE RE-ISSUE SERIES arched around it, and a rim crediting United Artists Music and Records Group, Inc., Los Angeles - the design United Artists used across its Blue Note reissue two-LP sets from 1975 on. The ℗ 1975 line on every side is the record's own date mark and cannot predate the label; this pressing therefore belongs to the 1975 manufacturing run or later, never to any earlier Imperial-era issue of these sides. The catalogue number and rim wording are shared across several pressing runs of this reissue (plant differences the label itself does not name), so this copy is placed among the first 1975 United Artists run rather than on a specific plant line beyond what the label shows.

Fidelity

This is a mid-1970s compilation mastering of early-1950s Imperial masters rather than a contemporary Blue Note session cut, so the fidelity reflects the limitations of the surviving tape sources as much as the reissue's own mastering. Collectors generally regard these Blue Note Re-Issue Series sets as serviceable, functional transfers rather than audiophile-grade cuts, valued chiefly for making scattered singles-era material available on LP. No audiophile reissue of this specific compilation is widely discussed, so this 1975 pressing stands as the primary vinyl source for this particular anthology.

Notes

This is a 1975 United Artists-manufactured Blue Note Re-Issue Series compilation of T-Bone Walker's early-1950s Imperial sides, mono throughout. The blue b-and-note label with the United Artists Music and Records Group rim credit carries a ℗ 1975 date, placing this pressing in the 1975 manufacturing run or later rather than the original Imperial era. The catalogue and matrix numbers run consistently across all four sides. The gatefold jacket, continuing the liner text onto an inner spread, is this edition's packaging.
