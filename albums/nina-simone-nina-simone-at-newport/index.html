The Album

Nina Simone at Newport captures her performance at the fifth Newport Jazz Festival on June 30, 1960, released by Colpix that August as her third album for the label and second live recording after Nina Simone at Town Hall. Backed only by her working trio of Al Schackman on guitar, Chris White on bass and Bobby Hamilton on drums, Simone reworked material spanning the Gershwins' "Porgy," Cole Porter standards, and traditional and gospel-rooted pieces like "Little Liza Jane" and "In the Evening by the Moonlight," all arranged by Simone herself. The set is prized for the freedom of a festival stage, letting her stretch out on the extended, largely instrumental "Nina's Blues" and the call-and-response "Flo Me La." Coming early in her career, it shows Simone already fusing jazz, blues, folk, and classical technique into a singular style that resisted easy genre labeling. The album helped cement her reputation as a uniquely commanding live performer and remains a touchstone recording from her formative Colpix years, valued by critics and collectors as a document of her early artistic range before her more overtly political recordings of the mid-1960s.

The Label

This copy carries the gold Colpix label with the red 'COLPIX' wordmark and Columbia Pictures torch/Statue-of-Liberty logo, 'STEREO' printed at right, and the 'COLPIX RECORDS · A DIVISION OF COLUMBIA PICTURES CORPORATION · NEW YORK, N.Y. · MADE IN U.S.A.' rim text - the design Colpix used for its stereo LPs from the label's 1960 launch. That gold-label design places this copy in the label's earliest stereo pressing window, well before Colpix's mid-1960s ownership changes and before the CP/SCP catalogue was later folded into the Colgems/Cinerama-era reissue runs. The runout on this copy is stamped in an 'N8OY-023x' job-number style rather than the simple etched 'SCP-412 A1/B' codes seen on the Discogs-documented Abbey Record Mfg. variant, so while the label design confirms an early-1960s stereo pressing, the exact stamper/plant generation could not be matched to a specific catalogued Discogs variant.

Fidelity

This is a straightforward analog stereo cut of a 1960 festival tape, mastered and pressed in the early 1960s without any documented audiophile remastering; Colpix pressings of this era are generally considered adequate but unspectacular by collector forums, with surface noise and modest dynamic range typical of the period's festival location recording. No major audiophile reissue (Mobile Fidelity, Analogue Productions, Speakers Corner) of this title exists; later reissues are generic 180g imports (Doxy, Wax Love) sourced from digital transfers, which collectors on forums such as Steve Hoffman's have found flat and uninspired next to an original. As an early gold-label stereo pressing, this copy sits at or near the top of the fidelity hierarchy for the title, well above the Japanese Pye reissue and the 2010s-2020s European reissue vinyl.

Notes

Gold Colpix label with torch logo and 'MADE IN U.S.A.' rim text places this within Colpix's earliest (1960-launch) stereo label design, consistent with a first-issue-era stereo pressing rather than a later reissue. The runout codes (N8OY-0238-2C1 RR / N8OY-0239-3A RR) are a job-number style that does not match the simple etched 'SCP-412 A1/B' codes documented on the known Discogs Abbey-pressing variant, so the precise plant/stamper generation is unconfirmed. Notably, the pressed side labels are transposed relative to the jacket's printed track list - the disc's actual Side 1 plays the 'Flo Me La' set that the cover lists as Side Two, and vice versa - a known variant quirk of this title.
