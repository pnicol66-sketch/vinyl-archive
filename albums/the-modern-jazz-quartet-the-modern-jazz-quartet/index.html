The Album

Recorded in a single New York session on April 5, 1957, this self-titled Atlantic LP arrived at the peak of the Modern Jazz Quartet's mid-1950s ascent, following Django and Concorde and coinciding with the group's growing reputation for marrying bebop virtuosity with chamber-music restraint. John Lewis, the group's musical director, built the program around a suite-like medley of ballads (Berlin, Kern, and other standards) alongside quartet staples like "Bags' Groove" and Dizzy Gillespie's "Night in Tunisia," giving Milt Jackson's blues-drenched vibraharp room against Lewis's more formal, contrapuntal piano writing. "La Ronde: Drums" spotlighted drummer Connie Kay, underscoring the group's democratic, chamber-jazz conception in which every member gets a featured turn. The album crystallizes the MJQ's defining tension between Lewis's cool, European-tinged compositional discipline and Jackson's hot, blues-rooted improvisational instinct, a push-pull that became the group's signature sound throughout its Atlantic years. Critically the record has long been viewed as one of the most accessible and satisfying distillations of the classic quartet lineup (Lewis, Jackson, Percy Heath, Kay), and it remains a touchstone of 1950s cool/chamber jazz alongside the group's other Atlantic staples. Its blend of swing, blues, and restrained arrangement helped define the "third stream" sensibility Lewis would pursue further in later MJQ recordings.

The Label

The side labels are dark grey/black with a large silver arched ATLANTIC logo, catalogue number 1265 at left, "Side 1/2 HIGH FIDELITY" at right, and the arc rim text "LONG PLAYING UNBREAKABLE" with "33 1/3 RPM" and "ATLANTIC RECORDING CORP., NEW YORK" beneath - no fan logo of any kind is present. This is the classic black-and-silver Atlantic label used from the label's 1955 debut through about 1960, before the blue/green fan-logo design (1960-62) replaced it. Combined with the collector-reported deep groove on both sides, this places the copy squarely in the earliest label-design window for Atlantic 1265, consistent with a 1957 first-pressing run rather than any of the later reissue label designs.

Fidelity

Recorded and engineered by Tom Dowd at Atlantic Studios with lacquers cut in-house; the original 1957 mono black/silver Atlantic pressings are generally well regarded by collectors for their warm, direct sound with prominent vibraharp presence. Atlantic's late-1950s New York pressings are considered solid but not audiophile-showcase quality compared to contemporaneous Blue Note or RCA Living Stereo work, and no dedicated audiophile reissue (Analogue Productions, Mobile Fidelity, Speakers Corner, etc.) of this specific title is widely documented. Within the album's own reissue chain, this mono deep-groove pressing sits above the later blue/green fan-logo and black target-label reissues, and well above later stereo reprocessed or budget reissues, in terms of mastering proximity to the original tapes.

Notes

The side labels show the black/silver Atlantic design with arched logo and "LONG PLAYING UNBREAKABLE" rim text, used before the c.1960 blue/green fan-logo redesign, and both sides are noted as deep groove - together these point to a 1957 mono first-pressing run of Atlantic 1265. The typed runouts (11431 AT 1 B / 11432 AT E) fall within the family of stamped/etched suffix variants (e.g. 11431-C, 11431-E, A-11432-D) documented across several early Discogs mono pressings of this title, all sharing the same base matrix numbers and black/silver deep-groove label. The exact stamper letter could not be matched to a single specific Discogs variant page, so this should be treated as an early mono pressing within that family rather than a single definitively-numbered stamper.
