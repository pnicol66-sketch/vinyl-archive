The Album

Recorded in New York during September and October 1955, this was the first album by Mulligan's short-lived sextet and his first session as a leader for EmArcy. Mulligan formed the group after dissolving his celebrated pianoless quartet and taking several months away to reassess his musical direction. The new band expanded the quartet's spare, contrapuntal language into a four-horn ensemble while retaining an unusually open rhythmic foundation. Mulligan originals such as "Apple Core" and "Nights Of The Turntable" sit beside radically reworked standards, while "Broadway" and "Bernie's Tune" give the musicians room for extended collective interplay. Mulligan was already a central figure in modern jazz through his writing for Miles Davis's Birth of the Cool sessions, his big-band arrangements, and his success in bringing the baritone saxophone forward as a nimble lead voice. Contemporary reviewers praised the sextet's organic interplay, swing, and taste, with Whitney Balliett calling it perhaps the best modern small band then working. The group's brief existence and the overshadowing fame of Mulligan's earlier quartet long kept the album underrated, but it remains an important bridge between his pianoless small groups and his later Concert Jazz Band.

The Label

This copy has the blue EmArcy Type B small-drummer deep-groove label, curved "LONG PLAYING HIGH FIDELITY" rim text, and "MERCURY RECORD CORPORATION / MADE IN U.S.A." at the bottom. EmArcy, Mercury's jazz subsidiary launched in 1954, used blue-printed back covers through MG-36056; MG-36056 was the last title known in that form, with black-back jackets indicating a second issue from approximately 1956-57. This copy's black-printed back and typed MF2/MSII and MF2/MS markings therefore place it behind the documented MF1/MI/A1/I blue-back first issue. The matrix combination matches the Discogs RCA Indianapolis variant family, even though the small-drummer blue label itself also appears on the earliest copies.

Fidelity

This is a contemporary all-analog mono pressing from stamped MF2 metal with etched MS markings, made at RCA Indianapolis; no cutting engineer is independently documented. It sits behind the documented MF1/MI/A1/I first-cut copies but remains an early EmArcy pressing, with warm, naturally balanced mono well suited to the largely pianoless four-horn arrangements. RCA's Indianapolis work for Mercury is generally well regarded, although title-specific audiophile and Steve Hoffman forum commentary is sparse. The 1958 US repress is a lower vintage alternative, the 1979/1982 Japanese pressings offer quieter surfaces, and the 2016 Mosaic EmArcy Sextet Recordings box, mastered from the original mono tapes by Kevin Reeves and Ryan Smith, is the reference modern source.

Notes

US EmArcy MG 36056 mono, best identified as a c. 1956-57 RCA Indianapolis second pressing rather than the blue-back first issue. The decisive evidence is the black-printed back cover, blue small-drummer deep-groove labels, and typed MF2/etched MS runouts; documented earlier copies pair the blue-back jacket with MF1/MI/A1/I metal. The music was recorded in September and October 1955 and was being reviewed as a new LP by February 1956.
