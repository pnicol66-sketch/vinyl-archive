The Album

These performances were captured on a single Sunday, November 3, 1957, at New York's Village Vanguard, during the same extended engagement that produced Sonny Rollins's celebrated A Night at the Village Vanguard. Rollins worked the room in two different trios that day - a matinee set with bassist Donald Bailey and drummer Pete La Roca, and an evening set with bassist Wilbur Ware and drummer Elvin Jones - exploring the pianoless saxophone-bass-drums format that became one of his signature settings. The repertoire leans on durable standards and bebop vehicles - Cole Porter, Dizzy Gillespie, Jerome Kern - giving Rollins open harmonic ground for the kind of extended, thematically developing improvisation that critics of the era were just beginning to catalogue as a hallmark of his playing. Shelved for nearly two decades, these tapes surfaced as part of Blue Note's mid-1970s campaign to mine its vaults for unreleased jazz performances from its golden era. The album arrived at a moment when Rollins's stature as one of the defining tenor saxophonists of postwar jazz was long since secured, and the release let listeners hear a arguably rawer, more searching Rollins than the officially sanctioned Vanguard album had offered. Critics and collectors have come to regard it as an essential supplement to the official Vanguard recordings, prized for the interplay between Rollins and Elvin Jones in particular. It remains a touchstone for study of Rollins's mid-1950s evolution into one of the music's most structurally adventurous improvisers.

The Label

All four sides carry a flat, all-blue label with a large cream or white b-and-note logo set in a dark circle at the upper left, the words THE BLUE NOTE RE-ISSUE SERIES curved around it, and rim text naming United Artists Music and Records Group, Inc., Los Angeles, California - Made in U.S.A. No deep groove is present on this flat label. The ℗ 1975 United Artists Music And Records Group line fixes this pressing to 1975 or later. This is the Re-Issue Series label used for Blue Note's mid-1970s archival LPs, placing the copy as the 1975 US issue of this title.

Fidelity

The tracks were mastered from the 1957 Village Vanguard tapes specifically for this 1975 archival release, with ECK engineer initials etched into the runouts of all four sides. Collectors generally treat the Re-Issue Series cut as a serviceable but unspectacular mastering rather than an audiophile-grade pressing, useful chiefly because it was for decades the only vinyl source for this material. The later Japanese pressing with obi is sometimes favored by collectors for its vinyl quality, but no audiophile remaster of this specific title is widely recognized.

Notes

This is the 1975 Blue Note Re-Issue Series mono pressing, catalogue BN-LA475-H2, identified by its all-blue b-and-note label and the ℗ 1975 United Artists Music And Records Group line, which fixes the pressing to that year or later. The runouts carry the BN-LA475-1 through -4 matrix sequence with ECK engineer initials, matching the reissue-era mastering rather than any earlier Blue Note cut. There is no deep-groove ring visible on these flat Re-Issue Series labels, consistent with this being a later archival pressing rather than an original-era Blue Note release. The gatefold jacket and two-record set match the documented format for this title's first vinyl appearance.
