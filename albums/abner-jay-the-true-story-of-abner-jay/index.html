The Album

Abner Jay billed himself as the last of the great traveling one-man-band minstrels, a self-taught songster from rural Georgia who accompanied his own banjo, harmonica and foot-pedaled bass drum and cymbals in a single performance. He toured the South and beyond for decades, playing schools, supper clubs, churches and street corners, cutting his songs himself on small private-press singles and LPs he sold from the trunk of his car. This compilation gathers highlights of that self-released catalog, mixing his own compositions - blunt, plainspoken protest and hard-times songs like "Cocaine," "Vietnam" and "The Reason Young People Use Drugs" - with his stripped-down readings of older standards such as "St. James Infirmary Blues" and "Ol' Man River." Jay's music reaches back past postwar blues toward nineteenth-century minstrel and songster traditions, filtered through his own idiosyncratic, deeply personal voice. He remained a marginal, largely unrecorded figure in his lifetime, better known to those who caught him live than to record buyers, which makes this gathering of his scattered singles and album sides a rare window onto an outsider artist rediscovered decades after the fact. Critics and collectors have since embraced him as a singular American voice, prized precisely for the raw, unclassifiable character that kept him outside the era's blues and folk revivals.

The Label

The label on this copy is pink with black text, reading MISSISSIPPI RECORDS, the 5202 N Albina Ave, Portland OR address, the artist name ABNER JAY, and LP-MR-036, with a Side A/Side B, 33 1/3 RPM, MONO designation but no design table exists to date this exact pink colourway against the label's other pressings. The etched runout on this copy (MRP/MR-036A CP16-053A TELEGRAPH-GONSA / MRP MR-036B CP16-053B TELEGRAPH-GONSA) matches, character for character, the matrix documented for a later tip-on-sleeve repress of this title, which is elsewhere reported with burgundy labels rather than pink. That match places this pressing among the label's post-2009 repress runs sharing the same lacquer/stampers, but the colour discrepancy means the exact repress year cannot be fixed from the label alone. Mississippi Records is a small independent reissue label operating from this Portland, Oregon address since the label's founding in the 2000s, specializing in vernacular blues, gospel and folk compilations sourced from private-press originals.

Fidelity

The recordings are sourced from Abner Jay's own private-press singles and LP masters, so fidelity reflects those low-budget mid-century sources rather than a modern studio recording; expect surface noise, tape hiss and variable levels inherent to the originals. The reissue mastering and pressing (cut by an outside mastering/plating supplier for the label) is clean and well-centered by the standards of small-label reissues, and collectors generally rate this compilation as a faithful, listenable gathering of otherwise hard-to-find material. Because there is no audiophile remaster of this title, this pressing sits at the top of the fidelity hierarchy simply by being the only widely available vinyl edition.
