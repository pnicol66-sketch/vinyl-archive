This Pressing

The Album

This album collects previously unreleased tapes of Billie Holiday performing live at George Wein's Storyville club in Boston across two nights in late October and early November 1951, surfacing on the small Recording Industries Corporation label in 1964, five years after her death. It documents Holiday deep into the difficult final decade of her career, when her voice had narrowed and roughened but, as the original liner notes by Nat Hentoff argued, her interpretive command and emotional directness had only sharpened. The set draws from her core repertoire of standards and signature songs - “Lover Man”, “Strange Fruit”, “Billie's Blues”, “Ain't Nobody's Business If I Do” - heard in the loose, informal setting of an actual club date rather than a studio session, giving listeners a rare glimpse of how she worked a room and shaped a song in real time. For an artist whose studio legacy was already enormous and closely studied, this club recording offered fans and critics something genuinely new: an intimate, warts-and-all document of her art in performance rather than in the controlled environment of a label session. It has since been valued by critics and historians as one of the clearest windows onto Holiday's late style, capturing both the wear in her instrument and the depth of expression that came to define her final years.

The Label

Both side labels are a gold/tan ground with dark brown print, carrying the RI/RIC logo at top centre and the single New York address, “295 Madison Ave., New York, N. Y.”, with no second (Hollywood) address printed. This is the New-York-address-only, small-pressing-ring design used for the 1964 issue of the title. The other variants of this catalogue number carry a second printed address or were run through a different (Hollywood) plant, placing this single-address, small-ring label with the earliest of the 1964 US pressings. Neither label carries a deep groove.

Fidelity

The recording is a mono club tape from Storyville in 1951, made with the limited equipment of an informal live date rather than a studio session, so surface detail and dynamic range are modest by the standards of the label's contemporaneous studio LPs. Collectors on the forums regard the sound as serviceable rather than hi-fi, valuing the disc primarily for the performances it preserves rather than its engineering. No audiophile remaster of this title exists; among vinyl editions this original US mono pressing and the later Storyville/Monmouth Evergreen reissues share essentially the same tape-limited sound, with the stereo reissues offering only a processed spread rather than true stereo information.

Notes

This is the 1964 Recording Industries Corp. mono pressing of M 2001/M2001. Both labels show the New York address alone with the small pressing ring, the design the label used before a second (Hollywood) address was added to later copies of the same catalogue number. The etched runouts read M-2001-1 mb and M-2001-2 mb, matching the catalogue number and matrix pattern used for this release. No deep groove is present on either label.
