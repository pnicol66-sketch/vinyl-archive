The Album

King Curtis was already the era's most in-demand R&B saxophonist, a session ace behind the Coasters, the Shirelles and countless Atlantic dates, when Atco captured him fronting his own road band for a live date at Small's Paradise, the storied Harlem club, in the mid-1960s. The set is a tour of the mid-'60s soul-instrumental landscape - Curtis's own gritty originals like "Preach" and "Soul Theme" sit alongside covers of the day's dance and pop hits, Rufus Thomas's "Philly Dog," the Holland-Dozier-Holland-penned "Road Runner," Henry Mancini's "Peter Gunn," and a bristling reading of Bob Dylan's "Blowin' In The Wind." His touring band - Cornell Dupree on guitar, Melvin Lastie on cornet, Willie Bridges on baritone, Charles Rainey on bass and Ray Lucas on drums - was a tight, funky unit that would go on to seed the first-call New York soul-session scene of the following decade. The album stands as one of the era's definitive documents of black nightclub showmanship, capturing the call-and-response energy, honking solos and dance-floor immediacy that made Curtis's live shows legendary among musicians and audiences alike. It further cemented Curtis's reputation as a bridge figure between jazz, R&B and soul, and its loose, horn-driven grooves have been cited by generations of soul and funk revivalists as a touchstone of instrumental soul. Critically it has endured as a high point in Curtis's discography, valued as much for atmosphere and band chemistry as for individual solos.

The Label

The label on this copy is the mid-1960s ATCO "split AT|CO" design: a magenta/purple upper half and tan/gold lower half divided by a white band carrying the stacked "AT" over "CO" lettermark either side of the spindle hole, with "STEREO" lower-left and "ATCO RECORDS" lower-right - no deep groove, no lyre logo. Per cvinyl/bsnpubs label-history references, this purple-and-brown livery was ATCO's standard stereo album label from 1966 through 1968 (mono copies of the same era used a yellow-and-blue version of the identical layout), replacing the earlier 1957-62 yellow "lyre" label. Because the album was first issued in February 1967 already under this design (per bsnpubs' Atco LP discography), this is the correct and only original-issue label for the title - there is no earlier ATCO label variant to distinguish it from, only the parallel mono pressing and white-label promo copies.

Fidelity

Recorded live by Atlantic's own team (Tom Dowd supervising, engineers David Braithwaite, Joe Atkinson and Bobby Wright) directly to the ATCO stereo masters used for this pressing; there is no separately mastered audiophile reissue of this title to compare against. Collectors on forums such as Steve Hoffman generally regard mid-60s Atlantic/ATCO in-house New York pressings as solid, warm, dynamic cuts, if occasionally limited by the boominess inherent to a nightclub live recording of the period. This stereo copy, being the first and only significant vinyl issue, sits at the top of the title's available fidelity hierarchy; the only alternative format is the contemporaneous mono LP, which club-soul purists sometimes prefer for a punchier, more unified horn sound, and later CD reissues, which are digitally remastered and generally rated as secondary to the original vinyl cut.

Notes

This is the original February 1967 ATCO stereo issue (SD 33-198), identified by the purple-over-tan split "AT|CO" label design that bsnpubs/cvinyl date to ATCO's 1966-68 stereo album standard. Typed matrix data (IS TC-66917-B / IS TC-66918-B, LW AT 1W) shows first-generation "-B" master suffixes with Atlantic's own stamper coding, consistent with an early pressing rather than a later re-cut. No alternate ATCO vinyl label design or reissue of this title is documented, so the pressing hierarchy for this album is essentially mono vs. stereo (and promo white-label vs. stock) rather than multiple chronological label eras.
