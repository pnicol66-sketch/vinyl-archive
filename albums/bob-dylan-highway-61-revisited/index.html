The Album

Recorded in New York in June and July 1965, this album marks the moment Bob Dylan turned fully from acoustic folk toward electric rock, pairing his surreal, cascading lyricism with a loose, blues-driven band sound. The opening track, built around an organ riff improvised on the spot by a young musician who had talked his way onto the session, became an unlikely hit single and reset expectations of what a rock song could be. The record takes its title and its restless, carnival-dark imagery from the actual highway that threads through the blues country of the Mississippi Delta up through Dylan's native Minnesota, and its songs populate that landscape with carnival barkers, desperate lovers, biblical figures and literary ghosts. It arrived in the wake of Dylan's electrified, booed set at a folk festival that same summer, cementing his break from the folk purist audience and alienating some while thrilling many more. Critically it is regarded as one of the pivotal rock albums of its decade, prized for its wit, its density of allusion, and the way its closing nine-minute epic reimagines the ballad form entirely. It remains central to Dylan's reputation as a songwriter and to rock's claim to literary seriousness, influencing songwriters across genres in the decades since.

The Label

The side labels are the Columbia "two-eye" red design, flat (no deep groove) - the walking-eye logo boxed at nine and three o'clock, arched COLUMBIA across the top, "360 SOUND" banked lower left and right, and MONO centred at the bottom - with "NONBREAKABLE" printed under the catalogue number on both sides. That "Nonbreakable" line was dropped from the label within the catalogue number's pressing run, so its presence here places this pressing in the early label-design window Columbia used for this title, ahead of the later repress labels that drop the word. The two-eye design itself dates the whole first-pressing era of this release, before Columbia's early-1970s label redesigns.

Fidelity

This is a mono cut from the original 1965 mastering, pressed in the format collectors prize as the record's intended listening experience over the simultaneous rechanneled stereo issue. Collectors on the forums consistently rate the early mono pressings of this title above the stereo counterpart and well above later reissues for the way the mono fold keeps the dense arrangement focused. Among the mono pressings, an early-stamper copy such as this one is considered sonically preferable to the later-stamper examples of the same label design, since earlier stampers come from less-worn metal.

Notes

1965 Columbia CL 2389 mono - an early pressing from the original release window. The two-eye labels carry the "Nonbreakable" text found only on the earliest label runs for this catalogue number, and the matrix suffixes read -1A on both sides, the earliest stamper generation documented for this title. Both cover and vinyl present in top condition, placing this among the better-preserved examples of the early pressing.
