The Album

Recorded in August 1965 when its leader was barely twenty, Spring is the second of drummer Anthony (Tony) Williams's two mid-1960s leader dates for the label, following Life Time, and stands as a remarkable early statement from a musician already reshaping the rhythmic language of jazz as a member of Miles Davis's quintet. All five compositions are Williams's own, pairing a front line of two tenor saxophones - the harmonically outward Sam Rivers alongside the more lyrical Wayne Shorter - with Herbie Hancock's piano and Gary Peacock's bass, an unusual instrumentation that lets Williams explore dense, freely pulsed group interplay rather than conventional theme-and-solos form. The record sits at the meeting point of hard bop's vocabulary and the loosening structures of the mid-1960s avant-garde, reflecting the same restless, advanced sensibility Williams brought to Davis's second great quintet during the same period. Though it was a commercial footnote on release, Spring has since been recognized by critics and musicians as a striking example of a teenage genius already composing and directing a band with the authority of a veteran, and it remains a touchstone for the Blue Note label's mid-sixties excursions into freer, more exploratory postbop.

The Label

The label on this copy is the modern cream-and-blue Blue Note design with "BLUE NOTE" lettering at left and bottom, a "33 1/3 STEREO LONG PLAYING" arc at top and "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC. - NEW YORK USA" beneath it, printed with a BIEM/SDRM credit line, a Label Code (LC00133) and a barcode-derived etched runout - none of which appear on any Blue Note pressing before 1980. This is the label used on Blue Note's current UMe-era Classic Vinyl Series reissues, a design introduced long after the original 1960s New York and later Liberty/United Artists labels that preceded it. Its presence, together with the barcode, places this copy at the foot of the pressing chronology, well after the 1966 original and the 1970s-80s reissues that carry the earlier label designs.

Fidelity

This pressing was cut by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio from the original analog master tapes and pressed on 180-gram vinyl by Optimal Media in Germany, part of Blue Note's current Classic Vinyl Series reissue line. Collectors generally rate Gray's Cohearent cuts and Optimal's European pressing quality highly among the label's recent reissue runs, offering clean, dynamic sound at a modest price. It ranks below the original 1966 Rudy Van Gelder-cut New York stereo and mono pressings, which remain the benchmark for this title, and sits among the better-regarded of the post-1970 reissues rather than at the top of the title's overall fidelity hierarchy.

Notes

This copy is the current Blue Note/UMe Classic Vinyl Series reissue of Spring, not an original or vintage pressing. The UPC barcode on the jacket and the corresponding etched code in the runout are marks that did not exist before 1980, placing manufacture decades after the 1966 original. The label design, catalogue formatting (BST 84216 on label vs. 84216 STEREO on the sleeve) and the 180-gram weight and mastering credit on the hype sticker further identify it as the 2023 reissue mastered by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio and pressed by Optimal Media in Germany.
