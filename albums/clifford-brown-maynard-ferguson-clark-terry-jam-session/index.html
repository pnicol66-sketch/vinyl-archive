The Album

On August 14, 1954 (the LP's liner notes claim October 14, but discographers have established August), EmArcy A&R chief Bob Shad staged a Norman Granz/JATP-style public jam session in Hollywood, renting a small auditorium, selling free admission, and rolling tape on the starriest band he could assemble - the whole thing thrown together on the spot after a Clifford Brown/Max Roach record date the same week. Three great trumpets - the then-ascendant Clifford Brown, high-note phenomenon Maynard Ferguson, and the Duke Ellington-bound Clark Terry - traded chorus after chorus over a rhythm section anchored by Max Roach, with Harold Land and Herb Geller on reeds; Dinah Washington, whose own set from the same concert became the companion LP 'Dinah Jams' (MG 36000), drops in for a luminous 'Darn That Dream.' The album captures Brown in the earliest flush of his brief, blazing career - within two years he would be the most influential trumpeter since Dizzy Gillespie and, by his death at 25 in June 1956, a jazz immortal. 'Move' is one of the great trumpet-battle performances on record, and the closing ballad medley showcases Richie Powell, Terry, Geller, and Brown at their most lyrical. Billboard praised it on release in February 1955 ('it's the three trumpets which walk off with top honors'), and AllMusic's Scott Yanow calls it 'a brilliant set that is highly recommended.' It remains one of the essential documents of the jam-session tradition and of Brown's西海岸 period.

The Label

This copy carries the original 'blue EmArcy type A' label: bright blue stock with the white drummer-boy logo, 'LONG PLAYING HIGH FIDELITY' curved rim text, and 'Mercury Record Corporation / Made in U.S.A.' at the bottom, with a deep groove. Crucially, the back cover is printed in blue ink - per the EmArcy pressing research (microgroove.jp), early 36000-series issues including MG-36000 through MG-36047 shipped with blue backs, and anything from c. 1956-57 onward switched to black-printed backs, making a black back on this title a second-issue marker. EmArcy was Mercury's jazz subsidiary (M-R-C = 'Em-Ar-Cy'), launched 1954, and this blue drummer design is its founding label before later Mercury/EmArcy 'double-oval' designs appeared. Blue label + deep groove + blue back cover together place this copy in the original 1955 first-issue window.

Fidelity

Recorded live-to-mono in Hollywood in 1954 under Mercury/EmArcy's 'Fine-Fairchild Margin Control' regime and cut at Mercury's own Chicago operation; original US deep-groove pressings like this one are the sonic benchmark - lively, upfront brass with real room ambience. Later Trip Jazz (1975) reissues are thin budget jobs and the 1980s EmArcy/Polygram reissues are competent but cooler-sounding; no modern audiophile reissue (Tone Poet/Analogue Productions-class) of this title exists, so a clean original mono remains the best-sounding option by a clear margin.

Notes

1955 EmArcy MG 36002 mono - original US first pressing. Decisive evidence: blue 'drummer' EmArcy type A labels with deep groove, blue-printed back cover (switched to black on c. 1956-57 second issues), and the typed runouts (YMG 36002A/B with stamped letters) matching the documented original matrix style. Released February 1955 from the August 14, 1954 Hollywood concert; Dinah Washington sings 'Darn That Dream.'
