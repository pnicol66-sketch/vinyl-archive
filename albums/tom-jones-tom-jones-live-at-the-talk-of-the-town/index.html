The Album

Recorded live at London's Talk of the Town cabaret venue, this 1967 album captured Tom Jones at the moment he was moving from teen-idol pop hitmaker into a full-blown Vegas-style showman, backed by a tuxedoed orchestra under Johnny Harris. The set mixes Jones's own hits - "It's Not Unusual", "What's New Pussycat?", "Green, Green Grass of Home" and the Bond theme "Thunderball" - with soul and R&B staples by Sam Cooke ("Ain't That Good News", "Shake") and the Chris Kenner/Fats Domino classic "Land of a Thousand Dances", alongside show-tune and pop standards like "Hello Young Lovers" and "That Old Black Magic". Produced by Peter Sullivan, Jones's regular Decca producer through the decade, the record documents the powerful, theatrical baritone and stagecraft already making Jones one of Britain's biggest live draws, a persona that would soon carry him into a long, hugely successful Las Vegas residency career. It stands as a notable document of the mid-1960s British vocal-pop and blue-eyed-soul crossover, showing a UK pop star confidently working American R&B repertoire for a supper-club audience, and remains a well-regarded snapshot of peak-1960s Tom Jones as a live performer rather than a studio hitmaker.

The Label

This copy shows the red Decca ffrr label with full 'MADE IN ENGLAND · THE DECCA RECORD CO. LTD.' rim text, the BIEM/NCB box with 'GEMA' printed beneath it, and a 'J/T' purchase-tax code bracketed with the matrix number, on a non-deep-groove pressing. Discogs documents the true 1967 first pressing of LK 4874 as deep-groove labels with no tax code at all; the J/T tax code was only added to Decca UK labels from 1968 onward, and this pressing's -2A stamper suffix (versus -1W on the first pressing) confirms a later cutting generation. The still fully-worded 'ffrr' label design (predating Decca's early-1970s blue boxed-logo redesign, seen for example on 1970 releases like LK/SKL 5045) places this copy in the c.1968-71 repress window - a genuine but non-first UK mono pressing.

Fidelity

Recorded and mixed for a live cabaret release, mastered from Decca's original tapes with engineering credited to Bill Price; collectors rate original-era UK Decca mono ffrr pressings of this title as solid, punchy period cuts well suited to the big-band-with-vocal live sound. This copy is a mono repress with -2A stampers rather than the first-cut -1W deep-groove pressing, so it sits a notch below the earliest stampers in outright transfer generation, though still ahead of later-1970s boxed-logo reissues. No audiophile remaster of this specific live title is documented, so vintage UK Decca pressings remain the reference copies for collectors.

Notes

This is a UK mono Decca pressing of LK 4874, but not the earliest issue: the true 1967 first pressing carries a deep-groove label with no tax-code text, whereas this copy shows the 'J/T' purchase-tax code Decca began adding to labels from 1968 onward. The collector's matrix transcription (-2A stampers on both sides) is also a later cutting generation than the -1W stampers documented on the first pressing. The still full-text 'ffrr' label design, predating Decca's early-1970s boxed-logo redesign, places this as a c.1968-1971 UK repress rather than a true first pressing or a later-1970s reissue.
