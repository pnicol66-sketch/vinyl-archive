The Album

This album gathers four cornerstone works of Samuel Barber in landmark performances by Howard Hanson and the Eastman-Rochester Orchestra, tireless champions of American orchestral music. The Symphony No. 1, Op. 9, a compressed one-movement work whose scherzo, slow movement and finale grow organically from the opening themes, premiered in Rome in 1936 and was revised for its 1944 New York Philharmonic premiere under Bruno Walter, announcing Barber as a major symphonist in his twenties. The Overture to 'The School for Scandal,' Op. 5, was Barber's Curtis Institute graduation piece, written at 21 and winner of the 1933 Bearns Prize, becoming his first work to enter the standard repertoire. The Medea Suite, Op. 23, derives from the 1946 ballet commissioned by Martha Graham, later revised as 'Cave of the Heart,' and reveals Barber's more astringent, dramatic side across seven mythological episodes. The Adagio for Strings, arranged from the slow movement of the String Quartet, Op. 11, received its immortal 1938 premiere under Toscanini and the NBC Symphony and has become the defining American elegy of mourning. Hanson, longtime director of the Eastman School of Music, premiered and recorded more American music than any conductor of his generation, and his Mercury Living Presence recordings with the Eastman-Rochester Orchestra remain a reference document of mid-century American orchestral art. These performances, among the earliest and most authoritative documents of these Barber works, helped fix the Barber canon for the LP era and remain interpretive touchstones.

The Label

This copy shows the lilac/light-purple Mercury 'Golden Imports' label: oval Mercury logo at top, 'Golden Imports' script banner beneath it, a boxed ELECTRONIC STEREO field, and 'MADE IN HOLLAND' printed under the catalog number SRI 75012, with standard 1970s Phonogram International rim text. Golden Imports was Phonogram Holland's mid-1970s budget line (SRI-75xxx, c. 1973-78) licensing US Mercury Living Presence recordings for European distribution, making this a Dutch reissue rather than a US Mercury pressing. The 'STEREO*' asterisk tied to 'Medea Suite, Op. 23*' flags that only the Medea is genuine stereo, the rest electronically reprocessed from mono, confirmed by the back-cover disclaimer and the credit line noting prior release as SR 90428, MG 50148, and MG 50075. This places the copy two generations below the 1955/1958 US Living Presence originals in the pressing hierarchy.

Fidelity

The sources are the celebrated Mercury Living Presence Eastman Theatre sessions, single-point recordings engineered by C. Robert Fine under producer Wilma Cozart, but this pressing presents three of four works in electronically simulated stereo, smearing the clean mono Living Presence imaging; only the Medea Suite is genuine stereo. Dutch Phonogram/Baarn pressing quality is quiet and well-centered for the era, and collectors regard Golden Imports as among the better-sounding budget reissue lines, but it remains a reprocessed budget cut. It sits well below the US mono originals (MG 50075/MG 50148) and the 1958 stereo SR 90428, and below modern Mercury Living Presence audiophile reissues (Speakers Corner, Analogue Productions) where applicable. Buy this copy for the repertoire, not for reference sound.

Notes

1974 Dutch Mercury Golden Imports SRI 75012, a licensed European budget reissue of 1955/1958 US Mercury Living Presence masters, not an original pressing. Decisive evidence: lilac Golden Imports labels with 'MADE IN HOLLAND' and Phonogram rim text; back-cover disclaimer 'Electronically altered to simulate stereo' plus 'Previously released as SR 90428, MG 50148, and MG 50075'; Phonogram Baarn matrix format (SRI 75 012 1Y/2Y ... 670) in the runouts. The 'STEREO*' flag on the label confirms only the Medea Suite is true stereo.
