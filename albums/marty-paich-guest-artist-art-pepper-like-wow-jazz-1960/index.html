This Pressing

Original and only documented US pressing (mono, 1960) - Interlude MO-514 gray deep-groove Hollywood label; a recompilation of 1956 Tampa masters.

The Album

Despite the "Jazz 1960" banner splashed across the cover, every note here was recorded in Hollywood in 1956 at Radio Recorders, during one of the most poignant stretches of Art Pepper's life: he had just been released from his first prison term in mid-1956 and cut these sides with pianist-arranger Marty Paich only weeks later, months before his career-defining "Art Pepper Meets the Rhythm Section" session. Five tracks feature Pepper's alto in a quartet with Paich, first issued on the rare Tampa LP pairing the two, including Pepper's own composition "Art's Opus"; the other five are Paich-led sides with Larry Bunker's vibes and Howard Roberts' guitar, drawn from an earlier Tampa 10-inch. By the time these masters resurfaced on Interlude with a glossy, bachelor-pad cover and a fabricated later release date, Pepper himself was once again behind bars, making the album an odd, bittersweet placeholder for an absent star. Paich, meanwhile, was emerging as one of the most important arrangers on the West Coast scene, later responsible for the charts on "Art Pepper + Eleven" and numerous Mel Torme sessions. Pepper's playing throughout is light, buoyant and melodically generous - the sound of a newly free man breezing through standards and originals - and the set is valued today as an important, if commercially obscure, early document of his mature alto voice.

The Label

This copy carries the gray-silver deep-groove label with the large white lowercase "i" Interlude logo and "INTERLUDE RECORDS INC - HOLLYWOOD" rim text, catalog MO-514 with printed "int 134-A/B" matrix numbers - the sole documented US pressing configuration for this title. Interlude Records Inc. was a short-lived Hollywood independent (c. 1959-62) run by Robert Scherman, and its catalog leaned heavily on earlier Tampa Records masters (TP series, 1956-57) that Scherman also controlled; MO-514 is a recompilation of that Tampa material rather than a new 1960 recording. The deep-groove pressing and single mono MO-500 series number place this firmly within the label's original 1960 issue window, with no second pressing or label-design change documented for the title.

Fidelity

The source tapes were engineered at Radio Recorders in 1956 by Val Valentin, later Verve's house engineer, which accounts for the unusually warm, present mono sound on this LP. Collector commentary (londonjazzcollector.wordpress.com) rates this Interlude pressing highly for the material, describing it as sonically superior to the rarer, reportedly rougher-sounding original Tampa 10-inch/LP issues. In the overall fidelity hierarchy for this material, this Interlude mono pressing sits at or near the top: the Tampa originals are scarcer collector objects but not better sounding, later reissues on Charlie Parker Records (as "Chile Pepper") and V.S.O.P. are a tape generation removed and more variable, and no audiophile reissue of this specific compilation exists.

Notes

1960 Interlude MO-514 mono is the original and only documented US pressing configuration for this title. The gray deep-groove labels with "INTERLUDE RECORDS INC - HOLLYWOOD" rim text and printed "int 134-A/B" matrix numbers match the expected original-issue design, and the deep-groove pressing plus the mono MO-500 series numbering place this firmly in the label's 1960 issue window. The typed runouts (134-A/B-21749 and 133-B/B-21750) are broadly consistent, though the Side 2 transcription reads "133-B" against the label's printed "int 134-B" - a discrepancy likely due to a transcription slip rather than a second variant, best confirmed with a dead-wax photo. The surviving original "Monaural Dimensional Realism" corporate inner sleeve further supports period provenance; the actual recordings date from August 1956 Radio Recorders sessions, not 1960 as the cover implies.
