The Album

Recorded live on April 9-10, 1965 at Club La Marchal in Brooklyn, The Night of the Cookers documents one of hard bop's great trumpet summits: Freddie Hubbard, then 27 and fresh off his early Blue Note breakthroughs, matched against Lee Morgan, his closest rival among the era's young trumpet stars. The date arose from 'Just Us,' a Brooklyn jazz-appreciation circle of young women (including wives of Hubbard, Cedar Walton and Billy Higgins) devoted to promoting the music and giving it a home at the club. Hubbard brought his working quintet of Spaulding, Mabern, Ridley and La Roca, augmenting it with Morgan and conguero Big Black for the occasion. Volume 1 rests on two extended performances: Clare Fischer's Latin-tinged 'Pensativa,' which became a Hubbard signature, and Richard Carpenter's blues-drenched 'Walkin',' a hard-driving vehicle for trumpet trading. Issued across two volumes in 1965-66, the set captures the tail end of Blue Note's golden club-recording era and stands as a career highlight for both trumpeters. It remains widely regarded by critics as one of the finest live hard bop documents of its time, prized above all for the ferocious Hubbard-Morgan interplay.

The Label

This copy carries the blue-and-white 'BLUE NOTE RECORDS - A DIVISION OF LIBERTY RECORDS, INC.' label introduced after Liberty's mid-1966 acquisition of Blue Note, used on reissues and new releases through 1970. The upper-and-lower-case 'Side 1'/'Side 2' typesetting identifies Keystone Printed Specialties label stock, Blue Note's original East Coast printer, distinguishing it from Bert-Co's all-caps West Coast 'SIDE' printing. That points to manufacture at an East Coast plant (All Disc, Keel, or Southern Plastics) using original Van Gelder metal shipped by Liberty, confirmed by the VAN GELDER stamp noted in the runouts. This places the copy in the Liberty-era (1966-70) second-press tier: sonically descended from the original RVG lacquer but issued after the 1965 NY USA first pressing, and the accompanying Liberty 'First Family of Recorded Entertainment' inner sleeve corroborates the era.

Fidelity

This East Coast Liberty pressing was stamped from Rudy Van Gelder's original metal, so the cut itself is the genuine RVG master, and collectors on Steve Hoffman forums and LondonJazzCollector rate these Keystone-labeled Liberty copies well above the West Coast Research Craft re-masters (Bert-Co labels, no Van Gelder stamp) and later Capitol/United Artists-era reissues. The source material is Orville O'Brien's remote club recording, with Van Gelder handling only re-recording and mastering, so the sound is energetic and lively but somewhat raw compared to a proper Van Gelder studio date. In the title's fidelity hierarchy this pressing sits just below the 1965 NY USA stereo original (same metal lineage but earlier stampers) and above non-Van Gelder reissues, with Japanese King/Toshiba cuts as the main vintage alternative.

Notes

Blue Note BST 84207 stereo, Liberty-era US reissue (c. 1966-70), not the 1965 NY USA first pressing. Decisive evidence: blue/white 'A Division of Liberty Records, Inc.' labels (post-1966 acquisition design), Keystone-style 'Side 1/2' typesetting indicating East Coast label stock, and the VAN GELDER stamp in the runouts confirming pressing from original Van Gelder metal. The Liberty 'First Family of Recorded Entertainment' company inner sleeve is consistent with this era. The typed runout numbers ('84208'/'84028') appear to be transcription slips, as the labels clearly print BNST 84207-A / 84207-B.
