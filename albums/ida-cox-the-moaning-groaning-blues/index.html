The Album

Ida Cox was one of the great classic blues singers of the 1920s, recording prolifically for Paramount alongside contemporaries Bessie Smith and Ma Rainey, and this collection gathers her Paramount sides cut between 1923 and 1927 with a rotating cast of outstanding jazz accompanists. Her earliest sides here find her backed by Lovie Austin’s Blues Serenaders with cornetist Tommy Ladnier, while the mid-1920s sessions bring in cornetist Joe Smith, trombonist Charlie Green, clarinetists Buster Bailey and Johnny Dodds, and a young Fletcher Henderson on piano - a who’s-who of the era’s finest session musicians. Cox’s voice carried an emotional directness and theatrical phrasing distinct from Bessie Smith’s power or Ma Rainey’s earthiness, and these recordings capture her blues at full force during the genre’s commercial peak on race-market labels of the day. The material ranges from mournful laments to defiant, humorous asides, reflecting the blues’ dual character as both lament and entertainment. Reissued from the original Paramount masters, the album helped restore Cox’s reputation during the traditional-jazz revival of the postwar decades, when collectors and historians were rediscovering the founding generation of blues and jazz recording artists. It remains valued as a document of Cox’s artistry and of the loose, hot accompaniment style that defined classic blues recording in Chicago in the 1920s.

The Label

The labels are the blue-ground, silver-print Riverside design with the two-reel tape logo boxed above the wordmark and the line “Bill Grauer Productions Inc. New York City” at the foot, the reels-with-Inc version of the company's silver-on-blue design that ran alongside the mono catalogue into the mid-1960s before a plain blue label without the reels logo replaced it late in 1965. Both sides carry a deep groove, a feature typical of Riverside's mono jazz-archive LPs of this period. This design and the deep groove mark this as the title's one release rather than a later, flat-edge repress.

Fidelity

The recordings were remastered from the original 1920s Paramount masters by Jack Matthews at Components Corp. using a Hydrofeed lathe, a process Riverside used across its Jazz Archives reissue series to tame surface noise while preserving the body of the early acoustic and electrical recordings. Collectors regard these Riverside Paramount reissues as a faithful and listenable restoration for recordings of this vintage, though inherent surface noise from the 1920s sources remains audible, as the back cover itself acknowledges. This mono pressing stands as the only vinyl edition of the compilation, so it is also the best-regarded version by default among collectors of early blues reissues.

Notes

This is the sole release of this title, issued in 1960 as part of the label's Jazz Archives reissue series drawn from the original Paramount masters. The labels are the blue-ground, silver-print reels design with the Bill Grauer Productions Inc. imprint, and both sides carry a deep groove, a mark of mono pressings from this phase of Riverside's production rather than the plain, flat-edge label of a later repress. The runouts read RLP-12-147A and RLP-12-147B, matching the catalogue number printed on the labels and jacket.
