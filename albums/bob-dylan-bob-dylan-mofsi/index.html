This Pressing

2016/2018 Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab numbered 45 RPM mono reissue (MFSL 2-458) of the 1962 Columbia debut - a later audiophile pressing, not an original Columbia issue.

The Album

Recorded in two brief sessions in New York in late 1961, this debut introduced Bob Dylan as an interpreter of traditional folk and blues material rather than as the songwriter he would soon become, with only two originals - a talking blues and a tribute to his idol Woody Guthrie - among a set of blues, gospel and folk standards. John Hammond, the Columbia talent scout who had championed artists from Billie Holiday onward, signed the young Dylan after hearing him play harmonica on a Carolyn Hester session and staked his own reputation on the unproven twenty-year-old, prompting skeptics at the label to dub him “Hammond's Folly.” The performances are raw and unpolished, built around Dylan's guitar, harmonica and a voice already distinctive for its phrasing and character, drawing directly on the repertoires of Guthrie, Eric Von Schmidt, Dave Van Ronk and the blues tradition of Blind Lemon Jefferson. Commercially the record made little impact on release, overshadowed almost immediately by the original songs Dylan began writing within months, but it stands as the essential documentary record of the Greenwich Village folk apprenticeship from which his singular career emerged. In retrospect it is prized less for its hit potential than for capturing, unfiltered, the voice and instincts that would reshape American songwriting within two years.

The Label

Each side's label is the Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab “GAIN 2 Ultra Analog” reissue design: dark brown or black grounds with white/silver-white print and rim band, the Columbia “eye” logo boxed at the right, the MoFi script logo at lower right, and the GAIN 2 Ultra Analog wordmark at lower left beside the catalogue number. The rim text credits manufacture to Sony Music Entertainment for Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab at 25 Madison Avenue, New York, and the copyright line reads “© 1962, 2016 Columbia Records,” a date this pressing cannot predate. This is a reissue-series label layout introduced for Mobile Fidelity's audiophile Bob Dylan catalogue in the mid-2010s, not any Columbia-era original design, and it places this copy firmly among the modern Mobile Fidelity reissues rather than any 1960s Columbia or CBS pressing.

Fidelity

Mastered by Krieg Wunderlich at Mobile Fidelity using the GAIN 2 Ultra Analog chain from the original 1/4-inch, 15 ips analog mono master, routed through a DSD256 digital intermediate before cutting, and plated for pressing at Record Technology Incorporated. Collectors generally rate Mobile Fidelity's Dylan mono series highly for quiet, well-plated 180-gram vinyl and a clean, detailed transfer, though some purists on the forums note a preference for a fully analog cut over any DSD-intermediate step. Within this title's fidelity hierarchy this 45 RPM Mobile Fidelity edition ranks above standard CD and digital reissues and above ordinary later Columbia vinyl repressings, sitting just below (or alongside, by some ears) the best early six-eye Columbia mono originals for tonal character, while offering far superior surface noise and pressing quality.

Notes

This copy is the Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab MFSL 2-458 numbered reissue, cut at 45 RPM in mono across two LPs. The GAIN 2 Ultra Analog label design and its own “© 1962, 2016” copyright line date manufacture to 2016 or later, well after any Columbia-era pressing of this title. The matrix/runout strings on all four sides read as plain Mobile Fidelity mastering codes (MFSL2-458 side letter, R1, KW@MOFI job number), consistent with this being the sole mastering used for the edition rather than evidence of an earlier source. It should not be confused with any six-eye or CBS mono original of the same album.
