The Album

Albert Collins, the 'Master of the Telecaster,' had spent the 1960s and early '70s largely as a regional Texas and West Coast attraction before Alligator Records signed him in 1978 and put his stinging, ice-pick tone in front of a national blues audience with a run of acclaimed studio albums. By the early 1980s he was touring internationally at the head of the Icebreakers, a tight road band built around saxophonist A.C. Reed, second guitarist Larry Burton, bassist Johnny B. Gayden and drummer Casey Jones. This set captures that band on a December 1982 date in Tokyo during a package blues tour, presenting Collins's signature blend of stabbing, capoed-guitar riffs, spoken asides and cool-toned solos in front of an enthusiastic Japanese audience. The program mixes Collins originals such as 'Tired Man' and 'If Trouble Was Money' with a long, exploratory reading of the standard 'Stormy Monday' and a cover of Jimmy McGriff's instrumental 'All About My Girl,' giving the band room to stretch out well beyond studio-length arrangements. A.C. Reed's tenor and vocal on 'Jealous Man' add a second voice to the show, underlining the album's identity as a full-band live document rather than a guitarist-plus-sidemen date. The record stands as a snapshot of Collins at a commercial and artistic peak, showing why he became one of the most influential guitarists of the modern blues era, and it remains valued by fans for its energy and for preserving a period of the Icebreakers lineup captured nowhere else on record.

The Label

The side labels carry Alligator's standard green design: a single outlined alligator logo walking across the field beneath the block-letter 'ALLIGATOR' logo arched above it, the catalogue number 'AL 4733-A' / 'AL 4733-B' at lower left, 'Stereo' at lower right, and the Alligator Records Box 60234, Chicago IL 60660 address printed beneath the track listing. No deep groove is present in either label. This Chicago Box-60234 address format was Alligator's standard label style through the label's mid-1980s vinyl era, consistent with a domestic US pressing of a title that had first appeared the previous year on a small Japanese label. The pressing plant is not identified on the label itself; it is placed instead by the runout, which carries a Frankford/Wayne New York mastering credit together with a 'HuB' stamp, identifying it as the Hub-Servall-pressed US edition of this catalogue number.

Fidelity

The album was mastered by Frankford/Wayne Mastering Labs in New York from tapes recorded on location in Tokyo and mixed at a Tokyo studio, and pressed domestically by Hub-Servall, a mid-tier commercial pressing plant rather than an audiophile facility. As a location live recording the source tape itself, not the mastering, sets the sonic ceiling, and collectors generally regard this era of Alligator vinyl as solid if unspectacular in fidelity. No audiophile reissue series (Analogue Productions, Music Matters, Mobile Fidelity or similar) exists for this title, so this Hub-Servall-pressed US edition sits alongside the contemporaneous European Sonet and Vogue pressings as one of the standard commercial issues, with the compact-disc reissues offering a cleaner but not sonically superior alternative.

Notes

This is the domestic US Alligator Records pressing of the album, issued in 1984 after the title's original 1983 appearance on a small Japanese label. The runout carries a Frankford/Wayne New York mastering credit together with a 'HuB' stamp, identifying the Hub-Servall Record Mfg. Corp. pressing of this catalogue number rather than any of the other US/Canada or European pressings sharing the same number. The green Alligator labels show the standard Chicago Box 60234 address format used by the label at the time, with no deep groove present. The jacket is printed in Canada, a detail noted on the back cover, while the recording itself was made at Kudan Kaikan in Tokyo.
