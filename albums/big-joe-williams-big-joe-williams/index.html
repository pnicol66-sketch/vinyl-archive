The Album

Big Joe Williams was one of the last great itinerant Delta bluesmen, a self-taught guitarist from Mississippi's Crawford community who built his signature nine-string guitar and spent decades riding the rails and playing wherever a job or a woman took him before settling into a long recording career from the 1930s onward. By the early 1970s, when this session was cut in Copenhagen, Williams was an old man still carrying the raw, hypnotic, one-man-band intensity that had made him a formative link between prewar country blues and the postwar electric tradition; younger blues and rock musicians on both sides of the Atlantic had already begun crediting him as an influence. The songs here are entirely his own compositions, following the loose, often improvised structure typical of his live performances - hard-luck laments about women leaving, wandering, and getting by, delivered with his droning, percussive guitar work and gruff, urgent vocal style. This kind of European field session was common for Williams and many of his generation, who found more consistent bookings and recording opportunities overseas than at home during the folk and blues revival years. The album stands as a late but vital document of a musician whose deceptively simple, idiosyncratic style shaped the sound of Delta and Chicago blues alike, and it has been valued by blues collectors and historians as a genuine, unvarnished performance from one of the music's foundational figures.

Fidelity

This is a straightforward field-style stereo recording of a solo performer, mastered for a small specialist blues label rather than for audiophile scrutiny; the French MPO pressing plant handled manufacture, and the source cut carries no notable stamped mastering engineer credit on the label or in the runout as transcribed. Collectors of vintage Storyville blues LPs generally regard these European Storyville pressings as adequate, unfussy transfers rather than sonic showcases, valued more for the performances and rarity of the material than for hi-fi qualities. There is no audiophile reissue of this specific title to rank it against; it stands as the ordinary commercial pressing of its era.
