This Pressing

1962 Colpix stereo pressing (SCP-425) - white/grey Colpix film-strip label, matrix N7OY 8604-2B/8605-2B with MR (Monarch) stamp, the standard configuration for the original-era stereo issue.

The Album

Recorded in New York in 1961 for Colpix, this was Nina Simone's fourth studio album and her first full-length tribute to a single composer, built entirely from the songbook of Duke Ellington. Simone, classically trained and never comfortable being boxed into a single genre, treats Ellington's standards - "Solitude," "Do Nothin' Till You Hear From Me," "It Don't Mean a Thing" - as vehicles for her own moody, jazz-inflected phrasing and piano work rather than straight covers, and she is credited with conceiving the arrangements herself. The Malcolm Dodds Singers add a choral backing on several tracks that was unusual for a Simone record and divides listeners to this day. Coming during her prolific early-1960s Colpix run, the album is a minor entry next to landmarks like Little Girl Blue or Nina Simone in Concert, but it documents her range and her willingness to reinterpret the American songbook on her own terms. Critical reception has been mixed-to-modest over the decades, with some tracks ("It Don't Mean a Thing," "Solitude") singled out as highlights and the choral arrangements as the record's most divisive element. Its lasting significance rests less on innovation than on being an early example of the full-album jazz-vocal tribute concept in Simone's catalogue, and a showcase for how thoroughly she could rework other composers' material.

The Label

This copy carries the plain white/grey Colpix label with the film-strip "COLPIX RECORDS" logo and torch-bearing figure, "A DIVISION OF COLUMBIA PICTURES CORPORATION" rim text and "33 1/3 LONG PLAY" at the bottom - the standard early-1960s Colpix stereo design used across the SCP-4xx series, with CP-425/SCP-425 issued in 1962. No deep groove or later corporate rebrand text is present, consistent with an early-1960s Colpix pressing rather than a later reissue label. The catalogue number, title setting ("NINA SIMONE sings ELLINGTON") and matrix prefixes (N7OY 8604/8605) are those of the original stereo pressing of SCP-425, placing this squarely as an original-era Colpix pressing rather than the 1980s Roulette reissue or the modern European 180g reissues, which use entirely different labels.

Fidelity

This is a Monarch-pressed (MR stamp) Colpix stereo cut typical of early-1960s Colpix jazz/pop LPs; it was not a Van Gelder-engineered session and has no audiophile reissue chain to speak of - subsequent editions are budget European DMM/180g colored-vinyl pressings (Vinyl Passion) rather than mastering upgrades. Collector commentary on this era of Colpix is thin; the original stereo pressing is generally regarded as the most faithful cut of the 1961 session tapes, ranking above the compressed-sounding Vinyl Passion reissues and well above any digital-sourced later editions.
