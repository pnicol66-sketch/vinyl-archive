This Pressing

1984 UK first pressing on JSP Records - pink/white split label, 112 Sunny Gardens Road address, catalogue JSP 1079.

The Album

This LP pairs two documents of the postwar country-blues tradition, undocumented in most standard references, united by their connection - real or claimed - to Robert Johnson. Johnny Shines, who actually travelled and played with Johnson across the Depression-era South, went on to build his own international career from the 1960s onward, becoming one of the most compelling links between prewar Delta style and the electric blues that followed; his side here was drawn from a 1978 festival performance in the Netherlands, delivered solo and largely in his own idiom rather than as pure Johnson repertoire. Blind Will Dukes is an undocumented, obscure figure outside this release, a Detroit guitarist who claimed to have been shown Johnson's style firsthand and whose entire discography effectively begins and ends with the informal 1975 field session heard on side two, built heavily around Johnson standards alongside a handful of originals. The pairing captures both the mainstream survival of Delta blues through a well-documented elder statesman and the raw, largely undocumented margins of the tradition through a performer almost no one else recorded. For collectors of vernacular blues the album's importance rests less on studio polish than on rarity and testimony: it is one of the only recorded windows onto Dukes at all, framed alongside Shines at a mature, confident stage of his live career. Critically the album has been valued chiefly as a folklore document rather than a front-rank artistic statement, prized by specialists in the genre for what it preserves rather than for chart or crossover impact.

The Label

The label is a flat (non-deep-groove) pink-and-white split design with a boxed JSP Records logo at centre-right, the JSP 1079 catalogue number and artist/title credit on the white half, and the 112 Sunny Gardens Road, London NW4 address printed on the pink half - the standard house style JSP used for its early-to-mid 1980s blues reissue and compilation series. This address and logo place the pressing squarely in JSP's first decade of operation (the label was founded in 1978), and the design is the only one documented for this catalogue number, so it also represents the sole first pressing of the title rather than a later re-set label sharing the same address.

Fidelity

Side one was cut from a 1978 festival tape of Johnny Shines performing solo, and side two from a 1975 informal field session with Blind Will Dukes, so both sources are modest, semi-professional recordings rather than studio masters. The cutting and pressing were handled through a UK jobbing facility, consistent with JSP's small-label reissue and compilation output of the period, and playback quality is generally regarded by specialist collectors as functional rather than audiophile. There is no audiophile or remastered vinyl edition of this title to rank against; the original UK LP is effectively the primary and best-regarded vinyl source, with a later compact disc reissue offering the only alternative format.

Notes

This is the 1984 UK JSP Records issue of the title, the only vinyl edition documented for catalogue number JSP 1079. The pink-and-white split label with the boxed JSP Records logo and the 112 Sunny Gardens Road address matches the label's design in its first decade of operation. The side A etched runout on this copy matches the identifiers recorded for the release exactly; the side B runout carries a different trailing suffix than the one recorded, a detail that does not affect the pressing's identification as this edition.
