The Album

Recorded at Fine Recording in New York between February and August 1959, this album was built around Dinah Washington's breakthrough crossover single, a dramatically retitled reworking of María Grever's 'Cuando vuelva a tu lado' with English lyrics by Stanley Adams. Producer Clyde Otis and arranger-conductor Belford Hendricks framed Washington's blues-rooted phrasing and gospel-tinged diction within lush, pop-oriented string and rhythm settings, a deliberate step away from her earlier straight blues and jazz sides. The title performance climbed into the pop Top 5 and won Best Rhythm & Blues Performance at the second Grammy Awards, later entering the Grammy Hall of Fame. Surrounding the hit are standards like 'I Remember You,' 'Cry Me a River,' 'Manhattan,' and 'A Sunday Kind of Love,' showcasing Washington's equal command of torch balladry and up-tempo swing. Arriving after fifteen years as a top blues and R&B star billed as 'Queen of the Blues,' the record marked the pivotal pop crossover of her career, paving the way for 'Unforgettable' and her celebrated duets with Brook Benton. Critics and historians regard it as a landmark of late-1950s vocal jazz/pop-R&B fusion and among the definitive statements of Washington's artistry.

The Label

This copy carries Mercury's early black/silver mono 'A2' label design: arched MERCURY logo, LONG PLAYING/MICROGROOVE rim text, catalog number and 'Side' at left, 'A Custom High Fidelity Recording' at right, no oval logo above the spindle hole, and a pronounced deep groove visible on both side photos. Mercury's mono MG-20000 labelography places this plain-arch, no-oval-logo A2 style in the earliest configuration used circa 1958-59, before the label added the three-stroke 'A3' variant and later the oval-logo 'B' design used into the early 1960s, and well before the red 'Mercury head in black banner' reissue label used by the mid-1960s. The typed runouts MG 20479A MS1 (side 1) and MG 20479B MS2 (side 2) sit within Mercury's original Richmond-era mono matrix run; Discogs documents an MS1/MS1 stamped variant with an etched 'F' finisher mark as one known configuration, so this MS1/MS2 pairing is a distinct, slightly later-lacquered stamper for side 2 within the same first-issue label design. This places the copy solidly in the 1959 original mono first-issue window, just not the earliest-possible both-sides-MS1 stamper combination.

Fidelity

George Piros engineered the New York sessions at Fine Recording, with separate mono and stereo masters cut; this disc carries the original mono tape via an MS1/MS2 Richmond-style lacquer pairing. No cutting-engineer initial appears in the typed matrix, so it cannot be credited as a Fine Recording 'FF' cut. Deep-groove manufacture ties it to Mercury's late-1950s National Record Pressing-era plants, generally a step above routine 1960s black-label and later red-label reissues. Collectors often prize the companion stereo SR-60158 for its wide, if unusually hard-panned, soundstage, while this mono pressing gives the more centered, focused vocal image typical of era mono cuts. The 2026 Verve Vault all-analog reissue (Ryan K. Smith, Optimal) is the modern benchmark for clean transfer quality, but this original mono pressing remains the reference vintage mono listening choice.

Notes

1959 Mercury MG-20479 mono - original US first-issue pressing. The black/silver A2 label design (no oval logo, arched MERCURY, deep groove) matches Mercury's earliest MG-20000 mono configuration, predating the A3 and oval-logo B designs. Typed runouts MG 20479A MS1/MG 20479B MS2 place it in the original Richmond-era mono matrix sequence, though side 2's MS2 indicates a second lacquer rather than the earliest possible both-sides-MS1 stamper pairing documented on some copies. No finisher mark was supplied to pin down the exact plant beyond the likely Mercury/National Record Pressing operation.
