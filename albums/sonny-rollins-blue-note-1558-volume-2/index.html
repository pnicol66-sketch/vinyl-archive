The Album

Recorded in 1957 for Blue Note, this second volume paired Sonny Rollins with two very different rhythm sections: a working quintet with J.J. Johnson, Horace Silver, Paul Chambers and Art Blakey, and a trio date alongside Thelonious Monk that produced “Misterioso” and “Reflections.” Rollins was already established as one of the leading tenor voices to emerge from the bebop generation, and this session captured him stretching into looser, more exploratory forms while still rooted in the blues and standards vocabulary of hard bop. The Monk collaborations are historically significant, documenting a rare studio meeting between two of the music's most original improvisers just as Monk's own compositions were gaining wider recognition. Johnson's trombone brings a harder-edged contrast to Rollins's tenor on the quintet tracks, while the closing standards give Rollins room for extended, inventive improvisation. The album has long been regarded by critics and collectors as a key entry in the Blue Note Rollins catalogue, prized for the interplay between its leader and his collaborators. It remains an essential document of late-1950s hard bop and of Rollins's rapid maturation as a composer and soloist in the years just before his celebrated trio recordings.

The Label

The label is the cream and blue Analogue Productions design, reproducing the old 47 West 63rd NYC address and BLUE NOTE rim lettering with no deep groove. Each side carries a 2009 EMI Music Special Markets pressing credit and a 2009 Analogue Productions design copyright, marked ORIGINALLY RELEASED AS BLP 1558 and 45 MICROGROOVE LONG PLAYING. This is the Blue Note Reissues series from Analogue Productions, cut at 45 RPM across two LPs and mastered at AcousTech. It is a later pressing of the title, below every Blue Note issue made before 2009, including the original West 63rd-era repress whose label design it copies.

Fidelity

This edition was cut by Kevin Gray and Steve Hoffman at AcousTech Mastering and pressed at RTI as a 45 RPM two-LP set, a format intended to maximize groove resolution over the single-LP original cut. Collectors on the forums generally rate the AcousTech 45 RPM Blue Note Reissues highly for quietness and clarity, placing them above standard reissues though still a step removed in character from an original Rudy Van Gelder-mastered first pressing. Within this title's fidelity hierarchy, this pressing ranks as one of the better-regarded audiophile cuts, below only the original lacquer but ahead of the various stereo and Japanese reissues from the 1960s-80s.

Notes

This is the 2009 Analogue Productions Blue Note Reissues edition, pressed as a 2x12-inch 45 RPM mono set and numbered on a sticker on the back cover. The side labels reproduce the vintage 47 West 63rd Blue Note design but carry an explicit 2009 EMI Music Special Markets pressing credit and 2009 Analogue Productions design copyright, dating manufacture no earlier than 2009. The runouts carry the AcousTech/RTI codes for this series rather than any period Blue Note stamp, and the jacket front credits the AcousTech mastering team. This is a later audiophile pressing of the title, not an original or early repress.
