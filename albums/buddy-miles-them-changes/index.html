The Album

Them Changes arrived in 1970 as Buddy Miles' first major post-Band of Gypsys statement, made after his brief but era-defining stint drumming alongside Jimi Hendrix and Billy Cox. Fronting a new large horn-and-rhythm outfit billed here as the Freedom Express, Miles fused hard funk, soul balladry and rock guitar into a single, muscular sound anchored by his own booming drumming and gospel-inflected vocals. The title track became his signature song, an instantly recognizable funk-rock groove that would be covered and sampled for decades and remains his best-known composition. The Label

The Side 1/2 labels are the standard orange Mercury 'globe' design with the oval Mercury logo top and small repeating oval logos around the rim, matching the Mercury corporate label used circa 1970. The rim text reads 'manufactured and distributed by Mercury Record Productions, Inc.' in lowercase, the original-release rim-text variation for SR-61280; a later state of the same label reset this text in uppercase. This lowercase rim text, combined with the catalogue number SR-61280 and stereo designation, places this copy within the original 1970 US first-issue label-design window rather than the later uppercase-text state or a subsequent reissue. No deep groove or other era marker is present on this 1970 Mercury pressing, as expected for the type.

Fidelity

No specific mastering engineer credit appears for the original 1970 Mercury cut; it was pressed in-house at Mercury Record Manufacturing Company (plant code MR) rather than by an outside audiophile mastering house. Collectors on the forums have raised the question of how the CD compares to the LP without reaching a settled consensus on the best-sounding pressing. Collector opinion on the original describes it as a 'fantastic sounding pressing' with 'good instrument separation,' while some Canadian pressings are separately reported with distortion on side A4/B1; the 2014 Music On Vinyl 180g reissue also draws praise for its quiet surfaces. With no audiophile remaster (no Analogue Productions, Mobile Fidelity, or similar edition) existing for this title, this 1970 US stereo original sits at the top of the available fidelity hierarchy, ahead of the 2003 CD remaster and the 2014 MOV vinyl reissue, both of which derive from it.

Notes

This is a 1970 US Mercury SR-61280 stereo pressing in the embossed textured gatefold jacket. The label's lowercase 'manufactured and distributed by' rim text places this copy in the original-issue window, as opposed to a later reset of the same address text in uppercase. The runouts read SR 61280-A-M6 A1 E / IS 1280-B-M6 A2 E, which do not match the two commonly known stamper states (M1 1-1 MR and M1 1-11 MR) for this catalogue number, pointing to an additional, undocumented stamper generation within the same original Mercury pressing run rather than a different release. The album was released in June 1970, reaching #35 on the Billboard 200, consistent with a first-run pressing of this scale. No deep groove or other secondary era marker is present, as expected for a 1970 Mercury pressing.
