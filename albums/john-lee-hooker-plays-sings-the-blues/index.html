This Pressing

1986 Chess/MCA reissue (CH-9199) of the 1961 Chess LP-1454 compilation; later pressing identified by its barcode, behind the 1961 mono original and earlier reissues.

The Album

These sides capture John Lee Hooker at the very start of his recording career, cut solo in Detroit in 1951-52 with nothing but his amplified guitar, his voice, and his foot tapping out a rhythm on a wooden pallet - the hypnotic one-man-band sound that would define him for the next four decades. Hooker had left Mississippi sharecropping country as a teenager, drifted through Memphis and Cincinnati, and settled in wartime Detroit, where he worked day jobs while playing house parties and clubs at night; these raw, loosely metered recordings are the sound of that apprenticeship turning into a style all his own. The material is a mix of his own compositions and one Muddy Waters associate's tune, delivered with the free, talking-blues phrasing and droning boogie riffs that became his signature. Hooker's primitive, hypnotic guitar style bypassed the more polished Chicago blues of the era and went on to influence a generation of rock and blues-rock guitarists. The album stands as an early, unvarnished document of an artist whose solo Detroit sides remain foundational to postwar blues, prized for their rawness rather than any studio sheen. Collectors and historians regard this body of work as the root from which Hooker's long, influential career grew.

The Label

The label is a light-blue to teal ground with cream print, the CHESS RECORDS wordmark curved around the top rim and a cream checkerboard band around the lower rim, with the MCA/Universal Plaza manufacturing line and a ℗ 1986 MCA Records credit beneath the track list - the design MCA/Chess used on its mid-1980s reissue program after acquiring the Chess catalogue. This is not the original 1961 Chess label design; the catalogue number CH-9199 and the Side 1/Side 2 wordmark layout seen here belong to the 1986 reissue series pressed for MCA. The jacket carries a barcode, a mark not used before 1977, so the pressing is from 1977 or later. Within that 1986 reissue run, more than one plant pressed copies of this catalogue number.

Fidelity

The source masters are Hooker's spare 1951-52 Detroit solo recordings, originally cut for the United/Sensation labels and licensed to Chess; this 1986 MCA-era reissue uses the Artisan Sound Recorders lacquer cut common to Chess's mid-1980s reissue program rather than a new audiophile remaster. Collectors generally treat these MCA-era Chess reissues as serviceable listening copies rather than sonic showcases, ranking well below the later audiophile vinyl reissues of this title (such as the 180-gram Chess 75th Anniversary Series and Doxy/DOL pressings) in pressing quality and surface noise, though still preferable to the compact disc issues of the same era for warmth.

Notes

This is a 1986 Chess/MCA reissue pressing of the 1961 compilation LP-1454, not an original pressing: the jacket carries a barcode, a mark not used before 1977, placing the pressing in 1977 or later, and the label is MCA/Chess's mid-1980s design with the Universal Plaza manufacturing address. The catalogue number CH-9199 was used across more than one 1986 pressing plant, and the pressing plant for this copy is not identified on the label or in its runout. The original material dates to Detroit sessions in 1951-52, first issued by Chess in 1961.
