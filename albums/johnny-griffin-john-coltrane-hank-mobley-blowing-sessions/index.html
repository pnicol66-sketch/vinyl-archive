This Pressing

1975 Blue Note Re-Issue Series twofer, mono - United Artists Music and Records Group pressing, not a first pressing of either original album.

The Album

This twofer gathers two hard-bop tenor-battle dates cut within a month of each other in the spring of 1957 at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio. The first brings together three towering but very different tenor stylists - Johnny Griffin, John Coltrane and Hank Mobley - for a marathon blowing date built on standards and originals, with Lee Morgan, Wynton Kelly, Paul Chambers and Art Blakey driving the rhythm. The second, led by the young Clifford Jordan and John Gilmore, pairs two Chicago-rooted tenor men in a similarly combative format, backed by Horace Silver, Curly Russell and Art Blakey. Both sessions capture the era's fascination with the unaccompanied cutting-contest format, in which the jam-session tradition of the big bands and after-hours clubs was brought into the studio as a formal recording concept. Griffin, Coltrane and Mobley each represented a distinct stage in tenor saxophone language - Griffin's blistering speed and bebop fluency, Coltrane's emerging harmonic exploration on the cusp of his mature style, and Mobley's lyrical, soulful hard-bop phrasing - making their joint appearance a rare document of three contrasting masters sharing the same material. Jordan and Gilmore, both still early in their careers and both steeped in the Chicago tenor tradition, offer a younger, less celebrated but equally fervent reading of the same combative format. Coltrane's participation, recorded only months before his historic stint with Thelonious Monk and his breakthrough as a leader, gives the first session particular historical weight within his own development. Collectively the two sessions stand as valuable documents of hard bop's competitive, blues-and-swing-rooted ethos in the mid-1950s, and they remain frequently cited examples of the tenor-battle tradition in jazz history.

The Label

All four labels are the all-blue Blue Note Re-Issue Series design: a large white “b” with a musical note in a grey or navy circle at the upper left, “THE BLUE NOTE / RE-ISSUE SERIES” curved around it, and a rim crediting United Artists Music and Records Group, Inc., Los Angeles, California 90028, Made in U.S.A. This design and the United Artists Music and Records Group manufacturing credit belong to Blue Note's mid-1970s reissue program, run after Liberty/UA consolidated the catalogue, and the label itself carries a 1975 copyright line. No deep groove is present on this flat label era pressing. The design places this copy squarely as a 1975 twofer reissue pairing the 1957 Griffin/Coltrane/Mobley and Jordan/Gilmore sessions, not as any form of first pressing of either original album.

Fidelity

The sides were mastered from the Van Gelder session tapes for this mid-1970s Blue Note Re-Issue Series pressing rather than cut directly by Van Gelder himself for the reissue program. Collectors generally regard these United Artists-era Blue Note twofers as solid but unspectacular, serviceable listening copies rather than audiophile-grade pressings. This pressing sits below any original single-LP mono first pressings of the source sessions (Blue Note 1559 and 1549) in the fidelity hierarchy, and below later audiophile single-album reissues of those same sessions where they exist, but it remains a perfectly listenable way to hear both dates on one release.

Notes

This is the 1975 Blue Note Re-Issue Series two-LP mono compilation (BN-LA521-H2), combining the 1957 Griffin/Coltrane/Mobley and Jordan/Gilmore tenor sessions, originally issued separately as Blue Note 1559 and 1549. The all-blue Re-Issue Series label design and the United Artists Music and Records Group manufacturing credit, together with the 1975 copyright line, mark this as a mid-1970s reissue pressing rather than an original mono first pressing of either source album. The runout carries BN-LA521 with side numbers and a trailing numeric suffix on each side.
