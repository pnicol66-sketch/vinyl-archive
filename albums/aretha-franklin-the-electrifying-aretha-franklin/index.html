The Album

Cut at Columbia's New York studios between July 1961 and January 1962, this was Aretha Franklin's second album for the label, made a week before her twentieth birthday. John Hammond, who had signed her out of her father's Detroit church circuit on the strength of a demo, had framed the debut with Ray Bryant's small group; for the follow-up he brought in arranger Richard Wess, fresh from Bobby Darin's big-band hits, with Bob Mersey handling two titles. The result pushes a gospel-reared voice into brass-and-strings showbiz material - “Rock-A-Bye Your Baby With A Dixie Melody,” the Jolson warhorse that became her first real pop hit, “That Lucky Old Sun,” “Ac-Cent-Tchu-Ate The Positive” - alongside three J. Leslie McFarland blues-soul originals including the stomping “Rough Lover.” Critics of the day marked her as the most original jazz singer on the horizon and Down Beat's readers had already named her new star female vocalist, even as the question of whether Columbia knew what to do with her hung over the whole project. Franklin herself sounds entirely unbothered, taking the standards at a tempo and with a fervour nobody had asked for. The record sits at the hinge of her Columbia years: too churchy for the supper clubs, too arranged for jazz purists, and in hindsight the clearest early evidence of the force that Atlantic would unleash five years later. It remains the most collected of her pre-Atlantic albums and the one that gave her a hit single before anybody called her the Queen of Soul.

The Label

Red centre label inside a broad black rim carrying six camera-eye devices, three down each side, with STEREO and FIDELITY set either side of a red-and-white double arrow at the top and CBS inside the arrow; COLUMBIA in large white capitals at the foot and the rim line ® “COLUMBIA” ℗ MARCAS REG. MADE IN U.S.A., with NONBREAKABLE under the catalogue number and the side matrix XSM 55475 / XSM 55476 printed at right. Columbia carried this six-eye design from the mid-1950s until June 1962, when the two-eye label replaced it, and 360 Sound followed from 1963; the album was issued on 19 March 1962, so the six-eye stereo label is the first-issue label for CS 8561. Copies wearing the two-eye or 360 Sound ring are later presses of the same number and rank below this one. The stereo six-eye is the senior configuration of the title, the mono CL 1761 six-eye standing beside it.

Fidelity

Cut in-house by Columbia from the three-track session tapes and pressed at Pitman, this stereo six-eye carries the big, open sound of early-sixties Columbia stereo: the Wess brass and strings are spread wide, Franklin's voice forward and unsqueezed, with the heavy early-sixties vinyl keeping surfaces quiet. Collectors consistently prefer the original six-eye cuts of Franklin's Columbia titles to the thinner later-sixties repressings of the same number and to the digitally sourced European reissues that carry the album now. Within the title's hierarchy only a clean mono six-eye is a serious rival, and it trades punch for a slightly tighter centre image.

Notes

1962 Columbia CS 8561 stereo - original six-eye pressing of the title. The red six-eye label with the CBS double arrow and STEREO FIDELITY rim is the design Columbia used up to June 1962, three months after this album's 19 March 1962 release, so it is the first-issue label; two-eye and 360 Sound copies of the same number are later. The runout reads XSM-55475-1B P and XSM-55476-1A P, the P marking the Pitman, New Jersey plant, with side 2 on the first lacquer and side 1 on a second cut of the same original-era metalwork. The jacket is the stereo issue, STEREO banner across the top and CS 8561 in the corner box above the monaural cross-reference.
