This Pressing

2007 US first and only pressing of this catalogue number - Cisco Music/Firebird 180g stereo LP, licensed from King Records.

The Album

Gary Karr, the foremost solo double-bassist of his generation and the protégé who popularized the instrument as a concert voice rather than an orchestral fixture, recorded this program in December 2005 at the Ark Hall and the Marquee Hall in Tokorozawa, Japan, with longtime pianist-organist collaborator Harmon Lewis. It was billed as his final album for King Records, closing out a long association with the Japanese label that had documented much of his solo career. The repertoire is a recital of transcriptions - Rodrigo's guitar concerto theme, Respighi's lute-derived dances, Tchaikovsky's famous Andante Cantabile, Lehár's operetta aria, Ravel's Pavane, Massenet's Elegie, and Barber's Adagio for Strings - arranged by Karr and Lewis themselves to suit the bass's register and vocal-like legato. The program continues Karr's lifelong project of expanding the double bass's recital literature through sympathetic transcription rather than original composition. Critically, Karr's tone and musicianship were long regarded as having rehabilitated the bass as a solo instrument, and this late-career recital was received as a fittingly lyrical farewell to his Japanese recording relationship, showcasing the warmth and singing quality that defined his sound throughout a decades-long international career.

The Label

Both sides carry a white/cream ground with silver-grey print, the Firebird logo at top centre, a silver bass-player figure, and the Cisco Music oval logo at the bottom; the rim reads “Recorded by KING RECORDS, Tokyo, Japan. ℗ © 2007 King Records” with “Manufactured & distributed by Cisco Music, Inc. under exclusive license from King Records… Made in U.S.A.” The label shows no deep groove. The 2007 copyright line is the only pressing this label design can belong to: it is the first and, on this catalogue number, the only US vinyl issue, placing it ahead of the later Japanese CD-era reissue on a different number.

Fidelity

The runout carries a mastering engineer's signature and an RTI three-step plating code, marks of a boutique audiophile pressing. Collectors rate 180g limited-edition Cisco Music/Firebird pressings of this era as clean, well-centered pressings with low surface noise. This is the only vinyl cut of the album produced for the US market, so it stands as the top of its own fidelity hierarchy with no rival domestic edition to rank against; the later Japanese CD-era reissue is a separate catalogue item rather than a competing audiophile cut.

Notes
