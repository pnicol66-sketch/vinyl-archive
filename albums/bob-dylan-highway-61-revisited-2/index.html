The Album

Recorded in New York in June and July 1965, this album marked Bob Dylan's full turn from acoustic protest folk to an electric, surreal rock and blues idiom, a shift that had already stunned and divided audiences that summer. The album opens with a six-minute single built around a snarling organ riff and an escalating vocal performance, a song whose length and tone upended what a hit record was supposed to be and became one of the most influential recordings in popular music. The remaining songs pair hard Chicago-style blues shuffles with some of Dylan's most vivid and absurdist lyric writing, stacking carnival imagery, literary and historical figures and black humor over a loose, live-in-the-studio band sound. The title track and its closing nine-minute piece pushed the length and ambition of a rock recording further than almost anything that preceded it, closing the record on an unresolved, epic note. The record arrived in the midst of the uproar over Dylan's electric sets that year, cementing his reputation as both a lightning rod and a visionary songwriter who had outgrown any single genre. It is now regarded as one of the defining albums of 1960s rock, a touchstone for the fusion of poetic lyric writing with electric blues-rock instrumentation, and a permanent fixture near the top of any accounting of the greatest rock albums ever made.

The Label

The side labels are the classic Columbia two-eye red design: the walking-eye logo boxed at each side of the spindle, an arc reading "360 SOUND" at the bottom rim with "MONO" centered beneath it, and the catalogue number with the word "NONBREAKABLE" printed beneath it next to the hole. That NONBREAKABLE line is a feature of the earliest Columbia pressing runs on this catalogue number before the label text was reset; both copies in hand carry it, placing them in that early label-design window. Within that same label era the runout suffixes separate the two copies by stamper generation rather than by label artwork - this copy's later-lettered stampers follow the other copy's earlier ones off the same set of masters.

Fidelity

This is a Columbia 360 Sound mono cut from the original 1965 masters, mastered and pressed domestically for the mono configuration that was still the primary commercial format that year. Collectors on the forums consistently favor the original mono mix of this album over Columbia's contemporary stereo mix, hearing the mono as the tighter, more focused presentation of the full band and Dylan's vocal. Within the mono pressing run itself, earlier-stamper copies are generally regarded as marginally fresher sounding than later-stamper repressings such as this one, though the difference is subtle rather than dramatic; later stereo reissues and remasters are not considered to supersede a clean original mono pressing for this title.

Notes

This is a 1965 Columbia CL 2389 mono pressing on the two-eye "Nonbreakable" label, the earliest Columbia label design used for this catalogue number. The runout reads a later stamper letter on this copy (-1B/-1H) than the earliest-lettered stampers (-1A/-1A) carried by the first issue of this pressing, making this copy the later of the two despite sharing an identical label design. The back cover shows the corrected wording in its closing paragraph rather than the uncorrected word printed on the misprint first issue of this release.
