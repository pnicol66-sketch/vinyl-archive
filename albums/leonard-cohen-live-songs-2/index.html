The Album

Live Songs was assembled from recordings made during Leonard Cohen's 1970 and 1972 European tours, stitching together performances from London, Paris, Brussels, Berlin and the Isle of Wight into a single continuous-sounding album rather than a straightforward concert document. It arrived as the follow-up to Songs of Love and Hate, at a point when Cohen's reputation as a brooding troubadour of the counterculture was already established, and it leaned into that persona with a stark, often unsettling tone - most notably on the extended, confrontational "Please Don't Pass Me By (A Disgrace)." The album is unusual among live records for favoring mood and atmosphere over polish, with Cohen backed by shifting small ensembles rather than a fixed touring band, lending each piece a different texture. It captured him at a transitional moment, moving away from the hushed intimacy of his debut toward a rawer, more theatrical stage persona. Critical reception at the time was mixed, with some listeners finding it uneven and difficult next to his studio albums, but it has since been reassessed by devoted listeners as a revealing document of Cohen's early live presence and his restless relationship with his own songs. The cover material, including handwritten inserts drawing on the work of Daphne Richardson, reinforced the album's confessional, fragile character. It remains a cult entry in Cohen's catalogue, valued less for hits than for the glimpse it offers into his live artistry in the early 1970s.

The Label

The label is a plain orange CBS design with black print: a large square box centred on the spindle hole holds the CBS lettering above the single walking-eye device, with the catalogue number, side letter, the ℗ 1973 CBS Inc. line and MADE IN ENGLAND printed around it; the label is flat, without a deep groove. This is the standard UK CBS orange-label design used through the 1970s on this catalogue number, and the ℗ 1973 date printed on the label is the one mark the pressing cannot predate - it places this copy in 1973 or later rather than on any earlier dated line. This copy's stamped runout reads S65224A1/S65224B1 with additional etched suffix marks; other copies of this edition carry different stamper pairings (A3/B3, and A1/B1 combinations with additional etched symbols), and these pairings are not distinguishable as earlier or later pressings from the label design alone.

Fidelity

The album was produced and mixed by Bob Johnston from stage recordings made across several 1970 and 1972 European dates, so fidelity varies song to song depending on the original concert tapes rather than on this particular pressing's cut. UK CBS pressings of this era are generally solidly manufactured and this copy's clean stamped runouts suggest an unworn pressing from early in the stamper's life. Collectors tend to rate the original UK CBS pressing as the standard reference for this title, since no audiophile reissue series has superseded it; this copy sits with the ordinary run of first-era UK pressings rather than at either extreme of the fidelity spectrum.

Notes

This is a UK CBS stereo pressing of Live Songs, catalogue S 65224, with the standard orange CBS label carrying the ℗ 1973 CBS Inc. date - a mark that confirms this copy was made in 1973 or later and rules out any pre-1973 dating.
