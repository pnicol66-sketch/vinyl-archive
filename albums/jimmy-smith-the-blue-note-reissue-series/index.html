The Album

Jimmy Smith's Blue Note years, spanning the mid-1950s through the mid-1960s, established the Hammond B-3 organ as a lead voice in modern jazz and built the vocabulary for the soul-jazz movement that followed. This anthology draws together performances cut across that span - from the 1956 quartet date that produced The Preacher and The Champ through the 1958 Horace Silver tribute The Sermon, the 1960 Midnight Special session with Stanley Turrentine and Kenny Burrell, and later studio work from the mid-1960s. Smith's command of the instrument, pairing percussive bass-pedal walking lines with a horn-like right-hand attack, turned the organ trio and quartet into a viable small-group format rather than a novelty. The Sermon in particular, an extended blues-based jam built around an all-star front line, became one of the defining long-form performances of the Blue Note organ catalogue. Collected here, the material traces how Smith's working units evolved from guitar-and-drums trios toward larger, horn-driven dates, and underscores his influence on every organ player who followed him into soul jazz and funk. Critics and listeners have long treated these sessions as foundational texts of the genre, and compilations of this kind kept that body of work circulating for a new generation of listeners in the 1970s.

The Label

All four labels are the all-blue Blue Note design used for the mid-1970s United Artists-era reissue line: a large white b-with-note logo inside a dark maroon circle at the upper left, THE BLUE NOTE RE-ISSUE SERIES arched around it, and a rim crediting Blue Note Records as manufactured by United Artists Music And Records Group, Inc., Los Angeles, California 90028. No deep groove is present on any side. The ℗1975 United Artists Music And Records Group credit on every label is the plant's own date stamp for this UA-distributed reissue era, which ran from the mid-1970s; a label of this design and copyright line cannot predate 1975, placing this copy among the later compilation pressings of the catalogue number rather than any issue that could precede it. The catalogue number BN-LA400-H2 and the Blue Note Re-Issue Series branding mark this as a two-record anthology drawn from earlier Jimmy Smith sessions, repackaged rather than an original studio release.

Fidelity

The source material spans multiple original Blue Note analogue sessions recorded at Van Gelder's Hackensack and Englewood Cliffs studios, remastered for this mid-1970s United Artists-era compilation rather than cut from the original single-LP metal parts. Collectors generally regard these 1970s UA-era Blue Note reissues as serviceable but flatter and less dynamic than the original first pressings of the individual sessions, with the compilation format and era-typical mastering ranking below any audiophile reissue series that later addressed these same recordings individually. This pressing sits below the original Blue Note issues of each constituent album and below the modern audiophile single-album reissues (such as the Tone Poet or Music Matters lines where they exist for these titles) in the overall fidelity hierarchy for this material.

Notes

This 1975 Blue Note Re-Issue Series two-record compilation gathers earlier Jimmy Smith sessions under United Artists Music And Records Group's management of the Blue Note catalogue. Each label is all-blue with a white b-and-note logo in a maroon circle and the arched Re-Issue Series text, carrying the ℗1975 United Artists copyright line and no deep groove on any side. The catalogue number BN-LA400-H2 runs across all four runouts. This is a later anthology edition of the recordings it gathers, not an original single-LP issue.
