The Album

"Pres" Is Blue documents Lester Young's working club band caught live at the Savoy Ballroom in New York in 1950, captured not by a record label but by an audience member with a portable tape recorder who happened to be present on several nights. Young, the tenor saxophonist whose light, floating tone and rhythmic innovation had reshaped jazz phrasing in the Count Basie band of the late 1930s, was by 1950 a few years past his wartime military ordeal and the personal decline that followed it, yet still capable of the melodic invention and behind-the-beat swing that made him one of the music's most influential stylists. The band here-trumpeter Jesse Drakes, pianist Kenny Drew, bassist Aaron Bell and drummer Jo Jones-was Young's regular working unit of the period, and the relaxed, dance-hall setting gives the performances an informality absent from his studio dates. The liner notes make clear the tapes were never intended for release: they were acquired years later from the fan who made them, then cleaned up and re-recorded at RCA Victor's New York studios in April 1962 before Charlie Parker Records issued them. Critically the album has always been valued less for fidelity than for content, prized by Young specialists and completists as a rare document of his in-person sound away from the recording studio, offering warm, spacious readings of standards like "Stardust" and "Pennies From Heaven" that reveal a side of his artistry the formal sessions rarely captured. It stands as a footnote but a cherished one in the Lester Young discography, evidence of a still-vital player working a dance floor rather than a control booth.

The Label

The side labels are cream/yellow with the oval "CP | Parker Records" logo and rooster mascot, catalogue number PLP-405, "MONO-PACT!" designation, and rim text reading "Charlie Parker Record Corp., New York - Distributed by MGM Records, New York." This is the only label design Discogs documents for this title; Charlie Parker Records (run by Aubrey Mayhew after acquiring rights to the Parker/Young name and catalogue) used this exact label format for its early-1960s mono jazz reissue line, placing this pressing in that 1962-63 window rather than any later reissue era. A near-identical copy exists on Discogs with the same label design but slightly different pressing rings, indicating two stamper generations from the same original run rather than a distinct later edition; no subsequent reissue (foreign or domestic) of this specific LP has been documented.

Fidelity

The source is a fan's portable-recorder aircheck of a 1950 Savoy Ballroom dance date, later cleaned up and re-dubbed at RCA Victor's New York studios in April 1962 before mastering; it is inherently a lo-fi, amateur-tape source rather than a proper studio recording, and period dealer descriptions note the sound is merely serviceable rather than hi-fi. No audiophile reissue, remaster, or alternate mastering of this LP is documented anywhere (Music Matters, Analogue Productions, Tone Poet, etc. have never touched it), so this original Charlie Parker Records pressing is by default the best and only vinyl fidelity option for the title; a listener's expectations should be set by the source recording, not the pressing.

Notes

This is a mono Charlie Parker Records PLP-405 pressing with the cream "CP Parker Records" rooster-logo label and "MONO-PACT!" designation, the only label design Discogs records for this title. The etched runouts ("PLP-405-S SIDE A/B SM") match Discogs's transcription for this exact variant almost verbatim, confirming the pressing rather than a later or foreign reissue (none is documented). The copy is factory sealed in its original shrink, with wear confined to the shrink itself rather than the jacket underneath, which is an unusually high-grade survivor for a small independent label release of this vintage.
