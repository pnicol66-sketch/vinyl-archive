The Album

Andrew Hill had already redefined what a Blue Note session could sound like earlier in the 1960s, and the three dates gathered here - from February 1965, August 1969 and January 1970 - show him continuing to push the music's structure and timbre years after those sessions were actually recorded. The 1965 quartet with Joe Henderson and Freddie Hubbard is dense, through-composed post-bop in the vein of Hill's celebrated mid-1960s run; the 1969 date pairs his trio with a string quartet led by Kermit Moore, an unusual chamber-jazz experiment for the label; and the 1970 session with Bennie Maupin, Pat Patrick and Charles Tolliver pushes further into open, modal territory. None of this material saw release at the time it was made, a victim of Blue Note's shifting ownership and changing commercial priorities in the late 1960s and early 1970s. Hill himself was known as one of jazz's most restless composer-pianists, equally versed in blues language and advanced harmony, and these sessions capture him moving fluidly between those poles. When the material finally reached listeners, it was received as a valuable addition to the Hill discography, filling in a documented gap in one of the era's most searching bodies of work. The album stands as a testament to how much significant music from the period remained unheard, and to Hill's consistency as a composer across a five-year span of change.

The Label

The side labels show the periwinkle-blue Blue Note Re-Issue Series design: a white “b” and music-note logo set in a brown (side 1-2) or black/grey (side 3-4) disc at upper left, with THE BLUE NOTE / RE-ISSUE SERIES arcing around it, and the rim crediting manufacture to United Artists Music and Records Group, Inc. of Los Angeles. This design and the United Artists manufacturing credit place the pressing squarely in the 1975 vault-release program that United Artists ran for Blue Note after it folded the label into its own operations in the early 1970s; the ©/℗ 1975 date on every side confirms the copy cannot predate that year. As the album's sessions had never been issued before, this design is also the album's first and only original-issue label, with the Terre Haute-plant and German promo-cover copies standing alongside it as contemporary manufacturing variants rather than later reissues.

Fidelity

The three sessions were recorded at Van Gelder Studio in Englewood Cliffs between 1965 and 1970, but this LP was assembled and mastered for release in 1975 from the vault tapes rather than cut at the time of recording, so it does not carry the sonic reputation of Blue Note's contemporaneous original pressings. Collectors generally regard the United Artists-era Re-Issue Series pressings as competent but unremarkable vault-release mastering rather than an audiophile-grade cut. No high-end audiophile reissue of this particular title (Tone Poet, Music Matters, Analogue Productions or similar) has been issued, so this 1975 pressing remains the primary way to hear the music on vinyl.

Notes

This is the 1975 Blue Note Re-Issue Series pressing of previously unreleased Andrew Hill material, manufactured by United Artists Music and Records Group as stated on every side label. The periwinkle-blue label with the Re-Issue Series rim text and the ©/℗ 1975 date are consistent across all four sides and match the catalogue number etched in the runouts. As the sessions had never been issued before 1975, this pressing is the album's first and only original release, with contemporaneous Terre Haute-plant and German promo-cover copies standing as parallel manufacturing variants rather than later pressings.
