The Album

Birth of the Cool gathers a dozen sides Miles Davis recorded with a specially assembled nine-piece band across three 1949-1950 New York sessions, drawing on the unusual instrumentation (French horn, tuba) and softer, more arranged sound the group had unveiled during a brief but influential engagement at the Royal Roost in 1948. Davis assembled the project with close collaborators Gil Evans and Gerry Mulligan, along with writing contributions from John Lewis, John Carisi and George Wallington, reacting against the density of bebop in favor of restrained ensemble voicings, unhurried tempos and chamber-like counterpoint. Coming at the very start of his solo career, just after Davis had stepped out from Charlie Parker's quintet, the sessions announced him as a bandleader and conceptualist rather than merely a soloist, setting a template he would return to throughout his career for reinventing jazz through arranging and instrumental color. Though the original singles sold modestly, Capitol did not compile the sides into this LP (retroactively titled Birth of the Cool) until 1957, by which point the 'cool' and 'West Coast' schools it anticipated had already flourished. The music is now recognized as a foundational document of cool jazz and third-stream experimentation, prized for its unusual voicings, unhurried lyricism and the collaborative writing of the Nonet's arrangers rather than any single composer's vision. Critically it is regarded as one of the most influential jazz records of its era, shaping arrangers and small-group writers for decades and cementing Miles Davis's reputation as an artist whose greatest impact often came through concept and personnel as much as through his own horn.

The Label

This copy carries the early turquoise Capitol label with the large white "dome" logo centered at the top and the plain "LONG PLAYING" legend at the bottom of both side labels (no "High Fidelity" text), with rim text reading "MANUFACTURED BY CAPITOL RECORDS, INC. \u2022 HOLLYWOOD, CALIFORNIA \u2022 U.S.A." Per cvinyl.com/bsnpubs label-design timelines, this large gray/turquoise dome design was Capitol's standard label from the mid-1950s until 1959, when the company switched to a smaller oval dome logo on a black colorband label to coincide with the launch of stereo. The plain "Long Playing" (rather than "Long Playing * High Fidelity") wording and the absence of a Scranton anvil/lathe symbol in the runout place this copy at the earliest documented stamper state of the T-762 mono LP, i.e. the first-pressing configuration cataloged on Discogs, made not long after the album's original February/March 1957 release.

Fidelity

Mono mastering from the original 1949-50 ten-inch-era session tapes, cut for Capitol's 1957 compilation LP; sound is inherently limited by the vintage source rather than by this particular cutting, though collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally rate the early turquoise-label Capitol pressings as warmer and more natural than later black-colorband reissues or the 1971 'Complete' compilation, which some feel is brighter/thinner. This early no-anvil turquoise pressing sits at or near the top of the vintage mono fidelity hierarchy for T-762; later Capitol reissues, European Pathe Marconi pressings, and the expanded Capitol Jazz Classics/Complete Birth of the Cool LPs are generally regarded as secondary in sound to this original run, while modern audiophile mono reissues (where available) offer quieter surfaces but a more overtly 'remastered' tonal balance.
