The Album

Birth of the Cool compiles twelve sides Miles Davis recorded with a specially assembled nine-piece band across three 1949-1950 New York sessions, growing out of a brief but pivotal engagement the group had played at the Royal Roost in 1948. Davis conceived the project with close collaborators Gil Evans and Gerry Mulligan, drawing on writing contributions from John Lewis, John Carisi and George Wallington, and on the unusual instrumentation of French horn and tuba to create softer, more arranged ensemble textures than the prevailing bebop idiom. Coming immediately after Davis stepped out from Charlie Parker's quintet, the sessions announced him as a bandleader and conceptual arranger rather than simply a virtuoso soloist, a role he would return to repeatedly across his career. Though the original singles sold modestly on release, the music's restrained tempos, chamber-like counterpoint and unusual voicings anticipated the cool and West Coast jazz movements that flourished in the years before Capitol finally compiled the sides as this LP. The record is now regarded as one of the most influential documents in jazz history, foundational to cool jazz and an early example of "third stream" thinking that blended jazz with classical arranging sensibilities. Its collaborative writing and unusual sonic palette shaped generations of arrangers and small-group composers, and it remains central to Miles Davis's legacy as an artist whose impact often came as much through concept, personnel and arranging as through his trumpet playing.

The Label

This copy shows the early turquoise Capitol label with the large white "dome" (Capitol building) logo centered at top and the plain "LONG PLAYING" legend at bottom of both side labels, with no "High Fidelity" suffix text and rim text reading "MANUFACTURED BY CAPITOL RECORDS, INC. \u2022 HOLLYWOOD, CALIFORNIA \u2022 U.S.A." Per Discogs variant listings and bsnpubs/cvinyl label-design timelines, this large dome-logo turquoise design with plain "Long Playing" wording (rather than "Long Playing * High Fidelity") and no Scranton anvil symbol in the runout is documented as the first-pressing configuration for T-762, made shortly after the album's original 1957 release; the near-identical label but with "Long Playing * High Fidelity" and a Scranton anvil stamper mark constitutes the second pressing. The plain wording plus absence of the anvil mark and the Hollywood, California manufacturing text together confirm this is a first-issue-window US pressing rather than a Scranton, export, or later colorband/reissue copy.

Fidelity

Mono mastering drawn from the original 1949-50 session tapes, cut for Capitol's 1957 LP compilation; the sound is inherently limited by the vintage 10-inch-era source rather than by this cutting itself. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally rate the early no-anvil turquoise-label Capitol pressings as warmer and more natural than the later black-colorband reissues or the 1971 expanded 'Complete Birth of the Cool' compilation, which some find brighter and thinner sounding. This early turquoise, plain-'Long Playing', no-anvil pressing sits at or near the top of the vintage mono fidelity hierarchy for T-762, ranking above the Scranton-anvil second pressing, the colorband reissue, Pathe Marconi European pressings, and the expanded Capitol Jazz Classics/Complete compilations, while modern audiophile reissues (where they exist) trade some of this vintage warmth for quieter surfaces.
