The Album

Django gathers the Modern Jazz Quartet's earliest studio work, gradually assembled by Prestige from three sessions spanning June 1953 to January 1955, at a moment when the group - pianist/musical director John Lewis, vibraphonist Milt Jackson, bassist Percy Heath, and drummer Kenny Clarke - was still defining itself after splitting off from Dizzy Gillespie's rhythm section. The title track, Lewis's stately, elegiac theme, was written in memory of the great Belgian-Romani guitarist Django Reinhardt, who had died in 1953, and it became the group's signature composition, its call-and-response melody and dark harmonic color epitomizing the quartet's fusion of chamber-music formality with blues-rooted swing. The album also includes Lewis's multi-part "La Ronde Suite," an ingenious set of variations that gives each member a featured turn, alongside readings of Gillespie's "One Bass Hit" and standards by Ellington and Gershwin that show the group's more relaxed, swinging side. Django cemented the MJQ's reputation as jazz's most disciplined and compositionally ambitious small ensemble, a reputation that would carry them through decades of recordings for Prestige, Atlantic, and other labels. Critically, the record is regarded as one of the defining statements of 1950s cool/chamber jazz, prized for the interplay between Jackson's blues-drenched improvisation and Lewis's formal, almost baroque writing. It remains a foundational text in the MJQ's catalog and a touchstone for the "Third Stream" merging of jazz and classical sensibilities that Lewis would champion throughout his career.

The Label

The side labels show the classic Prestige "Fireworks" design: bright yellow field with black/dark-green print, an arc reading "PRESTIGE RECORDS INC. \u2022 446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C." around the top, a spiral "@" logo and starburst/comet motifs scattered around the rim, and "LONG PLAYING MICROGROOVE" arced along the bottom - with a visible deep-groove ring near the label edge. This "446 W. 50th St." address is Prestige's earliest 12-inch LP-era label design, used circa 1954-56 before the company's move to 203 W. 50th St. in the later 1950s (per bsnpubs Prestige label history). Its presence here, together with the deep-groove pressing ring, places this copy squarely in the first-pressing window for PRLP 7057, issued in 1956 shortly after the LP was assembled from the 1953-55 MJQ sessions.

Fidelity

Rudy Van Gelder cut the lacquers from the original 1953-55 session tapes (WOR Studios and Hackensack), pressed by Abbey Record Manufacturing (AB code); collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally praise the warmth and immediacy of these mid-1950s Van Gelder mono Prestige masterings over later tape-generation reissues. This yellow-label 446 W. 50th St. mono pressing is the earliest and most sought-after cut of Django, ranking above the later black Prestige/Fantasy reissue, the 1983 Original Jazz Classics (OJC-057) pressing mastered from safety copies, and the 2000s Concord/OJC "Rudy Van Gelder Remasters" and SACD digital remasters, none of which fully replicate the original lacquer's presence.

Notes

This is the 1956 Prestige PRLP 7057 mono first pressing. The side labels show the yellow/black "Fireworks" design with the "446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C." address, matching Prestige's earliest 12-inch LP label used before the later-1950s move to 203 W. 50th St. Visible deep groove on both sides and the collector's transcribed "AB" runout code (Abbey Record Manufacturing Co.) together with hand-etched RVG initials corroborate the Discogs first-pressing variant. No later-address, black-label, or reissue markers are present, so this copy sits at the top of the pressing hierarchy for this title.
