The Album

Songs Of The Poets is a 1976 RCA compilation drawing together Nina Simone's interpretations of Bob Dylan and George Harrison alongside two of her own most personal compositions, framing all four writers as "poets" of the era. The tracks are pulled from her early-1970s RCA sessions, including the celebrated live Philharmonic Hall recordings later issued as Black Gold and the studio-and-live Here Comes the Sun album, catching Simone at the height of her engagement with the counterculture's songwriting voices. By this point Simone had already established herself through Nina Simone Sings the Blues and the civil-rights-era anthems that made her a defining voice of black political music in America, and her readings of Dylan's "I Shall Be Released" and "Just Like Tom Thumb's Blues" reimagine folk-rock staples as dramatic, blues-inflected torch pieces. The set also preserves "The Backlash Blues," her setting of a Langston Hughes poem, and "To Be Young, Gifted and Black," co-written with Weldon Irvine and among the most enduring anthems of the Black Power movement. Critical reaction to the compilation itself has been mixed - Simone's Dylan and Harrison covers are often singled out as highlights while some of the material is regarded as merely serviceable - but the set endures as an accessible entry point to her early-1970s RCA work and to the depth of her interpretive artistry.

The Label

This copy carries the tan/cream RCA Victor label with the outlined 'RCA' logo and script 'Victor' wordmark, printed 'MADE IN U.S.A.' and ℗ 1976 RCA Records at the rim - the standard US label design RCA used for its APL1-series pop/jazz LPs in the mid-1970s. The catalogue number APL1-1788 and hand-etched runout ('1⊙1 G APL1-1788A-1' / 'B-1') match Discogs release 1697519, the primary US pressing of this title. A second US-catalogued copy (Discogs r2494812) carries a different etched code ('J1 ai') indicating a later stamper/repress run rather than a distinct label design; an Israeli pressing (Eastronics, 1977) and a Japanese pressing (RCA RVP6142) exist as separate national editions of the same compilation.

Fidelity

Remastered by Ed Begley from the original multitrack session tapes (studio and live) for this 1976 compilation; the tapes were sourced from several different Simone RCA dates (1967-1972), so sound quality varies noticeably track-to-track rather than reflecting a single unified session sound. Collector commentary on Discogs rates Side A (the Dylan covers) as the more sonically and performance-wise satisfying side, with Side 2 generally regarded as less essential. There is no audiophile reissue of this specific compilation (no Tone Poet/Analogue Productions/Mobile Fidelity equivalent exists for this title), so this US first pressing represents the top of the format's fidelity hierarchy alongside the contemporaneous Israeli and Japanese pressings, which use the same mastering.
