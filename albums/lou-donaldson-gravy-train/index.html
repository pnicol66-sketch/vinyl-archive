The Album

Recorded April 27, 1961 at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio and released by Blue Note in July 1962, Gravy Train captured altoist Lou Donaldson's working quartet with pianist Herman Foster, bassist Ben Tucker and drummer Dave Bailey, augmented by Alec Dorsey's congas for extra rhythmic heat. Coming on the heels of Blues Walk and Here 'Tis, the album continued Donaldson's turn from straight bebop toward a bluesy, backbeat-driven "soul-jazz" sound built on jukebox-friendly standards and originals, straight-ahead jazz, bebop and blues done Donaldson's way. It stands as a solid, representative entry in Donaldson's prolific early-1960s Blue Note run rather than a landmark, valued more for its consistency and groove than for any single innovation.

The Label

The side labels are the white/blue Blue Note design reading "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC · NEW YORK, USA" with no street address, the transitional 1962 New York-address label used just before the plain "NEW YORK, USA" font reset and the later loss of the Van Gelder ear/deep groove combination. The matrix (BN-LP-4079-A/B) carries an RVG stamp and EAR/Plastylite mark etched in the deadwax, among the last Blue Notes to carry the RVG stamp before it was replaced later in 1961-62 by the full "VAN GELDER" etching. This combination of NY-address label, deep groove on side 1, RVG stamp and Ear places this copy in the earliest, first-pressing manufacturing run of BLP 4079, ahead of any later 1960s NY-label repress that dropped the deep groove and RVG stamp.

Fidelity

Van Gelder's original 1961 all-analog tape, cut to lacquer by RVG himself as shown by the RVG stamp in the runout; the Ear (Plastylite) mark confirms an original Plastylite-pressed disc rather than a later custom-pressed run. Collectors on the forums generally rate these early-1960s NY-label Van Gelder-cut Blue Notes very highly for warmth and presence, ranking them above the mid-60s Liberty-era NY/Div. of Liberty pressings and well above generic reissues; no audiophile remaster (Music Matters, Analogue Productions, Tone Poet) of this specific title exists, so this original pressing represents the top of the album's known fidelity hierarchy alongside other RVG/Ear first pressings.

Notes
