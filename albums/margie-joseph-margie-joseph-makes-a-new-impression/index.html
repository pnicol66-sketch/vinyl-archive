This Pressing

Original US press (stereo, c. 1970) - Monarch yellow-bolt-label plant-error variant; VS-0157/VS-0158, Mastercraft marks, Δ15323/Δ15323-X.

The Album

Mississippi-born and church-trained, Margie Joseph arrived at Stax's Volt imprint in 1970 after early sessions cut with Muscle Shoals players while she was still based in New Orleans, and this debut LP became her definitive introduction to soul audiences. Producers Fred Briggs and Darryl Carter, working as the Colsoul team, built the record around a striking reinterpretation of the Supremes' "Stop! In the Name of Love," stretching it into an extended, dramatically monologue-prefaced centerpiece that showcased Joseph's gospel-trained phrasing and improvisational daring. The remaining material blended fresh Stax-Memphis originals with Muscle Shoals-cut sides, giving the album a hybrid Southern-soul sound that sat between the grit of Memphis funk and the polish of Alabama session musicianship. Joseph's voice - flexible, melodic, and more restrained than the era's belters - distinguished her from contemporaries and helped establish her as an underappreciated but critically respected deep-soul singer. The album arrived at a pivotal moment for Stax, newly independent from Atlantic and reshaping its Volt subsidiary's identity, and it helped signal the label's continued vitality in the early 1970s soul marketplace. Though never a major commercial blockbuster, it has grown in stature among soul collectors and critics as a showcase for both Joseph's artistry and the fertile Stax/Muscle Shoals creative exchange of the period.

The Label

Both side labels show a bright yellow field with the black "VOLT RECORDS" wordmark and a single large freestanding black double-zigzag/bolt device beneath it, followed by the artist name, quoted album title, catalog number VOS-6012, STEREO, and side/matrix data (VS-0157/VS-0158) at upper right - no ring text or arc band is present. This bold yellow-and-black bolt design is Volt's earlier/mid-1960s Atlantic-distribution-era house style; by the time this LP was actually issued in the newly-independent post-1970 Stax/Volt era, the era-correct VOS-6000-series design had shifted to the dark-blue label with red bolt seen on most confirmed VOS-6012 copies (including the Terre Haute and standard Monarch pressings documented on Discogs). Collectors have documented that Monarch's pressing plant used leftover stock of this older yellow-bolt label in error on some VOS-6000-series titles, and the printed matrices (VS-0157/VS-0158) with Mastercraft/Monarch stampers on this copy are consistent with that recognized plant-error variant rather than the standard contemporary blue-label pressing.

Fidelity

This is the original-era analog US cut, with Mastercraft metalwork and Monarch pressing. The documented yellow-label variant's cutting engineer initials are typically read as HC (Howard Craft) on standard blue-label pressings; the supplied transcription reads HL, so that attribution should be treated as probable rather than fully confirmed. Monarch's work here is competent but not audiophile-grade, yielding a warm, dense midrange and the somewhat compressed presentation typical of early-1970s Stax/Volt product; clean vinyl is especially important through the extended eight-minute "Stop! In the Name of Love." No dedicated Steve Hoffman forum consensus or standalone modern audiophile LP reissue exists for this title, so a clean original US pressing (blue or yellow label) remains the sonic reference point; the 1993 CD twofer reissue is not a fidelity competitor to a well-preserved original vinyl copy.

Notes

Original US Volt VOS-6012 stereo, pressed by Monarch, bearing the scarce older-style yellow-and-black double-bolt Volt label rather than the era-correct dark-blue/red-bolt design used on most copies of this title. The decisive evidence is that yellow label design itself, the printed side matrices VS-0157 (Side One) and VS-0158 (Side Two), and the supplied Mastercraft marks with Monarch Δ15323/Δ15323-X. Discogs documents the standard issue with blue labels (both Terre Haute and Monarch pressings), reinforcing that this yellow-label copy is the deviant plant-error pressing. The album was first advertised/released in late 1970, though Discogs, RYM, and many dealers assign 1971 based on its chart run; it is not the 1972 "Phase II" follow-up, VOS-6016.
