The Album

Mississippi-born and church-trained, Margie Joseph made demonstration recordings at Muscle Shoals while studying speech and drama at Dillard University in New Orleans. After two little-heard Okeh singles, she joined Stax's Volt imprint in 1969, where Fred Briggs and Darryl Carter's Colsoul production partnership shaped her debut album. Its audacious centerpiece was Briggs's idea to slow the Supremes' "Stop! In the Name of Love" into an eight-minute deep-soul drama, to which Joseph added her own spoken "Women Talk" introduction. Recording split between Stax in Memphis and Muscle Shoals Sound in Alabama, with additional orchestral and vocal work lending a Northern polish, let Joseph move between earthy Southern testimony and more urbane, jazz-inflected phrasing. The album reached No. 7 on Billboard's soul chart and No. 67 on the pop chart, while the single reached No. 38 R&B and No. 96 pop but grew into her signature performance through radio play. Joseph went on to make a celebrated series of Atlantic albums with Arif Mardin, including her hit interpretation of "My Love," but this debut established the combination of gospel force, dramatic restraint and interpretive daring that defines her reputation. Retrospective critics especially prize the album's transformation of a familiar Motown song into an Isaac Hayes-scale Southern-soul suite, one of the great cover reinventions of the era.

The Label

This copy has Volt's first-generation yellow design: black "VOLT RECORDS" header and a black rectangular device containing a yellow lightning bolt. That design belongs to the Atlantic-distributed 400-series era; following the 1968 Atlantic split and Gulf+Western purchase, the VOS-6000 albums normally used dark-blue labels with a red bolt. Monarch mistakenly applied the obsolete yellow design, without the Atco distribution line, to some 6000-series titles through VOS-6017, so this is a recognized plant-error variant rather than the standard 1968-71 label. The supplied matrices and runouts correspond to the documented Monarch variant: Side One VS-0157 with Δ15323, Side Two VS-0158 with Δ15323-X, plus the Mastercraft marks; the matching Discogs entry expands the cutting initials as HC, while the supplied transcription reads HL. Jim Stewart and Al Bell repurchased Stax from Gulf+Western in July 1970, placing this original-era issue in the newly independent Stax period; there is no 1972 "Phase II" revision of VOS-6012, as Phase II was the separate follow-up album VOS-6016.

Fidelity

This is the original-era analog US cut, with Mastercraft metalwork and Monarch pressing. The matching documented variant identifies the cutting initials as HC for Howard Craft; because the supplied transcription reads HL, that engineer attribution should be regarded as probable rather than fully settled. Monarch's pressing is generally competent but not an audiophile showcase: expect the warm, dense midrange and somewhat compressed orchestral presentation typical of early-1970s Stax/Volt, with clean vinyl especially important through the long centerpiece. No dedicated Steve Hoffman or audiophile consensus and no standalone modern audiophile LP reissue were found, so original US pressings remain the vinyl reference; the later two-fer CD is not a fidelity challenger to a clean original.

Notes

Original US Volt VOS-6012 stereo, pressed by Monarch with the scarce Atlantic-era yellow-label plant error. The decisive evidence is the yellow "VOLT RECORDS"/black-bolt labels, printed side matrices VS-0157 on Side One and VS-0158 on Side Two, and the supplied Mastercraft marks with Monarch Δ15323/Δ15323-X. The collector's matrix a/b entries are reversed relative to the side labels. The album was first issued in October 1970, although Discogs and many dealers assign 1971 from its chart run. It is not a 1972 "Phase II" revision: Phase II is Joseph's separate follow-up album, VOS-6016.
