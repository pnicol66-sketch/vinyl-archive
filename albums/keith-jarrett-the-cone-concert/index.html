The Album

The Köln Concert documents a solo piano recital Keith Jarrett gave at the Cologne Opera House on the night of January 24, 1975, under circumstances that nearly scuttled the performance entirely: a long, exhausting drive left Jarrett depleted, and the house supplied a small, out-of-tune rehearsal instrument with a thin upper register and sticking keys rather than the concert grand he expected. Rather than walk away, Jarrett built the entire concert as an unbroken improvisation shaped around the piano's shortcomings, favoring ostinato bass figures and the instrument's stronger lower and middle registers. The producer who urged him onstage captured the performance for release later that year as a double album with no pre-written material whatsoever - a pure act of real-time composition. It became the best-selling solo piano recording and the best-selling title in its label's catalog, introducing an enormous mainstream audience to extended improvised performance and establishing Jarrett as the defining solo improviser of his generation. The record is widely regarded as a defining statement of its label's aesthetic and of 1970s jazz, celebrated for melodic, folk- and gospel-tinged invention sustained across sidelong improvisations. Its influence persists both as a commercial touchstone and as a model for solo improvised concert performance well beyond jazz.

The Label

The labels on this copy carry the plain grey ECM wordmark (not the earlier ECM Records script), a GEMA rights line, a (P) 1975 ECM Records GmbH credit, rim text beginning "Alle Urheber- und Leistungsschutzrechte", and the label code LC 02516 at the foot of the label. ECM's German labels moved from the script wordmark (1975) to the plain ECM logo with the "Alle" rim wording the following year, and the LC code was not added to German record labels generally until 1977 onward. Because this label carries that code, it cannot be the 1975 first issue or the circa-1976 boxed-logo reissue that still lacks it; it sits later in the pressing run, after the code's introduction.

Fidelity

This is an analog German pressing from ECM's late-1970s/early-1980s run, not a remastered or audiophile edition. German ECM pressings of this era are generally well regarded among collectors for quiet surfaces and warm, detailed sound, though the 1975 first-issue lacquers are usually rated by collectors on the forums as the most transparent-sounding cut of the title. Later 180g reissues exist and are sometimes favored for surface noise alone, but collectors still place original-era German pressings like this one above generic later reissues and well above the CD for tonal warmth. Within the overall fidelity hierarchy this copy sits in the middle: below the true 1975 first pressing and the c.1976 reissue that preceded it, but above later audiophile and digital reissues.

Notes

This German pressing of ECM 1064/65 shows the plain ECM wordmark label with GEMA text, a 1975 copyright line, and the label code LC 02516 in the rim text. The 1975 first issue carries the earlier ECM Records script wordmark and no code; a c.1976 reissue already uses the plain logo and the "Alle" rim wording but still lacks the code. Because the LC code was not introduced until 1977, this copy postdates both of those earlier issues and ranks as a later, third-tier pressing of the title.
