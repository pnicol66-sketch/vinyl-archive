The Album

Copulatin' Blues Volume One gathers sixteen recordings made mostly in the 1930s by blues and jazz figures whose frankly sexual, double-entendre-laden songs were too explicit for mainstream release or radio play in their day. The anthology pairs issued sides with several performances that had been held back from release altogether because of their content, among them cuts by Bessie Smith, Sidney Bechet, Jelly Roll Morton, Lucille Bogan, Tampa Red and Lil Johnson. It reflects the hokum and vaudeville-blues tradition that ran alongside more conventional prewar blues, in which humor and innuendo carried frank sexual subject matter past the era's broadcasting and recording restraints. Several of the singers here, including Bogan under her pseudonym Bessie Jackson, were specialists in the 'dirty blues' subgenre and recorded alternate takes specifically for private or regional sale. The compilation sits within a broader 1970s reissue movement that resurfaced marginalized or suppressed prewar recordings for a new generation of blues collectors and scholars. Its liner notes situate the material within the social history of Black urban migration and the loosening of sexual mores in Prohibition-era nightlife. The set is valued by blues historians less for polish than for its documentary window onto an uncensored, often female-voiced strain of the genre that mainstream catalogs of the period suppressed. It remains a touchstone reissue for collectors of prewar blues bawdiness and helped cement the reputations of Bogan, Johnson and others as pioneers of the style.

The Label

The labels on this copy show the plain grey Stash moneybag logo centred above the title “COPULATIN' BLUES,” with the catalogue number ST-101 at left and the side number at right on both sides; no distinct earlier or later label typeset has been established for this catalogue number, so this design cannot be ranked against an alternate Stash pressing of the same number. The jacket carries the Stash Records Brooklyn, NY address and a 1976 copyright line, consistent with the label's first active years after its 1975 founding. Nothing in the label print or jacket text points to a later reissue state of this catalogue number.

Fidelity

The lacquers were cut by Sam Feldman from the compiled 1930s 78 rpm masters and pressed by Goldisc Recording Products; as a reissue compilation sourced from vintage 78s rather than studio tape, its fidelity is bounded by the condition and equalization of the source discs rather than by the cut itself. Collectors generally rate the Stash transfer as a solid, listenable restoration for its era rather than an audiophile showpiece, and no later audiophile remaster of this compilation is in wide circulation to outrank it.
