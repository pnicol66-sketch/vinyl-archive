The Album

Recorded in a single session on December 9, 1964, at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio, A Love Supreme was John Coltrane's four-part suite of thanksgiving for his 1957 spiritual awakening. Coltrane wrote an original testimony and poem inside the gatefold dedicating the work to God, an unusually candid gesture that set the album apart from his prior instrumental-only releases. It was released by Impulse! in January 1965, was nominated for the 1966 Grammy for Best Jazz Instrumental Album, and is "widely considered one of the greatest masterpieces in the history of jazz."

The Label

The side labels show the classic mid-1960s Impulse! design: an orange centre field with a black outer ring carrying two mirrored "impulse!" wordmarks (top and bottom) and the double-bar "i!" logo repeated four times around the rim, with "A PRODUCT OF ABC-PARAMOUNT RECORDS, INC. / PRINTED IN USA" beneath the credits and no street address printed - the design Impulse used for its catalogue through the mid-1960s before later pressings added a full corporate address block. This label design, together with the "A-77-A/B VAN GELDER" hand-etched runout, places the copy in the original mono Impulse! issue window (1965) rather than any later reissue or the unrelated Reader's Digest RDA 77-A box-set compilation, a completely different, later (1969) multi-LP repackaging and not this record.

Fidelity

This copy carries the Van Gelder hand-etched runout ("A-77-A/B VAN GELDER"), indicating it was cut at Van Gelder's studio rather than by Bell Sound, whose competing cutting facility is described by collectors on the forums as noticeably murkier and less precise on other 1960s Impulse titles. Collectors on the forums debate whether Impulse mono pressings of this era, including A-77, were true discrete mono mixes or fold-downs from the stereo master tape, with some threads posing it plainly - this LP...is it a fold down? Collectors on the forums, comparing surface noise between the needles and the grooves of various copies, also note that Van Gelder's original A Love Supreme master tape was reportedly lost by Bell Sound decades ago, with only a second-generation copy later recovered from EMI's vaults, a fact that bears on the fidelity ceiling of all vintage pressings of this title, original or otherwise.
