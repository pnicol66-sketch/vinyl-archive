The Album

Conception is a compilation assembled by Prestige from separate small-group sessions cut between 1949 and 1951, gathering early recordings by Lee Konitz, Miles Davis, Stan Getz and Gerry Mulligan during the crucible years of the 'cool' jazz movement that grew out of Davis's Birth of the Cool nonet. The Konitz sides, cut with pianist Sal Mosca and guitarist Billy Bauer, showcase the angular, harmonically advanced 'Tristano school' conception (hence the compilation's title, borrowed from George Shearing's tune but reflecting the musicians' individual musical conceptions, per Ira Gitler's liner notes). The Davis sides pair the trumpeter with a young Sonny Rollins, an early document of Rollins's emerging voice before his own leader dates. The Getz tracks with Al Haig capture the tenor saxophonist honing the light, Lester Young-derived tone that would define white cool-toned tenor playing for a generation. The Mulligan/Zoot Sims sides reflect Mulligan's writing for larger units before his own famed piano-less quartet. Collectively the set functions as a snapshot of cool jazz's roots, valuable both for its individual performances and for illustrating how these musicians' innovations rippled outward to influence subsequent players. Critically it is prized less as a unified statement than as an anthology of formative, historically important sessions by major cool-era figures.

The Label

This copy carries the yellow/black 'fireworks' Prestige label with the '446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C.' rim address and visible deep groove, matching the label photos exactly. Prestige used this Manhattan address on its labels only through the mid-1950s before relocating to 203 South Washington Ave., Bergenfield, NJ by 1958-59, so this address places the pressing in the earliest issue window for this compilation (1956). The RVG etch in the matrix (as typed) confirms Van Gelder mastering consistent with the first-pressing era; no Bergenfield address or later blue-label design is present, ruling out the mid/late-1950s and 1960s reissues.

Fidelity

Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder (RVG etched in the runout), this is an original mono Prestige pressing predating any stereo remaster of these mono-era sessions (stereo was not possible for material recorded 1949-51). Prestige's late-1950s Bergenfield-plant pressings from the same stampers are generally considered slightly inferior in surface quality to these earlier NYC-era pressings, and collectors on forums such as Steve Hoffman's generally rate the original yellow-label Prestige pressings above the later Prestige/Bergenfield reissue, the 1960s Prestige blue label reissue, and the 1989 Original Jazz Classics (OJC) reissue, which is a solid but audibly more distant remaster. This copy, as an early NYC-address deep-groove pressing, sits at or near the top of the fidelity hierarchy for this title.
