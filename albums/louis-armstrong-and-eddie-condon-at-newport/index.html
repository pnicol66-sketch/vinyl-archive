The Album

Captured live at the American Jazz Festival in Newport, Rhode Island on July 5 and 6, 1956, this album pairs Louis Armstrong's touring All-Stars with Eddie Condon's Chicago-style Dixieland ensemble on the same bill, though the two leaders never actually share a bandstand on record. By 1956 Armstrong's All-Stars, with Trummy Young, Edmond Hall and Billy Kyle, were a well-drilled unit whose Newport appearances had already become annual events for producer George Avakian's Columbia documentation of the festival. Condon, the guitarist-impresario who had been a fixture of small-group Chicago and New York jazz since the 1920s, brought a rotating cast of veteran swing and Dixieland players including Wild Bill Davison and Bud Freeman. The album captures the informal, good-natured competitive spirit that characterized the Newport festival's early years, with each group running through its own signature repertoire of standards and blues. The Label

Both side labels are the classic red Columbia "6-eye" design: white "COLUMBIA" arc logo at top, six small "eye" logos in black side panels (three per side, matching the deep-groove/6-eye Columbia standard used c.1955-1962), red field, black rim bands, and "NONBREAKABLE"/"MADE IN U.S.A." rim text with no ring-band slogan. The vinyl is noted by the collector as deep groove, consistent with early-to-mid Columbia 6-eye pressings before Columbia phased out deep groove circa 1958-59. The runout suffixes here (-1B and -1D) are later stamper generations than the earliest documented -1A/1A copies, so this is an early but not the very first stamper pressing within the same 6-eye deep-groove first-issue label design.

Fidelity

This is a mono live recording from 1956, mastered for Columbia's standard 6-eye era LP cutting rather than any audiophile process; sound quality reflects the limitations of remote festival recording of the period, with reasonable balance but limited depth compared to studio Columbia jazz dates. Collectors on forums such as Steve Hoffman generally regard the original 6-eye mono pressing as the most authentic and best-sounding version, since no audiophile reissue (Mobile Fidelity, Analogue Productions, Classic Records, etc.) of this specific title exists. The only later variant is the 1994 CD box on Sony/Legacy, which offers cleaner surfaces but a different, remastered sound signature rather than a vinyl-cutting improvement over this LP.

Notes
