The Album

Round About Midnight was Miles Davis's debut for Columbia Records, cut at Columbia's New York studios in sessions spanning October 1955 and June and September 1956 while Davis was still technically finishing out his Prestige contract. It introduced his first great quintet - John Coltrane, Red Garland, Paul Chambers and Philly Joe Jones - a working band whose telepathic interplay reshaped small-group jazz and set Coltrane on the path to his own landmark career. Anchored by a moody reworking of Thelonious Monk's title tune, the program mixes standards ('All of You,' 'Bye Bye Blackbird') with bebop repertoire (Charlie Parker's 'Ah-Leu-Cha,' Tadd Dameron's 'Tadd's Delight'), spotlighting Davis's newly matured, harmon-muted lyricism against Coltrane's harder-edged tenor attack. The record marked Davis's leap from Prestige's hard-bop scene to a major label's resources and promotional reach, opening the decade of landmark Columbia albums that followed. It is widely regarded as a cornerstone of hard bop and one of the defining quintet statements of the mid-1950s, later inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. Its blend of restraint, harmonic sophistication and controlled intensity has kept it a fixture on best-jazz-album lists and an essential document of Davis's transformation from bebop sideman to major bandleader.

The Label

This copy carries Columbia's deep-groove 6-eye mono label (red field, black perimeter wedges each containing a small circled 'eye' logo, six in total, 'NONBREAKABLE' text, and the 'Lp' trademark ring at bottom) - the design used for CL 949 from its March 1957 release through the early 1960s before the deep groove and 6-eye rim graphic were retired in favor of the 2-eye 'Guaranteed High Fidelity' label circa 1962-63. Discogs documents several sub-variants of this 6-eye deep-groove issue distinguished mainly by stamper suffix (1A through 1K observed on side A) rather than by a differently laid-out label; this copy's stamped runout (XLP39109-1A / XLP39110-1A) matches the lowest, earliest-documented stamper pair for the catalog number. That places this disc at or very near the front of the six-eye deep-groove pressing run for CL 949, ahead of copies stamped 1C, 1G, 1H or 1K. The specific pressing plant (Hollywood, Bridgeport/NY, or Terre Haute) cannot be confirmed with certainty from the transcription alone; a clear dead-wax photo of the full plant symbol next to the 'H' mark would settle it.

Fidelity

This is an original 1957 Columbia mono cut sourced from Frank Laico's session tapes, not a later remaster; six-eye deep-groove Columbia pressings of this era are generally well-regarded on forums.stevehoffman.tv for their warm, direct mono presentation, with early low-number stampers like this 1A/1A pair theoretically representing the freshest metal parts, though most listeners find plant-to-plant and stamper-generation differences subtle. Within the album's overall fidelity hierarchy this early six-eye deep-groove mono sits at or near the top, ranked above the later 2-eye 'Guaranteed High Fidelity' mono, the 1970s orange-label reissue, and the stereo/rechanneled CS 8649 pressings. It is generally considered comparable to, and by some vintage-mono purists preferred over, the 2013 Sony Legacy/Kevin Gray-mastered 180g mono reissue and the Mobile Fidelity SACD, which are praised for added clarity but not universally judged to surpass the original six-eye pressing's tonal character.
