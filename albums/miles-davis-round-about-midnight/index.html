The Album

Round About Midnight was Miles Davis's debut for Columbia Records, recorded while he was still technically under contract to Prestige, with sessions in October 1955 and June and September 1956 at Columbia's New York studios. It introduced what became known as Davis's first great quintet - John Coltrane, Red Garland, Paul Chambers and Philly Joe Jones - a group whose interplay reshaped small-group jazz and launched Coltrane toward his own historic career. The album is built around Thelonious Monk's title tune plus a mix of standards ("All of You," "Bye Bye Blackbird") and bebop repertoire (Charlie Parker's "Ah-Leu-Cha," Tadd Dameron's "Tadd's Delight"), showcasing Davis's newly matured, muted-trumpet lyricism alongside Coltrane's harder-edged tenor. It marked the moment Davis moved from Prestige's small-label hard bop scene to the resources and reach of a major label, setting the stage for the decade of landmark Columbia albums that followed. Critically the record is regarded as a cornerstone of hard bop and one of the definitive statements of the mid-1950s jazz quintet format, later inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame. Its blend of controlled intensity and modern harmonic sophistication has made it a perennial entry on best-jazz-album lists and a foundational listen for understanding Davis's evolution from bebop sideman to bandleader innovator.

The Label

This copy carries the Columbia deep-groove 6-eye label with 'ROUND ABOUT MIDNIGHT' and 'THE MILES DAVIS QUINTET' each printed on a single line, the classic 1956-58 Columbia mono design used before the deep groove and 6-eye logo were phased out in the early 1960s. The stamped runout reads XLP39109-1A / XLP39110-1A with an 'H'-type plant mark, consistent with an early-generation stamper pressed at one of Columbia's regional plants (Hollywood being the most likely match given the single-line label layout documented on Discogs for that pressing branch) rather than the later multi-line-title label variant tracked under other Discogs entries. The low stamper suffix ('1A' on both sides) indicates this disc was cut very early in the pressing run for CL 949, placing it at or near the front of the six-eye deep-groove pressing sequence rather than a mid-run or later reissue stamper. Exact plant attribution (Hollywood vs. Bridgeport/NY vs. Terre Haute) cannot be pinned down with total certainty from the transcription alone; a dead-wax photo showing the full plant symbol would settle it definitively.

Fidelity

This is an original 1957 Columbia mono cut, mastered from the analog session tapes engineered by Frank Laico rather than any later remaster; deep-groove 6-eye Columbia pressings of this era are generally well-regarded by collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv for their warm, direct mono presentation, though quality can vary somewhat by pressing plant and stamper generation. Within the album's overall fidelity hierarchy this six-eye deep-groove mono sits near the top alongside other early-stamper six-eye copies, ranked above later 1960s-70s reissues (Guaranteed High Fidelity 2-eye, orange-label Columbia, and stereo remixes/rechannelings) but generally considered comparable to or just behind the most sought-after modern audiophile mono remasters, such as the 2013 Sony Legacy/Kevin Gray-mastered 180g mono reissue, which many forum listeners praise for added clarity while still crediting the original six-eye pressings with superior vintage tonal character.
