The Album

Recorded in a single marathon session in June 1964, this album marked Bob Dylan's deliberate turn away from the topical protest songs that had made his name toward more personal, surreal and romantically complicated writing. Coming on the heels of his emergence as the voice of the folk-protest movement, the record surprised and unsettled parts of that audience, who heard its shift toward interior, often ironic songwriting as a retreat from political commitment. Songs move between tender character studies, bitter farewells and stream-of-consciousness wordplay, reflecting Dylan's growing interest in symbolist and Beat-influenced poetry as much as in folk tradition. It stands as the last of his all-acoustic studio albums before he began incorporating electric instrumentation, making it a hinge point between his early folk persona and the rock-oriented work that followed. Critics at the time were divided, but the album has since been reassessed as a crucial transitional work that expanded the emotional and lyrical range of songwriting in popular music. Its songs, including compositions that were quickly covered by other major folk and rock acts, helped cement Dylan's reputation as a songwriter of unusual depth. The record remains a touchstone for understanding Dylan's rapid artistic evolution during the mid-1960s.

The Label

The labels show the common red Columbia two-eye mono design: an arched white COLUMBIA logotype at the top, a square-bordered 'eye' trademark at both the nine- and three-o'clock positions, NONBREAKABLE and the catalogue/matrix numbers flanking the spindle hole, and GUARANTEED HIGH FIDELITY / PRINTED IN U.S.A. around the bottom rim. This two-eye layout was Columbia's standard mono design from the early 1960s through the mono line's discontinuation around 1967-68, later replaced on many titles by a '360 Sound Mono' ring. The eye logos on these labels are flat rather than raised, so this is an ordinary two-eye mono pressing from within that production window rather than the scarce embossed-label issue of this catalogue number.

Fidelity

This is the original mono mix as cut for Columbia's domestic two-eye pressings of the mid-1960s, with no mastering engineer credited on the jacket or labels. Collectors generally favor the original mono mix of this album over the simulated or true stereo counterpart for its tighter, more immediate presentation of Dylan's voice and guitar. Within the mono pressing run, copies with the earliest stampers and the scarce embossed-label variant are regarded as the most desirable; this copy's -2D stamper family places it as a solid, if not the earliest, original-era pressing, ranking above later mono repressings and above modern reissues in authenticity if not necessarily in surface quality.

Notes

This is a mono, US-manufactured Columbia pressing of CL 2193 carrying the standard red two-eye label rather than the rare embossed-eye label variant tied to this catalogue number. The runout carries XLP-77042-2D and XLP-77043-2D stampers with additional suffix codes not listed on the matched catalogue entry, placing it within the broader 1964-1967 two-eye mono production run rather than pinning it to the earliest lacquer. Cover art, title treatment and the eleven-song sequence across two sides match the album's familiar first design.
