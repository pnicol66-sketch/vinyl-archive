The Album

"Love And Theft" was recorded quickly in New York with the touring band Dylan had been using on the road, a group that gave the songs a loose, live-in-the-room feel after the more heavily produced sound of his comeback record a few years earlier. Released on September 11, 2001, the album drew on old-timey blues, Tin Pan Alley pop, rockabilly and Southern gothic imagery, with lyrics that freely folded in lines and phrases from older American songs, minstrelsy and literary sources, a magpie approach that later drew scholarly attention and some controversy over unattributed borrowing. It arrived as the second installment in the late-career renaissance that began with his prior studio album and continued the run of acclaim that reframed Dylan, then sixty, as a vital rather than nostalgic artist. Critics responded with some of the best reviews of his career, and the record is widely regarded as a high point of his output from the 1990s onward. Its blend of humor, menace and deep roots-music scholarship has made it a touchstone for the Americana genre that flourished in the following decade. The album's title, taken from a work of cultural history, signaled Dylan's open engagement with tradition as raw material rather than something to be merely preserved.

The Label

The labels are red with the Columbia "note and microphone" twin-circle device at the top, the Columbia wordmark repeated around the rim, a Sony Music Entertainment 2001 copyright line, SACEM/SDRM and BIEM rights-society marks, and a boxed "LC 00162" label code - a code that did not exist before 1977 and so cannot date this copy any earlier than that. The jacket carries a UPC barcode, a mark not used before 1980, which likewise forbids dating the copy to any year before 1980. Both marks are consistent with, and do not contradict, the plainly 2001 Columbia red-label design printed here; this is the standard European release-era label for the title, not a later reissue redesign. The catalogue number is printed as "COL 504364 1" on the labels and as "Columbia 504364 1" on the front cover, a minor inconsistency common to this release rather than evidence of a different pressing.

Fidelity

The album was mastered at a well-regarded mastering facility for its original vinyl release and pressed at a respected European plant; collectors generally consider the original-era vinyl pressings, including this European edition, to sound full and warm compared with the digital-era CD master, which some find more compressed. Among vinyl versions, later audiophile 180-gram reissues are prized by some collectors for quieter surfaces, but the original-era pressings like this one remain well regarded for their tonal balance and are not considered inferior to the newer cuts.

Notes

This is the European 2xLP release on Columbia COL 504364 1, stereo, with the red Columbia label bearing the note-and-microphone logo, LC 00162 code and SACEM/BIEM marks appropriate to a European pressing of this title. The runouts read 5043641000 1A1/1B1/1C1/1D1, matching the release's etched matrix codes side for side. A back-cover "Made In: Germany" sticker identifies the manufacturing territory for this copy. Nothing on the copy suggests a later reissue redesign; it sits with the original 2001 European release of the title.
