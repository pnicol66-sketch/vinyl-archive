The Album

Decoy, released in June 1984, captures Miles Davis deep into his 1980s electric comeback following his return from retirement with The Man with the Horn and We Want Miles. The album blends dense, moody electro-funk grooves largely shaped with young keyboardist Robert Irving III alongside more open, guitar-driven material co-written with John Scofield, who was becoming an increasingly important foil in Davis's working band. Two tracks were captured live at the 1983 Montreal jazz festival, giving the record a mix of tightly produced studio funk and looser stage improvisation, while 'That's Right' features an arrangement by longtime collaborator Gil Evans, tying the record back to one of Davis's most significant artistic partnerships. Branford Marsalis and Bill Evans trade soprano saxophone duties on the album, underlining Davis's continued interest in showcasing new talent even as he leaned harder into synthesizers and programmed drums. Sitting between the more polished Star People and the pop-facing You're Under Arrest, Decoy is often viewed by critics as a transitional, moodier entry in Davis's 1980s catalog, and it earned a Grammy nomination for Best Jazz Fusion Performance. Its cool, brooding atmosphere and rhythm-forward writing helped cement the template Davis continued exploring through the rest of the decade, making it a key document of his evolution from acoustic hard bop pioneer to electric innovator.

The Label

This copy carries the standard 1984 red Columbia 'walking eye' label with catalog number FC 38991 and AL/BL-38991 matrix prefixes typical of Columbia's early-1980s in-house pressing scheme, under which titles were simultaneously stamped from Masterdisk lacquers at multiple CBS-owned plants (Pitman NJ, Terre Haute IN, Carrollton GA, Santa Maria CA) with differing stamper suffix letters. The -1B suffix on both sides places this among the earlier stamper generations cut shortly after the album's June 1984 release, though the multi-plant simultaneous release model makes precise plant identification from the matrix alone unreliable without a visible etched plant symbol. Columbia's 'walking eye' red label design was standard for the label throughout the late 1970s into the mid-1980s, so the label art itself only confirms the general era, not a specific pressing run. There is no meaningful first-pressing premium structure for this title as with rarer jazz LPs - all early-80s domestic Columbia pressings of Decoy trade in a narrow, low price band regardless of exact stamper letter.

Fidelity

Mastered by Bob Ludwig at Masterdisk from the original 1984 mix; this era of Columbia pressings is generally well regarded for a clean, punchy cut of Davis's synth-heavy 1980s productions, though the studio mix itself (dense synths, programmed drums) limits ultimate sonic transparency compared to his acoustic-era Columbia LPs. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally treat the various early-80s domestic Columbia stampers (-1A through -1E) as sonically similar, with no consensus 'best' stamper commanding a premium. There is no audiophile reissue series (Analogue Productions, Mobile Fidelity, etc.) for Decoy, so this original Masterdisk-cut Columbia pressing remains the best commercially available vinyl fidelity reference for the album, ahead of later Sony/Legacy CD remasters for tonal warmth if not ultimate noise floor.
