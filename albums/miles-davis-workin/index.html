The Album

Workin' with the Miles Davis Quintet was drawn from two marathon sessions - May 11 and October 26, 1956 - recorded to fulfill Davis's remaining contractual obligation to Prestige before his move to Columbia Records, sessions prolific enough to yield the four "Marathon" LPs Cookin', Relaxin', Steamin', and Workin'. The quintet of Davis, John Coltrane, Red Garland, Paul Chambers, and "Philly" Joe Jones is widely regarded as one of the most influential small groups in jazz history, its telepathic interplay helping to define the hard bop idiom and launching Coltrane toward his own singular path. The Label

This copy carries the classic yellow-and-black "fireworks" Prestige label with the "203 South Washington Ave., Bergenfield, N.J." address and pronounced deep groove on both sides, the label style used by Prestige from roughly 1957 into the early 1960s before the switch to the dark blue "trident" design in 1964. The album was first issued in late 1959/1960 despite the sessions dating to 1956, and this Bergenfield yellow-label pressing represents the true first-issue label configuration for PRLP 7166. RVG is stamped into the deadwax on both sides, consistent with Van Gelder-cut lacquers pressed during this Bergenfield era. This places the copy squarely as an early/first-pressing example, ahead of the later blue trident, Fantasy/OJC, and modern audiophile reissues.

Fidelity

Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder directly from his own Hackensack session tapes, this Bergenfield-era yellow label pressing is regarded by collectors (Steve Hoffman forums, jazz-collector circles) as sonically superior to later Fantasy/OJC reissues, delivering natural, well-balanced mono sound. It ranks below only the very earliest stamper generations within the Bergenfield run (differences there are subtle), and above the 1964-71 blue trident repress, the 1980s-90s OJC reissues, and most CD remasters; the 2000s Analogue Productions mono/stereo audiophile reissue is a respected modern alternative, but original Van Gelder-cut Bergenfield copies remain the sonic benchmark.

Notes

This is the original mono first pressing of PRLP 7166 on the yellow-and-black "fireworks" Prestige label with the Bergenfield, NJ address and deep groove on both sides, the design in use when the album was first released circa 1959/1960. RVG stamped in the deadwax on both sides confirms Van Gelder mastering, consistent with an early Bergenfield-era pressing rather than the later blue trident label (1964-71) or subsequent Fantasy/OJC reissues. The collector's transcription shows "PRLP-7166-A RVG" on side A and "PRLP-7166-B RVG VBW" on side B, both typical deadwax conventions for this pressing era.
