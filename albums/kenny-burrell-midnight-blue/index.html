The Album

Recorded in a single session at Van Gelder Studio on January 8, 1963, Midnight Blue found guitarist Kenny Burrell paring his working group down to a spare, blues-drenched quintet, deliberately omitting piano so his own chording could carry the harmony. Burrell had already built a reputation as one of the most versatile guitarists in jazz, equally comfortable in bebop, soul-jazz and blues settings, and this date leaned hard into the blues and Latin-tinged grooves that were then reshaping Blue Note's commercial sound. Tenor saxophonist Stanley Turrentine's warm, vocalized tone became the record's second voice, while Ray Barretto's conga added a subtle Afro-Cuban lift that distinguished tracks like Chittlins Con Carne and Wavy Gravy from straight 12-bar blues. The album's unhurried, late-night mood and its emphasis on feel over flash made it an immediate favorite with listeners beyond the jazz-specialist audience, and it remains Burrell's most enduring and best-selling Blue Note session. Critics have long cited it as a high-water mark of early-1960s soul jazz, prized for its economy, groove and the chemistry between Burrell and Turrentine. It cemented Burrell's standing as a leader in his own right after years as an in-demand sideman, and its title track and Chittlins Con Carne have become minor blues-guitar standards. The record's blend of blues authenticity and jazz sophistication continues to be cited as a touchstone of the genre.

The Label

The side labels photographed here show the classic white/blue Blue Note design with a single "BLUE NOTE" wordmark split vertically, "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC." arcing above "NEW YORK, USA" beneath the 33 1/3 MICROGROOVE LONG PLAYING legend - the same New York-address design used on the true 1963 first pressing. The collector confirms, however, that this copy does NOT show deep groove on either side, which rules out the earliest (1963) pressing run and instead matches the version Discogs catalogues as a 1966 reissue that kept the same New York labels and the Van Gelder/Plastylite-ear stamps in the dead wax but was pressed after Plastylite's deep-groove stamper rings had been retired (deep groove disappeared industry-wide, and at Blue Note specifically, by the mid-1960s). Because the label wording and address here still read "NEW YORK, USA" with no "A DIVISION OF LIBERTY RECORDS" text, this predates the post-1966 Liberty-era relabeling, placing it as a transitional, non-deep-groove NY pressing - the second rung in the BLP 4123 pressing hierarchy, below the deep-groove original but above the later United Artists/Liberty and 1970s reissues.

Fidelity

The Van Gelder-cut lacquers and Plastylite pressing are unchanged from the original configuration, so this non-deep-groove NY pressing sounds essentially identical to the 1963 first press, differing only in the cosmetic loss of the deep-groove ring from the stamper/press setup rather than any change in mastering. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally rate all Van Gelder/ear-stamped mono Blue Notes of this era as sonically superior to the later Liberty/United Artists reissues and to the stereo remix, prizing the punch and warmth of the mono cut. Within the fidelity hierarchy for BLP 4123, this pressing ranks just behind the deep-groove 1963 original (which commands a premium mainly for collectability, not audible superiority) and above the post-1966 Liberty-era and 1970s United Artists reissues, and well above the 1985 digital remaster; modern audiophile cuts (Music Matters, Analogue Productions) use fresh transfers and outclass all vintage mono pressings sonically but trade the vintage-mono character for higher fidelity and are generally stereo, not mono.

Notes

This is not the true 1963 first pressing: the collector confirms no deep groove on either side, ruling out the earliest deep-groove NY USA stamper run. The label design is otherwise identical to the original - full "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC. NEW YORK, USA" address, no Liberty-era text - and the runouts still carry the Van Gelder hand-etch and Plastylite ear, matching Discogs's cataloguing of a c.1966 non-deep-groove reissue on the same New York labels (before the label wording changed under Liberty ownership). That places this copy as a second-pressing variant: later than the 1963 deep-groove original but earlier than the Liberty/United Artists label changes that followed. Released May 1963; this pressing dates to the mid-1960s reprint run.
