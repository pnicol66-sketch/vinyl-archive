The Album

Both Feet On The Ground was cut in February 1973 at Village Recorders in Los Angeles during Kenny Burrell's Fantasy Records period, after he had left the Verve/CTI orbit and settled on the West Coast label for a run of early-1970s sessions that leaned into contemporary soul-jazz textures. Benny Golson supplied most of the arrangements, dressing Burrell's cool, blues-rooted guitar in flute, piccolo and electric-piano colors that reflect the era's crossover instincts without abandoning a jazz core. The program mixes originals like the title track and 'Listen to the Dawn' with covers of Billie Holiday's 'Good Morning, Heartache' and the bossa-tinged 'Nana' and 'All Mine (Minha)', and brings in Brazilian percussionist Moacir Santos as a guest colorist. By this point Burrell was a two-decades-plus veteran whose guitar sound had already defined a generation of hard-bop and soul-jazz sessions for Blue Note and Prestige, and this album shows him adapting that voice to the fusion-leaning jazz marketplace of the early '70s rather than chasing rock guitar trends outright. It was received as a solid, if not landmark, entry in his catalog - valued more by collectors of his Fantasy-era work and by soul-jazz/rare-groove diggers than by mainstream jazz critics - and it remains a minor but distinctive chapter bridging his hard-bop legacy and the smoother fusion direction jazz guitar took later in the decade.

The Label

The label is the tan/gold Fantasy 'FJ' swirl-logo design with a salmon-pink cross-band, used on Fantasy's Berkeley-era jazz catalogue through the mid-1970s; this is the same base design used on the standard stock stereo pressing of F 9427, but here it is over-stamped 'F 9427-DJ' with 'DJ Copy - Not For Sale' printed beneath the catalogue number, marking it as a radio/promotional service copy rather than the commercial stock pressing. Fantasy issued this design continuously from the label's 1971-72 Berkeley move (after the earlier 'stereo' rainbow-swirl design) through the mid-1970s, so the artwork alone does not separate promo from stock pressings made in the same 1973 window - only the printed DJ text and the runout suffixes do. This copy's DJ overprint places it in the small promotional-pressing run issued alongside the regular 1973 stereo release, made from the same 1973 lacquers rather than a later re-pressing.

Fidelity

Recorded by Baker Bigsby at Village Recorders and remixed by Jim Stern at Fantasy Studios, Berkeley, this is a well-produced but not audiophile-grade early-70s jazz-funk date; Fantasy's Berkeley/RCA-adjacent pressing plants of the period are generally solid but unremarkable sonically. There is no dedicated audiophile reissue (no Mobile Fidelity, Analogue Productions, Speakers Corner, or Blue Note-style Tone Poet edition) of this title, and it receives little discussion among collectors on the forums beyond passing mentions in Burrell discography threads. As an original-era US pressing (DJ promo, same 1973 lacquers/mastering as the stock release aside from an earlier side-A stamper), this copy sits at the top of the title's practical fidelity hierarchy alongside the standard 1973 US stereo pressing, well above the mid-70s reissue and the Japanese LFP-88128 pressing.

Notes

This is a 1973 Fantasy F 9427 DJ/promotional pressing, not the ordinary stock copy: the label is over-stamped 'F 9427-DJ' with 'DJ Copy - Not For Sale' beneath the catalogue number. The standard stock stereo pressing carries a side-A runout of F-9427-ARE1, while this copy's side-A runout reads F9427-A-1 - an earlier-suffix lacquer that points to Fantasy's separate US promo variant. Side B (F9427-B-RE1) matches the RE1 remix stamper on both the promo and standard release, so the same 1973 remix lacquer was used for side B across variants while side A on this copy carries an earlier cut.
