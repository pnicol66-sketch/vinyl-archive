The Album

Bob Dylan launched the Rolling Thunder Revue in the autumn of 1975 as a travelling carnival of folk, rock and vaudeville, assembling old friends and new collaborators - Rob Stoner, Howie Wyeth, Scarlet Rivera, Mick Ronson, T Bone Burnett, Steven Soles, David Mansfield and Bob Neuwirth among them - into a loose, theatrical touring company. The second leg, in the spring of 1976, found the show reshaped into bigger arenas with a harder, more amplified sound and a setlist that leaned into both the ‘Blood on the Tracks’ and ‘Desire’ material and reworked older songs with new arrangements. The May 16, 1976 performance at the Tarrant County Convention Center Arena in Fort Worth, Texas captured the band at a ferocious peak, with Dylan tearing through reinvented versions of his older folk staples alongside the newer, harder-edged material. The Rolling Thunder tours sit at a pivotal point in Dylan's career, following the raw confessional writing of the mid-1970s and preceding his turn toward gospel at the decade's close, and they are remembered as some of the most theatrical and unpredictable live performances of his career. The Revue's blend of masked and painted performers, surprise guests, and a sprawling, shifting cast of musicians made it a landmark in rock touring history, widely cited as an influence on later large-scale, loosely scripted rock tours. Fragments of the tour reached the public for decades only through the 1976 ‘Hard Rain’ album, bootlegs, and later archival releases, giving performances like this one an outsized mythology among Dylan's fans. Critics and historians have long regarded the second-leg shows as some of the most ferocious and emotionally direct of Dylan's career, with this performance standing as a prime document of that intensity.

The Label

Each side carries a one-off custom label made only for this release - Western-lettered “COLUMBIA RECORDS” around the rim, six small record-shaped devices on radiating rays, a central star in a circle, and no Columbia “eye” logo anywhere on the label. The label is a flat label; no deep groove ring sits around the spindle hole on any side. The copyright lines on sides A-D and F read ℗/© 1976, 2026 & © 2026 Columbia Records, while side E alone carries a 2024 copyright, an inconsistency the label itself prints. The 2026 date on the labels means this pressing cannot have been made before that year, placing it as part of the first vinyl run of this specific show.

Fidelity

The lacquers were cut by Ryan K. Smith at Sterling Sound from a new mix of the original multitrack concert tapes, with pressing at Memphis Record Pressing. Collectors generally rate Sterling/MRP cuts of recent Dylan archival live titles as clean and dynamic, if not quite as revelatory as the best audiophile reissue houses' work on his studio catalog. As the only vinyl edition of this specific show, this pressing has no competing reissue to rank against; it stands as the reference cut for the title.

Notes

This is the first and only vinyl release of the complete Fort Worth, TX show from May 16, 1976, pressed in 2026. The labels carry a ℗/© 2026 date (with an apparent 2024 misprint on side E) that rules out any earlier manufacture, and the runouts carry Sterling Sound mastering codes (RKS = Ryan K. Smith) together with a Memphis Record Pressing stamp. No deep groove is present on any side, and the custom label artwork was created specifically for this title rather than drawn from a standing Columbia family design.
