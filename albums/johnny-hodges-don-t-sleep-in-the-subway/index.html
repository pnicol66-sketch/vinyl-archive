The Album

Don't Sleep In The Subway was recorded across three August 1967 sessions in New York for Verve, pairing the Ellington orchestra's peerless alto voice with a large studio big band arranged and conducted by pianist Jimmy Jones. Producer Esmond Edwards built the set around a mix of a then-current pop hit (the Tony Hatch/Jackie Trent title tune, a Petula Clark smash) and standards alongside several Hodges originals, a commercial strategy typical of Verve's late-1960s jazz-with-strings-and-brass releases aimed at a broader listening audience. Hodges, already one of the most celebrated alto stylists in jazz through his decades with Duke Ellington and his own small-group Verve dates of the 1950s-60s, brings his instantly identifiable, vocal-like tone and gliding legato phrasing to material well outside the hard-bop mainstream. The supporting cast - Snooky Young, Frank Wess, Jimmy Hamilton, Hank Jones, Milt Hinton and Grady Tate among them - reflects the top-tier New York studio talent Verve could call on for these sessions. Critical reception was mixed-to-positive: Allmusic's retrospective view judged the orchestrations uneven while singling out Hodges himself as consistently sharp, a fair summary of how the album is remembered - a minor, late-period showcase valued chiefly for Hodges's own playing rather than for the material or arrangements.

The Label

The label seen here is the grey "Big V" Verve Records design (large stylized V-shaped watermark logo with "Verve Records" script above), MGM Records rim text ("MGM Records - A Division of Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, Inc. - Made in U.S.A."), and catalogue number V6-8726 with the MGS-1200/1201 internal matrix reference printed on the label itself. This grey Big-V design with MGM ownership text is Verve's standard 1963-67 house style under MGM Records' stewardship (bsnpubs), placing this pressing squarely in the album's first US stereo release window (1967). A US mono edition (V-8726) and a promo yellow-label DJ pressing were issued from the same session alongside this stereo commercial copy; no different label artwork is claimed for any of them since only this stereo grey label was photographed.

Fidelity

This is an analog-era Verve/MGM stereo cut from the 1967 National Recording Studios sessions, mastered and pressed under MGM Records' in-house operation rather than by an audiophile specialist; there is no known audiophile reissue (Analogue Productions, Speakers Corner, etc.) of this title to rank it against. Collector commentary on this relatively minor, late-1960s big-band Hodges date is sparse on forums like Steve Hoffman's, with little dedicated discussion of mastering quality; general consensus on late-60s MGM/Verve pressings is that they are solidly but not exceptionally engineered compared to the label's earlier Norman Granz-era jazz dates. Within the album's own pressing history, this original US stereo edition is the primary sonic reference point, ahead of later international pressings (UK, Canada, Germany, Spain) which derive from the same or dubbed masters with variable plant quality.

Notes

This is the original 1967 US stereo pressing on Verve V6-8726, grey "Big V" MGM-era label, gatefold jacket. Matrix/runout (V6-8726 S-1/S-2, MGS-1200/1201, REV, F.G.) matches Discogs release 3025099 for this catalogue number and format, confirming it as the standard US stereo commercial issue rather than the mono (V-8726), promo, or any of the UK/Canada/Germany/Spain stereo variants. No cover or label distinguishing marks suggest anything other than a routine first-run US stereo copy.
