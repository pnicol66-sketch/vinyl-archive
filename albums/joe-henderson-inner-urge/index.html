The Album

Recorded in November 1964 at Rudy Van Gelder's studio, Inner Urge is Joe Henderson's second album as a leader for Blue Note and one of the boldest statements of his early career. The title track is a long, harmonically open blues that moves through a sixteen-bar sequence stepwise rather than on conventional changes, pushing the quartet into exploratory, modal territory, while “El Barrio” draws on the Spanish-speaking Puerto Rican neighborhood Henderson knew in his time studying in the area and “Isotope” reworks a twelve-bar blues with a tribute to Thelonious Monk's angularity. Henderson is joined by McCoy Tyner and Elvin Jones, both on loan from John Coltrane's working quartet, whose harmonic freedom and rhythmic drive give the session its restless, searching character, with Bob Cranshaw anchoring the group throughout; the album has endured as one of the defining hard-bop-into-modal-jazz statements of Henderson's early Blue Note period and a touchstone for the direction tenor playing took in the mid-1960s.

The Label

Both sides show a white-ground label with a blue band, BLUE up the left edge and NOTE across the foot, a single ® beneath NOTE, and rim text reading “BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC • NEW YORK USA” with no street address — the design the Classic Vinyl Series uses to echo the original 1960s New York label. The rim also carries an LC00383 label code, a mark that did not exist before 1976, and the small print gives a ℗/© year of 2022, so the copy cannot have been pressed before that year regardless of how closely the artwork imitates the 1960s design. Matrix data name Cohearent Audio as the cutting room and Optimal Media as the pressing plant, both modern-era credits. Taken together, this places the copy firmly as the 2022 worldwide Blue Note reissue, below every US and Japanese pressing made between the 1960s and the 2010s Music Matters series.

Fidelity

This pressing is cut by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio from the original analog master tapes and pressed on 180-gram vinyl at Optimal Media in Germany. Collectors on the forums generally rate Optimal's vinyl formula as very quiet and note that Kevin Gray's cuts for the Classic Vinyl Series are well regarded sonically, but they also report inconsistent quality control at the plant, with some copies suffering from warps, non-fill, or surface noise. In the fidelity hierarchy for this title, this reissue sits below the Van Gelder-cut original stereo and mono pressings and below the Music Matters 45 RPM audiophile edition, but it is valued as a faithful, modern all-analog cut at a fraction of the originals' prices.

Notes

The labels reproduce the 1960s Blue Note New York USA design but carry an LC00383 code and a ℗/© 2022 date; the label code did not exist before 1976 and the printed date is 2022, so the pressing dates no earlier than that year. The runout names Cohearent Audio (Kevin Gray) as the cutting room and Optimal Media as the pressing plant. No deep groove, RVG stamp or Plastylite ear appears on the labels. The copy is the newest pressing of the title, below the original mono and stereo Blue Note pressings and below the Music Matters audiophile series.
