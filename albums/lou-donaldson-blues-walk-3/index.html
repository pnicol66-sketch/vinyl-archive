The Album

Blues Walk was cut in a single session at a New Jersey studio in the summer of 1958, at a moment when Lou Donaldson - an alto saxophonist who had come up in the bebop idiom of Charlie Parker - was steering his music toward a bluesier, more danceable groove that would become a hallmark of hard bop's soul-jazz wing. The addition of Ray Barretto's conga alongside a standard piano-bass-drums rhythm section gave the date a Latin-tinged backbeat that was unusual for a Blue Note date of this period and became one of the record's signature textures. The title track, a loping blues built on a simple riff, became one of Donaldson's most enduring compositions and a staple of jukeboxes and jazz jams for decades afterward. Flanked by a brisk reading of Denzil Best's “Move” and warm ballad turns on “The Masquerade Is Over” and “Autumn Nocturne”, the album balances uptempo swagger with lyrical restraint, showcasing Donaldson's full-toned, blues-drenched alto sound. The record arrived in the midst of Donaldson's productive late-1950s run for Blue Note and stands as one of the clearest statements of the soul-jazz direction that would influence a generation of organ-combo and funky hard-bop recordings to follow. It has remained a touchstone of the genre, frequently cited among the most accessible and best-loved entries in the Blue Note catalog, prized for its blend of blues feeling, conga-driven rhythm, and unpretentious swing.

The Label

The label reproduces the original Blue Note design exactly as it first appeared: a white centre with a blue outer band, BLUE NOTE lettered in white running up the left edge and across the bottom, and the rim text BLUE NOTE RECORDS · 47 WEST 63rd · NYC with no “INC” and no ® mark under NOTE. That particular wording was the label's own text window from the late 1950s into the early 1960s, but the jacket carries a plain white sleeve with only a catalogue sticker reading “DBLP 012 1593”, and the runout is etched with a Kevin Gray/Cohearent mastering code rather than the Van Gelder/Plastylite marks that address window is known for. Taken together, the label on this copy is a facsimile reproduction used on a 2012 Japanese mono reissue series, not a product of the original pressing run the address text would otherwise imply.

Fidelity

The runout carries the mastering initials of Kevin Gray, cut at his Cohearent Audio facility (successor to AcousTech), working from the original session tapes for this Japanese mono reissue series. Collectors generally regard Kevin Gray's cuts as clean, accurate transfers, though on this title the original 1958 Van Gelder-cut deep-groove pressing and the later audiophile 45rpm Music Matters and Analogue Productions reissues are rated above any standard-weight Japanese reissue for ultimate transparency and dynamics. This pressing sits in the middle of the fidelity hierarchy for the title: a faithful, well-regarded cut, but not the top tier collectors chase for the most revealing playback.

Notes

This copy carries the classic 47 West 63rd, no-INC label design, deep groove on both sides, but its runout is etched with a Cohearent-era Kevin Gray mastering code and a DBLP-012 series number rather than the Van Gelder stamp and Plastylite ear of the 1958 original pressing. The jacket is a plain white sleeve bearing only a catalogue sticker, not the printed pictorial cover of the original issue. Together these mark it as a 2012 Japanese mono reissue reproducing the vintage label design on later pressing stock.
