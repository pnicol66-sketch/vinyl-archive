The Album

Kai Winding and J.J. Johnson had already proven the commercial and musical appeal of a two-trombone frontline on their earlier Savoy and Prestige dates, and this Bethlehem session cemented the Jay and Kai partnership as one of the era's most distinctive small-group jazz identities. Recorded over two January 1955 sessions in New York, the album pairs the two trombonists' contrasting tones - Johnson's crisp, bop-schooled precision against Winding's warmer, more legato attack - across a set of standards and originals contributed by both leaders. The rhythm section is unusually deep for a working group, drawing on bassists Milt Hinton and Wendell Marshall alongside pianist Dick Katz and drummer Al Harewood, giving the frontline a supple, swinging cushion. The program mixes show tunes (Rodgers and Hart, Cole Porter, Noel Coward) with hard-swinging originals like Johnson's

The Label

The label on this copy is the plain red Bethlehem design - an arced "BETHLEHEM" logotype over a treble-clef-and-laurel mark, the words "EAST COAST JAZZ SERIES #7 / K & J.J." beneath it, full personnel credits, catalogue number at left, side number at right, and the rim reading "BETHLEHEM MUSIC CORP. TRADE MARK REG." and "A HI-FIDELITY RECORDING / LONG PLAY / 33-1/3" - with a visible deep groove ring on both sides. This album was first issued in mid-1955 as catalogue BCP-13 and was pulled from that slot in late 1956 to be renumbered BCP-6001, the number that appears on this cover and on both labels; the runout catalogue prefix on this copy reads the renumbered form rather than the original BCP-13 stamp. Fidelity

The record was cut from the original 1955 New York session tapes for Bethlehem's in-house Hi-Fidelity series, with mastering handled at the label's own facilities of the period. Collectors generally rate the trombone frontline as vividly captured for a mid-1950s independent jazz label, with good separation between Winding and Johnson's horns, though surface and mastering quality can vary noticeably between pressing generations. Notes

This copy carries the BCP-6001 catalogue number on cover and labels, the number Bethlehem assigned to the album in late 1956 after pulling it from its original BCP-13 slot. The runout prefix on both sides reads the BCP-6001 form rather than BCP-13, and the underlying matrix suffixes match the original lacquers, indicating this is an early pressing made soon after the renumbering rather than a later, re-mastered reissue. Both sides show a deep groove, consistent with the mid-1950s Bethlehem pressing era before the label moved to flat-edge labels around the end of the decade.
