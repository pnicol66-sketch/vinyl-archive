The Album

Jay McShann was one of the last great links to Kansas City's swing-era heyday: a blues-drenched pianist and vocalist whose late-1930s orchestra gave the young Charlie Parker his first important bandstand and his recording debut. Recorded at Atlantic's New York studios on August 3 and 10, 1978 and released in 1979, The Big Apple Bash was conceived as an all-star celebration of McShann's music, mixing veterans of his own era - Basie alumni Earle Warren, Doc Cheatham and Dicky Wells, plus bassist Milt Hinton - with younger admirers like Gerry Mulligan, Herbie Mann (playing tenor, not his usual flute), Joe Morello, Connie Kay and a then up-and-coming John Scofield, all framed by Ernie Wilkins' big-band arrangements. The program revisits McShann classics: 'Dickie's Dream,' the Basie flag-waver he co-wrote with Lester Young, and 'Jumpin' The Blues,' recreating the tune he recorded with Parker, whose lyric 'I'll be jumpin' the blues until the day I die' serves as McShann's own defiant credo. Manhattan Transfer's Janis Siegel drops in for a playful vocal duet on 'Ain't Misbehavin',' and McShann's weathered, rock-solid voice and two-fisted piano carry the session throughout. Liner notes by New York Times critic Robert Palmer frame the album as a statement of the timelessness of McShann's music at a moment when Kansas City jazz was enjoying renewed attention. Critics received it warmly - AllMusic's Scott Yanow praises the spirited blowing-session atmosphere - and it stands as one of the strongest major-label showcases of McShann's late career, bridging classic Kansas City swing and the modern mainstream.

The Label

This copy carries Atlantic's late-1970s green-over-orange stereo label with the big multicolor box/fan logo and the 'MFG BY ATLANTIC RECORDING CORP., 75 ROCKEFELLER PLAZA, N.Y., N.Y. / A WARNER COMMUNICATIONS COMPANY' rim text, with a (c) 1979 copyright line - the standard original-issue design for Atlantic LPs of 1977-79, before the early-1980s label revisions that mark the 1982 reissue of this title. The label-printed matrix ST-A-794249-RI / ST-A-794250-RI ends in the RI plant code, which Atlantic used for the Philips/PRC pressing plant in Richmond, Indiana (other 1979 copies carry PR, MO or SP codes for Presswell, Monarch and Specialty). The Rockefeller Plaza address plus RI suffix therefore places this copy squarely in the first 1979 pressing run, at the top of the hierarchy alongside the other 1979 plant variants, and ahead of the 1982 reissue.

Fidelity

Recorded and mixed by Bobby Warner and mastered by George Piros at Atlantic Recording Studios, New York - a clean, well-engineered late-70s Atlantic analog production. The RI plant (Philips/PRC, Richmond) pressed to Atlantic's usual decent late-70s standard, a notch below the Monarch and Presswell variants some collectors prefer but still a quiet, full-bodied cut. There are no audiophile reissues to compete with: no Analogue Productions, Music Matters or Speakers Corner edition exists, so any clean 1979 US original such as this one sits at the top of the album's fidelity hierarchy, with the 1982 reissue a step behind.
