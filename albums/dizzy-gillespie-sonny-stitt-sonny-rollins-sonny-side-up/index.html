The Album

Sonny Side Up was recorded on December 19, 1957 at Nola Penthouse Sound Studios in New York, uniting trumpet giant Dizzy Gillespie with tenor saxophonists Sonny Rollins and Sonny Stitt for a single, high-stakes session under Norman Granz's Verve banner. The pairing was conceived as a study in contrasting modern jazz sensibilities: Stitt's driving, Parker-descended intensity set against Rollins's more exploratory, thematically-minded improvising. The album's centerpiece, Stitt's original "The Eternal Triangle," became an enduring jam-session vehicle and a signature cutting-contest tune for both saxophonists, trading increasingly heated choruses over a relentless tempo. Gillespie, already a titan of bebop's founding generation, anchors the session and even takes a rare vocal turn on the opener, while pianist Ray Bryant, bassist Tommy Bryant, and drummer Charlie Persip supply a tight, propulsive rhythm section. Released in January 1959, the record arrived as both Rollins and Stitt were consolidating reputations as premier tenor stylists, and the session has since become one of the most celebrated tenor-battle records in jazz history. It remains prized as a hard-bop touchstone for its head-to-head improvisational fireworks and its enduring influence on jazz musicians as a jam-session standard.

The Label

Both side labels show the gray Verve "trumpeter" design: a single trumpet-playing silhouette logo at left, "VERVE RECORDS" script logo at top, "CLEF SERIES" catalog line, "Under the personal supervision of Norman Granz" credit, and "MADE IN U.S.A." rim text, with deep groove construction visible on the pressing. This design was Verve's house label from the company's 1956 founding through roughly 1960-61, before Norman Granz sold Verve to MGM in 1961 and the trumpeter graphic was dropped for a plain black label. The runout matrix numbers stamped on this copy (MGV-8262-A 50564 / MGV-8262-B 50565) match Discogs' catalogued first-issue stampers exactly, placing this copy at the top of the pressing hierarchy as the true January 1959 first issue rather than a later MGM-era black-label repress.

Fidelity

No mastering engineer is credited on the jacket, typical of Verve's late-1950s Clef/Norgran-descended releases produced under Norman Granz's direct supervision; the original US pressing plant work is generally solid though period-typical surface noise can occur. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv regard these early deep-groove trumpeter-label Verves as full-bodied and dynamic compared to the flatter, more compressed later 1960s MGM-era black-label pressings and 1990s/2000s European CD-era reissues. A 2025 Verve Vault Audiophile Series 180g reissue, mastered by Ryan K. Smith from the analog tapes and pressed at Optimal, is now the best modern audiophile option, but this original trumpeter pressing remains the vintage reference cut for the title.
