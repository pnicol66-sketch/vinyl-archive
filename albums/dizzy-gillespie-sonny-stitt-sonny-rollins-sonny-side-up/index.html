The Album

Sonny Side Up was recorded on December 19, 1957 at Nola Penthouse Sound Studios in New York, uniting trumpet giant Dizzy Gillespie with tenor saxophonists Sonny Rollins and Sonny Stitt for a single, high-stakes session under Norman Granz's Verve banner. The pairing was a deliberate showcase of two very different Charlie Parker-descended tenor voices - Stitt's driving, virtuosic bebop attack contrasted against Rollins's more exploratory, thematically-minded improvising, an approach the liner notes (written by Nat Hentoff, drawing on Gunther Schuller's writing) frame as a study in contrasting modern jazz sensibilities. The album's centerpiece, Stitt's original "The Eternal Triangle," became an enduring jam-session vehicle and a signature cutting-contest tune for both saxophonists, with the two men trading increasingly heated choruses over a relentless tempo. Gillespie, already a titan of bebop's founding generation, anchors the session with his trumpet and even takes a rare vocal turn on the opener, while pianist Ray Bryant, bassist Tom Bryant, and drummer Charlie Persip provide a tight, propulsive rhythm section. Released in January 1959, the record arrived at a moment when both Rollins and Stitt were consolidating reputations as premier tenor stylists, and the session has since become one of the most celebrated tenor-battle records in jazz, prized for its head-to-head improvisational fireworks and enduring status as a hard-bop touchstone.

The Label

This copy carries the gray "trumpeter" Verve label with deep groove, "CLEF SERIES" text and "Under the personal supervision of Norman Granz" credit visible on both side labels, matching Verve's earliest house design used from the label's 1956 founding through roughly 1960-61, before Norman Granz sold Verve to MGM and the trumpeter logo was dropped for a plain black label. The matrix numbers stamped in the runout (MGV-8262-A 50564 / MGV-8262-B 50565) correspond exactly to the catalogued first-pressing variant for this title. Deep-groove construction on a gray trumpeter label is the hallmark of Verve's earliest Clef-era LP pressings, so this placement puts the copy at the very top of the Sonny Side Up pressing hierarchy - the true 1959 first issue, not a later MGM-era or reissue pressing.

Fidelity

No mastering engineer is credited on the jacket, typical for Verve's late-1950s Clef/Norgran-descended releases produced under Norman Granz's direct supervision; original pressing plants for Verve at this time were generally high quality, and collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv regard these early deep-groove trumpeter-label Verves as full-bodied and dynamic, if occasionally variable in surface quality given 1950s manufacturing. This trumpeter-label pressing sits at the top of the fidelity hierarchy for the title - later 1960s black-label MGM-era pressings and 1990s/2000s European CD-era reissues are generally considered flatter and more compressed by comparison. No audiophile-grade reissue (Analogue Productions, Mobile Fidelity, Speakers Corner) of this specific title is known to exist, leaving the original trumpeter pressing as the reference cut for collectors.
