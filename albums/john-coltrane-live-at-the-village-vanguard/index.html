The Album

Recorded over several nights at New York's Village Vanguard in early November 1961, this album captured Coltrane's working group expanding into freer, more exploratory territory just as his quartet with McCoy Tyner, Jimmy Garrison and Elvin Jones was solidifying, with Eric Dolphy and second bassist Reggie Workman guesting on select performances. The set arrived at a pivotal juncture in Coltrane's career, immediately after his celebrated Atlantic period and early into his long, transformative association with Impulse!, documenting his shift from the harmonic density of "Giant Steps"-era playing toward modal and increasingly abstract improvisation. "Chasin' The Trane," a nearly sixteen-minute blues performed as a stark tenor-bass-drums trio with no piano, became one of the most discussed and controversial tracks of his career, prized by some critics as a breakthrough in extended improvisation and derided by others - alongside Dolphy's contributions - as "anti-jazz." "Spiritual" showcased Coltrane's growing interest in modal and quasi-devotional material that would culminate a few years later in "A Love Supreme," while "Softly As In A Morning Sunrise" reworked a standard into a vehicle for the quartet's interplay. The record stands as a key document of Coltrane at a genuine crossroads, straddling hard bop and the fully free improvisation he would embrace later in the decade, and it remains essential to understanding both his own evolution and the broader turn of early-1960s jazz toward extended, harmonically open group improvisation. Its reception has grown steadily more favorable with time, now widely regarded as a landmark live jazz recording and among the most important albums of Coltrane's Impulse! years.

The Label

The label is the classic early Impulse! orange-body/black-rim design with the double "i!" bar logo repeated at the four compass points and the arced "impulse!" wordmark reading correctly at top and inverted at bottom - the design used on Impulse's A-series mono issues from 1961 through 1963. Rim text reads "A PRODUCT OF ABC-PARAMOUNT RECORDS, INC." / "PRINTED IN U.S.A.", the address style used before the mid-1960s ABC-Paramount-to-ABC Records corporate rename, consistent with an early-1960s pressing; this label design is not to be confused with the Astor GG-409 Australian pressing, a different title entirely.

Fidelity

Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder directly for Impulse!, with the RVG stamp present in the runout on both sides; original Impulse! mono RVG pressings of this title are highly regarded by collectors on the forums for their punchy, immediate sound, especially on the trio tracks. This pressing sits at or very near the top of the fidelity hierarchy for the title, alongside the stereo A-10-S RVG pressing, both outranking later Impulse/MCA reissues. Modern audiophile remasters, such as the 2022 Verve/UMe 180g LP, offer quieter surfaces and more modern equalization but are generally considered a different, more polished listening experience rather than a straightforward upgrade over the Van Gelder original.

Notes

1962 Impulse! A-10 mono - consistent with an original, early-issue pressing. The orange/black "double-bar" Impulse label with ABC-Paramount rim text matches the 1961-63 design window, and the runout carries the RVG (Rudy Van Gelder) stamp on both sides, the standard mark of an Impulse original mastering. This copy is placed as an early flat-label mono first issue rather than the earliest deep-groove variant, and is not to be confused with the unrelated 1961 Astor GG-409 Australian LP, a different title entirely.
