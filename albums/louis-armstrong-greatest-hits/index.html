This Pressing

1970s reissue (stereo, rechanneled) - Columbia red label with repeating yellow "COLUMBIA" rim text, no 360 Sound banner; post-1967 pressing.

The Album

Assembled by Columbia in 1967, this compilation draws together Louis Armstrong's most enduring recordings, spanning a nearly forty-year arc from the visionary Hot Five/Hot Seven sides of the late 1920s to his mid-1960s work with the All-Stars. Tracks like "West End Blues," "Basin Street Blues" and "Struttin' With Some Barbecue" represent the foundational vocabulary of jazz trumpet and scat singing that Armstrong essentially invented, while "Ain't Misbehavin'" and "Black and Blue" showcase his gift for turning Tin Pan Alley and Fats Waller songbook material into personal statements. "Mack the Knife" and "Cabaret," recorded later in his career, show Armstrong's continuing commercial vitality and his ability to bring his instantly recognizable growl and horn to contemporary theater songs. By the time of this release Armstrong was already a beloved elder statesman of American music, and the album served as an accessible entry point summarizing why he remains one of the most influential and important figures jazz has produced. Critically, compilations like this cemented his crossover appeal to pop audiences even as scholars increasingly recognized the historical importance of his earliest work. Armstrong's blend of virtuosic musicianship, warmth, and showmanship on these tracks continues to be viewed as a cornerstone of the American songbook and jazz canon.

The Label

The side labels show a red field with yellow "COLUMBIA" text repeating around the entire rim and a small square Columbia "note-in-box" logo at top and bottom - there is no "360 SOUND" arrow band, which had appeared on Columbia's red two-eye labels used on the album's original 1967 pressing. Per bsnpubs' Columbia label chronology, the red label with the label name repeating around the edge replaced the red two-eye/360 Sound design starting July 1970 and remained in use through most of the 1970s, before Columbia moved to the "sunburst" label design around 1981-84. That places this copy as a later Columbia US reissue of the 1967 original, not a first pressing, consistent with the stamped runout suffix (-1G) sitting in the same stamper family (-1C through -2D) that Discogs documents for this catalogue number's post-1970 US pressings.

Fidelity

This LP is explicitly "Electronically Re-Channeled for Stereo," a fake-stereo process applied to Armstrong's mono recordings for this compilation; it is not a true stereo mix and is generally regarded by collectors as inferior to the mono original for sound quality, with some forum commentary calling the rechanneled effect thin or artificial next to the mono masters. No audiophile reissue of this specific rechanneled configuration exists; the mono CL 2638 pressing is preferred by purists for fidelity. As a later-1970s Columbia pressing rather than an early 1967 stamper, this copy is also unlikely to represent the tightest, freshest cut of the rechanneled mix.

Notes

This is a Columbia US reissue pressing of the 1967 original CS 9438, not a first pressing. The red label with yellow "COLUMBIA" repeating around the rim (no "360 SOUND" band) matches the design Columbia adopted from July 1970 onward, replacing the red two-eye/360 Sound label used on the 1967 original. The stamped runout suffix (XSM 116758-1G / XSM 116759-1G) sits within the same family of post-1970 US stamper variants (-1C, -1F, -1H, -2C, -2D) documented for this catalogue number, further supporting a 1970s dating rather than the original 1967 issue.
