The Album

Recorded over three nights in March 1971 at Bill Graham's Fillmore West in San Francisco, this album caught Aretha Franklin stepping outside the soul and gospel circuit to play for a largely white rock audience, backed by King Curtis's tight band rather than her usual studio session crew. The set mixes her own signature hits with contemporary covers by Stephen Stills, Paul Simon, the Beatles and Bread, recast in gritty gospel-soul arrangements that showed her range beyond the Atlantic hit singles. The most famous moment came on the final night, when Ray Charles was coaxed from the audience to join her for an extended, unplanned duet on “Spirit in the Dark,” a performance that became the emotional centerpiece of the record. Arriving at the height of her early-1970s commercial and artistic peak, the album stands as one of the defining live documents of the soul era, prized for its loose, in-the-moment energy and for pairing two of the genre's defining voices on a single stage. It was warmly received on release and has remained a touchstone live album in assessments of Franklin's career and of early-1970s soul performance generally.

The Label

This copy carries a flat (non-deep-groove) two-tone Atlantic label with a green upper half and a red-orange lower half divided by a white band, the large A-and-fan logo in a pink box over a yellow ATLANTIC box above the spindle hole, a small circled R beside the logo, and the 1841 Broadway, New York address at the rim. That two-tone green/orange design replaced the earlier black labels in the late 1960s and remained in use into the early 1970s, placing this pressing inside Atlantic's early-1970s label era rather than the 1974-and-later orange/purple redesign. The runout on both sides ends in a plain “B” stamper suffix rather than the “RI” suffix carried by the Philips-pressed gatefold edition of this catalogue number, identifying this copy as the plain US stereo pressing of the title rather than that specific plant's run.

Fidelity

The lacquer was cut at Atlantic Studios with mastering engineer George Piros's “AT/GP” stamp on both sides, the standard Atlantic in-house cut used across the label's catalogue from 1969 into the mid-1980s. Collectors generally rate the original-era Atlantic pressings of this live recording as full-bodied and punchy, suited to its raw concert energy, while the 2014 Speakers Corner 180g reissue is regarded by audiophile reviewers as the cleanest, quietest cut of the album and ranks above ordinary original copies for surface noise and tonal balance. This copy, an early-1970s stereo pressing rather than that audiophile remaster, sits in the main run of vintage copies rather than at the top of the title's fidelity hierarchy.

Notes

A 1971 Atlantic SD 7205 stereo pressing on the flat green/orange two-tone label used in the early 1970s, with the 1841 Broadway, New York address and no deep groove. The runout reads AT-A-712205B / AT-A-712206B, AT/GP PR, placing mastering with the Atlantic Studios/George Piros “AT/GP” team used between 1969 and 1984. The “B” stamper suffix sets this copy apart from the “RI” suffix carried by the Philips-pressed gatefold edition of this number, making this the plain US stereo pressing of the title. The jacket is the gatefold with the Aretha/Ray Charles photo insert issued with this title's first run.
