This Pressing

1st press (stereo, 1968) - Atlantic green/red-orange label, 1841 Broadway rim text; (ST-A-681373CT) label matrix, 1C/1B stampers, Terre Haute pressing.

The Album

Aretha Franklin arrived in Europe in May 1968 at the absolute crest of her powers, eighteen months into the Atlantic run that had produced “Respect”, “Chain Of Fools”, “Baby, I Love You” and “(You Make Me Feel Like) A Natural Woman”, and only weeks after she had sung at the funeral of Martin Luther King Jr. The seven-week tour took in seven European capitals, and the Paris stop on 7 May 1968 at the Olympia - the hall that had crowned Piaf and Brel - was taped for this album, her first live record. The set is effectively a victory lap through the hits plus a Rolling Stones opener, a Rascals cover, Willie Nelson's “Night Life”, Ray Charles's “Come Back Baby” and King Curtis's “Soul Serenade”, carried by an orchestra under her conductor Donald Townes and the three-voice backing of her sister Carolyn with Wyline Ivey and Charnessa Jones. The French press met her as a conquering heroine, christening her the new queen of the blues, and the audience noise on the record is a large part of its character: the gospel call-and-response that drove her studio sides becomes a whole theatre answering her. Commercially it broke her streak of Top Five albums, stalling at number 13, and reviewers then and since have ranked it below the great Atlantic studio LPs for its brisk, hit-parade pacing. As a document, though, it is the only full-length record of Lady Soul on stage in her imperial year, three years before the far better-known Fillmore West date. Her reading of “Dr. Feelgood” and the closing “Respect” show how freely she rebuilt her own records in front of a crowd, and the album remains the first chapter in a live legacy that defines post-war soul singing.

The Label

Both sides carry the green upper half and red-orange lower half split by a white band, STEREO at the left of the band and the side number (ONE, TWO) at the right, with the boxed Atlantic logo at top centre - the large A in a red box beside the black fan swirl on pink, over ATLANTIC in a yellow box - and SD 8207 printed at the upper right. The rim line reads MFG. BY ATLANTIC RECORDING CORP., 1841 BROADWAY, NEW YORK, N.Y., and the label matrix sits in brackets at the foot: (ST-A-681373CT) on side one, (ST-A-681374CT) on side two. Atlantic replaced the black-fan label with this green and red-orange design in the summer of 1968, after the Warner Bros.-Seven Arts purchase of the company in October 1967, and kept it to 1973, the 1841 Broadway rim line running until the move to 75 Rockefeller Plaza in the mid-1970s. Since the album was issued in October 1968, this design is the title's first-issue label - the rule that a green and orange label on a low stereo number is a reissue applies to the 1500 jazz series, not to this 8000-series number. The CT suffix on the label matrix marks the Terre Haute plant that pressed this copy of the first run.

Fidelity

An all-analogue 1968 chain: a French location recording by Jean-Michel Poudubois, mastered at Atlantic and pressed at Terre Haute, with the early 1C and 1B lacquer cuts in the dead wax. The sound is forward and brassy with a big, close-miked vocal and heavy crowd presence - a live tape of its day rather than a hi-fi showcase - and Terre Haute vinyl of this period is quiet and consistent when clean. Collectors rate the 1968 Atlantic cut above the 1990s digitally remastered issues, which tidy the tape at the cost of its punch, and no audiophile edition of this title has ever been cut, so an original stereo copy like this is the best the record has sounded.

Notes

1968 Atlantic SD 8207 stereo - original US pressing. The green over red-orange label with the white centre band, the boxed Atlantic logo and the rim line MFG. BY ATLANTIC RECORDING CORP., 1841 BROADWAY, NEW YORK, N.Y. is the design Atlantic took up in the summer of 1968 and the one this October 1968 release carried from the start. The label matrices read (ST-A-681373CT) and (ST-A-681374CT), and the dead wax carries ST A681373-1C and ST A681374-1B: early lacquer cuts with the CT suffix of the Terre Haute plant. The jacket prints STEREO and SD 8207 to match the record, with the 1968 Atlantic copyright line and Printed in U.S.A. on the back. It is not the companion first-run pressing whose label matrix carries no plant suffix and sets the catalogue number above the word STEREO at the left.
