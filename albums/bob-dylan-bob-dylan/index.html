The Album

Recorded in two brisk sessions in New York in November 1961, this debut introduced a twenty-year-old Bob Dylan who had arrived in Greenwich Village only months earlier chasing the example of Woody Guthrie. Columbia staff producer John Hammond, already a legendary talent scout, signed Dylan on the strength of his live sets and harmonica work as a sideman, and captured him essentially performing his coffeehouse repertoire live to tape with guitar and harmonica alone. The album is built mostly from traditional blues and folk material, songs learned from Dave Van Ronk, Eric Von Schmidt and old 78s, alongside two originals: the talking-blues travelogue announcing his arrival in the city, and a direct tribute to the dying Guthrie signaling whose mantle he meant to inherit. Commercially the record sold modestly and was reportedly dismissed inside Columbia as a costly misstep, overshadowed almost immediately by the original songs Dylan would unveil on his very next album. In hindsight it stands as the unadorned prologue to one of popular music's most consequential careers, valued less for its chart performance than for the raw, already-distinctive voice and interpretive instinct it preserves before Dylan became a songwriter of international consequence.

The Label

The labels on this copy are the dark grey Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab design used across the 2014 numbered 45RPM reissue series, printing a 1962/2014 dual copyright line, a 550 (sides 1, 2, 4) or 560 (side 3) Madison Avenue address, and text crediting manufacture for Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab by either Mobile Fidelity Entertainment or Sony Music Entertainment depending on the side. None of this design existed before Mobile Fidelity began issuing its Original Master Recording 45RPM double-LP series, and the 2014 copyright date on every side places manufacture no earlier than that year regardless of the Columbia catalogue elements the label reproduces. The design therefore identifies this copy as the Mobile Fidelity stereo reissue, standing well below the 1962 Columbia originals and alongside Mobile Fidelity's companion mono edition of the same title in the pressing hierarchy.

Fidelity

Mastered by Krieg Wunderlich at Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab using its Gain 2 Ultra Analog chain and cut at 45RPM across two LPs, plated by Record Technology Incorporated and pressed on 180-gram vinyl at RTI. Collectors and audiophile reviewers rate the 45RPM Mobile Fidelity cut highly for its quiet surfaces and clarity next to the well-worn original tapes, though because this early Dylan material was recorded and mixed in mono, the companion mono 45RPM edition from the same series is generally regarded by collectors as the more faithful rendering of the original mix, with this stereo edition's instrumental separation judged less natural. Against the 1962 Columbia originals, this reissue offers markedly cleaner surfaces and a more modern transfer, but the mono original-tape framing gives the mono Mobile Fidelity edition the edge in authenticity among the hi-fi pressings of this title.

Notes

This is Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab's 2014 numbered stereo 45RPM double-LP reissue of Bob Dylan's 1962 debut, not an original Columbia pressing. Every label side carries a 1962/2014 dual copyright line, dating manufacture no earlier than 2014 regardless of the Columbia-style address and catalogue text reproduced on the labels. The runouts carry Mobile Fidelity's own etched codes (KW@MOFI mastering initials) rather than any Columbia-era stamper marks, confirming a from-the-master-tapes remaster rather than a vintage lacquer. Side four's runout carries a different master-tape reference number from sides one through three, worth noting as a minor inconsistency in the etched codes for this copy.
