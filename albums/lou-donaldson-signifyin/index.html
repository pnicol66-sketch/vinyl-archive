The Album

Recorded on July 17, 1963 at A&R Recording Studio in New York, Signifyin' captures alto saxophonist Lou Donaldson at a pivotal turn in his career, as he moved decisively from his earlier Charlie Parker-indebted bebop toward the earthy, backbeat-driven soul jazz that would define his mid-1960s work. The session paired Donaldson with organist "Big" John Patton, whose Hammond work anchors the date's deep groove and gospel-blues feel, alongside trumpeter Tommy Turrentine, guitarist Roy Montrell and drummer Ben Dixon. It was Donaldson's debut recording for Chess's Argo subsidiary after years on Blue Note, produced under Esmond Edwards's supervision, and it leans on original blues-based compositions alongside a pair of standards, "Time After Time" and "Don't Get Around Much Anymore." The record is often cited as an early, fully-formed statement of the soul jazz/organ-combo sound Donaldson would refine on his celebrated Blue Note run later in the decade. Critics have retrospectively praised the band's tight, funky cohesion, with Donaldson's blues-rooted phrasing riding comfortably atop Patton's grooves. Within Donaldson's long career - which spanned Blakey-era hard bop through to acid-jazz-era boogaloo hits - Signifyin' stands as a transitional but assured document of the organ-combo direction that made him one of soul jazz's most enduring and commercially successful figures.

The Label

This copy carries the white/silver "AUDIO ODYSSEY BY ARGO" arc-text label with a single large ARGO logo at left, "ULTRA HIGH FIDELITY" rim text, deep groove, and "MADE IN U.S.A." - the mono Argo house design used through the early-to-mid 1960s before Chess/Argo moved to the blue Argo livery seen on the stereo LPS-724 pressing of this same session. The matrix carries the LP-724 mono prefix with side numbers 12603/12604, matching the mono configuration rather than the LPS-724 stereo matrix (PR4S-...) found on the blue-label stereo copy. This places the copy at the mono first-issue stage of the release, ahead of the blue-label stereo pressing and well ahead of the later Cadet-era reissues of the same catalogue number.

Fidelity

This is a mono Chess/Argo pressing from the label's Chicago-area mastering and pressing operation, not a Van Gelder or RVG-cut record; the session was engineered at A&R Studio in New York with mastering handled in-house for Argo. No audiophile reissue (Analogue Productions, Music Matters, Speakers Corner, etc.) of this title exists, and later reissues were routine Cadet-era commercial repressings rather than upgraded cuts, so this original mono pressing sits at the top of the album's practical fidelity hierarchy alongside its stereo LPS-724 sibling. Collector discussion on forums of this specific Argo soul jazz era is thin compared to Blue Note dates, reflecting the label's lower audiophile profile rather than any known sonic problem with the cut itself.
