The Album

Recorded in March 1965 at a New Jersey studio session, Oh Baby! was organist John Patton's fourth leader date for his label, arriving after he had spent several years as an in-demand sideman backing saxophonists and guitarists on the same label's soul-jazz roster. Patton assembled a tight quintet built around guitarist Grant Green, with whom he had a particularly close musical rapport, alongside trumpeter Blue Mitchell, tenor saxophonist Harold Vick and drummer Ben Dixon, a working unit that gave the date a loose, swinging cohesion rather than a one-off studio pairing. The material leans into the earthy, blues-rooted side of the organ-combo idiom popular at the label in the mid-1960s, with original compositions contributed by Patton, Vick and Dixon rather than standards, reflecting the quintet's emphasis on group-written material and interplay over covers. Patton's playing throughout favors a spare, percussive approach to the Hammond organ that distinguished him from the more orchestral stylists of the instrument, while Green's guitar lines add a vocal, blues-drenched counterpoint. The album sits within the label's mid-1960s push into soul jazz, a commercially attuned but still serious strain of the music that brought organ combos into heavier rotation on jukeboxes and radio. Though never a crossover hit, the record has long been prized by soul-jazz collectors for its unpretentious groove and the chemistry among its players, and it remains a representative document of Patton's mid-career work as both leader and foil for Green's guitar.

The Label

The labels show the New York USA rim design used on Blue Note's stereo pressings of this title from the mid-1960s on - cream-and-blue panel with BLUE up the left, NOTE along the foot, and the rim reading BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC • NEW YORK USA with no street address. On this copy that familiar design carries a 2022 copyright line, a label code (LC 00133) and a full EAN barcode, none of which existed on the label before 1976 at the earliest and none before 2022 at all. The design is therefore a modern reproduction of the New York USA layout rather than evidence of an original or early pressing; the same rim wording appeared on genuine mid-1960s stereo copies decades before this pressing used it again. This places the copy at the foot of the pressing hierarchy, below every US and Japanese pressing that came before it.

Fidelity

The runouts carry the KPG@CA signature identifying a cut by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio, sourced from the original analog tapes per the jacket sticker, and pressed on 180-gram vinyl at a European plant. Collectors on the forums generally regard Kevin Gray's Cohearent remasters of this catalog favorably for transparency and low surface noise compared to budget-era reissues, though the original 1965 Van Gelder stereo cut and the mono first pressing remain the benchmark for tonal richness and are preferred by purists over any reissue, including this one. Within the reissue tier, this pressing ranks above older Japanese and EU reissues that predate the Cohearent remastering program but below a from-the-source analog cut made directly for an audiophile-specific series rather than a general-catalog reissue line.

Notes

This is the 2022 Blue Note Classic Vinyl Series stereo reissue of Oh Baby!. The label reproduces the mid-1960s New York USA design, but a 2022 copyright line and an LC 00133 code appear on the labels, dating the pressing to 2022 regardless of the vintage design it reproduces. The runouts carry the KPG@CA mastering signature of Kevin Gray's Cohearent Audio cut, and the back cover carries a sticker reading "Made in Germany". The pressing plant is not named on the labels themselves.
