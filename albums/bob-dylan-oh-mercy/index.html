The Album

By the late 1980s Bob Dylan was widely seen as creatively adrift, and on Bono's recommendation he turned to producer Daniel Lanois, decamping to a converted New Orleans house in spring 1989 to record with a loose Louisiana crew of session players. Lanois built a swampy, reverberant sonic world of tremolo guitars and distant percussion around Dylan's songs, and the sessions were famously tense, the two clashing over arrangements even as they drew strong performances from one another. The material ranks among Dylan's strongest of the era, from the apocalyptic 'Political World' and noir-tinged 'Man In The Long Black Coat' to the world-weary 'Most Of The Time' and hymn-like 'Ring Them Bells' and 'What Good Am I?', with 'Everything Is Broken' supplying blues grit. Dylan later devoted a lengthy chapter of his memoir Chronicles: Volume One to the sessions, including regret that outtakes like 'Series of Dreams' and 'Dignity' didn't make the final cut. Critics greeted the album as an unambiguous comeback, the first Dylan record in a decade discussed alongside his classic 1960s-70s work, restoring his standing as a songwriter at a pivotal moment. Its atmospheric, weathered production directly foreshadowed the Lanois-produced Time Out of Mind (1997) and the acclaimed late-career run that followed, making it a foundational record of Dylan's third act.

The Label

The side labels show MoFi's standard matte-black design with the orange/red 'Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab' rim band, script MoFi logo, GAIN 2 Ultra Analog badge, STEREO arrow, and a single small licensed Columbia 'walking eye' box - one logo, not a Columbia-branded label. The dual copyright line '© 1989, 2018 Columbia Records...Manufactured for Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab, Inc. by Sony Music Entertainment' documents the Sony license governing MoFi's Dylan reissue series from this era. This is MoFi's Sebastopol Gain 2 Ultra Analog period, when Sony-catalog titles were plated and pressed at RTI as numbered 4,000-copy 45 RPM sets. Only one MoFi vinyl variant of this title exists, so the label design and runout etchings fully settle provenance; it sits as a licensed audiophile reissue rather than any Columbia-label pressing in the title's broader hierarchy.

Fidelity

Mastered by Krieg Wunderlich (assisted by Shawn R. Britton) at MoFi's Sebastopol facility on the Gain 2 Ultra Analog system and pressed at RTI, among the quietest 180g US pressings available. Spreading the ten tracks over four 45 RPM sides gives Lanois's swamp-reverb production noticeably more air, low-end weight and longer ambient decay than the 1989 Columbia LP or early CD issues. Steve Hoffman forum consensus is strongly favorable toward the drums, bass and atmosphere on this cut, with only scattered reports of non-fill defects on some RTI copies. As with other 2015-2022 MoFi titles, a DSD256 digital step reportedly sat between tape and lacquer, so this is a high-resolution-sourced rather than pure-analog cut. It sits at the top of the album's fidelity hierarchy alongside MoFi's companion SACD, well ahead of the original Columbia LP and later Sony represses, with no Analogue Productions, One-Step, or comparable audiophile rival for this title.

Notes

2020 Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab MFSL 2-488 - numbered limited-edition 45 RPM double LP, licensed from Sony/Columbia rather than an original Columbia pressing. Runouts MFSL 2-488 Ar3/Br2/Cr2/Dr2 carry 'Kw@MoFi' (Krieg Wunderlich; the collector's 'KEVIN@MOFI' transcription is a misread) and lacquer numbers 33249/32943, matching the single known Discogs variant for this title. Silver MoFi banner jacket, the rear 'Limited Edition of 4,000' line, and the hand-numbered box confirm the standard numbered retail edition rather than a promo or test pressing. The original US Columbia LP dates to September 1989; only one MoFi vinyl variant exists, leaving no ambiguity about pressing identity.
