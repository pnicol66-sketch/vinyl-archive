The Album

On 25 and 27 July 1970 Albert Ayler played two open-air concerts in the sculpture garden of the Fondation Maeght at Saint-Paul-de-Vence, invited by the French critic and producer Daniel Caux and taped in stereo by the state broadcaster ORTF. He brought a quartet of Mary Parks on soprano and voice, Steve Tintweiss on bass and Allen Blairman on drums, with the veteran pianist Call Cobbs - delayed by a missed flight - joining only for the second night. The sets fold together the hymn-like anthems that made Ayler's name, Ghosts, Truth Is Marching In, Spirits Rejoice and Spiritual Reunion, with the blues-and-rock-inflected material of his last studio album, Music Is The Healing Force Of The Universe, and a long sequence of free, spirit-driven improvisations the producers titled Revelations. They were his final recorded performances: Ayler returned to New York and was found dead in the East River that November, aged thirty-four. A fraction of the July 27 concert appeared in France as Nuits de la Fondation Maeght, but the bulk of both nights stayed in the broadcaster's vaults until Zev Feldman located the reels and assembled this edition with the blessing of the Ayler estate. Critics received it as one of the most important archival jazz discoveries of its decade, both for the sound quality - far beyond the murky earlier tapes - and for the two hours of unheard music that close the story of the saxophonist who, more than any other, carried the fire music of the 1960s into open-throated gospel ecstasy.

The Label

Each of the ten sides carries a custom label made for this release alone: a red-orange to copper-orange ground shading lighter across the five records, the album's own circuit-style ALBERT AYLER REVELATIONS lettering in white or cream with the subtitle THE COMPLETE ORTF 1970 FONDATION MAEGHT RECORDINGS in dark red or dark brown, a small black ina square and the Elemental music oval script logo side by side to the left of the centre hole, and the LP/side designation (LP1/SIDE A through LP5/SIDE J) to the right. The lower rim text prints the recording date for that record - July 25, 1970 on LPs 1 and 2, July 27, 1970 on LPs 3, 4 and 5 - with ℗1970 INA, © 2022 Elemental Music, Made in EU, the catalogue number 5990542 and the elemental-music.com address. The labels are flat, with no deep groove. The printed 2022 copyright fixes the copy at 2022 or later and places it on the Record Store Day numbered edition of 1,200 issued 23 April 2022, the first and so far only vinyl edition of this material; the back jacket carries the numbered edition sticker, this copy 0195/1200.

Fidelity

The audio was transferred in high resolution directly from the original ORTF stereo tape reels, mixed and restored for release, and cut to lacquer by Kevin Gray at Cohearent Audio, whose KPG@CA signature is etched in all ten sides; the 180-gram records were pressed at GZ Media. The source is a 1970 outdoor radio broadcast tape, so the sound is open and surprisingly full-bodied for a live capture of this vintage - saxophone and voice close and vivid, bass and drums set back under the night air - and it stands far above the thin, distant quality of the previously circulated Maeght material. Collectors and reviewers rate this cut highly, praising both the restoration and the quiet, well-centred pressing, with the usual caveat that the tapes themselves set the ceiling. No earlier or competing vinyl edition of this programme exists, so this box is the reference vinyl for the music.

Notes

2022 INA / Elemental Music 5990542, the Record Store Day release of 23 April 2022: a numbered edition of 1,200 on 180-gram vinyl, five LPs in gatefold and tri-fold jackets with a 24-page booklet inside the box. The ten custom labels print © 2022 Elemental Music and Made in EU, dating the pressing to 2022 at the earliest; the back jacket sticker numbers this copy 0195/1200. Every side's dead wax is etched 5990542-A to -J with KPG@CA for Kevin Gray's cut at Cohearent Audio and a GZ Media premaster code (234658E1 to E10), so the box is a Czech pressing of the US-cut lacquers. This is the first and only vinyl issue of the complete Fondation Maeght tapes, the earlier French LP having carried only part of the second night. The jacket keeps its original shrink with the red Record Store Day hype sticker.
