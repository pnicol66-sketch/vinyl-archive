This Pressing

1966 stereo first pressing - Blue Note NY USA label (BST 84229), Van Gelder-stamped runout, catalog number falls inside the NY USA design window that closed later that year.

The Album

Recorded April 29, 1966 at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio, Got a Good Thing Goin' captures organist Big John Patton at the height of his mid-1960s soul-jazz run for Blue Note, reunited once more with guitarist Grant Green in what proved to be their final recorded collaboration. The program pairs two extended Patton-Green originals, "The Yodel" and "Soul Woman," with three contemporary R&B covers - Marvin Gaye's "Ain't That Peculiar," Sam Cooke's "The Shake," and Duke Pearson's "Amanda" - reflecting Blue Note's mid-60s push toward a groovier, jukebox-friendly soul-jazz sound. Patton, a Kansas City-raised organist who cut his teeth in R&B bands before joining Lou Donaldson and later leading his own Blue Note dates, was among the most rhythmically direct and unpretentious organists in the label's stable, and this session is frequently cited as a high point of his catalog. Drummer Hugh Walker and conguero Richard Landrum push the record into a percussive, dance-oriented groove that helped define mid-60s organ-combo soul jazz alongside contemporaries like Grant Green's own leader dates and Lou Donaldson's records of the era. AllMusic's retrospective review awarded the album four and a half stars, and it remains a favorite among soul-jazz collectors for its hot, unfussy grooves, even as some listeners find the material simpler than Patton's more exploratory work. The session stands as a snapshot of Blue Note on the eve of its sale to Liberty Records, closing out a fruitful and influential chapter in both Patton's and Green's careers.

The Label

The side labels are the light-blue and white design reading "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC · NEW YORK, USA" with no Liberty Records credit, the fifth Blue Note label style used roughly 1962-1966 across the BLP/BST 4081-4247 catalog run. Blue Note was sold to Liberty Records in mid-1966, after which remaining NY USA-labeled stock was used briefly before the imprint was updated to read "A Division of Liberty Records, Inc." Because BST 84229 falls within the NY USA catalog window and this copy retains the unmodified New York address text, it represents the label's last independent-era design for this title, pre-dating the Liberty-credited pressings that followed later in 1966. The typed runout (BNST 84229 A/B, Van Gelder stamp, matrix 114) matches Discogs-documented Van Gelder-cut lacquers for this pressing; no Plastylite ear was noted in the transcription, which is common in casual write-ups and does not itself rule out a first-pressing plant.

Fidelity

Engineered by Rudy Van Gelder direct to the original stereo masters, this NY USA pressing sits at or near the top of the fidelity hierarchy for this title, ahead of the thinner-sounding Liberty and United Artists-era pressings from roughly 1967-1973. Collectors on forums such as Steve Hoffman generally favor these mid-60s Van Gelder-cut Blue Note stereo pressings for their warmth and immediacy over later reissues. No audiophile remaster (Music Matters, Analogue Productions, Tone Poet, etc.) of this specific title is documented, so this original pressing represents the best commonly available sonic option; a 2000s CD reissue exists but is a digital, not vinyl, alternative.
