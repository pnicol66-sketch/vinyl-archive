The Album

Recorded April 29, 1966 at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio, Got a Good Thing Goin' captures organist Big John Patton at the height of his mid-1960s soul-jazz run for Blue Note, reunited once more with guitarist Grant Green in what proved to be their final recorded collaboration. The program pairs two extended Patton-Green originals, "The Yodel" and "Soul Woman," with three contemporary R&B covers - Marvin Gaye's "Ain't That Peculiar," Sam Cooke's "The Shake," and Duke Pearson's "Amanda" - reflecting Blue Note's mid-60s push toward a groovier, jukebox-friendly soul-jazz sound. Patton, a Kansas City-raised organist who cut his teeth in R&B bands before joining Lou Donaldson and later leading his own Blue Note dates, was among the most rhythmically direct and unpretentious organists in the label's stable, and this session is frequently cited as a high point of his catalog. Drummer Hugh Walker and conguero Richard Landrum push the record into a percussive, dance-oriented groove that helped define mid-60s organ-combo soul jazz alongside contemporaries like Grant Green's own leader dates and Lou Donaldson's records of the era. AllMusic's retrospective review awarded the album four and a half stars, and it remains a favorite among soul-jazz collectors for its hot, unfussy grooves, even as some listeners find the material simpler than Patton's more exploratory work. The session stands as a snapshot of Blue Note on the eve of its sale to Liberty Records, closing out a fruitful and influential chapter in both Patton's and Green's careers.

The Label

The blue-and-white side labels read "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC · NEW YORK USA" with no Liberty Records credit - the label text Blue Note used before its sale to Liberty Records in mid-1966. The album was first issued by Liberty in July 1966, after the sale, on New York USA labels left over from Blue Note's stock, and these are the labels of its first stereo issue; later copies carry the "A Division of Liberty Records, Inc." text. Both sides carry the hand-etched BNST 84229 matrix (A and B) and the VAN GELDER stamp. The labels do not name the pressing plant.

Fidelity

Van Gelder cut the lacquers directly from the original stereo masters. This copy carries the New York USA labels of the title's first stereo issue, which Liberty had pressed at All Disc rather than at Blue Note's earlier Plastylite plant; no copy of this title carries the Plastylite ear. Collectors on the forums generally rate these early Liberty-era, Van Gelder-cut Blue Note stereo pressings as full-bodied and immediate, ahead of the later Liberty/United Artists pressings from the years that followed. A recent Tone Poet Series reissue offers a newly mastered audiophile cut of the title, and this New York USA label stereo copy ranks just behind it while remaining well ahead of the 2000s CD reissue.

Notes

1966 Blue Note BST 84229 stereo. The labels read "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC · NEW YORK USA" with no Liberty Records text - label stock left over from before Liberty bought Blue Note. The album was first released in July 1966, after the sale, and its first issue was pressed for Liberty at the All Disc plant in Roselle, New Jersey, which Liberty had bought, rather than at Blue Note's earlier Plastylite plant; no copy of this title carries the Plastylite ear. Both sides carry the hand-etched BNST 84229 A / B matrices and the VAN GELDER stamp. These are the labels of the title's first stereo issue; later copies carry the "A Division of Liberty Records, Inc." label text. The jacket credits Reid Miles for the cover photo and design and Rudy Van Gelder for the recording, and its back carries ad art for Patton's Along Came John, Blue John, The Way I Feel and Oh Baby!
