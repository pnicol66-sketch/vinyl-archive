The Album

Recorded in New York in late 1964 and January 1965, I Put A Spell On You arrived as Nina Simone's fourth studio set for Philips and pushed her further from the jazz-club piano recitals of her early Bethlehem and Colpix years into a fully orchestrated pop-soul-jazz idiom, arranged and conducted largely by Hal Mooney and Horace Ott. Its title track, a smouldering reworking of Screamin' Jay Hawkins's blues-shouter original, became one of Simone's signature performances and one of the most covered songs of the era. The album also introduced her enduring reading of "Feeling Good," drawn from the Broadway musical The Roar of the Greasepaint - The Smell of the Crowd, alongside a French-language performance of Jacques Brel's "Ne Me Quitte Pas" and songs by Charles Aznavour, reflecting Simone's restless, genre-crossing repertoire. Coming at the height of her involvement in the civil rights movement, the record captured her at a creative peak, balancing torch-song intimacy with the simmering intensity that increasingly defined her live and recorded work. Critically it is remembered as one of her most fully realized mid-1960s statements, and both the title cut and "Feeling Good" have gone on to enjoy a second life through countless samples, covers, and film/TV placements, cementing the album's status as a cornerstone of Simone's catalogue and of 1960s vocal jazz-soul more broadly.

The Label

This copy carries the grey Philips label with the multi-colour rainbow stripe, the shield logo at top, "MONAURAL" printed at left of the spindle, and rim text reading "PHILIPS RECORDS - VENDOR: MERCURY RECORD CORPORATION" with no Mercury street address - the design used on the earliest US Philips mono pressings of this LP in 1965. Discogs documents a later label variant (c.1966 on) with "MR"-prefix runout stampers, and by 1970 an explicit "Manufactured and distributed by Mercury Record Productions, Inc." line was added to the label - neither is present here. That places this copy in the first-issue label window for PHM 200-172, ahead of the Mercury-address repressings that followed later in the 1960s and into the 1970s.

Fidelity

This is a standard-issue 1965 Philips mono cut, not a Van Gelder or audiophile mastering; the PHM200172A/B M1 runout suggests an early stamper generation from the original metalwork. Collector consensus on mid-60s Philips/Mercury pop-jazz LPs treats these original mono Philips pressings as the most desirable vintage cut, ahead of the mid/late-1960s Mercury-address repressings and the 1970s Richmond-plant reissues, which are reported as thinner and more compressed-sounding. Modern reissues (e.g. the Verve/Acoustic Sounds 180g LP) offer cleaner, quieter vinyl and improved bass extension but a more modern equalization; collectors seeking the original 1965 tonal balance still prize a clean early mono copy like this one over the reissue.

Notes

1965 Philips PHM 200-172 mono - first-issue pressing. The grey rainbow-stripe label with no Mercury street address and the plain PHM200172A/B M1 runout suffixes match the earliest documented label design for this catalogue number, ahead of later Mercury-address and MR-runout repressings.
