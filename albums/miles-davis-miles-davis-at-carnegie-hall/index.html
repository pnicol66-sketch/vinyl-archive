The Album

Recorded live on the evening of May 19, 1961, this album documents a benefit concert at Carnegie Hall that marked a unique convergence in Miles Davis's career: his working quintet, featuring Hank Mobley on tenor sax alongside the rhythm section of Wynton Kelly, Paul Chambers, and Jimmy Cobb, performing both standalone and backed by a 21-piece orchestra arranged and conducted by Gil Evans, Davis's most important large-ensemble collaborator. The program mixed hard-bop quintet vehicles like "So What" and "No Blues" with orchestral reworkings drawn from the Miles Ahead/Sketches of Spain vein, including Ahmad Jamal's "New Rhumba" and "The Meaning of the Blues," giving listeners a rare live document of the Evans-Davis orchestral sound performed in concert rather than assembled in the studio. Coming at a transitional moment for Davis - between the classic quintet years and his later shift toward Wayne Shorter and a more modal, exploratory direction - the record captures him at a peak of technical and expressive command, with contemporary critics singling out the trumpet's tone and the drama of the evening. Critical reaction at the time, excerpted on the original jacket from the New York Times, New York Herald Tribune, and Down Beat, was enthusiastic, with reviewers noting Davis's shift from restraint to an impassioned, brassy exuberance over the course of the performance. The album stands as an important entry in the Davis-Evans discography, valued both as a rare live big-band jazz recording and as a document of Davis's showmanship and interpretive range mid-career, and it remains a touchstone for listeners tracing the arc from Miles Ahead and Sketches of Spain through his live performance practice of the early 1960s.

The Label

This copy carries the red-and-black 6-eye Columbia label with the "Nonbreakable" notation under the catalog number and the diagonal pink "DEMONSTRATION RECORD / NOT FOR SALE" overprint, identifying it as a Columbia promotional pressing rather than a commercial stock copy. The 6-eye label design was Columbia's standard from the mid-1950s through mid-1962, when it began transitioning to the 2-eye design (a transition Discogs dates to June-July 1962, right around this album's release); the presence of the 6-eye label here places this copy squarely in that first pressing window rather than the later 2-eye reissue. Because promotional copies were drawn from the same early pressing runs sent to press and radio ahead of/at general release, the 6-eye demo label indicates this is a first-pressing-era pressing, distinguished from a general commercial 6-eye copy only by its promotional overprint and lack of a for-sale designation.

Fidelity

This is a mono pressing from Columbia's 6-eye era, mastered from the original 1961 concert tapes under producer Teo Macero; mono Columbia jazz pressings of this vintage are generally well regarded by collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv for their warmth and presence compared to the era's "electronically re-channeled for stereo" counterpart, which purists consider inferior since it is a fake-stereo derivative rather than a true stereo recording. As a promotional pressing drawn from early stampers, sound quality should be comparable to a standard 6-eye commercial mono copy of similar vintage, i.e., among the better-sounding versions of this title. No audiophile remaster (Mobile Fidelity, Analogue Productions, Music Matters, etc.) of this specific live album is known to exist, so within the album's fidelity hierarchy this 6-eye mono pressing sits at or near the top, well above the electronically re-channeled stereo pressings and later 2-eye/orange-label reissues.
