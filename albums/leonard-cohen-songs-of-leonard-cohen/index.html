The Album

Songs of Leonard Cohen was the debut album of the Montreal poet and novelist, cut in New York after John Hammond's illness handed production to the young John Simon, whose arrangements added strings, winds and backing voices to songs Cohen had originally wanted performed with guitar alone. Drawn from Cohen's already-published poetry and fiction, the record opens with "Suzanne," a song that had already circulated through Judy Collins's cover before Cohen ever released a note of his own. The album moves between spare folk balladry and more ornamented, almost baroque arrangements, carrying the literary density and romantic fatalism that marked Cohen's writing. It was received more warmly in Britain and Europe than in his native North America, where Cohen's unconventional, near-spoken vocal style initially divided critics. In the decades since, it has come to be regarded as one of the foundational singer-songwriter albums of its era, securing Cohen's transition from poet and novelist to recording artist and establishing a sound and sensibility he would develop across his subsequent career.

The Label

This copy carries the red CBS label used on Dutch Columbia Broadcasting System pressings, with the white CBS wordmark top centre, dark-red print, BIEM and Copyright Control marks, and the rim text ending MADE IN HOLLAND / CBS AND (c) CBS INC - a plain, device-free design with no deep groove. The label is stamped (P) 1968, which fixes the earliest possible date for the copyright but not the pressing date itself; this red CBS house style was used on Dutch CBS product into the early-to-mid 1970s, after the label that accompanied the title's first Dutch stereo pressing. The catalogue number printed on the label (S 63241, with the US cross-reference CS 9533) matches the title throughout its Dutch pressing run, so the number alone does not separate first pressing from later repress; only the label style does, and the style on this copy points to a later Dutch pressing in that run rather than to the first.

Fidelity

The Dutch CBS pressing derives from the same Columbia master tapes used worldwide for this title but was cut and pressed locally rather than at the US or UK source plants; collectors generally regard the UK and US first pressings as the more desirable cuts for this title, with continental European pressings such as this one ranked below them for ultimate sonic pedigree, though the difference is modest on a straightforward folk-vocal recording of this vintage. No audiophile remastering of this specific pressing exists; later reissue series on 180g vinyl are mastered separately and are not compared here.
