The Album

Brownie McGhee and Sonny Terry first joined forces in 1939, forging one of the defining partnerships in postwar acoustic blues: McGhee's warm, Piedmont-style guitar and singing set against Terry's whooping, hollering harmonica. The pair carried their duo act from rural roots into the folk revival, touring internationally and taking their music to Broadway, most notably in the long-running stage and touring productions of "Cat On A Hot Tin Roof" through the mid-to-late 1950s. Terry, blinded as a boy in a childhood accident, had already apprenticed under Piedmont bluesman Blind Boy Fuller before teaming with McGhee, giving the duo's sound a direct lineage back to the country blues of the 1930s. This set gathers original material and blues standards, with two tracks bringing in fellow Texas blues giant Lightnin' Hopkins for informal, guitar-driven duet performances that widen the record's stylistic range. By the time of this session the duo were elder statesmen of the folk-blues circuit, their hollers and moaning vocals prized by a new generation of folk and blues revivalists for their unselfconscious, front-porch authenticity. The album stands as a representative late-period document of one of the most enduring and influential duos in American blues, capturing the intimate, conversational interplay that made their live shows legendary.

The Label

This copy carries the gold/yellow Everest label with the boxed "Archive of Folk & Jazz Music" guitar-and-book logo, the catalogue number FS-242-A/B at the right rim, and the title and artist credit set in the plain serif/sans lettering used across the Archive series; no deep groove ring is visible on either side. Everest's Archive of Folk & Jazz Music imprint reissued vintage folk, blues and jazz sessions from the mid-1960s into the 1970s under this same yellow-label design and Wilshire Blvd., Los Angeles address, so the design alone does not distinguish an early stamping from a later one within that run. The back cover's "Everest stereo records may be played..." text and the S-3943 back-cover reference number are consistent with the documented first issue of this pairing under the Everest Archive series rather than any later foreign or budget reissue. No stamper, mother, or plant-code detail beyond the catalogue number is legible in the transcribed runouts, so this copy is placed as the single documented Everest Archive pressing of this catalogue number rather than ranked against any other variant.

Fidelity

The Archive of Folk & Jazz Music series, by Everest's own stated purpose, drew on both original studio masters and older, sometimes well-worn 78-era recordings that its engineers cleaned up and remastered for LP; the back cover's own note describes hand-splicing and de-clicking work done to make older recordings presentable. Collectors generally treat these Archive-series folk and blues reissues as solid if unspectacular transfers, useful for the music itself rather than prized as audiophile pressings. There is no better-regarded audiophile remaster of this specific McGhee/Terry/Hopkins pairing to rank this copy against, so it stands as the primary available cut of this material.

This Everest Records "Archive of Folk & Jazz Music" stereo LP pairs Brownie McGhee and Sonny Terry with guest turns from Lightnin' Hopkins on two tracks, issued under catalogue number FS-242.
