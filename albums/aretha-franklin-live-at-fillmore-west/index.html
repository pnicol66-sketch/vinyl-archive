The Album

Aretha Live at Fillmore West captures Franklin's three-night stand of March 5-7, 1971 at Bill Graham's San Francisco rock palace, a booking producer Jerry Wexler pursued to bridge the Queen of Soul to the Fillmore's rock audience. Wexler paired her with King Curtis and the Kingpins - Cornell Dupree, Bernard Purdie, Jerry Jemmott and Billy Preston - augmented by the Memphis Horns and her own Sweethearts of Soul, yielding one of the hardest-driving bands she ever recorded with. The set straddled worlds deliberately: signature hits like "Respect" and "Dr. Feelgood" alongside radical soul reworkings of Stephen Stills, Simon & Garfunkel, the Beatles and Bread. Its legendary, unplanned highlight came when Ray Charles, sitting in the audience, was called onstage for an extended jam reprising "Spirit in the Dark." Released in May 1971 on Atlantic, the album topped the Billboard R&B chart, reached #7 pop, and became Franklin's first gold album since 1968's Aretha Now. It is routinely ranked among the greatest live soul albums ever made, capturing Franklin at her creative peak, and carries added poignancy as one of saxophonist King Curtis's final recorded triumphs before his murder later that year.

The Label

Both sides bear Rainbo Records' generic white 'TEST PRESSING' plant labels (gold script logo, '1738 Berkeley Street, Santa Monica, California 90404', unfilled Customer/S-/Matrix#/Date fields, marked SIDE A / SIDE B) - no 4 Men With Beards branding appears anywhere on the disc. Rainbo occupied the Santa Monica address from 1974 to 2006 and pressed the 2003 retail edition for 4 Men With Beards, the budget reissue imprint run by Berkeley distributor Runt LLC under Atlantic license. The etched 4M-115 A/B runout matches the documented 4MWB retail matrix on Discogs, and the S-59426/S-59267 codes are Rainbo's standard S-prefix stamper numbers, confirming plant and stamper run. As a proofing pull from the same lacquers/stampers as retail copies, this disc predates commercial pressing but sits at the bottom of the title's overall label hierarchy, well below the 1971 Atlantic original and the Speakers Corner audiophile pressing.

Fidelity

The 4 Men With Beards edition credits no mastering engineer or tape source, and Runt/4MWB reissues of this era are widely described on forums.stevehoffman.tv and Discogs as inconsistent and often digital- or CD-sourced, with surface-noise and QC complaints frequently tied to Rainbo pressings. Expect a flat, somewhat compressed transfer in which the 180g weight is cosmetic rather than sonic. In the album's overall fidelity hierarchy, the Speakers Corner 180g edition (all-analogue, Kevin Gray cut at Cohearent Audio from the original tapes, Pallas-pressed) sits at the top, followed by a clean US Atlantic SD 7205 original; this 4M115 pressing ranks at the bottom alongside other budget 2000s reissues. As a test pressing from the same lacquers as retail copies it offers no sonic upgrade, and unapproved trial stampers can occasionally run noisier than final production pressings.
