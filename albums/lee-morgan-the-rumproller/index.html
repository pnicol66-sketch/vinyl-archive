The Album

Recorded at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio on April 9 and 21, 1965, The Rumproller found Lee Morgan chasing the funky, boogaloo-inflected momentum of his massive 1964 hit "The Sidewinder," this time leaning on outside writers - including Andrew Hill for the loping title cut - rather than his own pen for the lead single. By this point Morgan was Blue Note's reigning young star, a former Jazz Messengers and Art Blakey alumnus whose trumpet combined dazzling technique with an unmistakably soulful, blues-rooted sensibility. Joining him is tenor saxophonist Joe Henderson, then emerging as one of the label's most vital voices, alongside a fresh rhythm section of pianist Ronnie Mathews, bassist Victor Sproles and drummer Billy Higgins. The record captures the mid-1960s hard-bop mainstream at its most commercially minded, pairing groove-oriented material with the harmonic sophistication that defined the era's Blue Note house style. Critical reception was mixed-to-positive at the time and has remained so since, with some critics viewing it as a somewhat formulaic attempt to replicate "The Sidewinder's" success, while others praise the strength of the individual performances, particularly Henderson's soloing and Higgins' propulsive drumming. Nonetheless, the album holds a solid place in Morgan's prolific mid-1960s Blue Note run and remains a favorite among soul-jazz and boogaloo collectors for its instantly catchy title track.

The Label

The label on this copy is the classic Blue Note "New York, USA" design: white top band reading "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC. " over "NEW YORK USA", blue lower band with "BLUE"/"NOTE" in large white letters, no "Division of Liberty Records" text and no deep groove (deep groove had ended by the early 1960s). This design ran continuously on Blue Note catalog numbers roughly BLP 4081 through BLP 4247 (c. 1962-1966), before being replaced by the Liberty-era label following the January 1966 sale of Blue Note to Liberty Records. Since BLP 4199 was recorded in April 1965 and released in January 1966, this NY USA label is the correct and only label design for a true first pressing of this catalog number - there is no earlier

Fidelity

This is a Rudy Van Gelder original cut, mastered directly from his session tapes at his own Englewood Cliffs facility and pressed by Plastylite - collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv consistently rate original Van Gelder Blue Note masterings as the gold standard for the label's mid-60s sound, prized for warmth, presence, and Higgins' cymbal detail. This NY USA mono pressing sits at the top of the fidelity hierarchy for this title, ahead of the Liberty-era NY reissues (still Van Gelder-mastered but without the ear/Plastylite pressing), the 1970s United Artists-era blue label reissues, and later 1980s-90s EMI/Manhattan reissues, all of which use lesser-quality vinyl and mastering chains. The modern Blue Note Classic Vinyl Series reissue (Kevin Gray remaster from analog tapes, RTI pressing) is excellent but voiced differently and is generally regarded by purists as a fine alternative rather than a replacement for this original pressing.

Notes

This is the original 1966 Blue Note mono first pressing of BLP 4199. The "New York, USA" label design (no Liberty text) matches the label's continuous 1962-1966 run, exactly the window in which this April 1965-recorded, January 1966-released album appeared. The typed runout shows Van Gelder hand-etched stamps on both sides and the Plastylite "ear" on side B, the two decisive marks of a true Blue Note/Plastylite pressing predating the 1966 Liberty buyout. No club, promo, or reissue markings are indicated.
