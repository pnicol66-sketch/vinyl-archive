The Album

Concorde was recorded in New York in July 1955 and was among the first Modern Jazz Quartet albums conceived from the outset as a long-playing statement, and the first to feature drummer Connie Kay, who had just replaced Kenny Clarke. The set showcases the group's signature blend of chamber-music restraint and blues/swing feeling, built around Milt Jackson's blues writing ("Ralph's New Blues"), standards given the MJQ's contrapuntal treatment ("All Of You," "I'll Remember April," the Gershwin medley, "Softly As In A Morning Sunrise"), and John Lewis's ambitious title-track fugue, whose Baroque counterpoint echoes Bach's The Musical Offering and exemplifies the emerging "Third Stream" fusion of jazz and classical form. The Modern Jazz Quartet grew out of the rhythm section of the early Dizzy Gillespie big band and became one of the most influential and longest-lived small groups in jazz, prized for its restrained elegance, tight ensemble interplay, and John Lewis's compositional ambitions as musical director. Concorde arrived at a pivotal early moment in that trajectory, cementing the quartet's refined identity just as it was gaining wider recognition on the festival and concert-hall jazz circuit. Critics have long cited the album, especially the title fugue, as an early and influential example of jazz musicians engaging seriously with classical formal devices without sacrificing swing. It remains a touchstone in the MJQ's catalog and in the broader history of the Third Stream movement.

The Label

This copy shows the earliest Prestige yellow label: a black perimeter ring decorated with abstract yellow arrow/spiral scribbles (not yet the denser "firework" pattern of later 1957-58 pressings), the address reading "Prestige Records Inc. 446 W. 50th St., N.Y.C.", and a visible deep-groove ring per the collector's grading. Per bsnpubs/bebop-discography label-history research, 446 W. 50th Street was Prestige's original New York office used from the label's founding until the move first to 203 South Washington Ave., Bergenfield, NJ by roughly 1958-59. That NYC address, combined with the deep groove and the RVG-only (no ear, pre-dates any stamper suffixes) runout stamp, firmly places this pressing in the first-issue year, 1955, ahead of the yellow firework label update and the later NJ-address labels documented on Discogs/bsnpubs.

Fidelity

Cut and stamped by Rudy Van Gelder from his own session tapes (RVG etch in the deadwax on both sides), this NYC deep-groove pressing represents the earliest and most direct mono cutting of the session. Prestige's mid-1950s pressings from this era are generally solid if not deluxe, with warm, present sound typical of Van Gelder's Hackensack studio work; collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv rate original Prestige yellow-label mono pressings as the most natural-sounding versions of these MJQ sessions, ahead of the later Bergenfield-era repressings and the OJC reissue, which was mastered from safety copies with somewhat flatter results. No audiophile reissue (Analogue Productions, Music Matters, etc.) of this specific title is documented, so this original pressing sits at or near the top of the fidelity hierarchy for Concorde.
