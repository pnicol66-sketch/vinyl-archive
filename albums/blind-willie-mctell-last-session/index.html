The Album

Recorded in Atlanta in September 1956 in the back room of a record shop, this was an impromptu session set up when a folklorist and record dealer learned that the ageing, blind Piedmont songster was still in town and persuaded him to sit for a portable tape machine. McTell, then in poor health and only a year from his death, ran through a mixture of blues, spirituals, hokum numbers and old pop and hillbilly songs he had played on the streets and in juke joints for three decades, occasionally joined by his old partner Curley Weaver on a second guitar. He had already recorded prolifically in the 1920s-40s for a string of labels under different names, and by the 1950s was a relatively forgotten figure whose intricate fingerpicked 12-string style and warm, conversational vocal delivery represented one of the high points of Piedmont blues musicianship. The loose, unhurried nature of the session - McTell talking between songs, calling for a drink, singing whatever came to mind - gives the record an unusually intimate, documentary quality rather than a polished studio performance. It stands as the last substantial recorded testament of an artist widely regarded as one of the finest guitarists and most versatile songsters to come out of the Georgia blues tradition, and it has been treated by blues scholars and later generations of musicians as an essential late chapter in his catalogue and in the history of pre-war-rooted acoustic blues.

The Label

The label on this copy reads PRESTIGE BLUESVILLE in the familiar blue-and-white two-line logo with a HIGH FIDELITY band at the foot, and the catalogue line on the label itself reads OBC-517 STEREO with (BV-1040) given underneath in parentheses - the cover, by contrast, prints only BV 1040 with no OBC number, a mismatch that itself marks this as a later, catalogue-renumbered pressing rather than the original single-number Bluesville issue. Bluesville used a mono blue-and-silver deep-groove label at its 1961 launch; a stereo pressing carrying the OBC prefix belongs to the Original Blues Classics reissue line that Fantasy/Prestige ran through the 1970s-80s after acquiring the Prestige catalogue. The copy also carries a UPC barcode, a mark absent from anything pressed before 1980, which places it in that later Original Blues Classics stereo reissue run and rules out any claim to first-pressing status regardless of how closely the label wording echoes the original.

Fidelity

The source is a 1956 amateur tape recording made on portable equipment in a shop back room, inherently limited in fidelity regardless of later mastering care; this edition was remastered in 1987 at Fantasy Studios from that same tape. Collectors regard the recording's rough, informal origins as part of its character rather than a flaw, and this stereo Original Blues Classics cut is considered a solid, listenable transfer, though it ranks below the deep-groove mono Bluesville original in collector esteem for authenticity of era rather than outright sound quality, since both draw on the same modest source tape.

Notes

This is the 1987 Original Blues Classics stereo reissue of the 1961 Prestige Bluesville album, remastered at Fantasy Studios and pressed at the Columbia Records Pressing Plant in Carrollton, GA. The label prints OBC-517 STEREO with (BV-1040) as a secondary reference, while the jacket still carries only the original BV 1040 number - a mismatch typical of these later Fantasy-distributed reissues rather than the first Bluesville pressing. A UPC barcode on the jacket is conclusive: no copy carrying it could have been made before 1980, which places this pressing well after the 1961 mono original and the 1966 UK reissue.
