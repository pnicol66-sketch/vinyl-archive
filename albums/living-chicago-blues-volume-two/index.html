The Album

This volume is the second installment of a landmark series that set out to document the working, unsung players of Chicago's blues clubs rather than the genre's established stars, pairing three separate acts on a single LP. Carey Bell, a harmonica player steeped in the Little Walter and Sonny Boy Williamson lineage, leads a quartet featuring his son Lurrie Bell on guitar, an early glimpse of a next-generation talent who would go on to a long career of his own. Magic Slim and the Teardrops represent the raw, hard-driving West Side bar-band sound that Slim carried from Mississippi through decades of nightly Chicago gigs, built around his and his brother's rhythm section. Johnny “Big Moose” Walker, a veteran pianist whose résumé stretched back through Sonny Boy Williamson's and Howlin' Wolf's bands, closes the set with a set of barrelhouse-rooted piano blues backed by guitarist Louis Myers. Captured live-in-the-studio with minimal polish, the series as a whole became prized by critics and historians for preserving a generation of journeyman players who rarely made it to vinyl under their own names, and this volume is regarded as a key document of the late-1970s Chicago blues club scene.

The Label

Both sides carry a flat label: a grey/silver field with white print, a single alligator logo centred at the bottom of the label, the series title “LIVING CHICAGO BLUES” arcing around the rim, the side letter and catalogue number AL 7702 at left, and the artist names and track titles printed directly on the label face. There is no street address, legal rim text, or deep groove on either side, and this grey alligator-logo design is the only one documented for the US Alligator LP of this title, so the label design itself does not distinguish one manufacturing run from another. The runout on this pressing is stamped ANVIL with no date etch, a different stamp from a run of the same catalogue number that carries an ALT stamp with an etched date of 11-6-80; the two stamps mark separate manufacturing runs of the same 1978 issue, and neither the label nor the stamp alone fixes which run came first.

Fidelity

This is a standard commercial late-1970s Alligator Records pressing, cut for wide trade distribution rather than as an audiophile product, with no mastering engineer separately credited on the jacket or label. Collectors generally rate the series for the immediacy and rawness of its performances rather than studio polish, and no audiophile reissue of this particular volume exists to compare against. As the sole vinyl edition of this title pressed in its country of origin, this pressing stands as the primary listening source for the album.

Notes

1978 Alligator Records AL 7702 stereo compilation, the second volume of the label's Living Chicago Blues series. The grey alligator-logo label carries the full track listing printed on its face, matching the catalogue number, with no second label design documented for this title to rank it against. The deadwax is stamped ANVIL with no accompanying date etch, differing from the dated 1980 runout recorded for other copies bearing this same catalogue number, which points to a separate manufacturing run of the same 1978 issue. Both the jacket and the vinyl are graded NM-.
