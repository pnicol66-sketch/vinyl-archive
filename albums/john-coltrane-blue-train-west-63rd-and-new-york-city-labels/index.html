This Pressing

Mixed-label mono pressing (Inc./47 West 63rd side paired with a New York USA side) - deep groove both sides, RVG stamp and Plastylite ear with the 9M mark found on the first pressing, dated by the newer label to the 1961-1966 window.

The Album

Recorded in September 1957 at the midpoint of a year in which John Coltrane was reshaping his playing after leaving Miles Davis's group and working through a period of personal transformation, this session stands as his only outing as a leader for Blue Note during his contractual run on Prestige, made possible because Blue Note borrowed him for a single date. He assembled a front line of Lee Morgan and Curtis Fuller alongside his working rhythm section of Kenny Drew, Paul Chambers and "Philly" Joe Jones, giving the record a harder, brass-driven sound than his Prestige sessions of the same period. The album is built around four originals, including the title blues and the harmonically dense "Moment's Notice" and "Lazy Bird," plus a single standard, "I'm Old Fashioned," that lets Coltrane stretch out as a ballad interpreter. It arrived at a moment when Coltrane's improvising was growing noticeably more exploratory, foreshadowing the dense, rapid-fire approach that would soon be dubbed "sheets of sound." Critics and listeners responded to the record's combination of blues feeling and advanced harmonic writing, and it quickly became recognized as one of the defining hard bop statements of the era. In the decades since, it has remained a touchstone recording both for Coltrane's own development and for the Blue Note label's identity as a home for serious, blues-rooted modern jazz.

The Label

This copy carries two different Blue Note label designs rather than one: the Side 1 label prints BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC with the 47 West 63rd, NYC address, the design the company used from 1959 to 1962 with its registered-trademark mark at the bottom, while the Side 2 label prints BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC with NEW YORK USA and no street address at all, the design that replaced it and ran from late 1961 to 1966. Because the two sides come from different label printings, the pressing is dated by the newer of the pair: the New York USA design did not exist before late 1961, so this assembled copy cannot have left the plant any earlier than that, placing it in the 1961-1966 window rather than with the 1958 mixed-address first pressing or the 1959-1962 Inc./registered-mark repress. A deep groove is pressed into both sides and the runout still carries the RVG stamp, Plastylite ear and the same 9M mark found on the first pressing, even as the paper labels were drawn from two different printing runs. The catalogue number on the jacket is printed simply as 1577, without the BLP prefix used on the labels - the normal house style for this jacket and not itself a dating point.

Fidelity

The runout carries the same RVG stamp and 9M mark found on the earliest pressings of this title, so the groove source itself is unchanged from the original cutting. The mixed Inc./New York USA label pairing, however, places the actual manufacture of this copy in the early-to-mid 1960s rather than at the 1958 pressing run, and a repress of this generation sits below the true first-label and 1959-1962 Inc. pressings in the hierarchy collectors maintain for this title. Collectors on the forums still rate the Van Gelder-cut mono pressings of this recording well ahead of the contemporaneous stereo cut and of subsequent audiophile reissues.

Notes

This copy pairs a 1959-1962 Blue Note Records Inc./47 West 63rd label on one side with the later New York USA label, used from late 1961 to 1966, on the other. A mixed pair is dated by its newer label, so this copy was assembled no earlier than late 1961, placing it after the 1958 first pressing and, in the label sequence, after the 1959-1962 Inc./registered-mark repress. The runout still carries the RVG stamp, Plastylite ear and the 9M mark found on the first pressing, so the groove source is unchanged even though the paper labels came from two different printing runs. This is a mixed-label mono repress of the early-to-mid 1960s, not an original or first-era pressing. Vinyl grades VG with a VG+ cover.
