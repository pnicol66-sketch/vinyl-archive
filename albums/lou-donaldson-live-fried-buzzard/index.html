The Album

Lou Donaldson had already built a substantial reputation as a bebop-rooted alto saxophonist through his 1950s and early 1960s Blue Note work when this live set was captured over a weekend at the Bon Ton Club in Buffalo, New York, in August 1965. Cadet (the rebranded Argo, Chess Records' jazz imprint) shelved the tapes for several years, finally issuing them in 1970 as Donaldson's soul-jazz sound and reputation for crowd-pleasing, groove-driven playing had grown. The quintet, featuring the underrated trumpeter Bill Hardman, organist Billy Gardner, guitarist Warren Stephens and drummer Leo Morris (later known as Idris Muhammad), plays a mix of Donaldson originals like the title track and blues-drenched standards such as "Summertime" and "The Best Things in Life Are Free." The back cover's own liner notes frame the record as a deliberate rejection of over-produced studio jazz in favor of raw, spontaneous performance, complete with the crowd's reactions and Donaldson's between-song banter left intact. It captures Donaldson at a pivotal midpoint between his hard-bop roots and the funkier, boogaloo-inflected direction he would soon pursue on Blue Note albums like Alligator Bogaloo. While never as celebrated as his studio classics, the album is valued by soul-jazz collectors as an unvarnished document of a working club date and of Hardman's underappreciated horn work alongside Donaldson.

The Label

The label is the blue-fading-to-white "CADET" block-letter design with the pinwheel/starburst logo at the right and "MFG. BY CHESS PRODUCING CORP. CHICAGO, IL 60616" printed around the bottom rim - the first-era Cadet jazz-series label used from 1965-1970, which per the label's own catalogue run (LPS-758 to LPS-848) covers this LPS-842 issue. No deep groove or alternate address is present, and no later Cadet/Chess Records-era or GRT-era redesign is visible, so this is the standard and, so far as documented, only domestic label variant for this catalogue number. Its placement within that 1965-1970 block-letter window is consistent with the album's 1970 release date printed on the back cover credits.

Fidelity

This is a straightforward late-1960s Cadet (Chess) in-house production, mastered and mixed at Ter Mar Studios in Chicago rather than by an audiophile-grade engineer, and pressed via GRT-era Chess manufacturing; the recording itself is a single-microphone-era club live tape, so surface noise and modest tape hiss are part of the source rather than the pressing. No audiophile reissue (Analogue Productions, Music Matters, Speakers Corner, etc.) of this title exists, so this original Cadet stereo pressing is, by default, the top of its own fidelity hierarchy alongside any later CD reissue, which collectors on the forums generally regard as sonically comparable rather than superior.
