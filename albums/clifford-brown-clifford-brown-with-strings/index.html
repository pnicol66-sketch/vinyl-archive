The Album

Recorded over three January 1955 sessions at Fine Sound in New York, the album placed Clifford Brown at the center of a deliberately romantic standards project produced for EmArcy by Bob Shad. Neal Hefti's compact charts framed Brown's working group with a nine-piece string section, but the focus is almost entirely on Brown's melody reading, tone, and brief embellishments rather than hard-driving bop solos. The twelve-song program draws on Kern, Rodgers and Hart, Gershwin, Carmichael, and other canonical American songbook writers, effectively presenting Brown as an instrumental ballad singer. It came at the height of his partnership with Max Roach and only about eighteen months before Brown and pianist Richie Powell were killed in the June 1956 automobile accident that ended one of jazz's most promising careers. Alongside Charlie Parker's string sessions, it became one of the defining jazz-with-strings albums, demonstrating how Brown's warm, articulate trumpet could carry a record with almost no conventional blowing space. Critical opinion has long split over Hefti's decorous settings—AllMusic found them close to Muzak—but later assessments, notably All About Jazz, have praised the album as one of the most beautiful records in the form, with Stardust a particular peak.

The Label

This copy has the blue EmArcy Type A big-drummer label in silver, with a deep groove and curved LONG PLAYING HIGH FIDELITY / MERCURY RECORD CORPORATION / MADE IN U.S.A. rim text. EmArcy was Mercury's jazz imprint, and this big-drummer/high-fidelity blue label is the circa-1955 design, preceding the small-drummer and later Mercury EmArcy Jazz oval-label variants. The decisive jacket marker is the blue-printed back: Mercury used blue backs on original 12-inch issues through roughly February 1956, including MG-36000 through MG-36047, before switching to black backs. Because MG-36005 was a 1955 title, this blue-back/deep-groove/YMG-prefix combination is the first-issue configuration; the supplied YMG36005A transcription lacks the mother, stamper, and plant suffixes needed to prove the exact metal parts or pressing plant.

Fidelity

This is an original-era mono cut from the January 1955 Fine Sound session tape; neither the jacket nor the supplied matrix identifies the original cutting engineer. The incomplete runout transcription also prevents a firm mother, stamper, or pressing-plant attribution. A clean first US pressing is the fidelity benchmark, giving Brown's trumpet strong center-image presence and the small string group a deep, warm mono blend, though the recording favors richness over wide-bandwidth brilliance. Later 1950s drummer-label and Mercury EmArcy Jazz copies are serviceable period alternatives but sit below the blue-back first pressing in collector preference. The best documented modern vinyl alternative is Universal Japan's 2004 UCJU-9012, remastered from the original cutting-master tape by Joseph M. Palmaccio on 180g vinyl; no Tone Poet, Music Matters, Analogue Productions, or Speakers Corner edition of this title was found.
