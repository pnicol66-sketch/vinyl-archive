The Album

Recorded over three sessions in January 1955 at Fine Sound in New York, this project paired trumpeter Clifford Brown with a nine-piece string section arranged and conducted by Neal Hefti, produced for EmArcy by Bob Shad. Rather than showcasing the fiery bebop lines that had made his reputation, Brown here plays as a melodic balladeer, drawing on twelve songbook standards by Kern, Rodgers and Hart, Gershwin, and Carmichael among others, with his working rhythm section of Max Roach, Richie Powell, George Morrow, and guitarist Barry Galbraith supporting rather than soloing. The Label

This copy carries the blue EmArcy 'big-drummer' label in silver ink, showing the drummer pouring paint onto a cymbal logo once (not the later small-drummer redesign), a deep groove pressed near the label edge, and curved rim text reading LONG PLAYING HIGH FIDELITY / MERCURY RECORD CORPORATION / MADE IN U.S.A. This large-drummer, deep-groove EmArcy design is the label company's earliest 12-inch jazz-series look, used circa 1954-56 before Mercury shrank the drummer graphic and eventually moved to the EmArcy Jazz oval-logo label in 1958. Discogs collectors confirm the blue-printed (not black) back cover as the decisive first-issue marker for MG-36000-series titles through early 1956, and this copy's back cover print matches that blue-ink first state, placing it at the top of the pressing hierarchy for this title.

Fidelity

This is an original-era mono cut from the January 1955 Fine Sound session tape; neither the jacket nor the supplied matrix identifies the cutting engineer, and the incomplete runout transcription prevents a firm mother/stamper/plant attribution. A clean first US pressing remains the fidelity benchmark, delivering strong center-image presence for Brown's trumpet and a warm, if narrow-bandwidth, mono blend with the small string group. Later 1950s drummer-label and Mercury EmArcy Jazz oval-label copies are acceptable period alternatives but rank below this blue-back first pressing among collectors. The best-documented modern alternative is Universal Japan's 2004 UCJU-9012, remastered from the original cutting-master tape by Joseph M. Palmaccio on 180g vinyl; no Tone Poet, Music Matters, Analogue Productions, or Speakers Corner edition of this title exists.

Notes

1955 EmArcy MG-36005 mono - original US first pressing. The blue/silver big-drummer deep-groove labels and blue-printed back cover are the first-issue pairing; black backs mark later 1950s jackets. The typed early YMG36005A matrix supports that placement, though the supplied transcription omits complete mother/stamper and plant codes. Recorded January 18-20, 1955 and issued the same year.
