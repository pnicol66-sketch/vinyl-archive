The Album

Bags' Groove compiles two Miles Davis sessions from 1954: a December 24 date with vibraphonist Milt Jackson, pianist Thelonious Monk, Percy Heath and Kenny Clarke, and an earlier June 29 session with Sonny Rollins on tenor and Horace Silver on piano. The December date has become one of the most mythologized in jazz history, fueled by later accounts (some embellished by Davis himself) of tension between Davis and Monk over how the pianist should comp behind his trumpet solos. Whatever the truth of the friction, the results - two contrasting takes of Jackson's title tune - capture the loose, blues-soaked interplay that defined the era's small-group jazz. The Label

This copy carries the original yellow-and-black Prestige label with the "PRESTIGE RECORDS INC. \u2022 446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C." address and pronounced deep groove on both sides, the design and address Prestige used from the label's launch through its 1958 move to Bergenfield, New Jersey (per bsnpubs.com's Prestige label-history timeline). The hand-etched "RVG" in the runout (per the collector's transcription) plus the "AB" suffix identifying Abbey Record Manufacturing as the pressing plant are both consistent with Prestige's earliest custom-pressed runs of this catalog number. Placement of the 446 W. 50th St. address is the single clearest marker separating this first-pressing labeling from the visually similar but later Bergenfield-address second pressing, which also changed the side-1 title justification.

Fidelity

Mastered from Rudy Van Gelder's original Hackensack tapes and lacquers, with his hand-etched RVG stamp confirming a Van Gelder-cut original pressing rather than a later re-cut. Collectors on forums.stevehoffman.tv generally regard these mid-1950s Prestige/Van Gelder mono originals as warm but somewhat narrow-band by modern standards, a function of the era's mastering chain rather than pressing quality, with Abbey's pressings considered solid if unspectacular. Within the album's fidelity hierarchy this RVG/deep-groove 446 W. 50th St. original sits at the top for vintage collectors, ahead of the later Bergenfield-address repress and the 1960s-70s blue-label reissues, and ahead of the OJC reissue; the more recent Analogue Productions/Craft audiophile remaster from the original tapes is generally rated the most refined-sounding cut but is prized for different reasons (surface quiet, extended top end) rather than vintage authenticity.
