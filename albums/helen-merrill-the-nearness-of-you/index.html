The Album

Recorded in two separate sessions - December 1957 in Chicago with a working local rhythm section, and February 1958 in New York with an all-star sextet including Bill Evans, Bobby Jaspar, Oscar Pettiford and Jo Jones - The Nearness of You was Helen Merrill's fifth studio album and her final release on the EmArcy label before she relocated to Italy and later Japan. Merrill had already established herself with her acclaimed 1954 self-titled album (featuring Clifford Brown) as one of the most distinctive vocal stylists in jazz, prized for her breathy, intimate delivery and unconventional phrasing on standards. This record leans into a warmer, more relaxed mood than her earlier, more dramatic outings, pairing familiar songbook fare - Porter, Gershwin, Arlen, Kern - with a set of top-tier sidemen who give the date real jazz weight, particularly the New York quartet tracks where Evans's harmonic sensibility is already audible just before his rise to fame with Miles Davis. Critics have long treated Merrill as a singer's singer whose readings of standards prioritize mood and space over technical display, and this album is generally viewed as a solid, late-period entry in her formative EmArcy run rather than a landmark on the order of her 1954 debut. It remains valued chiefly for the rare studio pairing of Merrill with Evans and for its place capping her important, if commercially modest, late-1950s American output before her long expatriate period.

The Label

This copy carries the blue EmArcy label with the oval 'EmArcy' cursive logo and the silhouetted artist-drawing-a-circle graphic, rim text 'LONG PLAYING HIGH FIDELITY,' and 'MERCURY RECORD CORPORATION MADE IN U.S.A.' at the base - the design EmArcy used on its mono jazz line from its 1954 founding through the early 1960s. The presence of a pronounced deep groove on both sides is a hallmark of the earliest Mercury/EmArcy pressing runs before the label transitioned to flat-edge stampers around 1960. Combined with the '-1' first-stamper suffix in the matrix, this places the disc at or very near the original 1958 pressing rather than a later flat-edge repress, though the plain 'M' mastering mark (rather than an engineer's full name) leaves some ambiguity about which specific mastering pass this is.

Fidelity

This is an original-era EmArcy mono cut, mastered under Mercury's 'Sound Margin Control' custom high-fidelity process referenced on the jacket, with no indication of later remastering. Collectors generally regard early deep-groove EmArcy jazz vocal pressings as offering a warmer, more natural presentation than the label's later flat-edge repress era, though this title has not attracted significant audiophile reissue attention (no known Analogue Productions, Mobile Fidelity, or Classic Records cut exists). Within the album's limited pressing history, this deep-groove first-pressing mono copy sits at or near the top of the fidelity hierarchy, ahead of 1960s flat-edge reissues, 1980s-90s vinyl reissues, and the CD/SHM-CD editions.
