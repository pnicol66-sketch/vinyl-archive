The Album

Recorded for Blue Note in January 1961, Here 'Tis captures alto saxophonist Lou Donaldson at the moment he pivoted his hard-bop language toward the earthier, funkier feel that would define soul jazz, introducing New York audiences to two young sidemen he had discovered on tour, guitarist Grant Green and organist John "Baby Face" Willette. The set mixes originals steeped in gospel and blues vamping ("Here 'Tis," "Watusi Jump," "Walk Wid Me") with a bop-tinged reading of Charlie Parker's "Cool Blues" and a warm ballad treatment of "A Foggy Day," letting Donaldson show both his bebop pedigree and his growing commercial instincts. Green's clean, single-note guitar lines and Willette's churchy Hammond organ swing gave the date a groove-heavy, blues-drenched character that anticipated the organ-combo boom Blue Note would ride through the mid-1960s. Donaldson, a North Carolina-born altoist who came up through Charlie Parker's shadow before finding his own populist voice, used albums like this to bridge jazz's improvising rigor with dance-floor and jukebox appeal. The record helped launch Grant Green's own prolific Blue Note career and stands as a foundational document of the soul-jazz movement, still cited by critics and DJs as one of the genre's most durable and influential grooves.

The Label

Side 1 carries the "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC. \u00b7 47 WEST 63rd \u00b7 NYC" rim text used on Blue Note's true first-issue mono labels (1961-62), while Side 2 has already switched to the address-less "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC \u00b7 NEW YORK USA" rim text that Blue Note introduced as it phased out the street address, generally placed in the c.1962-66 window. Neither label shows a pressed-in deep groove, consistent with the flat-edge West 63rd-era stock rather than the earlier deep-groove Lexington Ave labels (1956-58). This side-to-side mismatch is a documented Blue Note phenomenon (plants exhausting old label stock on one side while drawing new stock for the other) and places this copy as a mono repress made from leftover early labels and new labels rather than the strict simultaneous-both-sides-63rd first pressing; it still carries the era-correct RVG/ear stampers, so it sits just behind the true firsts in the pressing hierarchy.

Fidelity

Cut by Rudy Van Gelder directly from the January 1961 session tapes and pressed at Blue Note's Plastylite plant (ear mark, "9M" etch), this mono pressing shares the same first-generation metalwork lineage as the strict first-issue copies, so its sonics are essentially indistinguishable from an original despite the mixed labels. Collectors on the forums generally rank any RVG/ear-stamped mono BLP 4066 well above the later stereo Liberty-era and Music Matters/United Artists reissues for warmth and presence, though slightly below a pristine simultaneous-both-sides-63rd copy in collector prestige (not necessarily sound). Modern audiophile remasters - the Analogue Productions 45rpm 2LP (Kevin Gray/Ron Rambach, mastered from tape) and the Blue Note Tone Poet mono LP (Kevin Gray) - are quieter and more resolved on modern equipment but are prized as separate, complementary listening options rather than replacements for the vintage RVG cut's tonal character.

Notes
