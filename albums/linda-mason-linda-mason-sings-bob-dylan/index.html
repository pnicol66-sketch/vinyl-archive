The Album

Linda Mason was a young Queens-born folksinger who had worked the Greenwich Village coffeehouse circuit, including stints at the Gaslight and Gerde's Folk City, before cutting this set of twelve Bob Dylan songs cut over two days at RCA Victor's Studio C in May 1964. Arriving just as Dylan himself was moving from topical protest songwriting into more personal territory, the album was conceived and marketed as one of the earliest full-length tribute records to his catalogue, pairing his then-recent political songs with his newer love ballads. Mason's clear, direct voice and the small folk-combo backing - featuring guitarist and arranger Walter Rain alongside session players including Al Gorgoni and a young John Sebastian on harmonica - aimed to present the material in a straightforward, unadorned folk-revival style rather than imitate Dylan's own phrasing. The record arrived at a moment when Dylan's songs were rapidly becoming standards covered across the folk and pop fields, and this LP stands as one of the first dedicated single-artist song-cycle tributes to his writing, offered to listeners drawn to the material before Dylan's own stylistic shift toward electric rock later that year. It received modest attention within the folk press of the period as an example of the growing cottage industry of Dylan interpretation, and it remains chiefly of historical interest today as an early marker of his songs' rapid absorption into the broader folk repertoire rather than as a landmark recording in its own right.

The Label

Side 1 carries an off-white Recording Industries Corp. label and Side 2 a tan label, both with the stacked RI/K shell logo, the Nashville and New York addresses, and a deep groove pressed into the vinyl on both sides; the Side 2 rim also repeats the full Recording Industries Corp. address block. The copyright line reads 1964, the year the company itself cut this title, so the pressing cannot predate that year. With no later pressing of catalogue number M 1005 turning up in the label's own history, this deep-groove copy sits as the issue-era pressing of the mono edition, with the ST 1005 stereo catalogue number standing as the companion release rather than a later variant of this same number.

Fidelity

The album was cut from a straightforward mono session tape recorded at RCA Victor's New York studio, with no mastering engineer credited on the jacket. There is no audiophile reissue of this title, so this original pressing is the only widely available cut, and collectors regard it mainly as a decent-sounding period folk recording rather than a sonic showcase.

Notes

This is the 1964 mono first pressing of M 1005. Both sides carry a deep groove and the Recording Industries Corp. label with the 1964 copyright date, and the runout numbers R-41501-A/B tie directly to the catalogue number. No alternate label design or later pressing of this mono catalogue number is known, placing this copy at the head of the pressing history for M 1005.
