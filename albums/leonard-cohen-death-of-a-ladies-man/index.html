The Album

Death Of A Ladies' Man paired Leonard Cohen's spare, literary songwriting with Phil Spector's dense, orchestral Wall of Sound production, an unlikely collaboration born when the two writers began trading songs in Los Angeles in the mid-1970s. The sessions, cut at Spector's favored Los Angeles studios, became notoriously fraught, with Spector reportedly taking control of the mixes and, by some accounts, barring Cohen from the studio during final mixing. The result was a jarring departure from the hushed folk settings of Cohen's earlier records, burying his voice beneath layered strings, horns, and choruses of backing singers. Arriving in Cohen's catalogue between the austere New Skin For The Old Ceremony and the stripped-down Recent Songs, it stands as the most sonically maximalist and most divisive record of his 1970s output. Contemporary critics were largely baffled or hostile, and Cohen himself later expressed deep ambivalence about the finished album, at one point saying he wished he could withdraw it. Over the decades it has undergone considerable critical reappraisal, with the title track and “Memories” now counted among the more compelling vocal performances of his career.

The Label

The side labels show the CBS orange-to-yellow gradient design with the white CBS wordmark and the eye-in-box logo above it, the double-headed stereo arrow at left, and the rim text naming CBS Inc. as the exclusive UK licensee - the standard CBS UK label format used through the mid-to-late 1970s. Both labels carry the ℗ 1977 CBS Inc. credit and MADE IN ENGLAND, placing this pressing within the label's 1977 UK release window for the title rather than any later reissue era. This is the UK CBS counterpart to the simultaneous US Warner Bros. Records issue (BS 3125) of the same November 1977 release, not a subsequent repressing with a redesigned label.

Fidelity

Engineered by Larry Levine under Phil Spector's production, the mix is famously thick and reverb-heavy, often cited by collectors as one of the murkier-sounding entries in Cohen's catalogue regardless of pressing. This UK CBS pressing, manufactured at CBS's own Aston Clinton plant, is generally regarded as solid but unremarkable fidelity-wise, neither notably better nor worse than the contemporaneous US Warner Bros. cut. No audiophile remastering or specialist reissue series exists for this title, so collectors largely treat original-era pressings, UK or US, as sonically equivalent, with differences in condition mattering more than mastering source.
