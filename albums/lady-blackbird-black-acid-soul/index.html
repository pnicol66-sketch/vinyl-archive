The Album

Black Acid Soul is the debut album of Lady Blackbird, the reborn performing identity of Los Angeles singer Marley Munroe, who had spent years as a journeyman vocalist under other names before producer Chris Seefried helped her recast herself in the mold of the great jazz-soul divas. Recorded largely live with a small combo, the record strips soul, jazz and blues down to voice, piano, double bass and brushed drums, placing Munroe's huge, weathered, Nina Simone-evoking instrument at the absolute center. The set leans on daring reinterpretation: Simone's civil-rights standard "Blackbird" opens as a statement of purpose, Irma Thomas's "Ruler Of My Heart" becomes a slow-burn showcase, and Tim Hardin's "It'll Never Happen Again" closes the cycle with devastating restraint, sitting alongside originals including the title track co-written with Seefried. Trombone Shorty contributes a trumpet cameo on the gospel-tinged "Nobody's Sweetheart," underlining the record's New Orleans soul undercurrent. Critical reception was enthusiastic on both sides of the Atlantic, with writers singling out Munroe's ability to sound simultaneously ancient and new, and the album quickly established Lady Blackbird as a major jazz-club and festival draw. It taps directly into the lineage of Simone, Billie Holiday and Esther Phillips while remaining unmistakably contemporary in its stark, minimalist production.

The Label

The labels are flat (no deep groove) matte black discs with the stacked white wordmark - "BLACK ACID SOUL" on Side A, "LADY BLACKBIRD" on Side B - Foundation and BMG logos at the base, and rim text reading "Manufactured in Germany, GEMA/BIEM" with the label code LC19813 and the barcode printed around the perimeter. Foundation Music Productions licensed the album to BMG Rights Management (UK) for European manufacture at Optimal Media, and the label code places this pressing no earlier than 1977 - not a meaningful constraint here since the record is a 2021/2022-era release in any case. This exact catalogue-numbered, printed-runout configuration corresponds to the wide-retail black-vinyl pressing that followed the initial limited colour-vinyl runs, distinguishing it from the etched FM0008 matrix found on those Bandcamp/Rough Trade exclusives.

Fidelity

Mastered by Bernie Grundman from Seth Atkins Horan's mixes, a high-end credit for an independent soul-jazz release whose spare voice-and-piano production plays to Grundman's strengths in warmth and midrange clarity. All vinyl variants of this title, black and coloured alike, share this same master, so the sonic signature is consistent across the family; the caveat collectors raise is pressing consistency rather than mastering, with surface noise and occasional warps reported from copy to copy regardless of colourway. No audiophile-tier reissue exists for this title, so the Grundman-mastered LP, in whichever colourway, remains the top vinyl tier currently available.
