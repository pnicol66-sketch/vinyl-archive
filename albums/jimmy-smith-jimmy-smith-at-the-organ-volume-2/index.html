This Pressing

Mono repress (c. 1961-1966) - a deep-groove 47 West 63rd St label on side 1 paired with a deep-groove New York, USA label on side 2, hand-etched RVG and Plastylite ear in both runouts.

The Album

Jimmy Smith arrived at Blue Note in 1956 as the artist who single-handedly made the Hammond B-3 a serious jazz instrument, and the label documented his explosive early development with an unusually dense run of trio and quartet sessions. This volume gathers additional cuts from the same prolific 1957 sessions that produced the first At the Organ album, pairing Smith with altoist Lou Donaldson, guitarists Kenny Burrell and Eddie McFadden, and drummers Art Blakey and Donald Bailey in shifting combinations. The material ranges from Smith's own blues-drenched originals to a brisk reading of Charlie Parker's Billie's Bounce, showing the group moving comfortably between bebop vocabulary and the greasy, gospel-inflected feel that would define soul jazz. The Duel is a stripped-down showcase for Smith and Blakey alone, a rhythmic sparring match that highlights both men's percussive attack. Smith's effortless command of the organ's registers and pedals, combined with the unforced swing of these small groups, helped cement the instrument's place in modern jazz and opened the door for the wave of organ combos that followed through the 1960s. Critics of the era praised the loose, after-hours intimacy of these dates, and the album remains a key document of Smith's foundational Blue Note period.

The Label

Side 1 carries a deep-groove blue-and-white label with the full street address, BLUE NOTE RECORDS · 47 WEST 63rd · NYC, and the BLUE NOTE wordmark in white on the left and lower blue panels. Side 2 carries a deep-groove label of the same blue-and-white family but with the shortened address BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC · NEW YORK USA and no street number. The 47 West 63rd St address was current from 1957 through the early 1960s, while the New York, USA address was introduced in late 1961 and remained in use for several years; a side bearing it is never earlier than that address's introduction, whatever the other side reads. The record therefore carries one label from each of two successive address eras, with the New York, USA side setting the floor for when this copy was assembled - late 1961 at the earliest. The catalogue number BLP 1552 prints identically on both sides, agreeing with the sleeve.

Fidelity

Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder directly from the session tapes, as the Plastylite ear and the hand-etched RVG in the runouts attest, and pressed before Blue Note's mid-1960s move away from deep-groove, Plastylite-pressed stock. Because one side carries the later New York, USA address, this copy is a repressing rather than the 1958 first issue, though its runouts carry the same hand-etched RVG and Plastylite ear as that first issue. It still outranks the later non-deep-groove Liberty-era pressings and the overseas reissues of this title in the fidelity hierarchy.

Notes

The two sides carry labels from two successive Blue Note address eras rather than a single print run: side 1 reads 47 West 63rd, NYC, an address current from 1957 into the early 1960s, while side 2 reads the shortened New York, USA address, which was not introduced until late 1961. A record carrying a New York, USA label is never earlier than that address's introduction, so this copy was assembled no earlier than late 1961, placing it among the repressings of this catalogue number rather than among the original 1958 issue. Both sides retain the deep groove, the hand-etched RVG and the Plastylite ear, marking it as a Plastylite-pressed copy from before the mid-1960s changeover to non-deep-groove, non-Plastylite production.
