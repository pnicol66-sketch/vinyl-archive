The Album

You Want It Darker is Leonard Cohen's fourteenth and final studio album, written and largely recorded at his home in Los Angeles while he was in failing health, and released just weeks before his death in November 2016. The record continues the spare, incantatory songwriting of his late-career trilogy with producer and co-writer Patrick Leonard, built around Cohen's deep spoken-sung baritone set against hushed keyboards, strings and choir rather than full-band arrangement. The title track folds in a cantor and a synagogue choir, turning Cohen's lifelong engagement with Jewish liturgy and prayer into an explicit farewell meditation on mortality and surrender. Elsewhere the album revisits recurring Cohen themes of love, faith, and reckoning with age and death, delivered with unusual directness for an artist long associated with elliptical imagery. Critics received the album as a stark, unflinching final statement, frequently ranking it among the strongest work of his career and a fitting capstone to a songwriting life devoted to confronting large questions in plain language. Its release amid his declining health and death shortly after cemented its status as a deliberate and clear-eyed artistic farewell, discussed ever since as one of the most moving closing statements in popular song.

The Label

The side labels show a grey/taupe photographic ground with cream and white print and the boxed Columbia "eye" logo set beside the spindle hole, the rim carrying the 2016 Sony Music Entertainment and Old Ideas, LLC copyright lines, the Columbia trademark notice, and the catalogue numbers 88985365071 and C-176553 with the speed and channel marking "33 1/3 RPM STEREO." This is the Sony Music Entertainment-era Columbia box-eye design used across the label's catalogue from 1991 onward, so the design itself only confirms a post-1991 pressing. The label's own 2016 copyright lines fix this copy to the album's release year window and place it among the first group of Columbia LP pressings of the title, with the audiophile 180-gram reissues and the limited colored-vinyl variants standing apart from it.

Fidelity

The album was mastered at Marcussen Mastering and plated at Record Technology Incorporated, a combination well regarded among collectors for its clean, well-balanced vinyl cuts of modern major-label releases. Collectors on the forums generally rate this standard black-vinyl pressing as a solid, quiet-sounding standard edition, with the limited colored-vinyl and 180-gram variants prized mainly for their rarity and packaging rather than for any meaningfully superior cut. There is no audiophile remaster of this title from a specialist reissue series, so this pressing and the contemporaneous 180-gram vinyl issues represent the full range of the album's fidelity options.
