The Album

Concorde was recorded on July 2, 1955 in New York and marks the first Modern Jazz Quartet album to feature drummer Connie Kay, who replaced founding drummer Kenny Clarke that year and remained with the group for decades. Under John Lewis's musical direction, the quartet had already begun distinguishing itself from typical bebop combos by favoring contrapuntal writing, chamber-music restraint, and classically-informed forms, and Concorde deepened that identity with its title track, a Lewis-composed fugue that stands among the era's boldest fusions of baroque counterpoint and swinging jazz. The program balances originals like Milt Jackson's blues-based opener with standards, a show-tune-derived Gershwin medley, and a reworking of a Sigmund Romberg theme, letting Jackson's bluesy vibraphone lines play off Lewis's more austere, architectural piano voicings. The album helped cement the MJQ's reputation as a group capable of appealing to both jazz and classical-leaning listeners without sacrificing swing, a balancing act that would define their long career together. It arrived during a banner year for the group, who won critics' polls and played prestigious venues and festivals, cementing their position among the era's leading small jazz ensembles. Concorde remains a touchstone early statement of the MJQ's 'third stream' aesthetic and a key document of Connie Kay's integration into the band's sound.

The Label

This copy carries the original yellow Prestige label with black print, the arrow/star/spiral 'constellation' rim graphics, and the 446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C. address printed under the Prestige logo, matching Prestige's first NYC label design used from the label's founding through 1957 (bsnpubs/Discogs timeline). Deep groove is present on both sides per the collector's vinyl grading. This address and label design places the pressing in the earliest issue window for PRLP 7005, released in 1955 shortly after the July 2, 1955 recording session, well before Prestige's move to Bergenfield, NJ in 1958. The all-caps musician-credit line matches the Discogs 'variant 1' etched-runout pressing rather than the later stamped-runout or Bergenfield-address copies.

Fidelity

Engineered by Rudy Van Gelder at his Hackensack studio and mastered by him as well, this early Prestige pressing carries the direct-to-lacquer RVG stamp collectors prize for warmth and immediacy on MJQ's chamber-jazz sound. Prestige's mono pressings from this 1955-57 NYC-address era are generally well regarded by Steve Hoffman forum posters for natural tonal balance, though surface noise and pressing consistency can vary more than contemporaneous Blue Note pressings. This deep-groove 446 W. 50th St. copy sits at the top of the fidelity hierarchy for Concorde, ahead of later Bergenfield-address Prestige pressings, the 1965-70s Prestige trident-label reissues, the 1982 OJC reissue (cut from tape, competent but a generation removed), and the 2008 Rudy Van Gelder Remasters CD, which is convenient but not vinyl-sourced.
