The Album

Concorde was recorded in a single session on July 2, 1955 at Rudy Van Gelder's Hackensack studio and is the first Modern Jazz Quartet album to feature Connie Kay, who replaced founding drummer Kenny Clarke after Clarke left New York to freelance in Europe. Kay's background in rhythm-and-blues and swing settings, alongside Lester Young, Stan Getz and Coleman Hawkins, gave John Lewis the quiet, precisely shaded pulse his writing wanted, and Kay would stay with the group for nearly forty years. Under Lewis's direction the quartet had already begun moving away from the standard bebop combo model toward counterpoint, chamber-scale dynamics and classically derived forms, and the title track, a Lewis fugue with improvised episodes, is one of the boldest attempts of the period to make baroque procedure genuinely swing. The Label

Both side-label photos show the yellow Prestige "fireworks/constellation" design used continuously from 1955 to mid-1958 - spiral upper left, starburst upper right, PRESTIGE flag at top, LONG PLAYING MICROGROOVE arc at foot, rim reading PRESTIGE RECORDS INC. \u2022 446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C. - and this address/artwork is identical on both the collector's 1955-style copy and this one, so the label design alone cannot separate the two issues. What does separate them is the front cover and the label typography: this copy carries the SECOND cover design, the all-text "typewriter" jacket repeating "concorde / the modern jazz quartet / prestige 7005" (Discogs dates this cover to 1957, cf. release 5287797), replacing the 1955 obelisk-photograph cover with "Prestige / LP 7005" in red. Fidelity

Recorded and lacquer-cut by Rudy Van Gelder, with RVG etched in both runouts, so this copy still plays from Van Gelder-mastered metal with the close, dry mono balance suited to the MJQ's chamber textures. However side B is a later, Abbey-stamped 4th-cut lacquer rather than the earliest cut documented on the 1955 issue, and Abbey Record Manufacturing's pressing quality in the later 1950s was generally considered a notch below the earliest Prestige NYC pressings on collector forums (Steve Hoffman consensus on mid/late-1950s Prestige). In the album's fidelity hierarchy this typewriter-cover, all-caps-label copy sits below the obelisk-cover 1955 first issue, above the later Bergenfield-address pressings and the 1960s trident reissues, and well above the 1982 OJC-002 and the digital Van Gelder Remaster CD, neither of which has displaced the NYC-era RVG cuts.

Notes

Prestige PRLP 7005, mono, US, c. 1957 - this copy is the SECOND issue, identified by the "typewriter" text front cover (Discogs 5287797, dated 1957) rather than the 1955 obelisk-photo cover, and by the ALL-CAPS personnel line on the labels (matching Discogs 11801829, pressed by Abbey Record Manufacturing Co.). The 446 W. 50th ST. yellow fireworks label design and deep groove are shared with the 1955 first issue and do not by themselves distinguish pressings. The runouts read PRLP-7005-A-1 with a stamped A, and PRLP-7005-B-4 with an Abbey stamp - a recut, later side-B lacquer (4th cut) versus the earlier A-1/B (no stamp) pairing on the collector's first-issue copy. Verdict: a genuine NYC-era RVG pressing but a second-issue configuration, ranking below the 1955 first issue.
