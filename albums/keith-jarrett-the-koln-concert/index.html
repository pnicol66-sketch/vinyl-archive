The Album

Recorded live at the Cologne Opera House on January 24, 1975, this fully improvised solo piano concert became the best-selling solo album in jazz history and the best-selling piano album of any genre. Jarrett arrived exhausted and in pain after a sleepless drive, on an ill-prepared, out-of-tune rehearsal piano the promoter had substituted for the requested Bösendorfer, and nearly cancelled before ECM producer Manfred Eicher persuaded him to go on. Working around the instrument's thin upper register and weak bass, Jarrett built a continuous, hymn-like improvisation of ostinato vamps, gospel-tinged melody and rolling left-hand patterns that transcended the piano's limitations. The performance sits at the center of Jarrett's mid-1970s emergence as ECM's flagship solo artist, following his earlier Facing You and preceding the extended Sun Bear Concerts. The Label

Both labels photographed show the standard green ECM Records design (white ECM logo box top centre, 'RECORDS' beneath it, rim text 'DISTRIBUTED BY POLYDOR INCORPORATED, NEW YORK, N.Y.' running the full lower arc, no additional ring/arc band) with catalogue number ECM 1064/65 and 'STEREO' at left. Deep groove: not seen - flat label. This green Polydor-distributed design was used across the 1975-76 US pressing runs at both plants that pressed this title (Terre Haute and Pitman), so the label photo alone cannot assign this copy to either plant - that distinction rests on the etched plant letter and stamper suffix in the deadwax, which the collector's typed runout (ST-ECM 1064-65-A/B/C/D) does not record. The Discogs release matched by catalogue number lists a Terre Haute pressing with a 'T' etch, STERLING stamp and stamper suffix in its runout, none of which appear in this copy's transcription; absent those marks the plant cannot be confirmed as Terre Haute, and the copy is placed only as a 1975 US pressing on the green Polydor-era label, not on a specific plant's line. No collector-recorded pressing tells (deep groove, promo, eye count, or other) are noted for this copy.

Fidelity

Mastered/cut at Sterling Sound (the Discogs-matched Terre Haute copy carries a STERLING stamp) from the original session tapes; US pressings from this era (Terre Haute or Pitman) are generally regarded by collectors as solid, quiet 1970s vinyl, though opinions on forums.stevehoffman.tv vary on which US plant edges out. This original US stereo pressing ranks above later 1980s-90s reissues and the CD, and is generally considered comparable to or slightly ahead of contemporaneous German pressings in transparency, though many collectors prize the 2015-era 180g audiophile reissue for the quietest surfaces at some cost to the immediacy of the original cut.

Notes

1975 US ECM 1064/65 stereo issue - green Polydor-distributed labels and Sterling Sound-cut Terre Haute deadwax per the matched Discogs release, though the collector's simplified runout transcription (ST-ECM 1064-65-A/B/C/D) omits the plant etch, stamper suffix and STERLING stamp that would confirm Terre Haute over the concurrent Pitman pressing of the same catalogue number. Jacket back carries the 810 Seventh Avenue/Polydor Incorporated address and 1975 ℗/© lines consistent with a first-year US pressing. Label design (flat, no deep groove) matches the standard 1975-era US ECM design used across both plants, so it cannot itself distinguish the two.
