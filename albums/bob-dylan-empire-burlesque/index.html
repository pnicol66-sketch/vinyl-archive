The Album

Empire Burlesque arrived in 1985 as Bob Dylan's first fully secular studio album of new songs since his late-1970s conversion to Christianity, recorded across several Los Angeles and New York studios with a rotating cast of session players and guitarists including Mick Taylor and Mike Campbell. Dylan brought in dance and hip-hop producer Arthur Baker to help mix and sweeten several tracks, giving the record a glossy, reverb-heavy mid-1980s studio sheen that set it apart sonically from his earlier work. The songs range from the romantic blues of “Tight Connection to My Heart” and the yearning “Emotionally Yours” to the extended, almost cinematic “When the Night Comes Falling from the Sky,” closing with the stark, solo acoustic “Dark Eyes,” recorded live in a single take with none of the album's studio polish. Critics at the time were divided, praising Dylan's songwriting and vocal performances while questioning the contemporary production choices layered over them, and the record has since been reassessed as a transitional, oddly compelling entry bridging his born-again years and his later elder-statesman period. It stands as a snapshot of an artist still restlessly reinventing his sound in the middle of a long and shifting career, and “Dark Eyes” in particular is frequently singled out by critics and fans as one of the quieter high points of his 1980s output.

The Label

Both sides carry the pink-red Columbia label of the mid-1970s-into-80s design, with the yellow rim lettering, the repeating ‘walking eye’ logos between the COLUMBIA words, and the ℗ 1985 CBS Inc. line printed on the label itself - a design CBS/Columbia used across this whole era, so it alone only narrows the copy to a CBS Inc.-era pressing rather than any specific plant. The 1985 copyright line on the label is the one date the copy cannot predate: this is a pressing from 1985 or later, never earlier, whatever any other feature might otherwise suggest. The label is a flat label, with no deep groove ring present on either side. The catalogue number FC 40110 / AL 40110 (side 1) and FC 40110 / BL 40110 (side 2) printed on the labels match the US stereo LP issue of this title.

Fidelity

The lacquers were cut at Sterling Sound in New York, with the runout etchings naming that facility on both sides; one later-sale description of this catalogue number specifically names mastering engineer Lee Hulko on an early stamper. Collectors generally regard the album's mid-1980s production - built around dance-era remix techniques and heavy studio reverb - as the record's defining sonic character rather than a pressing fault, and reactions to the sound are tied more to that production style than to any particular cut. There is no widely regarded audiophile reissue of this title that collectors rank above the original US pressing; this domestic stereo copy represents the standard first-year sound of the album.

Notes

This is a 1985 US stereo pressing of Empire Burlesque on Columbia FC 40110. The labels are the pink-red Columbia design with repeating walking-eye logos and a ℗ 1985 CBS Inc. line printed directly on the label, which rules out any pressing earlier than 1985. The runouts read P-AP STERLING AL.40110-1A on side 1 and P-AP STERLING BL.40110-1A on side 2, with a Ⓗ mark on both sides; the P prefix and the Sterling Sound mastering credit point to a Columbia-manufactured domestic pressing of this catalogue number. Nothing in the labels or runouts points to any territory other than the US.
