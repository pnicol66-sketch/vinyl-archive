This Pressing

1972 US Chess promotional pressing (Not For Sale stamp), Sterling-cut stereo lacquer, standard GRT-era flat white label.

The Album

Cut in the early 1970s as Bo Diddley sought to reconnect with a rock audience that had rediscovered him through the British Invasion bands who covered his songs, the record paired the pioneer with producers Johnny Otis and Pete Welding for a session steeped in funk and blues-rock rather than the stripped-down Chess sound of the 1950s. Diddley, already a foundational figure whose signature rhythm underpins much of rock and roll's DNA, used the album to stretch into contemporary soul-funk textures alongside his familiar guitar attack. Shuggie Otis's guest guitar work and Johnny Otis's involvement tie the record to the wider Los Angeles R&B scene of the period, while backing vocalists and a horn-less, groove-driven band give several tracks a loose, jam-session feel. Songs like "Bad Trip" and "Bo Diddley-Itis" extend his self-mythologizing streak into a new decade, while "Take It All Off" and "A Good Thing" lean toward the raunchier funk vein popular in early-70s soul. The album did not match the commercial impact of his 1950s and 1960s Chess singles, but it has since been reassessed by blues and funk collectors as an interesting late-period document of an innovator adapting to a changing musical landscape. Its striking die-cut, fold-out cover art became one of the more collectible visual packages of Diddley's catalog, reinforcing the record's cult standing among genre enthusiasts.

The Label

The label on this copy is the white Chess design used in the GRT-distribution era: the swirled sunburst/fan "eye" logo at left, the block CHESS wordmark beneath it, and "PROMOTION COPY / NOT FOR SALE" stamped in the lower-left field with a 1972 GRT Corp. copyright line - no colour rim band or ring text is present. By 1971-72 Chess had passed to General Recorded Tape (GRT), and its white GRT-era label replaced the earlier 1960s design; this copy carries the promotion stamp on that standard label. It is a flat label, typical of pressings from this period.

Fidelity

The original-era cut, mastered at Sterling Sound by Bob Ludwig ("RL" in the side one runout).

Notes

This is a 1972 promotional (Not For Sale) copy of the US Chess stereo pressing: a white GRT-era label with the promotion stamp, a Sterling "RL" mastering credit in the side one runout and a plain Sterling stamp on side two - the original-era cut, not a later reissue. The label is flat, as pressings of this period are, and the die-cut fold-out jacket is the title's original cover design.
