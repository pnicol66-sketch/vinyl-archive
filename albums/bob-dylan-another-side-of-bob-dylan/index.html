The Album

Recorded in a single marathon overnight session in June 1964, this was Bob Dylan's fourth album and the one in which he turned away from the topical protest songs that had made his name toward frankly personal, surreal and romantic writing. Gone are the finger-pointing anthems of the previous record; in their place are confessional break-up songs, stream-of-consciousness humor, and the extended meditation on youth and certainty that is its centerpiece. The album was made with just Dylan, his acoustic guitar, piano and harmonica, no band, a stripped-down sound that threw the words into sharp relief. Critics and fellow songwriters immediately recognized a leap in ambition and craft, and several of its songs were quickly covered by other artists and became hits in their own right, carrying Dylan's new style to a far wider audience than his own records reached. The record marked the moment Dylan's peers in the folk world began to see him moving beyond protest music into a more literary, interior mode of songwriting that would shape the folk-rock explosion the following year. It remains regarded as a pivotal hinge point in his 1960s work, the record that bridges the finger-pointing Dylan of his earlier albums and the electric, imagistic Dylan who followed.

The Label

The side labels on this copy carry a red field with the word COLUMBIA repeated in yellow all the way around the rim, each repetition flanked by a small logo box, with STEREO printed in plain black type at the lower left and no '360 SOUND' banner anywhere on the label. That all-perimeter COLUMBIA treatment is the label design the company phased in on reissues from the 1970s onward, replacing the earlier red label that carried just two logo boxes at the 9 and 3 o'clock positions flanking a '360 SOUND' band around the word STEREO. The catalogue number printed on the label itself reads JC 8993, while the back cover's promotional box instead shows KCS 8993 - the K-prefix is the mark Columbia added to continuously-available back-catalogue titles rather than evidence of a 1964 release. Together these place this copy as a later repressing run of the stereo edition, not the original 1964 issue.

Fidelity

The stereo master used on this copy is the same XSM 77044/77045 mastering lineage carried on the original 1964 stereo release, recut for a later pressing generation rather than freshly remastered. Collectors generally rate the original Pitman-pressed two-eye stereo pressing as the most desirable-sounding vintage cut of this title, with this later repressing sitting below it in the fidelity hierarchy though still playing from the same lacquer family. Specialist audiophile remasters were not made of this title in the heyday reissue series, so the modern 180-gram Legacy vinyl edition is the main alternative for a quiet, in-print pressing, ranking alongside rather than above the best original stereo copies for tonal character.

Notes

This is a later stereo pressing of the album, not an original 1964 copy. The side labels carry the red all-round COLUMBIA perimeter design that replaced the two-eye '360 Sound' label from the 1970s onward, and the back cover's promotional box shows the K-prefixed KCS 8993 number Columbia used on its long-available back catalogue rather than the plain CS 8993 of the original release. The runout suffixes transcribed for this copy also differ from the stamper codes recorded for the original Pitman-pressed stereo release, and no Pitman plant etch is noted. The back cover carries a printed 'FOR PROMOTION ONLY' box reserving ownership to CBS.
