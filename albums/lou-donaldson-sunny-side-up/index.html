The Album

Recorded at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio in February 1960, Sunny Side Up finds alto saxophonist Lou Donaldson at the height of his classic Blue Note run, backed by a hard-swinging quintet featuring trumpeter Bill Hardman and pianist Horace Parlan. Donaldson had already established himself as one of the label's most reliably soulful, blues-drenched voices on alto, a stylistic heir to Charlie Parker who leaned toward gutbucket funk and R&B-inflected phrasing rather than the harmonic exploration favored by some contemporaries. The album mixes original blues lines like the title-adjacent burner "Goose Grease" and "The Truth" with warmly played standards such as "The Man I Love" and "Softly As In A Morning Sunrise," giving the record a relaxed, jukebox-friendly appeal that was a hallmark of Donaldson's late-1950s/early-1960s output. Parlan, fresh from his stint with Charles Mingus, brings a percussive, blues-rooted comping style that meshes naturally with Donaldson's earthy attack, while Hardman's brassy trumpet adds bite to the front line. The date came at a pivotal moment for Blue Note as it moved further into the hard bop and soul-jazz idiom that would define much of its 1960s catalog. Critically, the album has long been regarded as a solid, unpretentious entry in Donaldson's discography, prized by collectors more for its groove and its stellar sidemen than for any single iconic composition. Its enduring appeal lies precisely in that unfussy consistency, a hallmark of Donaldson's decades-long career as one of jazz's most durable and danceable saxophonists.

The Label

The Side 1 label is the generic 'Classic Records / Test Pressing' design (blue banner logo, blue rim text 'CLASSIC RECORDS' arcing over 'TEST PRESSING', hand-inked catalogue and date) used across the Classic Records/Blue Note Signature Mono Series test runs beginning in the early-2000s; Side 2 carries no printed information at all, only a faint fingerprint smudge, which is typical of these one-sided test stampers. The hand-written '4-24-08' and 'BN 4036 A/B' on the label match the printed sticker on the blank jacket ('BN-4036-200G, A/B, 4/24/2008, BSCR'), tying this pressing directly to the commercially issued 2008 Classic Records mono reissue of Blue Note BLP 4036 (matrix suffix 'BG' for mastering engineer Bernie Grundman), confirmed on Discogs release r2286517. Because it predates the commercial pressing run and carries no printed cover art (a plain white die-cut jacket rather than the Reid Miles design), this copy sits outside the normal issue/reissue hierarchy as a pre-release quality-control artifact rather than a numbered pressing.

Fidelity

The 2008 Classic Records mono reissue was remastered and cut by Bernie Grundman from the analog master tapes onto Classic's 200-gram Quiex SV-P vinyl, pressed by Bill Smith Custom Pressing; collector consensus (Steve Hoffman forum discussions of the Classic Blue Note Signature Mono series) rates these Grundman/Classic mono cuts very highly, generally ranking just behind an excellent original RVG/Plastylite pressing but ahead of later Blue Note reissues such as the Liberty/UA-era or standard Music Matters stereo-only counterparts for this mono-only title. As a test pressing cut from the same lacquers/stampers as the commercial run, this copy should sound identical to a standard-issue 2008 Classic copy, with any difference limited to quality-control variance rather than mastering; it has not been directly compared on audiophile forums against the more recent 2022 Blue Note Classic Vinyl Series 180g reissue, which uses a different, more recent remaster.

Notes

This is not an original Blue Note pressing but a 2008 Classic Records test pressing of their mono 200-gram reissue of BLP 4036 (matrix BN-LP4036-A/B BG, 'BG' for remastering engineer Bernie Grundman), confirmed against Discogs release r2286517 for the commercial BN 4036-MONO-200G issue. The generic blank white jacket with a typed identification sticker ('BN-4036-200G, A/B, 4/24/2008, BSCR'), the one-sided printed 'Classic Records Test Pressing' label matching the hand-written date, and the completely blank Side 2 label are all hallmarks of a pre-release quality-control pressing rather than a numbered commercial copy. As such it predates and stands outside the standard original-vs-reissue hierarchy for this title; it is not comparable in rarity or desirability terms to either the 1960 originals or the retail 2008 Classic Records copies.
