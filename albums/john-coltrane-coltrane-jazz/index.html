This Pressing

Early mono pressing (1961) - red/purple Atlantic 'fan' label, deep groove, 157 West 57 St back-cover address places it at the front of the pressing run.

The Album

Coltrane Jazz gathers material Coltrane recorded for Atlantic in late 1959 and 1960 while he was simultaneously finishing his tenure with Miles Davis's sextet and about to form his own classic quartet, making it something of a transitional document between the Giant Steps harmonic experiments and the modal explorations to come. The bulk of the album pairs Coltrane with his working rhythm section of the period, pianist Wynton Kelly, bassist Paul Chambers and drummer Jimmy Cobb - all Miles Davis sidemen - while one track, 'Village Blues,' previews the personnel (McCoy Tyner, Steve Davis, Elvin Jones) that would soon become his permanent quartet. The record mixes standards like 'Little Old Lady' and 'My Shining Hour' with originals such as 'Harmonique,' on which Coltrane experiments with self-generated multiphonics, and 'Fifth House,' built on the changes of 'Hot House.' Released in 1961 though drawn from earlier sessions, it arrived as Coltrane's popular and critical stock was rising rapidly following Giant Steps, and it further established him as Atlantic's flagship jazz artist. Critics have long viewed it as a lower-key, more relaxed date than its landmark neighbors, valuable for showing Coltrane's hard-bop fluency and compositional range just before he pivoted toward the modal and spiritual directions of My Favorite Things and A Love Supreme. It remains an important, if sometimes underrated, entry in the run of albums that cemented Coltrane's reputation as the decade's most consequential saxophonist.

The Label

The side labels show the classic Atlantic red-over-magenta 'fan'/pinwheel logo design with the small catalogue number (1354) and the recording-department numbers (11697)/(11698) printed at the bottom - this is the early-1960s Atlantic mono label scheme, distinct from the blue/green fan-logo labels Atlantic used for the contemporaneous SD-1354 stereo pressing. Atlantic's mono series kept this red/purple design into the early 1960s before the black/multicolor 'swirl' label took over circa 1963-64. Deep groove. The back cover carries the '157 West 57 Street' address and the liner notes' second column begins 'Before I go into detail,' the tell for the earliest issue of this title, before Atlantic's rear-sleeve address moved to Broadway on a slightly later repress - placing this copy at the front of the mono pressing run.

Fidelity

This is an original-era Atlantic mono cut, likely mastered from the analog session tapes at Atlantic's own facility (Tom Dowd/Phil Iehle engineered) rather than a later remaster; collectors on the forums generally regard these early-1960s Atlantic mono pressings as full-bodied and dynamic, arguably preferable to the contemporaneous stereo mix for this particular record. Within the album's fidelity hierarchy this deep-groove, early-address mono pressing ranks near the top of the vintage originals, ahead of later Broadway-address mono/stereo represses and well ahead of the various later reissues (Japanese pressings, 1970s-80s Atlantic repress, 2010s reissues); it has not received a dedicated audiophile remaster program comparable to Blue Note's Tone Poet series.
