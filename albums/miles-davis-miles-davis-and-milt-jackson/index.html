The Album

Recorded on August 5, 1955 at Rudy Van Gelder's Hackensack studio and issued by Bob Weinstock's Prestige label in August 1956, this session paired Miles Davis with vibraphonist Milt Jackson for a one-off "all-star" date that used none of Davis's regular working-group members of the period. The billed "quintet/sextet" distinction refers to the addition of a young Jackie McLean, who sits in on alto sax for his own two compositions, "Dr. Jackle" and "Minor March," while the remaining tracks feature the core quintet of Davis, Jackson, pianist Ray Bryant, bassist Percy Heath, and drummer Arthur Taylor. The record arrived during Davis's prolific mid-1950s run of Prestige sessions that preceded his signing to Columbia, capturing him at a pivotal moment just before he assembled his classic quintet with John Coltrane. Thad Jones's "Bitty Ditty" and Bryant's blues-based closer "Changes" round out a compact, blowing-session program that showcases the easy interplay between Davis's understated lyricism and Jackson's fluid, blues-drenched vibraphone lines. The album has long been valued by hard-bop collectors as an early, informal document of two major voices of the era working together outside their usual bandstands, and it remains a minor but respected entry in Davis's vast Prestige discography, later reissued under the alternate title "Odyssey!" and as part of the Original Jazz Classics reissue series.

The Label

This copy carries the earliest Prestige label design for PRLP 7034: yellow labels with the black curved "Prestige" fireworks graphic, deep-groove ring stamped into both sides, and the "Prestige Records Inc. • 446 W. 50th St., N.Y.C." address arced around the rim. Prestige used the 446 W. 50th Street NYC address from its founding through 1957, after which the company relocated to Bergenfield, New Jersey and the labels were updated to reflect that address (a change documented on bsnpubs' Prestige label-history timeline). Deep-groove pressing plus the NYC address places this copy squarely in the first-pressing manufacturing run made at the time of the album's original August 1956 release, ahead of the later Bergenfield-address and subsequent silver/black-label repressings.

Fidelity

Mastered by Rudy Van Gelder directly from his own Hackensack session tape, this deep-groove NYC-address pressing represents the closest generation to the original 1955 masters and is the cut most prized by Prestige collectors and Steve Hoffman forum posters for its warmth and presence, though period Prestige pressings can vary in surface quality plant-to-plant. It ranks above the later Bergenfield-address and silver-label Prestige repressings, the 1968 "Odyssey!" reprocessed-stereo reissue, and the OJC vinyl/CD reissues, none of which carry the same first-generation Van Gelder lacquer; there is no widely noted audiophile (Analogue Productions/Music Matters-style) remaster of this specific title to compare against.
