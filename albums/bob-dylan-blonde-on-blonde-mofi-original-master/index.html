The Album

Blonde on Blonde closed the electric trilogy Dylan began with Bringing It All Back Home and Highway 61 Revisited, and it was dragged into being across two cities. Sessions with the Hawks at Columbia's New York studios through late 1965 and January 1966 yielded only One of Us Must Know (Sooner Or Later) in usable form, and on producer Bob Johnston's urging Dylan moved to Nashville in February and March 1966, bringing Robbie Robertson and Al Kooper into a room of crack Music Row players led by Charlie McCoy and Kenny Buttrey. Those musicians waited through the night while Dylan wrote at the studio piano, then cut the songs almost immediately - Sad Eyed Lady of the Lowlands, written in the small hours, ran past eleven minutes and took a whole side on its own. Dylan later described what he was after as that thin, that wild mercury sound, and the Nashville band's loose, lyrical swing behind the organ and harmonica is exactly that. Rainy Day Women #12 & 35, cut with the players swapping instruments for a Salvation Army-band shamble, became a hit despite radio nerves over its refrain, while Visions of Johanna and Just Like a Woman stand among the finest songs he has written. As rock's first studio double album, it set a scale and an ambition the form had not attempted, and it closed the 1965-66 run that ended with his motorcycle accident that July. It is routinely placed at or near the top of the album canon and remains the record by which Dylan's mid-sixties peak is measured.

The Label

Each of the six labels is black with a gold-brown band round the upper rim reading ORIGINAL MASTER RECORDING®, the MoFi script logo at the top centre, a single boxed Columbia “walking eye” logo at the left beneath the catalogue line, the GAIN 2™ ULTRA ANALOG logo at the foot and a double-headed STEREO arrow below it; the type is silver-white and the labels are flat, with no groove ring visible. The catalogue line reads MFSL 3-45009 with the side number and 45 RPM, and the side-letter suffixes 88697948091A to 886979480915 run across the six sides. The small print carries the Sony Music Entertainment address at 550 Madison Avenue, New York, NY 10022-3211, in use since the 1991 Sony renaming, and a ℗/© 1966, 2013 Columbia Records line: the 2013 date is printed on the record itself, so this is a 2013-or-later pressing, licensed from Sony and manufactured for Mobile Fidelity. This is the first, 2013 issue of the Special Limited Edition box, hand-numbered 013338; the later repress of the same number carries a different box and label design. It is an audiophile reissue of a 1966 Columbia album and sits below every 1966 Columbia and CBS pressing in the chronology.

Fidelity

Cut by Krieg Wunderlich with Shawn R. Britton at Mobile Fidelity in Sebastopol on the GAIN 2 Ultra Analog System, the chain running from the 1/2-inch 30 IPS analog remix master through DSD 64 and an analog console to the lathe, with plating and 180g pressing by Record Technology. Spreading the album over six 45 RPM sides buys wide, shallow-modulation grooves and the quietest surfaces the title has had: collectors rate it the most open and transparent Blonde on Blonde on vinyl, with the bass fuller and the Nashville room deeper than any Columbia pressing shows. The DSD stage keeps it off the all-analogue list, and listeners who want the 1966 balance unretouched still reach for a Columbia stereo original or the mono. Against the Sundazed mono reissue it is a different proposition entirely - stereo, wide-groove and far quieter - and it remains the reference stereo cut of the record.

Notes

2013 Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab MFSL 3-45009 stereo - the Special Limited Edition numbered 180g 45 RPM three-LP box, copy 013338, in its original box with booklet. The labels print the Sony Music 550 Madison Avenue address and a ℗/© 1966, 2013 line, dating the pressing to 2013 or later; the box differs from the later repress of the same number, which carries a new box and label design. The dead wax runs MFSL3-45009 A2 to F2 with KW@MOFI for the Mobile Fidelity lacquers and 20704.1(3) through 20704.6(3) for the RTI three-step plating. Six sides at 45 RPM across three discs, cut from the 1/2-inch 30 IPS analog remix master. A modern audiophile edition, ranked beneath the 1966 Columbia mono and stereo pressings in the release history.
