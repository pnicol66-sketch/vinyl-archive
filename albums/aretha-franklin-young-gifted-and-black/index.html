The Album

Cut between 1970 and 1971 at Atlantic Studios in New York and Criteria in Miami, this is the album where Aretha Franklin took full command of her own records - writing or co-writing five of the twelve songs, playing piano and electric keyboards throughout, and shaping the arrangements alongside Jerry Wexler, Tom Dowd and Arif Mardin. The sessions gathered the finest rhythm players of the moment, Donny Hathaway's organ and electric piano threading through Cornell Dupree, Chuck Rainey and Bernard Purdie, with Memphis and New York horn sections layered on afterwards. Her own “Rock Steady” and “Day Dreaming” became signature sides, the first a lean funk workout, the second a drifting, flute-lit reverie, while the Nina Simone title song turned a Black-pride anthem into a statement of artistic independence. Around them she reclaimed material from Bacharach and David, Lennon and McCartney, Elton John, Otis Redding and Thom Bell, bending each to her gospel phrasing rather than the other way round. Released in January 1972, it topped the US R&B chart, reached the pop top ten and won her a Grammy for Best Female R&B Vocal Performance. Critics then and since have placed it with Lady Soul and I Never Loved A Man at the summit of her Atlantic run, and it is routinely ranked among the essential soul albums of the 1970s. Coming just before the Amazing Grace sessions, it marks the moment the Queen of Soul stood as composer, pianist and producer-in-all-but-name as much as singer.

The Label

Both sides carry the Atlantic green-over-red/orange label: green upper half, red/orange lower half, a white band across the middle reading STEREO at the left and ONE (side two: TWO) at the right, and above the hole the large A with black fan in a pink box set over ATLANTIC in a yellow box, a small ® beside it. The rim reads MFG. BY ATLANTIC RECORDING CORP., 1841 BROADWAY, NEW YORK, N.Y., with SD 7213 at the upper right, ℗ 1972 ATLANTIC at the lower right and the label-copy code (ST-A-712393A PR) / (ST-A-712393B PR) at the foot; the labels are flat, with a pressing ring and no deep groove. This green and red/orange design came in around SD 1500 and stayed in use into 1973, and the 1841 Broadway rim address ran until the company's move to 75 Rockefeller Plaza, which reached the labels in late 1973 - so a 1972-dated label with this address falls inside the first-issue window for this catalogue number. Later US issues of SD 7213 either reset the address, come from the 1975 reissue run, or are the later 180-gram pressings whose dead wax carries extra lacquer marks.

Fidelity

An all-analogue 1972 Atlantic production: recorded at Atlantic Studios in New York and Criteria in Miami by Gene Paul, Ron and Howie Albert and Chuck Kirkpatrick, remixed by Arif Mardin, with the horn and string overdubs printed to tape. The period cut has the dry, forward midrange and the hard snap on Purdie's snare that makes “Rock Steady” land, and collectors consistently prefer these early 1841 Broadway stereo pressings to the thinner later Atlantic and Rhino-era repressings. Surfaces from this first run are quiet when clean, as here. Against the 2025 Analogue Productions 45rpm double LP, which opens the bass and the top end further, this copy gives up some air and holds the advantage in immediacy and in being the cut released alongside the record itself.

Notes

1972 Atlantic SD 7213 stereo, US first-issue pressing. The green over red/orange labels print MFG. BY ATLANTIC RECORDING CORP., 1841 BROADWAY, NEW YORK, N.Y. and ℗ 1972, the address Atlantic used until the move that reached its labels in late 1973, which puts this copy in the original issue window. The dead wax reads 5032-SD 7213-1 (A) S42635 and 5032-SD 7213-1 (B) S42636, first-cut lacquer numbers without the added lacquer marks found in the later 180-gram pressings of the same number. Not the white-label mono promo and not the Monarch-plant stereo run. The jacket keeps its shrink.
