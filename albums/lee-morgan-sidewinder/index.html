The Album

Recorded on December 21, 1963 at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio, The Sidewinder was cut as a fairly routine Blue Note date built entirely from five new Lee Morgan originals, with the loping title track added almost as an afterthought to round out the program. Morgan, a prodigy who had joined Dizzy Gillespie's big band as a teenager and later anchored Art Blakey's Jazz Messengers, was rebuilding his career after a period of personal decline when this session became his unexpected comeback vehicle. Joining him were tenor saxophonist Joe Henderson, newly signed to Blue Note himself, pianist Barry Harris on loan from Riverside, bassist Bob Cranshaw, and drummer Billy Higgins, who together lock into a loose, blues-drenched boogaloo groove that leaves wide-open space for extended solos over simple harmonic vamps. The title track's easy backbeat and earworm bassline caught on unexpectedly with radio and jukebox audiences, turning what Blue Note had pressed in only a few thousand copies into the label's best-selling record and, according to producer Michael Cuscuna's account, a financial lifeline for the company. Its crossover success also proved influential and controversial in equal measure, spawning a wave of similarly groove-based "boogaloo" jazz-funk sides across the mid-1960s hard bop catalog and drawing some critical grumbling that commercial appeal was overtaking artistic ambition. Decades on, The Sidewinder is regarded as a cornerstone of the hard bop/soul-jazz idiom and a high point in Morgan's celebrated run of Blue Note leader dates, prized by critics and listeners alike for its irresistible groove and the quality of the blowing from Morgan and Henderson.

The Label

The side labels read BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC • NEW YORK USA with no street address and no Liberty credit, the New York, USA label design of late 1961-66, and neither side shows a deep groove. Each runout carries the BNLP 4157 A/B Van Gelder matrix, and the side 1 runout carries the Plastylite ear, the mark of the plant that pressed Blue Note's records until the label's 1966 sale to Liberty. The 1964 first pressing carries the ear together with a deep groove; this copy has the ear and no deep groove, and is one of the Plastylite pressings made, c. 1964-66, after the 4,000-copy first issue sold out within days of its July 1964 release. It therefore ranks behind the deep-groove, ear-stamped 1964 first pressing, alongside the other non-deep-groove copies that carry the ear, and ahead of the pressings without the ear and those on the Division of Liberty label.

Fidelity

This is a Van Gelder mono cut, mastered by Rudy Van Gelder from the original session tape. The runouts carry the Van Gelder stamp and the side 1 runout the Plastylite ear: the copy was pressed at Plastylite before Blue Note's 1966 sale to Liberty, c. 1964-66. Collectors on the forums rate the deep-groove, ear-stamped 1964 pressings of this title highest for punch and dynamics; early Liberty pressings from the Van Gelder master are held to sound nearly as good and rank ahead of the later United Artists/Manhattan reissues. Among modern reissues, the Music Matters 45rpm and Blue Note Classic Vinyl series are valued for clarity but are generally described as smoother rather than carrying the rawer impact of the Van Gelder-Plastylite cut. This pressing's fidelity sits just behind the deep-groove first pressing, pressed at the same plant from the same Van Gelder cut, and ahead of the later Liberty-era pressings remastered without the Van Gelder stamp and the CD-era reissues.

Notes

This is a Blue Note BLP-4157 mono pressing on New York, USA labels with no deep groove on either side. Both runouts carry the BNLP 4157 A/B Van Gelder matrix, and the side 1 runout carries the Plastylite ear, the mark of the plant that pressed Blue Note's records until the label's 1966 sale to Liberty. The first pressing carries a deep groove as well as the ear; Blue Note's first issue of 4,000 copies sold out within days of its July 1964 release, and Plastylite went on to press tens of thousands more. This copy, with the ear and no deep groove, is one of those later Plastylite pressings, c. 1964-66. The jacket credits Barry Harris as appearing by courtesy of Riverside Records.
