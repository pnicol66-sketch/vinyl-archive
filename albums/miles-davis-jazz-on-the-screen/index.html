The Album

Jazz On The Screen' is a US Fontana compilation pairing the ten-track score Miles Davis improvised in Paris for Louis Malle's 1958 thriller 'Ascenseur pour l'échafaud' (Elevator to the Gallows) with three cues Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers, featuring Benny Golson's arrangements, recorded for the French film 'Les Femmes Disparaissent' (The Women Disappear). Davis cut his side of the music in a single overnight session on December 4-5, 1957, with a quartet of French and expatriate musicians - Barney Wilen, René Urtreger, Pierre Michelot and Kenny Clarke - largely improvising to the projected film, an approach later celebrated as one of the earliest and most successful marriages of modal jazz and cinema. The Blakey/Golson material, cut later in Paris with the classic Golson-era Jazz Messengers lineup of Lee Morgan, Bobby Timmons and Jymie Merritt, showcases the harder-driving, hard-bop side of the group in contrast to Davis's spare, atmospheric mood pieces. The Davis soundtrack came at a pivotal turning point in his career, just before he assembled the sextet that would record 'Milestones' and 'Kind of Blue,' and it is now regarded as a foundational work of modal jazz and film-scoring craft. Critics of the day, including Martin Williams and Ralph Gleason, praised the Davis score for elevating film music beyond mere mood-setting into fully realized, self-sufficient jazz, while the Blakey/Golson cuts are valued for their concentrated bop firepower. Decades on, 'Ascenseur pour l'échafaud' is cited as a landmark that legitimized jazz composed specifically for cinema, and this compilation remains a compact, historically important document of that moment for both bandleaders.

The Label

This copy carries the aqua/light-blue Fontana US label with the black-circle 'fontana' logo used on the Mercury-distributed Fontana US series from roughly 1962-1966, matching the design seen on other MGF-275xx titles in the same catalog run (e.g. MGF-27533, MGF-27537). The label reads 'Fontana Records, A Division of Mercury Record Productions, Inc. - Vendor: Mercury Record Corporation,' the standard US Fontana rim text of this era, and the label is pressed with a deep groove, consistent with a first-run US pressing rather than a later reissue. Cat. no. MGF-27532 (mono) sits opposite the stereo counterpart SRF-67532; this is the mono edition. There is no evidence of a later reissue of this specific compilation, so this US Fontana mono pressing represents the sole significant variant collectors track for this title.

Fidelity

The Davis material was recorded quickly and informally to a projected film image in a Paris studio in December 1957, so the source tape has an intimate, somewhat close-mic'd character rather than pristine hi-fi polish; the Blakey/Golson cuts, recorded separately, are tighter and punchier. This Fontana US mono pressing is a solid, if unglamorous, straightforward transfer with no evidence of alternate 'audiophile' mastering; collector chatter (Steve Hoffman forums) tends to favor the original French Fontana/Barclay pressings of the Davis score and later audiophile-grade reissues (e.g. Speakers Corner, Universal Music/Verve remasters of 'Ascenseur pour l'échafaud') for critical listening over this US compilation cut, which is valued more for rarity and historical packaging than absolute sonic fidelity.
