This Pressing

2009 Analogue Productions 45 RPM reissue (ABNJ 84130, stereo) - labels print the 2009 copyright and Analogue Productions design credit; a later pressing of the 1963 Blue Note original.

The Album

Recorded in 1962 for Blue Note at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio, Along Came John was organist John Patton's debut as a leader after prior work backing Lou Donaldson, and it introduced him to the label's roster of soul-jazz talent. The quintet pairs Patton with guitarist Grant Green, drummer Ben Dixon and two tenor saxophonists, Fred Jackson and Harold Vick, giving the session a thicker frontline than the typical organ-trio date. The material mixes originals contributed by Patton and Dixon with the standard "I'll Never Be Free," grounding the set in the blues-and-groove vocabulary that defined the era's soul-jazz boom. Patton's playing favors a direct, percussive attack and rhythmic economy over the churning virtuosity of some contemporaries, a style that would mark his subsequent run of Blue Note dates through the 1960s. The album arrived as Blue Note was deepening its commitment to organ-combo jazz alongside artists like Jimmy Smith and Baby Face Willette, helping cement the subgenre's commercial and stylistic footprint. It has since been regarded by soul-jazz collectors as a strong, unpretentious example of the formula, valued as much for Grant Green's concise, blues-rooted guitar work as for Patton's own contributions.

The Label

Each of the four sides carries the blue-and-cream Blue Note layout with BLUE up the left edge and NOTE along the foot, a registered-trademark mark under NOTE, and the rim text BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC • NEW YORK USA - but every side also prints 45 STEREO LONG PLAYING, an 'Additional design © 2009 Analogue Productions' credit and 'ORIGINALLY RELEASED AS BST 84130', identifying the design as the 2009 Analogue Productions 45 RPM reissue rather than an original-era label. The period-style rim wording, the registered mark and the New York address are reproductions of the 1960s design faithfully copied for this series and date nothing on their own; the label's own 2009 copyright line is the mark the copy cannot predate. No deep-groove ring appears on the labels - a flat reissue label. The jacket's back cover prints ST-84130 while the labels print BST 84130-A/B, a minor inconsistency between the jacket and label printings of the same reissue.

Fidelity

The lacquers were cut at AcousTech by Kevin Gray and Steve Hoffman from the analog session tapes, pressed at RTI across two 45 RPM discs for extended headroom and quieter surfaces than a standard 33⅓ LP. Collectors regard Analogue Productions' Blue Note 45 RPM series generally as a transparent, dynamic cut that improves on the standard-speed Blue Note reissues of the same era, though an original stereo or mono Van Gelder-cut pressing remains the benchmark many collectors still prefer for tonal character. Within this title's fidelity hierarchy this 45 RPM numbered edition ranks above the Japanese and European standard-speed reissues and above the CD/SACD remasters, but below the two original 1963 Blue Note pressings for collectors prioritizing the original cut.

Notes

This is the 2009 Analogue Productions 45 RPM stereo reissue, numbered #0186, not an original Blue Note pressing. The labels carry a 2009 copyright/design-credit line for Analogue Productions alongside the reproduced period-style Blue Note artwork, and the runouts show the Kevin Gray/Steve Hoffman AcousTech mastering signature rather than any 1960s-era stamp. The 2009 date on the labels is the one mark a later pressing cannot predate, placing this copy well below the original mono and stereo first pressings in the release chronology regardless of how closely the artwork imitates them.
