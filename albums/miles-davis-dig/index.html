The Album

Dig collects the bulk of a single October 5, 1951 session - among the first jazz dates conceived with the new long-playing format in mind, allowing the musicians to 'stretch out' beyond three-minute 78 constraints. It documents the first extended studio partnership between Miles Davis and Sonny Rollins, who had recorded together before but never at this length, alongside a teenaged Jackie McLean then deep in his Charlie Parker apprenticeship - Parker himself reportedly dropped by the studio during the date. The material is significant: 'Dig' and 'Denial' are Davis contrafacts on 'Sweet Georgia Brown' and 'It's Only a Paper Moon' changes respectively, 'Bluing' is a long blues meditation, and 'Out of the Blue' became a jazz staple. Coming during Miles's early-1950s Prestige years - after Birth of the Cool but before his heroin-habit nadir and triumphant 1954-55 comeback recordings - the album captures him consolidating a harder, more driving post-bop language with the explosive Art Blakey pushing the rhythm. The remaining two performances from the session, 'Conception' and 'My Old Flame,' were issued on the companion LP Conception (PRLP 7013). Ira Gitler's original notes frame the set as a milestone in the Davis-Rollins axis, and the record has since been recognized as a foundational document of early hard bop and of Rollins's emergence as the era's great tenor voice.

The Label

This copy carries the classic yellow-on-black 'fireworks' Prestige label rim-reading 'PRESTIGE RECORDS INC. • 446 W. 50th ST., N.Y.C.', with deep groove on both sides. Prestige moved from its 782 Tenth Ave. address to 446 W. 50th St. in 1955, the year Dig was released, making the 50th Street address the correct first-issue configuration for this title; the later Bergenfield, N.J. address (from c. 1958) marks second pressings. The typed runouts show hand-etched 'RVG' with 'AB' (Abbey Record Mfg.) plant stamps and the 'PRLP-7012-A-1' first-lacquer marking on side 1 - all consistent with the original 1955 pressing cut and remastered by Rudy Van Gelder, as also credited on the back cover. The jacket's 447 West 50th Street catalog address further locks this copy into the 1955-56 first-issue window.

Fidelity

Cut and remastered for LP by Rudy Van Gelder (hand-etched RVG in the deadwax; back-cover credit) from the 1951 session tapes, pressed at Abbey (AB stamps). Original mono deep-groove copies like this are the benchmark for the title on collector forums - warm, present midrange typical of early Van Gelder remastering, though the underlying 1951 session was not an RVG recording, so it lacks the bloom of his Hackensack work. Later Bergenfield-address pressings use the same metal and sound close; OJC/Fantasy reissues and modern reissues are respectable but a step removed from this first-generation cut.
