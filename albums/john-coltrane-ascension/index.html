The Album

Recorded on June 28, 1965 at Van Gelder Studio, Ascension was John Coltrane's most radical statement to date, assembling an eleven-piece front line of his working quartet plus a young guard of avant-garde players - Freddie Hubbard, Archie Shepp, Pharoah Sanders, John Tchicai, Marion Brown and Dewey Johnson - for a single, continuous collectively improvised piece built around brief written ensemble fanfares separating individual solos. It arrived at a pivotal turn in Coltrane's career: fresh off the acclaim of A Love Supreme, he used Ascension to push past the quartet format into the freer, more collective language associated with Ornette Coleman and Albert Ayler, effectively opening the door for the 'New Wave' of free jazz that Impulse! marketed heavily on the back cover. The recording produced two usable full takes, and the swap between them after initial pressing became one of the most famous mix-up stories in jazz reissue history. Critically the album divided listeners on release - hailed by some as a visionary breakthrough and dismissed by others as noise - but it is now regarded as a landmark of 1960s free jazz and a key document of Coltrane's late-period turn toward collective improvisation. Its influence radiates through the loft-jazz and free-improvisation scenes that followed, and it remains one of the most studied and debated recordings in the Impulse! catalogue.

The Label

The label is the classic mono-era Impulse! design: solid orange centre field, a black perimeter band carrying two mirrored 'impulse!' logos and the split-oval 'i!' logo top and bottom, with 'A PRODUCT OF ABC-PARAMOUNT RECORDS, INC.' printed small at the foot of the label - the ABC-Paramount credit (used before the 1966 corporate rename to ABC Records) that dates this pressing to the label's first Ascension pressing run in February 1966. Deep groove: not present - flat label. A small dark rectangle over part of the Side 1 label field reads as a marker or sticker obscuring a prior owner's notation rather than a printed label element, and is not a pressing tell. The catalogue number A-95-A is not to be confused with the unrelated 1970 Reader's Digest box-set release (RDA 95-A), a different compilation entirely.

Fidelity

Cut and stamped by Rudy Van Gelder direct from his own session tapes, this mono Edition I pressing is the earliest and most sought-after cut of Ascension; collectors on the forums generally rank the original mono Van Gelder pressings above the contemporaneous stereo gatefolds and well above later 1970s-80s ABC/MCA target-label repressings and the digital-era CD transfers. The 2020s Verve/Impulse! 'Edition I & II' audiophile reissue (180g, mastered from the analog tapes) is well regarded but is a modern remaster rather than a Van Gelder lacquer, so purists still favor this original mono cut for its immediacy and the period-correct RVG lathe.

Notes

Runout etch 'A-95-A LW' / 'A-95 B LW' with a Van Gelder stamp identifies this as the original February 1966 mono pressing carrying the second-recorded take - the version that became the standard issue after Bob Thiele's initial take swap. The orange label with black perimeter ring and 'A PRODUCT OF ABC-PARAMOUNT RECORDS, INC.' credit places it in the pre-1966-rename Impulse! label window. No deep groove is visible on the flat label. This pressing's catalogue number, A-95-A, is unrelated to the 1970 Reader's Digest box-set compilation numbered RDA 95-A, a coincidental digit match with a different release entirely.
