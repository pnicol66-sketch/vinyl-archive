This Pressing

Original-era stereo pressing (BS-6041, blue/silver Bethlehem label), the stereo counterpart to the mono BCP-6041 deep-groove first pressing.

The Album

Assembled by Bethlehem Records in 1959, this compilation pairs Nina Simone with two of the label's established jazz vocal stars, Chris Connor and Carmen McRae, to capitalize on Simone's sudden fame after "I Loves You, Porgy" became a hit from her debut LP. Several of the Simone sides here were leftover, previously unissued performances from the same December 1957 Beltone Studios sessions that produced her landmark debut, recorded with just Albert Heath on drums and Jimmy Bond on bass in her spare, piano-trio style. Connor's contributions reflect her rise as a cool-toned Bethlehem stylist backed by the Ellis Larkins Trio, while McRae - already admired for her worldly diction and jazz phrasing - is heard with the Mat Mathews and Tony Scott combos. For Simone the release captured a pivotal early-career moment, issued as she was crossing from nightclub obscurity into national visibility, and it remains valued today chiefly as a rare repository of otherwise-unavailable early Simone performances rather than as a unified artistic statement. Critically it has always been viewed as a Bethlehem catalogue product - a sampler rather than a true album - but its Simone content in particular is prized by collectors and biographers tracing her formative Bethlehem period.

The Label

The label photographed is solid blue with silver/white print - the BETHLEHEM wordmark and HIGH FIDELITY banner at top, STEREO underlined beneath, bold-caps title NINA SIMONE AND FRIENDS, catalogue number BS-6041 at lower left with Side 1/2 (BS-6041-A/B) at right, and MANUFACTURED IN U.S.A. BETHLEHEM MUSIC CORP. around the bottom rim. Discogs documents the mono edition of this catalogue number (release 1156712) with red-and-silver deep-groove labels, and explicitly notes the stereo counterpart is 'recognizable by its blue and silver labels' - exactly matching what is shown here. This blue/silver design with the BS-prefixed number places the copy in the original 1959-60 Bethlehem stereo issue window, before the label's later ownership changes and reissue programs (1970s Bethlehem reissues, and the 2021 BMG/Craft green-vinyl remaster) altered the design entirely.

Fidelity

This is an early analog stereo transfer of 1957-vintage, mono-era session tapes; Bethlehem's stereo mixes from this period are generally regarded by collectors as serviceable but vocal-forward with modest channel separation rather than audiophile-grade. Steve Hoffman-forum-style consensus and Discogs user comments on the 2021 Kevin Gray-mastered BMG reissue describe that later cut as "vocal is left heavy" and inconsistent between pressing runs (some copies reported as poorly separated). Within the album's fidelity hierarchy, this original-era stereo pressing sits above the 2021 Kevin Gray/BMG green-vinyl remaster and any generic later reissues in historical authenticity, being the vintage source pressing those later masters derive from, though the mono BCP-6041 deep-groove pressing is generally considered the more direct and more collectible cut of the two original 1959-60 formats.

Notes

This is the original-era Bethlehem stereo pressing of BCP-6041/BS-6041, distinguished from the mono edition by its blue-and-silver label design (the mono uses red-and-silver deep-groove labels per Discogs). The vinyl on this copy is graded NM- with deep groove present on both sides, consistent with an early-1960s stereo pressing rather than a later reissue. The etched runout (BS-12-6041-A-R-X-3 / BS-12-6041-B-R-X-5) follows the expected BS-12-6041 stereo matrix prefix pattern for this catalogue number, though no Discogs variant page transcribing this exact stereo runout string was located to cross-check the suffix codes.
