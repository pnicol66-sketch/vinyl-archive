The Album

Pres gathers small-group sides Lester Young cut for Norman Granz across several separate rhythm-section dates rather than one continuous session, pairing him first with Hank Jones, Ray Brown and Buddy Rich and then, on the remaining tunes, with John Lewis, Gene Ramey, Jo Jones and the lesser-known Joe Shulman and Bill Clark. By the mid-1950s Young - the tenor saxophonist whose light, floating tone and behind-the-beat phrasing had rewritten the instrument's vocabulary during his years with Count Basie in the late 1930s - was deep into his postwar period with Granz's labels, revisiting popular standards and his own originals in intimate combo settings. The liner notes make a point of the literate lyricists behind several of the chosen standards, tying Young's famous insistence on knowing a song's words to the way he phrases the melody. The sessions catch an artist whose postwar sound had grown more weathered after a difficult wartime Army stint, yet whose lyrical, melancholic improvising remained utterly distinctive and hugely influential on the generation of tenor players that followed. Jazz historians have generally treated these Norgran/Verve small-group dates as a looser, more variable body of work than Young's celebrated 1930s Basie and Kansas City Six recordings, but essential nonetheless for documenting the singular sound of his later horn.

The Label

Both side labels are the yellow Norgran design with the trumpet-playing figure at the upper left of Side 1 (upper centre on Side 2), “NORGRAN RECORDS” arced across the top rim and “LONG PLAYING MICROGROOVE” arced across the bottom rim, with “NORGRAN SALES CORP. — MADE IN U.S.A.” printed beneath the selection list; both sides are deep-groove pressings. This is the house design Norgran used from its founding in the mid-1950s until its absorption into Verve in 1957, placing this copy in that original mono release window and ahead of the later Verve MGV-8162 reissue that followed Granz's label consolidation. A small area of label loss near the spindle hole on each side exposes the inner sleeve beneath, a condition issue rather than a variant marker.

Fidelity

As an early-to-mid-1950s Norgran production, mastering and pressing quality varied from release to release under Norman Granz's operation, and no dedicated audiophile reissue of Pres has since been produced. Collector commentary on related Norgran/Verve small-group Lester Young titles of this period describes the mono sound as warm and solid on well-pressed copies, though surface and mastering quality can vary across 1950s Norgran vinyl generally. Being the first cut of the LP master, this deep-groove original pressing sits at the top of the fidelity hierarchy for this title, ahead of the 1957 Verve reissue and the later Japanese pressings, which are later-generation dubs.

Notes

This is the original 1956 Norgran issue of Pres: the yellow Norgran label with the trumpet-playing figure and deep-groove pressing on both sides match the label's mid-1950s house design, predating the 1957 Verve reissue that followed Granz's consolidation of his labels. The H1 matrix suffix points to an early Norgran stamper for this catalogue number. No Verve branding, club-edition text or reissue markers appear on the labels or jacket, consistent with a first pressing, though a small area of label loss near the spindle hole on each side is a condition issue rather than a pressing variant.
