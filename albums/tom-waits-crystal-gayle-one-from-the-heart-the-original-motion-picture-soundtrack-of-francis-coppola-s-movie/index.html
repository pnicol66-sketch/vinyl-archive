The Album

Francis Coppola conceived One from the Heart as an intimate, stylized romance following Apocalypse Now, and commissioned Tom Waits to write a song cycle charting a couple's breakup and reconciliation in a neon-lit Las Vegas. Waits spent nearly two years on the project, writing every song and recording with longtime producer Bones Howe and arranger Bob Alcivar, whose lush, string-and-brass orchestrations gave the material a Nelson Riddle-era romanticism unlike anything else in Waits's catalog. Crystal Gayle was brought in to voice the female half of the story - reportedly at the suggestion of Waits's wife Kathleen Brennan - and the pairing of her polished country-pop voice with Waits's ragged, whiskey-soaked delivery became the record's defining device, most vividly on the duets. The film was a lavish, expensive flop that helped sink Coppola's Zoetrope Studios, and the soundtrack's release was delayed by the ensuing turmoil before finally appearing in 1982. Waits earned an Academy Award nomination for the score, and critics came to regard the album as the most sophisticated, orchestrated statement of his early songwriting period. It effectively closed out his Asylum-era lounge-jazz balladeer persona; within a year he had reinvented his sound entirely on Swordfishtrombones, making this soundtrack a singular, beloved outlier in his body of work.

The Label

The label photos show the red Columbia design with 'COLUMBIA' repeated around the rim interspersed with CBS note logos, 'PRINTED IN U.S.A.' rim text, and '(c) 1982 CBS Inc.' - the layout Columbia introduced c. 1981 alongside the FC catalog prefix for full-price pop, replacing the earlier JC-era design. The AL/BL 37703 side-specific numbers are standard Columbia practice for this era. The typed matrix transcription shows an etched 'T1' before the KDISK mark, which Discogs and collector references confirm is Columbia's Terre Haute, Indiana pressing plant designation (distinct from Pitman's 'P' stamp), so this copy is a Terre Haute pressing rather than Pitman. Both plant variants are equal-status original 1982 first issues cut from Kendun Recorders, Burbank ('KDISK') lacquers.

Fidelity

Mastered at Kendun Recorders, Burbank (the 'KDISK' etching) and pressed at Columbia's Terre Haute, Indiana plant - generally viewed by collectors as a slightly less consistent presser than Pitman but capable of clean, quiet copies. The Bones Howe production, tracked at Wally Heider's in Hollywood, is a big, polished analog studio recording, and the original Kendun cut is warm and dynamic. In the album's fidelity hierarchy, the 2016 Mobile Fidelity Original Master Recording LP (MFSL 1-448) is the acknowledged top tier, praised by audiophile reviewers for a deeper, more three-dimensional presentation; a clean original 1982 US pressing like this one is the reference vintage alternative, while the recent colored-vinyl reissue and the CD editions sit below both for pure analog sonics.
