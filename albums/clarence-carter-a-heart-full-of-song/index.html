The Album

By the mid-1970s Clarence Carter, the blind Alabama soul singer who had broken through in the late 1960s with hits built on wit, innuendo and a weathered Southern-soul voice, had moved from his Atlantic/Fame home to ABC Records, and this album finds him producing himself at his own Future Stars Studios in Atlanta. The record leans into the lush, string-and-horn-laden soul sound of the era, pairing Carter originals like the title track's companion pieces with outside songs, including a cover of Ray Charles's blues standard and compositions from his longtime Fame collaborators George Jackson and Clarence Wilkins. Carter's writing and arranging team - horn charts by J. Alfred Cook, strings by Ted Stovall, his own vocal arrangements - reflects a veteran artist asserting full creative control over his sound rather than relying on an outside production house. The album arrived as Southern soul was being absorbed into the broader disco-era soul marketplace, and it stands as a transitional, self-produced statement from an artist whose earlier work helped define the gritty, humor-inflected end of the soul spectrum. It was received as a solid, if less commercially dominant, entry in Carter's catalog, valued by soul collectors more for its glimpse of Carter as auteur than for chart impact.

The Label

The label carries the gold ABC Records logo over a rainbow of concentric pink-to-yellow rings, with the album and artist name set in the same bold type on both sides and the catalogue number ABCD-943 printed top right - a flat label with no deep groove, a design ABC used across its mid-1970s soul and pop releases rather than one unique to this title. The runout on each side carries an SXT designation, the mark ABC used when lacquer metalwork cut at one plant (Santa Maria) was shipped to a second plant (Terre Haute) for pressing; that etch is the only tell distinguishing this stock from the parallel Santa Maria-pressed run of the same release, since both runs share the identical label design and catalogue number. The two pressings are parallel manufacturing runs of the same 1976 release rather than an earlier and later issue, so this copy's place in the pressing is established by the SXT etch alone, not by the label artwork.

Fidelity

The runout's SXT etch indicates the lacquers were mastered at the company's Santa Maria plant before being shipped for pressing at the Terre Haute facility, a common arrangement for ABC titles of this period rather than a mark of special audiophile attention. Collectors generally regard ABC's mid-1970s pressings as solid if unremarkable commercial product, with Santa Maria-mastered/Terre Haute-pressed copies considered on par with the parallel Santa Maria-pressed run of the same release rather than clearly superior or inferior. No audiophile remastering or specialist reissue of this title is known to exist, so this pressing sits at the top of the album's practical fidelity hierarchy by default.

Notes

This is the 1976 US ABC Records stereo pressing of ABCD-943, identified as coming off the Terre Haute pressing line using lacquer metalwork cut and mastered at the company's Santa Maria facility, a relationship the runout's SXT etch signals. The jacket is a demonstration (not-for-sale) copy rather than a standard retail stock copy. The catalogue number, label design and 1976 copyright line on the labels are consistent with a first-year pressing of this ABC release; no deeper dead-wax photograph was available to confirm the precise stamper generation.
