The Album

Recorded over two sessions in New York in December 1961, this album brought together Modern Jazz Quartet vibraphonist Milt Jackson and the fast-rising guitarist Wes Montgomery for one of the era's most celebrated hard-bop summit meetings. Producer Orrin Keepnews surrounded the two leaders with a first-call rhythm section - pianist Wynton Kelly, bassist Sam Jones and drummer Philly Joe Jones - that gave the date a relaxed, swinging foundation rather than a cutting-contest atmosphere. Jackson, already one of jazz's most blues-steeped improvisers through his work with the MJQ, found an unusually sympathetic melodic partner in Montgomery, and the two trade ideas with an easy, conversational warmth across the set's originals and standards. The material balances compositions contributed by both principals with jazz staples, letting each musician's voice emerge clearly within a shared groove. The record arrived at a pivotal moment for Montgomery, whose octave-and-chord-driven guitar style was winning him wide attention beyond the hard-bop audience that already knew Jackson. Critically, the album has endured as a benchmark meeting of two very different but complementary melodic sensibilities, frequently cited among the finest two-horn-style (here vibes-and-guitar) summit dates of the period. It remains a touchstone in both musicians' discographies and a favorite entry point for listeners exploring soulful, blues-rooted hard bop.

The Label

The side labels show Riverside's grey-ground tape-reel-and-microphone design with the RIVERSIDE wordmark at top, STEREOPHONIC beneath it, and BILL GRAUER PRODUCTIONS INC. / NEW YORK CITY at the foot - the stereo (black/silver) version of the Inc.-era tape-reel label used on RLP 334-476 from roughly 1960 to 1964, before the plainer thin-serif blue label of 1965-66. Both sides show a deep-groove ring, a pressing feature carried by the earliest stampers for this catalogue number. This places the copy within Riverside's original-issue window for RLP 9407 rather than the mid-1960s reissue or the later Fantasy/Milestone/OJC pressings, which use a different, logo-free label.

Fidelity

The session was engineered by Ray Fowler at Plaza Sound Studios rather than by Rudy Van Gelder, giving it a somewhat drier, roomier balance than contemporaneous Van Gelder-cut Riverside dates; collectors generally rate the original stereo cut as warm and well-separated for its era. This original tape-reel-label stereo pressing sits at the top of the album's fidelity hierarchy among vintage copies, ahead of the later plain-label Riverside pressings and the Fantasy/Milestone/OJC reissues, which are considered flatter and more compressed. A 2004 45 RPM audiophile reissue mastered from the analog tapes is rated by collectors on the forums as the sonic reference for the album, surpassing even clean originals in clarity and dynamics, with the standard-speed reissue falling in between.

Notes

This is a 1962 US Riverside stereo original of RLP 9407. The tape-reel/microphone label with Bill Grauer Productions Inc. and the deep-groove ring on both sides match Riverside's original-issue label design used from about 1960 through 1964, well before the plain blue logo-free label of 1965-66. The matrix numbers begin RLP-12-9407 A/B-1, consistent with an early stamper generation for this catalogue number. Nothing on the labels or jacket points to a later plain-label Riverside pressing or to a Fantasy/Milestone/OJC reissue.
