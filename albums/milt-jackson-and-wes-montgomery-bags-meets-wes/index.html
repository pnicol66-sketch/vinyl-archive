The Album

Recorded over two December 1961 sessions in New York, Bags Meets Wes! paired Modern Jazz Quartet vibraphonist Milt Jackson with the rapidly ascending guitarist Wes Montgomery for what became one of the era's most beloved hard-bop summit meetings. Producer Orrin Keepnews built the date around a first-rate rhythm section - pianist Wynton Kelly, bassist Sam Jones and drummer Philly Joe Jones - giving both leaders a supple, swinging foundation. Jackson, already established as one of jazz's most soulful blues-based improvisers, found in Montgomery a kindred melodic sensibility, and the two trade choruses with an easy, conversational rapport rather than cutting-contest fireworks. The Label

This copy carries Riverside's second-generation label design: dark blue background with silver print, a tape-reel-and-microphone logo above the "RIVERSIDE" wordmark, "STEREOPHONIC" beneath it, and "BILL GRAUER PRODUCTIONS INC. / NEW YORK CITY" at the foot of the label, with visible deep-groove ring on both sides. Per bsnpubs and LondonJazzCollector, this dark-blue tape-reel/mic label was Riverside's standard design from roughly 1959/60 through 1965, when it was replaced by a plain blue label (no logo, thinner print) used in 1965-66. The design, deep groove, and RLP-12-9407 matrix prefix place this squarely as an original-era 1962 stereo pressing rather than a mid/late-1960s or Fantasy-era reissue.

Fidelity

The session was engineered by Ray Fowler at Plaza Sound Studios rather than by Rudy Van Gelder, giving it a slightly drier, roomier balance than contemporaneous Van Gelder-cut Riverside/Blue Note dates; collectors generally rate the original stereo cut as warm and well-separated for its era. This original tape-reel-label stereo pressing sits at the top of the album's fidelity hierarchy for vintage copies, ahead of the later plain-blue-label Riverside pressings and the Fantasy/Milestone reissues, which are reported as flatter and more compressed. The 2004 Analogue Productions/Acoustic Sounds 45 RPM 180g reissue, mastered from the analog tapes, is regarded by forum consensus as the sonic reference for the album, surpassing even clean originals in clarity and dynamics, with the standard-speed OJC reissue falling in between.

Notes

This is a 1962 US Riverside stereo original of RLP 9407. The dark blue tape-reel/mic label with silver print and deep-groove pressing match Riverside's standard 1959-1965 label design, well before the plain blue label without the logo used in 1965-66. Matrix numbers begin RLP-12-9407 A/B-1, consistent with an early stamper generation for this catalog number. Nothing on the labels or jacket points to a later Fantasy/Milestone or OJC reissue.
