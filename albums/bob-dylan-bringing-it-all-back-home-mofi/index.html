The Album

Recorded in a burst of sessions in New York in January 1965, this record marks the moment Bob Dylan turned from the acoustic protest-song mode of his early records toward electric rock and roll, opening with a full band on side one before closing with four extended acoustic pieces. The songs range from the free-associative wordplay and talking blues of the electric cuts to the long, image-dense meditations of the acoustic side, reflecting Dylan's immersion in Beat poetry, surrealist imagery and contemporary folk and blues traditions. It arrived at a pivotal point in his career, following three albums that had made him the central songwriter of the American folk revival, and it signaled the start of the mid-1960s run that would carry him through his next two albums into rock stardom. The record's fusion of poetic lyric writing with rock instrumentation is widely credited with helping open folk music to electric arrangements and expanding what a popular song could address. It was received as a startling departure by much of the folk audience of the day but was quickly recognized by critics as a major artistic leap, and it has remained a foundational text of 1960s songwriting. Its songs have been covered repeatedly across popular music and its influence runs through generations of singer-songwriters and rock lyricists who followed.

The Label

The labels are a dark/black ground with a silver-grey rim band reading ORIGINAL MASTER RECORDING / MOBILE FIDELITY SOUND LAB, a boxed Columbia eye at the right, the MoFi script logo and a GAIN 2 Ultra Analog badge, with 550 Madison Avenue New York address text and a 1965/2012 dual copyright line printed in the small type. This is a modern Mobile Fidelity audiophile reissue design, not a 1960s Columbia label, and the 2012 copyright date fixes the earliest possible date this specific pressing could have been made - no earlier. The address, Sony Music Entertainment manufacturing credit and the 2012 copyright line place this copy at the start of the Mobile Fidelity 45 RPM numbered reissue program for this title, well after any of the 1965 Columbia/CBS originals in the hierarchy above.

Fidelity

Mastered and lacquer-cut by Krieg Wunderlich at Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab using the Gain 2 Ultra Analog system, pressed at a specialist audiophile plant on 180-gram vinyl at 45 RPM for lower surface noise and higher resolution per side. Collectors on the forums generally rate this cut very favorably against the stock-speed Columbia originals, prizing the added clarity and quieter surfaces that the 45 RPM spread and audiophile pressing bring to Dylan's mid-1960s recordings. Within this title's reissue family it sits alongside a later mono 45 RPM edition; both outrank ordinary Columbia reissues for sound quality, with opinion divided over whether the stereo or mono 45 RPM cut is ultimately preferred for this particular album.

Notes

This is a 2012 Mobile Fidelity Sound Lab 45 RPM numbered stereo reissue, not an original 1965 Columbia pressing. The label's own 1965/2012 dual copyright line fixes 2012 as the earliest the copy could have been made, regardless of how closely the catalog number and design echo the original release. The matrix identifies it specifically as MFSL 2-380 with mastering engineer initials and Mobile Fidelity plant codes cut for the Gain 2 Ultra Analog cutting system. One side shows a stamper suffix variant from the catalogued version, a normal occurrence across a limited-edition pressing run rather than a sign of a different release.
