The Album

Recorded June 21, 1957 at Rudy Van Gelder's Hackensack studio, After Hours belongs to a loose string of unrehearsed blues-based jam sessions Prestige built around its bullpen of contract musicians in the mid-1950s, nominally fronted here by trumpeter Thad Jones. Jones, fresh from his celebrated run in the Count Basie orchestra which he had joined in 1954, leads a group of Prestige regulars - Frank Wess on flute and tenor, guitarist Kenny Burrell, pianist Mal Waldron, bassist Paul Chambers and drummer Arthur Taylor - through four extended, mostly blues-form performances that stretch past eight and even twelve minutes apiece. Mal Waldron, the label's de facto house pianist and a prolific composer for these dates, is credited with all four originals, giving the loosely structured jam a real compositional backbone beneath the solos. The album's concept, as the back-cover notes frame it, is mood music for musicians' own after-work hours - relaxed, unhurried blowing sessions that let individual voices, Wess's flute on the title track and Burrell's guitar on the uptempo numbers, stretch out at length over Waldron's simple blues frameworks. The result stands as one of the more expansive and unguarded blowing dates in Prestige's mid-1950s catalogue, a showcase for a set of first-call sidemen operating with total ease in loose, informal company.

The Label

This copy's label is the yellow-ground, black-rim Prestige design with a top-arc PRESTIGE wordmark and small doodle/arrow motifs around the perimeter, and prints no street address or city line on either side. Prestige dropped its 446 W. 50th St./Bergenfield NYC-address labels after Bob Weinstock sold the company to Fantasy, Inc. in 1971, moving thereafter to address-free Fantasy-era pressings; this no-address design and the absence of any Van Gelder or ear stamp in the runout place the copy in that post-1971 Fantasy manufacturing era rather than the 1957 first-issue window. The runout's plant letter further narrows that era: it is a mark a Columbia-operated contract plant used only between 1982 and 1992, so this copy was pressed within that window of Fantasy's ownership rather than at the full span of Fantasy's tenure.

Fidelity

The runout carries no Van Gelder stamp and no ear mark, and the (T) letter identifies a Columbia-operated contract plant active between 1982 and 1992 rather than Van Gelder's own cutting and Abbey/Monarch-era pressing used for the original lacquers. Pressings from this later Fantasy manufacturing period are generally rated well below the original Hackensack-cut Prestige discs for surface quietness and tonal body, and collectors consistently place them near the bottom of the title's fidelity ranking. This copy sits below the 1957 deep-groove original and below the 2025 Craft Recordings reissue, which collectors rate as the best-sounding reissue cut from the original tapes.

Notes

This copy carries the no-address, black/yellow Prestige label used after Fantasy, Inc. took over the company in 1971, rather than the 446 W. 50th St./Bergenfield address of the 1957 original. The runout reads PR-7118 A1/B1 with a (T) plant letter and no RVG or Plastylite-style stamp, a mark used only by a Columbia-operated contract pressing plant between 1982 and 1992. Together these place the pressing in that Fantasy-era manufacturing window rather than the 1957 Van Gelder-cut first issue.
