The Album

Recorded June 21, 1957 at Rudy Van Gelder's Hackensack studio, After Hours is one of a loose string of unrehearsed blues-based jam sessions Prestige built around its bullpen of contract musicians in the mid-1950s, nominally fronted here by trumpeter Thad Jones. Jones, fresh from his celebrated run in the Count Basie orchestra (which he'd joined in 1954), leads a group of Prestige regulars - Frank Wess on flute and tenor, guitarist Kenny Burrell, pianist Mal Waldron, bassist Paul Chambers and drummer Arthur Taylor - through four extended, mostly blues-form performances that stretch past eight and even twelve minutes apiece. Mal Waldron, the label's de facto house pianist and a prolific composer for these dates, is credited with all four originals, giving the loosely structured jam a real compositional backbone beneath the solos. The Label

Prestige dropped the 446 W. 50th St./Bergenfield NYC-era label after Bob Weinstock sold the company to Fantasy Records in 1971, replacing it with the symbol-ring design (no street address) seen on this copy. Fantasy-era Prestige pressings are generally regarded as feeble pressings relative to the NYC originals, placing this copy in the post-1971 Fantasy manufacturing era rather than the 1957 first-issue window.

Fidelity

Fantasy-era Prestige pressings made after the 1971 Berkeley takeover are generally regarded as feeble pressings compared to the original Van Gelder-cut NYC-era discs, and this copy's non-RVG runout places it in that later, sonically lesser tier, well below the 1957 original and also below the audiophile 2025 Craft Recordings OJC reissue.

Notes

This copy's symbol-ring Prestige label with no street address dates to after Fantasy's 1971 purchase of Prestige, and its runout (PR-7118 A1/B1, no RVG or ear stamp) confirms it is a later Fantasy-manufactured pressing rather than the 1957 Van Gelder-cut original.
