The Album

Jazz Track pairs two landmark but very different Miles Davis dates: the improvised score he cut in Paris on December 4-5, 1957 for Louis Malle's film noir "Ascenseur pour l'échafaud" (Elevator to the Gallows), and three standards recorded in New York on May 26, 1958 with the sextet that would soon make Kind of Blue. The Paris session is jazz history's most celebrated example of pure improvisation to picture: Davis and a pickup band of French and expatriate musicians reportedly watched screened footage and played spontaneously with almost no rehearsal, producing a moody, minimalist score that became foundational to the language of jazz film scoring. The New York side captures the first studio meeting of Davis with Bill Evans alongside Cannonball Adderley, John Coltrane, Paul Chambers, and Jimmy Cobb - the only other studio recordings by that exact lineup outside of Kind of Blue - and its version of "Stella By Starlight" in particular is prized by musicians and critics as one of the finest readings of the song on record. Coming at the hinge between Davis's hard-bop Prestige years and the modal breakthroughs of 1959, the album captures him restlessly expanding his vocabulary in two very different contexts simultaneously. Columbia's decision to package a European film score alongside a domestic studio date made for an unusual, somewhat stopgap release, but it preserved both dates for American listeners at a time when the film soundtrack itself was not yet issued domestically. Critically the album has always lived somewhat in the shadow of the artist's more unified statements, but collectors and critics single out the Evans/Adderley/Coltrane trio of tracks as essential precursors to Kind of Blue, and reissues of both halves (ind the soundtrack and reissues of individual sessionsions) is are essential precursors to Kind of Blue, and reissues of both halves have beenended.

The Label

This copy carries the Columbia "6-eye" red label with deep groove and the pre-1958 rim text order (walking-eye between circled "Lp" and "Marcas Reg."), the design bsnpubs and Discogs place as Columbia's flagship mono label from roughly 1956 through 1961. The runout stampers (XLP44601-2AE III / XLP44602-2G) carry no plant-suffix letter (no "TA" for Terre Haute or "P"/"H" for Pitman/Hollywood), which Discogs variant data associates with Columbia's original New York (30th Street/799 Seventh Ave) pressing run rather than the later regional-plant repressings of the same stampers. This places the copy at the head of the pressing hierarchy for CL 1268, essentially contemporaneous with the album's original December 1958/early-1959 release, ahead of the Terre Haute ("TA"), Hollywood ("H"), and Pitman ("P") stamped copies that follow using descendant stamper generations (2AG, 2AH, 2AB, 2BD, etc.).

Fidelity

Mastered from the original Columbia tapes (Paris December 1957 score, NY May 1958 sextet date) for the original 6-eye mono pressing; Columbia's late-1950s NY pressings are generally well regarded by collectors for quiet vinyl and warm, present mono sound, though the source tapes for the Paris date are inherently modest (small studio, minimal mic setup) compared to Columbia's own 30th Street sessions. Notes

1959 Columbia CL 1268 mono - original first pressing. Red/black 6-eye deep-groove labels with correct 1956-61 rim-text order, and runout codes XLP44601-2AE III (side 1) and XLP44602-2G (side 2) lack the TA/P/H plant-suffix letters seen on later Terre Haute, Pitman, and Hollywood repressings of this title, placing this among the earliest NY-plant stampers cut for the release. Deep groove present on both sides per collector grading, consistent with the 1956-61 Columbia 6-eye era. No promo or club markings present.
