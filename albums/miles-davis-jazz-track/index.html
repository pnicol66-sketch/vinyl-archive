The Album

Jazz Track brings together two pivotal but very different Miles Davis dates: the improvised score he recorded in Paris on December 4-5, 1957 for Louis Malle's "Ascenseur pour l'échafaud," and three standards cut in New York on May 26, 1958 with the sextet that would soon record Kind of Blue. The Paris session is one of jazz's most celebrated instances of scoring to picture on the spot, with Davis and a mostly French pickup band reportedly improvising while watching projected footage, yielding a spare, atmospheric music that helped define the vocabulary of jazz film scoring. The New York side documents the first studio pairing of Davis with Bill Evans alongside Cannonball Adderley, John Coltrane, Paul Chambers, and Jimmy Cobb, the same lineup that would shortly make Kind of Blue, and its reading of "Stella By Starlight" is widely regarded by musicians and critics as among the finest on record. Arriving at the hinge between Davis's hard-bop years on Prestige and his modal breakthroughs of 1959, the album shows him working in two starkly different contexts within months of each other. Assembling a European film score alongside a domestic studio date made for an unusual pairing, but it preserved both performances for American audiences before the film's own soundtrack saw domestic release. Critically the record has often stood in the shadow of Davis's more unified statements, yet the Evans-Adderley-Coltrane tracks are prized as essential precursors to Kind of Blue and the Paris material remains a touchstone of jazz-and-film history.

The Label

The side labels show Columbia's classic red/black "6-eye" design with deep groove and the pre-1958 rim-text ordering (walking-eye logo between the circled "Lp" mark and "Marcas Reg."), the mono flagship format bsnpubs and Discogs date to roughly 1956-1961. The runout stampers XLP44601-2AE III and XLP44602-2G carry no plant-suffix letter, distinguishing them from the later Terre Haute ("TA"), Pitman ("P"), and Hollywood ("H") regional repressings of this same title that used descendant stamper generations. That absence of a plant code, combined with the deep-groove 6-eye label, places this copy among Columbia's original New York-plant pressings, essentially contemporaneous with the album's initial late-1958/early-1959 release and ahead of every subsequent regional or later-label variant in the CL 1268 hierarchy.

Fidelity

Mastered from the original Columbia tapes for the December 1957 Paris score and May 1958 New York sextet date, this 6-eye mono pressing represents Columbia's original NY-plant mastering of CL 1268. Columbia's late-1950s New York pressings are generally well regarded by collectors for quiet surfaces and warm, present mono sound, though the Paris film-session tapes are inherently more modest in fidelity than Columbia's own 30th Street studio dates. Steve Hoffman forum consensus favors these original 6-eye mono pressings over the later 360 Sound red-label and Half-Speed/Mastersound-era reissues, and this early no-plant-suffix stamper places the copy at the top of that mono hierarchy; no widely recognized audiophile remaster (Mobile Fidelity, Analogue Productions, etc.) of this specific title exists to supersede it.

Notes

1959 Columbia CL 1268 mono - original first pressing.
