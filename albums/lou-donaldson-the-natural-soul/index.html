The Album

Recorded May 9, 1962 at Rudy Van Gelder's Englewood Cliffs studio and issued in 1963, The Natural Soul finds alto saxophonist Lou Donaldson deep into the soul-jazz groove he pioneered for Blue Note, pairing his blues-drenched, gospel-tinged alto with the young organ-guitar-drums combo of John Patton, Grant Green and Ben Dixon plus trumpeter Tommy Turrentine. Donaldson had already helped define the funky, backbeat-driven strain of hard bop that made jukeboxes and jazz clubs move, and this session - a direct follow-up to his earlier organ-combo dates - doubles down on that formula with original blues lines like "Funky Mama" and "Sow Belly Blues" alongside a warm, unhurried reading of the Gershwin standard "Love Walked In." The album crystallizes a period when Donaldson, criticized by some purists for playing to the room rather than the avant-garde, was quietly building one of the most influential bodies of accessible, blues-based jazz of the era. Its easy, danceable grooves and Patton's percolating organ work helped cement the organ-combo sound that would echo through soul jazz and later soul and funk records. Contemporary liner notes framed Donaldson as one of the music's most consistently satisfying and underrated players, a reputation the album's relaxed, groove-forward sequencing bears out. Decades on, The Natural Soul remains a touchstone of the Blue Note soul-jazz catalogue and a favorite among collectors and DJs drawn to its unpretentious, foot-tapping swing.

The Label

The side labels show the classic white and blue Blue Note design with the arced "33 1/3 MICROGROOVE" legend, "BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC · NEW YORK USA" beneath it, and BLP 4108 with no West 63rd Street or Liberty text - the plain "NEW YORK USA" address used from roughly 1957 into 1963/64, before Blue Note added the "A DIVISION OF LIBERTY RECORDS" line around 1966. This pressing's labels show no deep-groove ridge, corresponding to the no-deep-groove variant of this catalogue number (variant reference 3792268), unlike the deep-groove copies that represent the very earliest stamper runs. That places this copy in the second wave of the original 1963 mono pressing run - a genuine New York-label first edition, just not the earliest deep-groove lacquer.

Fidelity

Cut and pressed from Rudy Van Gelder's original analog masters, with his stamp and the Plastylite "ear" marking an original-era lacquer and pressing plant. Collectors on the forums consistently rate original Blue Note New York mono pressings of this era among the best-sounding versions of these sessions, prized for Van Gelder's punchy, immediate organ-combo sound. This no-deep-groove New York mono copy sits just behind the very earliest deep-groove stamper copies in the collector hierarchy, but well ahead of the later Liberty-era "Division of Liberty Records" pressings and any digital reissues, which soften the tape's original warmth and dynamics.
