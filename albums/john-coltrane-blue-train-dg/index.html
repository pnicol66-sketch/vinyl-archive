The Album

Recorded on September 15, 1957, Blue Train was John Coltrane's only date as a leader for Blue Note during a period when he was still under contract to Prestige, made possible by a one-off agreement that let him front the session. It came at a pivotal moment in Coltrane's rise, shortly after his tenure with Thelonious Monk had sharpened his harmonic daring and while he was still a sideman in the Miles Davis group that would soon make him famous. The front line of Lee Morgan and Curtis Fuller gave the band an unusually rich horn voicing, and the title blues, built on Coltrane's own theme, became one of the defining compositions of the hard bop era. The Label

Both labels on this copy print BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC with the 47 WEST 63rd address on both sides, over a deep-groove label field. Blue Note's labels did not carry the word INC until the end of 1959 - the last catalogue number issued without it was BLP 4016, with BLP 4017 in October 1959 the first to carry it - so a label bearing both the 63rd Street address and INC on both sides was pressed no earlier than that point. That places this copy within the West 63rd Street Inc-label pressing run that continued into the early 1960s, after the catalogue number's true first issue, which carried New York 23 on one side without INC, and after the 63rd Street labels that still lacked INC.

Fidelity

The mono cut was mastered by Rudy Van Gelder from his own Hackensack session tapes and pressed by Plastylite, and this copy's runouts carry the RVG stamp, the Plastylite ear and the same 9M etching found on the first issue, even though the Inc label text places the pressing itself in the 1959-62 window rather than at the catalogue number's first issue. Collectors on the forums consistently rate the original-matrix mono Blue Note pressings of this session as the most natural-sounding versions of the record, ahead of the stereo issue. Among later remasters, the audiophile mono reissues are well regarded for clarity and quietness of surface, but a mono pressing carrying the RVG stamp and the Plastylite ear still ranks close to the top of the record's fidelity hierarchy, just behind copies from the catalogue number's true first-issue labels.

Notes

Blue Note BLP 1577 mono, pressed in the West 63rd Street Inc-label window, roughly 1959 through 1962. Both labels carry BLUE NOTE RECORDS INC at 47 West 63rd over a deep groove; Blue Note did not add INC to its labels until late 1959 (first appearing with BLP 4017), so a copy with INC on both sides cannot be the catalogue number's true first issue, which carried New York 23 on one side without INC, or a pressing on the 63rd Street labels that still lacked INC. The runouts carry the RVG stamp, the Plastylite ear and a 9M etching, the same runout marks as the first issue, indicating this repress was struck from Van Gelder's original mono cut rather than a later remastered one. The collection's other mono copy of this catalogue number carries the same runout marks and grades higher; its labels differ - the 47 West 63rd Inc label on one side and a New York USA label on the other - which date it from late 1961 onward.
