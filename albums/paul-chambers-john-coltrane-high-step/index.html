The Album

High Step gathers three separate mid-1950s sessions led by bassist Paul Chambers, each one featuring a young John Coltrane still several years from his own breakthrough as a leader. The earliest music dates from a 1955 Boston/Detroit date assembled under a shifting leadership credit between Chambers and trombonist Curtis Fuller; the two 1956 dates, one cut in Los Angeles with pianist Kenny Drew and one in New York with Donald Byrd, Kenny Burrell and Horace Silver, had previously appeared as Chambers' Music and The Whims of Chambers. Blue Note compiled the three sessions into this double LP in the mid-1970s as part of a series devoted to unearthing vault material and out-of-print early sides by major artists. The set captures Chambers, one of the defining bassists of the hard bop era and the anchor of the Miles Davis rhythm section of the period, in a series of leader dates that let his own compositions and ensemble ideas stand alongside standards and group originals. For Coltrane it documents a formative period, heard here as a strong but still-developing voice within someone else's band rather than the forward-looking leader he would soon become. Critics have valued the album chiefly as a showcase for the whole cast - Byrd, Burrell, Silver, Fuller and Philly Joe Jones all get real room to play - with Coltrane and Chambers drawing listeners to a record that rewards attention to the group as a whole.

The Label

The side labels are the blue Blue Note Re-Issue Series design with the b-and-note logo in a dark circle at upper left and the United Artists Music And Records Group rim credit, the design this title carried from its 1975 release. This copy's labels print a label code (LC 02516) beside the United Artists rim text, a mark that did not exist on any record before 1976. A label code can only date a pressing from 1976 forward, so despite the ℗1975 copyright line reproduced on the label, this copy was manufactured in 1976 or later, after the title's first pressing run. It therefore sits below the earliest, code-free copies of BN-LA451-H2 in the pressing hierarchy even though the label artwork and catalogue number are unchanged.

Fidelity

High Step was mastered from the mid-1950s session tapes for its mid-1970s Blue Note Re-Issue Series release rather than cut from a dedicated audiophile source, and the United Artists-era US pressing plants used for the series are generally regarded by collectors as adequate but unremarkable compared to the original Blue Note Lexington/Plastylite-era pressings of this music's component sessions. No audiophile remastering of this specific compilation exists, so this mono pressing - a later repress within the same Re-Issue Series run - sits at the ordinary end of the title's fidelity spectrum rather than near the top of it.
