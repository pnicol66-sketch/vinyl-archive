The Album

Recorded across a handful of January 1965 sessions, this album marks the hinge point where its author turned from the solo acoustic topical-folk singer of his early records toward electric rock and surreal, stream-of-consciousness songwriting. The first side plunges into full electric band arrangements - rollicking, talking-blues energy driven by guitar, bass and drums - while the second retreats to solo acoustic guitar-and-harmonica settings, now carrying far stranger, more visionary lyrics than before. The record followed a period of frenetic touring and rising fame, and its songs reflect a restless, satirical, increasingly abstract sensibility that moved away from direct protest writing toward imagery, wordplay and interior monologue. The shift alienated part of the folk audience that had embraced him as a protest-song figurehead, setting up controversies that followed him onto stages later that year, but it also opened a new audience and vocabulary for rock songwriting. Critics recognized it immediately as a major artistic leap, and it remains one of the most studied and influential records in popular music, prized for its wit, density of allusion, and the audacity of an artist reinventing his own sound in real time. It stands as the first part of a celebrated mid-1960s trilogy that redefined what a folk or rock lyric could attempt.

The Label

The side labels show Columbia's red two-eye 'NONBREAKABLE' mono design: a notes-and-microphone eye logo boxed at the left and right rim positions, 'COLUMBIA' arched across the top, and '360 SOUND' arrows with 'MONO' set at the foot of the rim. Columbia used this two-eye mono layout through the mid-1960s into 1967 before the eye logo was eventually phased out; the presence of the '360 SOUND' / 'MONO' rim wording (rather than the earlier 'GUARANTEED HIGH FIDELITY' legend used on the very first label print for this title) places this copy within the label's slightly later two-eye print run rather than its absolute first layout. The Side 1 'LOVE MINUS ZERO' title is set with an underline, matching the early textual layout of this title. No pressing-plant letter appears in the runout on this copy, so the plant cannot be fixed from the label alone.

Fidelity

This mono cut was mastered from the 1965 session tapes for Columbia's in-house mono release, cut for the label's standard mono LP chain of the period. Collectors generally rate the early two-eye mono pressings of this title among the better-sounding copies, prizing the mono fold-down for the way it presents the electric side's band interplay. Against later reissues and the stereo cut, this two-eye mono pressing sits toward the top of the album's fidelity hierarchy for collectors who prefer the original mono mix, though it is not distinguished here by any audible mastering-stamp lineage in the runout.

Notes

Columbia CL 2328 mono, an early two-eye pressing of this title. The label carries the red two-eye 'NONBREAKABLE' design with '360 SOUND' and 'MONO' rim text rather than the 'GUARANTEED HIGH FIDELITY' wording of the very first label print, placing it just behind the earliest label layout in the print run. The runout reads XLP-79421-1C 0 A / XLP-79422-1C 0 C, which does not carry the 'T' Terre Haute plant letter or the 'o'/suffix pattern seen on some copies of this catalogue number, so the pressing plant is not identified from the runout alone.
