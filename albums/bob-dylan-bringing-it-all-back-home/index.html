The Album

Recorded in two breathless sessions in January 1965, this album marks the hinge point where Bob Dylan turned from the acoustic topical-folk singer of his early records toward electric rock and surreal, stream-of-consciousness songwriting. Side one plunges into full electric band arrangements - rollicking, talking-blues energy driven by guitar, bass and drums - while side two retreats to the solo acoustic guitar-and-harmonica setting that had made his name, now carrying far stranger, more visionary lyrics. The record followed a period of frenetic touring and rising fame, and its songs reflect a restless, satirical, and increasingly abstract sensibility, moving away from direct protest writing toward imagery, wordplay, and interior monologue. Dylan's shift alienated some of the folk audience that had embraced him as a protest-song figurehead, setting up the controversy that would follow him onto stages later that year, but it also opened an entirely new audience and vocabulary for rock songwriting. Critics recognized it immediately as a major artistic leap, and it has remained one of the most studied and influential records in popular music, prized for its wit, its density of allusion, and the audacity of an artist reinventing his own sound in real time. It stands as the first part of a celebrated mid-1960s trilogy that redefined what a folk or rock lyric could attempt.

The Label

The side labels are the red Columbia two-eye design, with the concentric-circle eye logo printed once at each side of the spindle, "NONBREAKABLE" and "360 SOUND" rim text, and the Side 1 track "LOVE MINUS ZERO" set with an underline rather than a slash. This two-eye NONBREAKABLE design was Columbia's mono label through the mid-1960s for this title. The runout carries no plant-identifying stamp, placing this copy within that broader two-eye mono pressing run rather than pinning it to a specific plant or stamper generation.
