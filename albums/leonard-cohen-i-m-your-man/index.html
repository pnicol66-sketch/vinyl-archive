The Album

Released in February 1988, I'm Your Man marked a decisive turn in Leonard Cohen's career, trading the spare acoustic guitar of his earlier records for synthesizers, drum machines and a wry, world-weary baritone that had deepened over the preceding decade. Written and recorded in Montreal, Los Angeles and Paris, the songs were largely composed on keyboards, and collaborators such as Jeff Fisher brought a cinematic, almost noirish production sheen to tracks like “First We Take Manhattan” and “Jazz Police”. The album opens with a brooding fantasy of vengeance and renewal and closes with “Tower of Song”, one of Cohen's most quoted self-portraits of the aging troubadour. “Everybody Knows”, co-written with Sharon Robinson, became one of his most covered and sampled songs, its cynical litany of societal hypocrisy proving durable well beyond its era. “Take This Waltz” sets Cohen's translation of a Federico García Lorca poem to music, linking the record to his lifelong engagement with poetry. The album became Cohen's most commercially successful to date in several markets and is now regarded by critics and longtime listeners as a career-redefining comeback, reestablishing him as a vital songwriter for a new generation while retaining the literary density and dark humor of his earlier work.

The Label

The side labels show a modern salmon-red Columbia design with four Columbia “eye” devices spaced around the rim and a Music On Vinyl script logo at lower-right of the centre hole, printed with a small-print credit line reading “Originally released ℗ & © 1988 Sony Music Entertainment. This release ℗ & © 2011 Sony Music Entertainment” and “Manufactured & distributed by Music On Vinyl B.V.”. This design and credit block belongs to the Music On Vinyl era of Columbia licensing reissues that began in the early 2010s, well after Columbia's 1980s “stock” label designs. The 2011 copyright line fixes this pressing as a later reissue of the 1988 album, sitting below the original Columbia/CBS pressings in the release history.

Fidelity

This Music On Vinyl pressing was remastered for the 2011 reissue program and manufactured at a plant known for consistent, well-centered 180-gram pressings. Collectors on the forums generally rate Music On Vinyl's Cohen reissues as a clean, quiet upgrade over worn original copies, though they are not treated as a definitive audiophile event on the level of a dedicated mastering-house cut. Within the album's fidelity hierarchy, the original 1988 Columbia/CBS pressings remain the baseline for purists chasing a first-generation cut, with this reissue valued chiefly for its quiet surfaces and availability rather than for surpassing the originals sonically.

Notes

This is the 2011 Music On Vinyl 180-gram reissue of Leonard Cohen's 1988 album, pressed in Europe for Columbia/Sony. The side labels carry a 2011 copyright line (“This release ℗ & © 2011 Sony Music Entertainment”) alongside the original 1988 copyright, which fixes manufacture at 2011 or later regardless of the catalogue number or jacket design reproducing the 1988 release. The hand-etched runouts (91027 1A/1B MOVLP 424) match the catalogue number and are consistent with the Music On Vinyl pressing chain. It is a later, remastered reissue sitting below the 1988 Columbia/CBS originals in the pressing hierarchy.
