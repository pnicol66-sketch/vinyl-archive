This Pressing

Danish Debut pressing (c. 1957-early 1960s) - grey "debut" label with "MADE IN DENMARK" rim text, licensed by Skandinavisk Grammofon A/S/Brande Boghandel; stamped DEB-120-A/DEB-120-B matrices, not the 1955 US first pressing.

The Album

Blue Moods was recorded on July 9, 1955, at Van Gelder Studio in Hackensack, New Jersey, for Charles Mingus and Max Roach's fiercely independent, artist-owned Debut Records, cut as Miles Davis was climbing back from the personal low point of his heroin years toward the triumphant Prestige and Columbia periods that followed within months. Rather than assembling a conventional hard-bop unit, Mingus paired Davis with an unusual chamber-jazz lineup built around Teddy Charles's vibraphone and Britt Woodman's trombone, with Mingus himself on bass and a young Elvin Jones on drums, producing a spare, unhurried session built on standards plus Mingus's own arrangement of "Alone Together" (the remaining tracks were arranged by Charles). The album's brevity - just four tunes across a lean twenty-seven minutes - and its moody, unhurried restraint anticipated the cool, spacious aesthetic Davis would refine within a few years on Kind of Blue, making it a small but telling document of Davis mid-transformation. Davis reportedly resented the unconventional instrumentation Mingus assigned him, and the resulting session captures an uneasy but fruitful tension between a leader eager to move forward and a co-op label pursuing its own experimental agenda. Blue Moods has long been regarded by historians less as a commercial landmark than as a fascinating way-station in Davis's stylistic evolution, and it remains a significant artifact of the short-lived but historically important Mingus-Roach Debut label. Critical assessment has generally been favorable if modest, with the album prized chiefly by collectors of Davis's complete discography and of the Debut catalogue rather than ranked among his major statements.

The Label

The label photos show the grey Debut label with the star-topped script "debut" logo and small "33 1/3 rpm LONGPLAY" banner, with "MADE IN DENMARK" arced at the top rim in place of the New York street address printed on the 1955 US original grey label. Danish label history (documented via steppeulvene.com and Danish discography sources) records that in 1957 the Brande Boghandel bookshop, working with Skandinavisk Grammofon A/S, secured rights to press the American Debut catalogue for the Scandinavian market, retaining the original US catalogue number (DEB 120) and cover artwork. That places this copy in the post-1957 Danish licensed pressing run - later than the true 1955 US first pressing but predating Debut's absorption into the Fantasy reissue program (1962) and the later French and OJC reissue generations. The stamped (rather than etched) DEB-120-A/DEB-120-B matrix format is consistent with an early-generation Danish plate rather than a later re-cut, though without a runout photo the exact stamper/plant details cannot be confirmed beyond the label design itself.

Fidelity

The back cover documents the original 1955 mastering approach: cut at 160 lines per inch instead of the usual 210-260, deliberately widening and deepening the grooves to allow greater bass extension and level - a hallmark of the Debut/Mingus-supervised Van Gelder cutting rather than a later remaster. This Danish pressing reproduces that mastering philosophy via a European-cut lacquer derived from the US plates/tape rather than the original US metal parts, so fidelity is generally regarded by collectors as respectable but a step below a clean US first pressing pressed directly from Van Gelder's original masters. Steve Hoffman forum consensus favors the original 1955 US Debut pressing or the 1962 Fantasy red-vinyl reissue for the most faithful, full-bodied transfer, with the Danish pressing occupying a middle tier and the later French fake-stereo and OJC reissues ranked lowest in the overall fidelity hierarchy. Surface quality on Danish Debut pressings varies by copy since the exact stamping/plant chain is not independently documented.

Notes

This is a Danish licensed Debut pressing of DEB-120, not the 1955 US first pressing, produced under the Brande Boghandel/Skandinavisk Grammofon A/S arrangement that began in 1957. The "MADE IN DENMARK" rim text on an otherwise US-style grey Debut label, paired with stamped (not later fake-stereo etched) DEB-120-A/B matrices, places this within the early Danish run rather than the 1960s French or US reissue lines. Cover and label copy reproduce the original 1955 back-cover text and mastering notes verbatim, indicating the Danish pressing used plates derived from the US artwork and liner notes rather than a redesigned Scandinavian issue. No runout photo was supplied, so stamper/plant-level confirmation beyond the label design itself is not possible.
