The Album

Recorded on February 7, 1958 at Van Gelder Studio in Hackensack, New Jersey, Soultrane captured John Coltrane at a pivotal moment - freshly returned to the Miles Davis fold after kicking heroin, playing with the quartet-turned-trio-plus-Trane lineup of Red Garland, Paul Chambers and Arthur Taylor that had backed him on his prior Prestige dates. The album is built almost entirely on standards and jazz staples rather than originals, letting Coltrane stretch his emerging "sheets of sound" approach across familiar changes, most dramatically on the extended workout of Tadd Dameron's "Good Bait." Liner notes by Ira Gitler, who is often credited with coining the "sheets of sound" phrase around this period, frame the record as a showcase of Coltrane's harmonic density and relentless self-critical drive to improve. "Theme For Ernie," a Fred Lacey tune dedicated to the recently deceased saxophonist Ernie Henry, gives the album a note of elegy amid the otherwise driving program. Soultrane arrived during Coltrane's prolific 1955-1958 run as a prominent sideman and emerging leader on Prestige, just before his stint with Thelonious Monk and his eventual signing to Atlantic, and it stands as one of the key documents of his transition from promising tenor man to a fully formed and increasingly exploratory voice. It remains a touchstone of hard-bop tenor playing and is widely regarded by critics and historians as one of the strongest of Coltrane's Prestige-era leader dates.

The Label

The side labels are the lemon-yellow "fireworks" Prestige design with the 203 South Washington Ave., Bergenfield, N.J. address and "HIGH FIDELITY" printed in caps at the right of the spindle - the classic 1958 New York-era Prestige label used before the company's mid-1960s design changes. Both sides show a deep groove, consistent with the deep-groove Bergenfield-address labels typical of Prestige's late-1950s pressings. The label typography and address place this copy squarely in the 1958 first-issue manufacturing window for PRLP 7142, ahead of the 1960s reprocessed-stereo and Japanese/European reissues.

Fidelity

Mastered and recorded by Rudy Van Gelder at his Hackensack studio directly to lacquer, this original Prestige pressing carries the punchy, close-miked Van Gelder sound collectors prize on 1950s Prestige hard-bop titles. Collectors on the forums generally rank early deep-groove Prestige originals with RVG stamps above the 1970s reprocessed-stereo and most later reissues for tonal immediacy, though surface noise and mastering roughness (a known Van Gelder trait of the era) are common complaints. Within the Soultrane fidelity hierarchy, this mono deep-groove first-pressing sits at the top alongside other early-stamper copies, ahead of the 1960s stereo "reprocessed" Prestige pressings (which are electronically rechanneled and generally disfavored), the OJC reissues mastered from later-generation tapes, and modern audiophile cuts such as the Analogue Productions 180g mono edition, which improves surface quality and quiet but is sourced from tape generations further from Van Gelder's original lacquer.
