The Album

Songs From A Room is Leonard Cohen's second album, recorded in Nashville after sessions begun in Hollywood with David Crosby were abandoned, with producer Bob Johnston bringing in a leaner country-inflected band to frame Cohen's increasingly austere songwriting. Cohen had emerged from the Montreal and New York literary scenes as a published poet and novelist before turning to songwriting in his thirties, and this second record consolidated the persona of weary troubadour-prophet established on his 1967 debut. The album includes 'Bird on the Wire,' which Cohen would later call something close to a personal credo, and 'Story of Isaac,' a meditation on war and sacrifice drawn from the biblical story of Abraham and Isaac; it also features his stark reading of the resistance-movement song 'The Partisan.' Sparer and starker than its predecessor, with Cohen's voice set against minimal guitar, banjo and bass rather than lush orchestration, the album marked a deliberate move toward plainness that would define much of his subsequent work. It was received by critics as a bleaker, more disciplined successor to the debut, cementing Cohen's reputation as one of the era's most distinctive and uncompromising singer-songwriters. Decades on, it remains a touchstone of the confessional folk tradition and a key entry in the Cohen catalogue for the songs it introduced into his permanent repertoire.

The Label

Both sides carry a plain orange ground with black print, a single boxed CBS walking-eye logo centred on the spindle, and MADE IN ENGLAND printed at the foot, identifying this as a UK-manufactured copy rather than the red-label Dutch/European pressing sometimes found under the same catalogue number. CBS used this orange walking-eye design on its UK albums through the late 1960s and into the 1970s before later re-typeset variants appeared, so the design alone places the copy generally in that era window rather than pinning a precise year; the publishing credits and ℗ dates printed on the labels (1944, 1967, 1968, 1969) are consistent with the album's 1969 release. The side 1 and side 2 labels differ slightly in the arrow symbol beside STEREO and in a minor line-spacing variation in the publisher credits, but both share the same orange one-eye design and rim text, so they are the same pressing generation.

Fidelity

This pressing carries the ordinary CBS UK stereo cut of the period rather than any identified audiophile remastering; mastering engineer is not credited on the jacket or labels. Collectors generally regard the original CBS pressings, mono or stereo, as solid, warm-sounding cuts typical of late-1960s CBS UK product, without the brightness some later reissues introduce. Later specialist reissues on 150g and 180g vinyl exist and are prized by some for their quieter surfaces, but the original stereo UK pressing remains a well-regarded source cut in its own right among collectors of this title.

Notes

This is a 1969 UK CBS stereo pressing of Songs From A Room on the orange walking-eye label with MADE IN ENGLAND printed at the foot of both sides. The runout reads A3/B6 stamper codes along with additional mastering letters/numbers; a Dutch-printed, red-label copy exists under the same catalogue number, but the labels on this copy are orange and read MADE IN ENGLAND, placing it as the UK-made stereo issue rather than that Dutch-printed pressing. Publishing (P) dates of 1944, 1967, 1968 and 1969 on the labels are consistent with the album's original 1969 release window.
