The Album

Burglar arrived in 1974 as Freddie King's first album for RSO, cut mostly at Chipping Norton Studios in rural England with producer Mike Vernon and a crew of British session players, with one Miami-cut track rounding out the set. King was by then an established veteran of the Texas blues scene whose early 1960s instrumentals had already shaped a generation of guitarists, and his move to a rock-oriented label reflected the blues-rock audience Eric Clapton and other admirers had helped build for him. The session brought King together informally with Clapton, whose own Texas-blues fandom had long championed King's playing, lending the record some of its loose, rock-leaning energy alongside straight blues and funk-inflected numbers. Songs ranged from barbed commentary on hard times to horn-driven shuffles, sourced from a mix of outside writers and band originals credited jointly to King and his backing musicians. Critics have long regarded the album as one of the stronger entries in King's 1970s run, prized for the interplay between his guitar and the tight UK backing band even though it produced no chart hit on the order of his early singles. It stands as a late-career high point from an artist who would record only a little more before his death two years later, and it remains a touchstone for collectors of his post-Shelter, RSO-era work.

The Label

The side labels on this copy are flat (no deep groove), tan/cream in colour, carrying a single red bull wearing a collar beside the white script "RSO" ampersand mark, with the artist name and album title set below it in black, "STEREO" and the catalogue number at left, the side number and a 1974 copyright line at right, and rim text crediting distribution by Atlantic Recording Corporation at 75 Rockefeller Plaza, New York. This tan bull-logo design is the only US label design recorded for RSO's domestic releases of this catalogue number, so its presence here does not by itself separate an earlier from a later pressing of SO 4803 - any such distinction would rest on the stamper and runout detail, which is a separate question from the label artwork.

Fidelity

The album was produced largely by Mike Vernon at Chipping Norton with engineer Barry Hammond, with the Criteria-cut "Sugar Sweet" engineered separately by Steve Klein under Tom Dowd and remixed by Vernon; no audiophile remaster or specialist reissue of this title exists, so the original vinyl mix is the only cut collectors have to judge. Collectors generally describe the sound as solid but unspectacular 1970s rock-studio blues, with the guitar tones favored over deep low end, and there is no competing hi-fi reissue to outrank it - this pressing is simply the record's reference cut.

Notes

1974 RSO SO 4803 stereo - the standard US first pressing of this title. The side labels carry the stock RSO tan/red-bull design with a -MO plant code pointing to Monarch Record Mfg. Co., and the runout carries a Specialty Records Corporation logo alongside a Monarch stamp: SRC-cut stampers run on Monarch's presses. Nothing on the labels or jacket marks a later reissue or a different catalogue design for this number.
