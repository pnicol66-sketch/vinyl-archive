This Pressing

First-issue pressing (mono, 1957) – black Atlantic label, deep groove both sides, plain rim text with no fan logo.

The Album

Recorded in New York on January 5 and 7, 1957, this album finds vibraphonist Milt Jackson stepping outside the Modern Jazz Quartet to lead two different all-star sessions — one arranged by a young Quincy Jones with Cannonball Adderley, Art Blakey and Percy Heath, the other a tighter unit built around Lucky Thompson, Oscar Pettiford and Horace Silver, who plays on both dates. The record captured Jackson at the crest of the emerging soul-jazz movement, its bluesy, gospel-tinged themes and driving grooves reflecting the hard-bop-meets-blues sensibility Adderley and Silver were championing elsewhere in the era's jazz. Jackson had already established himself as the era's premier vibraphonist through his own quartet dates and ongoing MJQ work, and this session let him stretch into a bigger, funkier idiom outside that group's chamber-jazz restraint. Largely built on originals by Jackson and Jones, the material gave the horns room to blow over relaxed blues changes, and the chemistry between Jackson's crystalline vibes and Adderley's earthy alto became a highlight of the date. The record has long been cited as a high point of late-1950s soul jazz, prized for its unforced swing and the interplay of musicians who were each becoming leaders in their own right.

The Label

Both sides carry a deep-groove label: a dark grey ground with a large white arced ATLANTIC script across the top, 'LONG PLAYING'/'UNBREAKABLE' wrapping the lower rim, a 33 1/3 RPM circle, and a 'HIGH FIDELITY' callout, with no fan/target logo of any kind. This plain black design was Atlantic's standing label through the mid-to-late 1950s, before the white fan-logo design replaced it around 1960-61. The deep groove on both sides places this pressing within that black-label first-issue window, ahead of the later black-label pressings that lost the deep groove and well ahead of the fan-logo mono reissue.

Fidelity

Mastering for the original 1957 Atlantic sides is credited to Atlantic's in-house engineers of the era; the runouts are otherwise hand-etched apart from a pressing-plant stamp, typical of Atlantic's 1950s mono jazz pressings farmed out to an RCA pressing plant. Collectors on the forums generally rate original black-label Atlantic mono jazz pressings as full-bodied and warm, ahead of the later stereo remix (which narrows the soundstage) and well ahead of 1970s reissues and a 2020s colored-vinyl pressing with reduced channel separation. Within the album's fidelity hierarchy, this deep-groove mono first pressing sits at the top, followed by the 1960s black-label non-DG mono repress, the fan-logo mono reissue, the stereo reissue, and finally the compact-disc remaster and the 2020s audiophile colored-vinyl edition.

Notes

1957 Atlantic 1269 mono – consistent with a first pressing. Side labels show the plain black Atlantic design (no fan/target logo) with deep groove on both sides, the title's 1st-release black deep-groove variant. The runouts are hand-etched, with pressings of this variant typically also carrying an RCA Indianapolis plant stamp. The plain black rim design without the later fan logo places this within Atlantic's earliest 1200-series design window, and the deep groove narrows it toward the front of that run.
