The Album

Recorded in New York on January 5 and 7, 1957, this album finds vibraphonist Milt Jackson stepping outside the Modern Jazz Quartet to lead two different all-star sessions, one arranged by a young Quincy Jones with Cannonball Adderley, Art Blakey and Percy Heath, the other a tighter unit built around Lucky Thompson, Oscar Pettiford and Horace Silver, who plays on both dates. The record captured Jackson at the crest of the emerging soul-jazz movement, its bluesy, gospel-tinged themes and driving grooves reflecting the hard-bop-meets-blues sensibility Adderley and Silver were championing elsewhere on Atlantic and Blue Note. Jackson had already established himself as the era's premier vibraphonist through his own quartet dates and ongoing MJQ work, and this session let him stretch into a bigger, funkier idiom outside that group's chamber-jazz restraint. Largely built on originals by Jackson and Jones, the material gave the horns room to blow over relaxed blues changes, and the chemistry between Jackson's crystalline vibes and Adderley's earthy alto became a highlight of the date. Critics have long cited the record as a high point of late-1950s soul jazz, prized for its unforced swing and the interplay of musicians who were each becoming leaders in their own right. The Label

The side labels show Atlantic's plain black house design: arced white "ATLANTIC" logo, no fan/target logo, "LONG PLAYING"/"UNBREAKABLE" wrapping the lower rim, and "HIGH FIDELITY" callout - the design used from the label's inception through roughly 1960-61 before the white fan-logo label appeared. Deep groove is present on both sides per the collector, and Discogs documents this exact combination (plain black label + deep groove) as the title's 1st-release variant, release r3497352. That placement puts this copy at the front of the black-label run, prior to the later non-DG black pressings and well before the early-1960s fan-logo mono reissue.

Fidelity

Mastering for the original 1957 Atlantic sides is credited to Atlantic's in-house engineers of the era; the runouts are otherwise hand-etched apart from an RCA Indianapolis pressing-plant stamp, typical of Atlantic's 1950s mono jazz pressings farmed out to RCA's Indianapolis plant. Collectors on forums such as Steve Hoffman generally rate original black-label Atlantic mono jazz pressings as full-bodied and warm, ahead of the later stereo remix (which narrows the soundstage) and well ahead of the 1970s reissues and the 2020s DMM colored-vinyl pressing, which reviewers note lacks adequate channel separation. Within the album's fidelity hierarchy, this deep-groove mono first pressing sits at the top, followed by the 1960s black-label non-DG mono repress, the fan-logo mono reissue, the stereo (SD) reissue, and finally the CD remaster and 2020s audiophile colored-vinyl edition.

Notes

1957 Atlantic 1269 mono - consistent with a first pressing. Side labels show the plain black Atlantic design (no fan/target logo) with deep groove noted on both sides, matching Discogs' documented first-issue black deep-groove variant (release r3497352) for this title. That release's runouts show a hand-etched "11447 AT A" / "11448B ATI A" with an RCA Indianapolis "I" pressing-plant stamp, consistent with the collector's "11447B AT"/"11448B AT 3" transcription though the plant stamp itself isn't noted in the typed matrix. The plain black rim design without the later fan logo places this within Atlantic's earliest 1200-series design window, and the deep groove narrows it toward the front of that run.
