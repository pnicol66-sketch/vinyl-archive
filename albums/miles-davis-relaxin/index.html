The Album

Relaxin' with the Miles Davis Quintet was drawn from two marathon 1956 sessions - May 11 and October 26 - in which Miles Davis, needing to fulfill his Prestige contract before departing for Columbia Records, cut enough material with his newly formed working band to fill four LPs (the others being Cookin', Workin', and Steamin'). The quintet paired Davis with John Coltrane, whose tenor was still developing the harmonic aggression that would later define him, alongside the tight, blues-rooted rhythm section of Red Garland, Paul Chambers, and Philly Joe Jones. Rather than over-rehearsing, producer Bob Weinstock and engineer Rudy Van Gelder let the band run through mostly first or near-first takes of standards and a couple of jazz staples, capturing the ensemble's remarkable in-the-moment interplay, including studio patter that became part of the record's charm. This quintet is widely regarded as the First Great Quintet, a pivotal unit in Davis's career that bridged his cool-toned early work and the harder-driving, blues-inflected hard bop he would refine through the late 1950s. The album's relaxed-tempo readings of ballads like 'You're My Everything' alongside the incisive swing of 'Oleo' and 'Woodyn' You' showcase the group's dynamic range and remain touchstones of the hard bop repertoire. Critically, the record and its sister releases are viewed as essential documents of a band operating at peak chemistry just before Coltrane's departure and Davis's move to a bigger label and format, cementing the album's lasting stature in the jazz canon.

The Label

This copy carries the earlier Prestige yellow-and-black "fireworks" label design reading "PRESTIGE RECORDS INC. \u2022 446 W. 50TH ST., N.Y.C.", the address Prestige used for its NYC office before relocating operations to Bergenfield, New Jersey around 1958-59. Discogs and dealer forensics consistently identify the 446 W. 50th St. label as the earliest label variant for PRLP 7129, predating the otherwise-identical yellow/black label bearing the Bergenfield, N.J. address. Both labels here show deep-groove rings, a hallmark of Prestige/Van Gelder pressings from the RCA Custom-pressed era of the late 1950s. The combination of the NYC address, deep groove, and hand-etched RVG in the (transcribed) runout places this copy at the top of the PRLP 7129 pressing hierarchy - the first-issue label variant.

Fidelity

Mastered and cut by Rudy Van Gelder direct from his Hackensack studio tapes, this original mono pressing represents the sound closest to the source before any RVG remastering for later reissues or the CD era. Collectors on forums like Steve Hoffman generally prize these first-issue Prestige mono pressings for their warmth and immediacy, though quality can vary with stamper generation - the 'C' stamper here is a later cut in the run rather than the very first. In the overall fidelity hierarchy for this title, this NYC-address deep-groove mono original sits above the later Bergenfield-era pressings and the 1970s-80s Prestige/Fantasy reissues, and above the RVG-remastered CD; it ranks below only an earlier-stamper (A or B) copy of the same NYC label pressing, with modern audiophile vinyl reissues (OJC, Speakers Corner, APO) offering cleaner surfaces but a different, more modern-EQ'd take on the mastering.

Notes
